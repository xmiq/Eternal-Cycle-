# Institutions and Academies

## Status

This document defines the canonical structure, operation, continuity, and adjudication of institutions and academies for Phase 5. Read [Human Class Philosophy](HUMAN_CLASS_PHILOSOPHY.md), [Human Classes](CLASSES.md), [Professions](PROFESSIONS.md), and [Social Advancement](SOCIAL_ADVANCEMENT.md) first.

These rules describe reusable social structures. Particular institutions, members, offices, resources, disputes, and current conditions are campaign state and remain outside this repository.

## Core Rule

An Institution is a durable social arrangement that coordinates roles, authority, knowledge, resources, procedures, and expectations beyond one immediate relationship.

An Academy is an Institution whose sustained purposes substantially include organized learning, inquiry, assessment, preservation, translation, or transmission of knowledge and practice.

Neither is a character class, capability package, collective mind, moral authority, building, rank, or source of automatic mastery. An institution can act only through the people, procedures, relationships, resources, powers, and other causes it can currently coordinate.

One institution may host several Classes, Professions, Martial Traditions, and Magical Schools. One Class or Profession may span several institutions or none. Institutional membership does not install any of them in a person.

## What Is Not an Institution

### Not Merely a Group

A temporary gathering, crowd, adventuring party, audience, friendship circle, or one-time coalition is not automatically an Institution. It may become one if durable coordination, continuity, roles, procedures, resources, and expectations emerge through current history.

### Not Merely a Building

A campus, fortress, temple, workshop, archive, ship, territory, or network node may support an Institution. The physical site is infrastructure. A site may outlive the Institution, and an Institution may migrate, distribute itself, or operate without permanent property.

### Not a Human Class

A Class is a socially sustained framework for interpreting and transmitting expertise. An Institution is an organized arrangement that may teach, regulate, suppress, sponsor, reinterpret, or compete over Classes. Owning a name or school does not make the two identical.

### Not a Profession

A Profession is a field of integrated work and responsibility. An Institution may employ, license, educate, coordinate, or exploit practitioners without becoming the Profession. Independent and informal practitioners may remain part of the Profession outside it.

### Not a Faction or Culture

A faction is a coalition or organized interest pursuing goals, while a culture is a historically changing pattern of shared meanings and practices. Either may create, inhabit, divide, capture, or oppose an Institution. The Institution remains the particular arrangement of roles, authority, procedure, resources, and continuity.

### Not a Collective Person by Default

An Institution does not possess one mind, intention, memory, Skill set, loyalty, or moral character merely because people speak in its name. Institutional action must be traced to authorized people, procedures, distributed cognition, automated systems, spirits, or other actual agents.

Some monster or magical institutions may include a genuine distributed person, governing spirit, construct, or collective consciousness. That person's agency and capabilities belong to its own rules. It does not erase the separate agency of members or turn all institutional acts into its personal acts.

## Institution Validity Test

A proposed Institution should establish all of the following at a scale appropriate to its form:

1. **Continuing purpose or function.** Participants can identify what recurring need, value, activity, obligation, or claim the arrangement serves.
2. **Participants and roles.** Particular people, collectives, offices, or other agents perform differentiated contributions or hold recognizable relationships to it.
3. **Coordination.** Decisions, communication, timing, standards, or mutual expectations connect those contributions beyond coincidence.
4. **Current means.** The arrangement can access some relevant people, knowledge, records, resources, infrastructure, authority, relationships, or practices.
5. **Continuity.** Memory, procedure, succession, routine, commitment, or repeated re-formation lets it persist beyond one meeting or one relationship.
6. **Social reality.** Participants or affected audiences currently treat at least some institutional claims, acts, boundaries, or consequences as meaningful.

Formal law, a written charter, property, hierarchy, permanent membership, a building, universal recognition, and benevolent purpose are not required.

If only a name, founder, abandoned building, historical record, legal shell, or claimant remains, record the remnant truthfully. It may be a dormant, hollow, disputed, successor, reconstructed, or extinct Institution rather than a fully operating one.

## Institutional Forms

Institutions may be:

- formal or informal;
- centralized, federated, distributed, cellular, itinerant, seasonal, or site-bound;
- voluntary, compulsory, inherited, imposed, negotiated, or contested;
- public, private, communal, sacred, commercial, military, familial, professional, educational, or mixed;
- short-lived but durable across many participants, or ancient but repeatedly transformed;
- individual-led, council-governed, democratic, hereditary, algorithmic, ritual, distributed, spirit-mediated, or governed through several competing processes;
- human, monster, mixed-species, artificial, symbiotic, or otherwise nonhuman in embodiment and communication.

These descriptions do not form a ladder. A centralized academy is not inherently stronger or more advanced than an oral travelling academy. The relevant question is whether each form can perform its claimed functions under current conditions.

## Institution Profile

Use an Institution Profile when a claim requires more than the Institution's name. Record only fields that affect the current purpose.

| Field | What it records |
| --- | --- |
| **Identity and version** | Names, translations, branches, predecessors, successors, period, disputed identities, and the current arrangement being assessed. |
| **Purposes and claims** | Stated purposes, actual recurring functions, contested purposes, and claims the Institution makes about itself. |
| **Constituencies and affected parties** | Members, workers, students, clients, subjects, neighbors, ecosystems, spirits, species, and others who support or bear consequences. |
| **Participation relationships** | Membership, employment, study, office, patronage, service, regulation, captivity, alliance, dependence, opposition, or another actual relationship. |
| **Roles and offices** | Defined responsibilities, permissions, limits, selection routes, terms, delegation, review, and succession. |
| **Governance** | Who may propose, decide, veto, interpret, enforce, appeal, audit, or change rules, and through which procedures. |
| **Authority and jurisdiction** | What the Institution may validly authorize, forbid, certify, represent, or enforce, according to which sources and audiences. |
| **Resources and infrastructure** | People, property, money, tools, sites, supply, information, magic, technology, protection, and maintenance dependencies. |
| **Collective capabilities** | Functions the actual coordination can perform, with component roles, procedures, conditions, evidence, and failure points. |
| **Procedures and standards** | Repeated methods for work, safety, admission, review, records, discipline, allocation, care, or decision. |
| **Knowledge and memory** | Records, trained people, oral practice, embodied media, archives, habits, relationships, access rules, loss risks, and contested interpretations. |
| **External relationships** | Law, communities, patrons, suppliers, rivals, allies, professions, classes, governments, gods, spirits, ecosystems, and other Institutions. |
| **Legitimacy and reputation** | Which audiences accept, tolerate, fear, resist, misunderstand, or deny its claims and why. |
| **Accountability** | Duties, review routes, complaints, remedies, sanctions, transparency, responsibility, and who can make them effective. |
| **Continuity and succession** | How roles, knowledge, resources, obligations, identity, and authority survive departure, death, division, migration, or disaster. |
| **Current capacity and condition** | What can function now, what is strained or unavailable, and which dependencies or conflicts limit action. |

The Institution Profile is a reusable structure. A completed profile for one current academy or guild belongs in an external Campaign Record.

## Institutional Roles and Offices

An Institutional Office is a defined institutional role carrying current responsibilities, permissions, limits, procedures, and accountability.

An Office may be selected through election, appointment, inheritance, rotation, examination, lottery, service, ritual, conquest, automation, emergency necessity, or another causal route. The route can make the Office real within its scope without proving the officeholder's competence or legitimacy to every audience.

An Office does not:

- grant personal Skills or Development;
- make every statement an institutional decision;
- transfer all institutional authority to the holder;
- erase term limits, procedure, oversight, resources, or jurisdiction;
- survive resignation, removal, death, or Reincarnation unless the Institution has a valid current succession rule.

Titles such as dean, master, captain, elder, examiner, archivist, or rector are local labels. Their powers must be established from the current Institution rather than inferred from the word.

## Governance and Institutional Action

Governance coordinates how an Institution forms intentions, makes decisions, resolves disputes, and changes itself. It may include:

- proposal and agenda control;
- deliberation and consultation;
- voting, consensus, command, ritual, precedent, or delegated judgment;
- eligibility and representation;
- quorum, timing, notice, secrecy, and emergency procedures;
- veto, appeal, review, audit, and correction;
- enforcement and practical implementation;
- amendment, succession, and dissolution.

### Institutional Action Claim

An Institutional Action Claim asserts that an Institution decided, knew, promised, authorized, prohibited, certified, taught, owned, owed, or performed something.

Resolve the claim by identifying:

1. the exact act or fact claimed;
2. the relevant version, branch, office, and time;
3. who perceived, proposed, decided, communicated, performed, and enforced it;
4. the authority and procedure each contribution used;
5. the information available to those agents;
6. required resources, consent, coordination, and external recognition;
7. dissent, error, fraud, capture, delay, or communication failure;
8. the actual result and who is bound, persuaded, affected, or still contesting it.

A seal, uniform, office title, majority statement, founder's wish, or leader's command is evidence only within its actual source and procedure.

## Membership, Participation, and Agency

Institutional relationships are distinct. A person may be a:

- member;
- founder;
- employee or contractor;
- officeholder;
- student or teacher;
- client, patient, subject, resident, or regulated practitioner;
- patron, donor, shareholder, supplier, or creditor;
- ally, delegate, prisoner, dependent, opponent, or unwilling participant.

One relationship does not imply the others. Membership need not grant employment, ownership, vote, access, belief, competence, or permanent identity.

Institutions may demand conduct, service, secrecy, payment, obedience, residence, belief, or sacrifice through law, contract, culture, dependence, coercion, or force. Those pressures and their consequences can be real. They do not overwrite a person's deliberate thought, loyalty, identity, consent, or action without an explicit Owning System.

People may comply while disagreeing, participate partially, organize internally, defect, reform, resist, appeal, sabotage, reinterpret, or leave where causally possible. Institutions are therefore rarely one perfectly unified actor.

## Authority, Legitimacy, and Enforcement

Institutional authority requires a current source. Possible sources include:

- law or treaty;
- member agreement;
- contract;
- property or resource control;
- professional recognition;
- community trust;
- religious or ritual recognition;
- divine, spirit, or territorial permission;
- inherited custom;
- military or coercive control;
- technical dependence;
- practical necessity;
- recognition by another Institution.

Formal Authority, Cultural Legitimacy, practical enforcement, moral justification, and factual capability remain distinct. An occupying academy may enforce admission rules without local legitimacy. A respected oral council may have legitimacy without legal recognition. A legal regulator may be unable to inspect the territory it claims.

Authority applies only to the acts, people, resources, places, times, and purposes its source supports. Institutional prestige cannot expand jurisdiction by implication.

## Resources, Property, and Access

Institutions may coordinate resources that no individual controls alone. Every material claim should identify:

- the owner, steward, source, or contested claimant;
- who can access, allocate, move, maintain, consume, or revoke it;
- relevant law, contract, consent, custom, or force;
- scarcity, renewal, storage, security, and maintenance;
- external suppliers and dependencies;
- consequences when access fails.

Institutional Access is not possession and does not persist when its supporting relationship ends. A librarian may access sealed records without owning them. A student may use a ritual chamber under supervision without gaining its magical source.

Gods, spirits, Soul Weapons, intelligent constructs, distributed persons, monsters, and other person-like sources cannot lose agency because an Institution calls them property, infrastructure, curriculum, or office. Agreements and coercion remain what they actually are.

## Collective Capability and Institutional Capacity

An Institution may produce a Collective Capability that no participant possesses alone. The result may depend on:

- distinct Skills and Development held by particular contributors;
- practised coordination and communication;
- offices and decision procedures;
- records, tools, sites, technology, magic, or infrastructure;
- schedules, logistics, maintenance, supply, and recovery;
- trust, supervision, redundancy, and correction;
- external authority, contracts, relationships, or consent;
- environmental and world-law conditions.

Institutional Capacity is the Institution's current ability to sustain one stated function under named conditions. It is not a universal capacity score.

Assess each function separately. An academy may be excellent at preserving records, weak at practical instruction, politically influential, financially unstable, and unable to verify one magical claim. Prestige in one function does not repair another.

Collective capability does not become:

- a leader's personal Skill;
- a member-wide aura or bonus;
- proof every participant understands the whole process;
- unlimited action at every branch simultaneously;
- immunity from absence, bottlenecks, sabotage, fatigue, scarcity, or communication delay.

When a contributor, site, resource, or procedure is lost, identify the affected function rather than lowering one institution level.

## Institutional Knowledge and Memory

Institutional Knowledge is understanding of how a particular Institution actually operates. Institutional Memory is the world-bound continuity of information, interpretation, procedure, relationships, habits, and lessons preserved through the Institution's people and media.

Memory may reside in:

- living practitioners;
- oral, written, sensory, magical, biological, architectural, or distributed records;
- repeated procedures and tools;
- landscapes, territories, and constructed environments;
- office traditions and social relationships;
- gods, spirits, constructs, Soul Weapons, or other consenting persons;
- redundant branches and external allies.

Institutional Memory is fallible. Records can be incomplete, forged, inaccessible, decontextualized, censored, obsolete, or misread. Routine can preserve a useful practice after its explanation is lost, or preserve a harmful mistake because authority discourages review.

No member knows everything the Institution knows. A record existing somewhere does not make it available to a decision-maker. Access, indexing, language, interpretation, trust, time, and communication still matter.

## Academies

### Academy Validity

An Academy must satisfy the Institution Validity Test and sustain meaningful organization around at least one of these functions:

- teaching and supervised practice;
- inquiry, experiment, criticism, or research;
- assessment or credentialing;
- preservation, recovery, translation, or transmission of knowledge;
- preparation for one or more Classes, Professions, offices, or public responsibilities.

A single teacher, library, examination, building, lecture, bloodline, Magical School, or famous name is insufficient by itself. A small travelling group can be an Academy if the durable organization is real; a vast campus can cease to be one if it no longer sustains learning functions.

### Academy Profile

An Academy Profile extends an Institution Profile with:

| Field | What it records |
| --- | --- |
| **Learning remit** | Subjects, questions, practices, responsibilities, and audiences the Academy claims to serve. |
| **Hosted frameworks** | Classes, Martial Traditions, Magical Schools, Professions, research programs, or independent curricula, each retained as a separate framework. |
| **Admission and participation** | Who may learn, teach, research, observe, reside, use facilities, or seek assessment, under what process and accommodation. |
| **Teaching roles** | Instructors, mentors, peers, technicians, archivists, source partners, supervisors, affected communities, and other contributors. |
| **Learning routes** | Instruction, observation, apprenticeship, practice, service, experimentation, reflection, field work, translation, and current-life feedback. |
| **Practice conditions** | Bodies, tools, sites, materials, partners, sources, risks, environments, and safety requirements. |
| **Assessment and recognition** | What is examined, by whom, under which conditions, what evidence results, and what any Credential or rank actually means. |
| **Research and revision** | How questions are selected, evidence challenged, error corrected, findings shared, and doctrine changed or protected. |
| **Learner rights and duties** | Access, privacy, safety, support, attribution, consent, service, conduct, accountability, appeal, and exit. |
| **External obligations** | Duties to clients, communities, ecosystems, funders, regulators, source persons, and those bearing research or training risk. |

### Academies, Classes, and Schools

An Academy may:

- host several competing Human Classes or Magical Schools;
- teach a shared curriculum interpreted differently by several Classes;
- support Martial Traditions through facilities, partners, and Pressure Practice;
- prepare learners for several Professions;
- conduct research outside any established Class;
- recognize external teachers or reject their standing;
- preserve a Class after its Profession disappears;
- survive after its former doctrines become obsolete;
- disappear while its Classes continue through other institutions.

An Academy may claim exclusive ownership of a Class or School name. That claim may have legal or social force, but it does not make the framework metaphysically dependent on the Academy or make its doctrine true.

### Admission, Exclusion, and Accommodation

Admission creates only the stated institutional relationship and access. It does not grant aptitude, capability, belief, Magical Access, a Profession, or eventual graduation.

Admission and participation may be shaped by:

- available teachers, places, tools, sources, and funding;
- required prior evidence or preparation;
- body, species, language, sensory, lifecycle, or scheduling compatibility;
- safety and responsibility;
- citizenship, caste, lineage, wealth, patronage, religion, politics, or prejudice;
- labour needs, military service, contracts, or coercion;
- legal, ecological, divine, spirit, or territorial restrictions.

The Academy's stated reason is not automatically factual. Apply the gatekeeping rules in [Social Advancement](SOCIAL_ADVANCEMENT.md). Where practical accommodation, translation, adapted equipment, different scheduling, or another learning route can address a real mismatch, exclusion is a social decision rather than metaphysical incapacity.

### Teaching Does Not Install Capability

Academies create access to teaching, feedback, tools, peers, records, practice, and opportunity. Development still requires the relevant learner's engagement, integration, embodiment, recovery, and meaningful use under the Owning System.

Attendance, tuition, initiation, obedience, years enrolled, lessons completed, exercises repeated, examinations passed, or sacrifices endured are not universal learning currency.

Teachers and institutions may fail. A curriculum can be incomplete, harmful, obsolete, politically distorted, mismatched to a body, or excellent only under conditions the Academy no longer possesses.

### Assessment, Rank, and Credentials

An Academy may issue local ranks, grades, certifications, degrees, licences, seals, teaching permissions, or other Institutional Measures. Each must state:

- issuer and jurisdiction;
- purpose and intended audience;
- evidence and conditions assessed;
- assumptions, omissions, and date;
- rights or access created;
- duties and accountability;
- recognition and portability;
- review, expiry, or revocation where applicable.

Assessment provides evidence. It does not create the capability it measures, guarantee current competence, establish unrelated mastery, or become a universal level.

Failure may reveal a learning need, mismatch, bias, inaccessible format, outdated standard, unsafe condition, fraud, or actual lack of the assessed capability. The result must be interpreted from the process and evidence rather than treated as metaphysical truth.

### Inquiry and Research

Institutional inquiry can coordinate long projects, specialist roles, archives, replication, criticism, field access, and shared tools. Research authority does not make a conclusion true.

An Academy should distinguish:

- observation from interpretation;
- institutional doctrine from world truth;
- source testimony from proof;
- practical reliability from complete explanation;
- uncertain evidence from deliberate secrecy;
- current findings from inherited prestige.

Research involving people, communities, ecosystems, gods, spirits, monsters, Soul Weapons, constructs, or other agents retains consent, risk, ownership, and accountability. Institutional approval does not erase personhood or make imposed harm a valid Development route.

## Accountability, Capture, and Reform

Institutions distribute responsibility without dissolving it. A harmful result may involve:

- individual action;
- negligent supervision;
- unsafe procedure;
- resource decisions;
- information concealment;
- incentive design;
- governance failure;
- professional misconduct;
- structural exclusion;
- external coercion or sabotage;
- an unavoidable risk handled responsibly.

Assessment should identify each causal contribution and the evidence available at the time. Do not blame every member equally, hide individual wrongdoing behind the Institution, or make one leader personally responsible for every distributed failure.

Institutional Capture occurs when a person, office, faction, patron, external power, or narrow interest gains enough control over decision routes, resources, records, or enforcement to redirect institutional action away from its stated or previously maintained purposes.

Capture can be partial, contested, legal, covert, gradual, temporary, or normalized. It does not automatically erase every honest member, useful function, opposing branch, or former obligation.

Reform requires causal change to people, authority, procedure, resources, incentives, records, relationships, or enforcement. Announcing new values without changing those supports may alter reputation while leaving operation intact.

## Institutional Continuity and Change

Institutions are durable, not immortal. Continuity may survive:

- member turnover;
- a founder's death;
- relocation;
- leadership succession;
- legal or cultural change;
- loss of one site or archive;
- merger, federation, or division;
- suppression and underground operation;
- replacement of tools or teaching media.

For a continuity claim, compare:

1. purposes and recurring functions;
2. participants, constituencies, and recognized relationships;
3. offices, procedures, authority, and accountability;
4. knowledge, records, practices, and interpretation;
5. resources, sites, and dependencies;
6. obligations, debts, rights, and external recognition;
7. succession, transfer, seizure, or reconstruction route;
8. which audiences accept or contest continuity.

A shared name, building, charter, relic, founder, bloodline, legal registration, or archive is evidence, not decisive proof by itself.

An Institution may become:

- **strained** - core functions continue with material limits;
- **hollow** - name, office, or legal shell persists while claimed functions no longer operate;
- **fragmented** - branches preserve incompatible parts and contest identity;
- **captured** - control redirects enough action to change practical purpose;
- **dormant** - coordinated function pauses while a credible route to reactivation remains;
- **successor** - a new arrangement inherits some but not all identity, obligations, knowledge, and recognition;
- **reconstructed** - later actors deliberately rebuild from surviving evidence and present resources;
- **extinct** - no operating continuity or credible current institutional arrangement remains.

These are contextual descriptions, not universal stages. Institutional change does not automatically transform or erase associated Classes. Detailed transformation of Class frameworks is defined in [Class Evolution](CLASS_EVOLUTION.md).

## Monster and Mixed-Species Institutions

[Intelligent Monster Societies](../monster-evolution/MONSTER_SOCIETIES.md) may sustain Institutions through communication, bodies, lifecycles, territory, migration, ecology, architecture, distributed cognition, symbiosis, magic, or memory unlike human forms.

Examples include:

- a migratory Academy whose lessons are encoded in seasonal routes;
- a distributed colony that assigns institutional roles to temporary bodies;
- a territorial council whose records are maintained through scent and regrowth;
- a mixed-species guild with parallel workspaces and translated decision procedures;
- an Institution that renews its offices at each metamorphic lifecycle stage.

Do not require writing, buildings, money, permanent individual membership, human speech, elections, or a human lifespan. Do require explicit action ownership, communication routes, continuity, and agency.

Mixed Institutions should state whose bodies, senses, schedules, languages, risks, and needs the procedures assume. Accommodation does not make bodies interchangeable; body-bound work still requires appropriate current capability.

## Reincarnation and Soul Interactions

### Reincarnation

[Reincarnation](../soul/REINCARNATION.md) preserves no institutional membership, Office, employment, vote, credential, licence, property, authority, access, debt, obligation, reputation, or legal identity automatically.

Retained knowledge, Skills, judgment, Soul Imprints, and memories may help a new incarnation understand or re-enter an Institution. Current identity, embodiment, evidence, relationships, procedures, law, and institutional choice still govern access and standing.

### Soul Echoes and Retained Instincts

[Soul Echoes](../soul/SOUL_ECHOES.md) may remember former procedures, people, and failures. [Retained Instincts](../soul/RETAINED_INSTINCTS.md) may cue old routines or Institutional Knowledge. Both can be outdated, body-mismatched, or dangerous after institutional change.

### Soul Resonance and Soul Constellations

[Soul Resonance](../soul/SOUL_RESONANCE.md) and [Soul Constellations](../soul/SOUL_CONSTELLATIONS.md) may reopen evidence, relationships, or opportunities. They do not restore consent, membership, office, loyalty, or authority.

### Soul Titles and Soul Avatars

[Soul Titles](../soul/SOUL_TITLES.md) may create Symbolic Authority or institutional pressure where their Basis matters to an audience. [Soul Avatars](../soul/SOUL_AVATARS.md) may synthesize former institutional experience. Neither can decide for current members or confer a present Office.

### Akashic Archive

[Akashic Archive](../soul/AKASHIC_ARCHIVE.md) Records may preserve evidence of institutional history. A Record does not make every claim complete, current, legally valid, or correctly interpreted. Present agents decide what they recognize and remain responsible for their choices.

## Institution and Academy Claim Resolution

When an Institution or Academy is invoked in play:

1. **Name the claim.** Is it about identity, continuity, decision, authority, knowledge, capability, access, ownership, teaching, assessment, membership, or responsibility?
2. **Identify the version and scope.** Name the branch, period, jurisdiction, office, audience, and function involved.
3. **Apply the validity test.** Establish the durable arrangement rather than relying on its name or building.
4. **Identify actual agents.** Determine who knows, decides, performs, communicates, resists, and enforces.
5. **Trace authority and procedure.** Check source, limits, participation, dissent, appeal, and practical effectiveness.
6. **Trace capability and resources.** Identify component contributors, coordination, tools, sites, supply, consent, and bottlenecks.
7. **Separate adjacent facts.** Resolve Classes, Professions, Skills, Development, Social Position, reputation, Soul facts, and world law through their owners.
8. **Check information and memory.** Distinguish available records, Institutional Knowledge, doctrine, belief, secrecy, error, and world truth.
9. **Preserve agency and accountability.** Do not treat the Institution as one mind or erase the choices of members and affected people.
10. **Apply current consequences.** Update external campaign state for access, resources, relationships, legitimacy, duties, risk, continuity, and off-screen response.

Do not resolve the claim through prestige, institution size, age, academy rank, wealth, founder, building, member count, or one institutional level.

## Worked Examples

### One Academy, Several Schools

The River Glass Academy supplies dormitories, teachers, field boats, a flood archive, laboratories, and a review council. It hosts two rival Magical Schools and one civic Martial Tradition.

The Academy controls access to its facilities and may issue local Credentials. It does not own either school's doctrine, grant Magical Access, or install the tradition's Skills. A student can participate in one framework, several, or none while using Academy resources under current rules.

### Prestigious but Obsolete

An ancient medical Academy still holds legal authority and a celebrated name. Its current curriculum assumes bodies and diseases common before the last Age transition. Independent practitioners possess better evidence for present conditions.

The Academy's licences remain socially powerful until institutions and law respond. Prestige does not make the treatment safe, while one obsolete doctrine does not prove every teacher, archive, or service is useless.

### Academy Without a Building

A spider society preserves navigation, architecture, and weather interpretation through seasonal webworks. Mentors, learners, record-keepers, and route stewards migrate together, rebuilding the teaching medium at each site.

The Academy's continuity lies in coordinated roles, practice, sensory records, and succession rather than one campus or written charter. A human observer who sees only temporary webs may mistakenly classify it as no Institution at all.

### Capability No Member Holds Alone

A storm-warning Institution combines distant observers, trained interpreters, messenger relays, maintained instruments, local councils, and emergency supply stores. No person can detect, interpret, communicate, and answer every threat alone.

Its Collective Capability depends on coordination and infrastructure. Killing the director does not erase every component, but losing the only calibrated relay may cripple warning in one region without changing the Institution's unrelated teaching work.

### Hollow Guild

A guild retains its hall, charter, officers, fees, and monopoly after generations of declining practice. Its members can still exclude rivals legally, but its claimed Professional Competence is poorly evidenced.

The Institution remains socially real and potentially powerful. It is hollow in the relevant service function, not nonexistent, and its legal authority does not become Skill.

### Reincarnated Founder Refused

The founder of an Academy reincarnates centuries later and recalls its earliest procedures. The current Institution recognizes a different successor, teaches revised doctrine, and refuses the stranger access to sealed records.

An Archive Record or Soul Resonance may support investigation. Former identity does not restore property, office, membership, loyalty, or the right to reverse current decisions. The claim may still trigger debate, sponsorship, fear, exploitation, or reform through present causality.

### Captured Academy

A military patron controls appointments, funding, publication, and field access at an Academy while its charter still promises independent inquiry. Some teachers comply, some conceal research, and one branch breaks away.

The patron's capture redirects major institutional action without making every member loyal or every record false. Reform requires changes to the actual decision routes and dependencies, not a ceremonial declaration.

### A Source Refuses

A Magical Academy treats a sentient gate-spirit as inherited infrastructure. The spirit withdraws consent after repeated unsafe research.

The Academy may lose access, negotiate, coerce, seek alternatives, or challenge prior agreements. Calling the spirit property does not supply Magical Authority or erase its agency. Researchers retain only capabilities they can express without that external source.

## Safeguards

### No Institution Equals Class

An Institution may host, regulate, or claim a Class. Membership and organizational survival do not create, own, or preserve the entire class framework automatically.

### No Academy Equals Magical School

An Academy is an Institution. A Magical School is a socially sustained model and curriculum. Either may exist without the other.

### No Membership or Graduation Bundle

Admission, employment, initiation, rank, graduation, credential, office, inheritance, donation, or years served never grant Skills, Development, Profession, Magical Access, mastery, or authority outside their actual scope.

### No Leader Aura

A founder, ruler, dean, commander, Soul Title bearer, or powerful member does not give an Institution their personal Skills, stats, Soul traits, legitimacy, or unlimited authority.

### No Institutional Hive Mind

Knowledge and intention require actual agents, communication, procedure, records, and access. Members may disagree, misunderstand, resist, or act beyond authority.

### No Infinite Collective Capability

Collective action retains component capabilities, coordination, throughput, resources, sites, timing, maintenance, consent, and failure points. Size and prestige do not waive them.

### No Bureaucratic Truth

Institutional doctrine, records, ranks, licences, decisions, and measurements can be useful and enforceable without becoming world truth or proof of capability.

### No Permanent Institution

Names, charters, buildings, founders, relics, laws, and records do not guarantee operating continuity. Institutions can transform, divide, become hollow, collapse, or end.

### No Reincarnated Standing

Former membership, Office, credential, property, authority, access, obligations, and reputation remain world-bound. Soul continuity may provide evidence or learning, not restoration.

### No Person as Mere Infrastructure

Institutional classification cannot remove the agency, consent, ownership, or ordinary safeguards of gods, spirits, Soul Weapons, monsters, constructs, distributed persons, or other agents.

### No Human Organizational Default

Monster and mixed Institutions may use nonhuman bodies, senses, lifecycles, media, cognition, territory, and decision forms. Evaluate their actual coordination rather than resemblance to human academies.

### No Campaign Data

Institution Profiles and Academy Profiles are interfaces for external campaign records. Do not store current rosters, officers, property, budgets, disputes, or institutional state in this repository.
