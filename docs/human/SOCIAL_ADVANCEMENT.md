# Social Advancement

## Status

This document defines the canonical structure and adjudication of Social Advancement for Phase 5. It extends [Social and Leadership Development](../progression/SOCIAL_AND_LEADERSHIP_DEVELOPMENT.md), which remains authoritative for how communication, cultural understanding, social judgment, coordination, leadership, and governance capability develop.

Read [Human Class Philosophy](HUMAN_CLASS_PHILOSOPHY.md), [Human Classes](CLASSES.md), and [Professions](PROFESSIONS.md) first.

## Core Rule

Social Advancement is an audience- and structure-specific change in a person's recognized role, access, responsibility, authority, legitimacy, reputation, network position, rights, obligations, or material support through current world causality.

It answers:

> What changed in this person's social position, according to whom, through which process, with what rights, access, duties, dependencies, and opposition?

It is not a social level, a reward track, proof of capability, moral worth, or permanent character unlock.

## Advancement and Development Are Different

Social Development is change in a person's capability. Social Advancement is change in the person's current social position.

| Fact | Example | Owner |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Social or leadership capability** | improved negotiation, teaching, delegation, or cultural judgment | [Social and Leadership Development](../progression/SOCIAL_AND_LEADERSHIP_DEVELOPMENT.md) |
| **Class recognition** | accepted as a teacher by one Martial Tradition | [Human Classes](CLASSES.md) and the relevant tradition |
| **Professional standing** | licensed for one scope of practice | [Professions](PROFESSIONS.md) |
| **Institutional position** | admitted, employed, elected, appointed, or removed | Later Phase 5 institution rules |
| **Public Reputation** | believed heroic by one city and criminal by another | Current audiences and world causality |
| **Relationship Strength** | trusted by one colleague after shared work | The participating people and their history |
| **Formal Authority** | permitted to command one unit or sign one kind of record | The current office, law, contract, or institution |
| **Cultural Legitimacy** | accepted by a community as a proper representative | The relevant community and its current values |
| **Symbolic Authority** | a Soul Title makes a claim meaningful to a particular audience | [Soul Titles](../soul/SOUL_TITLES.md) and receiving culture |
| **Material support** | access to salary, land, tools, assistants, or supply | Current ownership, economy, institution, and World Engine |

These facts can support or contradict one another. None silently becomes the rest.

## No Single Direction Is Up

Social position is multidimensional.

A change may be:

- **vertical within one structure** - greater or lesser authority, responsibility, seniority, or recognized standing;
- **lateral** - movement into another role, branch, Profession, institution, or audience without a higher rank;
- **expansive** - recognition or access spreads to another audience or jurisdiction;
- **specializing** - broader standing narrows into deeper responsibility within one domain;
- **formalizing** - an informal role gains office, charter, licence, or procedure;
- **informalizing** - formal standing is lost while relationships or community influence remain;
- **contested** - several audiences assign incompatible positions;
- **declining** - access, authority, legitimacy, reputation, support, or relationship weakens;
- **exiting** - a person resigns, refuses, defects, retires, renounces, or is expelled;
- **transformative** - the structure itself changes what the position means.

An apparent promotion can be harmful, coercive, ceremonial, politically isolating, or outside the person's goals. A demotion can remove authority while preserving capability and trust. The player is not required to pursue the highest available office.

## Social Position

A Social Position is a person's current placement within a named audience, relationship network, institution, Profession, class framework, jurisdiction, or cultural structure.

It is always scoped. "High status" is incomplete without naming where, among whom, and why.

### Social Position Profile

Record only the fields relevant to the claim:

| Field | What it records |
| --- | --- |
| **Person and current identity** | The identity to which this position is attached; it does not automatically follow the soul. |
| **Audience or structure** | The class, Profession, institution, community, faction, jurisdiction, relationship network, or public that recognizes the position. |
| **Role and standing** | The local role, rank, membership, reputation category, office, or informal place being claimed. |
| **Source** | Appointment, election, demonstrated work, recognition, inheritance, contract, ritual, sponsorship, purchase, conquest, reputation, service, or another causal route. |
| **Evidence and procedure** | Records, witnesses, examinations, votes, rites, conduct, relationships, or enforcement supporting the claim. |
| **Rights and access** | Information, places, tools, resources, audiences, protection, decision rights, or opportunities currently available. |
| **Responsibilities and obligations** | Work, service, conduct, reporting, care, debt, representation, risk, or accountability expected. |
| **Formal Authority** | Any defined permission to decide, command, certify, spend, judge, or represent. |
| **Cultural Legitimacy** | Which communities regard the role or route as properly entitled and which contest it. |
| **Public Reputation** | Audience-specific beliefs and expectations attached to the person, role, or identity. |
| **Relationships and sponsors** | People or groups whose trust, patronage, opposition, coalition, or dependence affects the position. |
| **Material support** | Pay, property use, tools, staff, supply, security, or infrastructure, each with an actual owner and condition. |
| **Dependencies** | Law, institution, source, election, patron, coalition, performance, secrecy, force, location, identity, or resources required to sustain the position. |
| **Challenges and alternatives** | Rivals, appeals, succession claims, exit routes, competing audiences, and consequences of refusal or loss. |
| **Portability** | Whether any part is recognized elsewhere and for what purpose. |

The profile is campaign state. This document defines its structure but does not store any current character's position.

## Advancement Routes

An Advancement Route is a causally available process through which one or more Social Position facts may change.

Possible routes include:

- demonstrated work;
- completion of a curriculum;
- examination or credential;
- apprenticeship or supervised responsibility;
- peer recognition;
- community trust;
- professional service;
- election;
- appointment;
- sponsorship or patronage;
- coalition agreement;
- inheritance;
- marriage or kinship;
- purchase or investment;
- military or civic service;
- ritual selection;
- divine or spirit recognition;
- discovery or invention;
- public reputation;
- legal reform;
- protest, resistance, or revolution;
- conquest or coercion;
- institutional succession;
- emergency necessity;
- founding a new class, Profession, or organization;
- recognition of a prior external qualification.

The route determines what it can establish. Passing an examination may create a Credential. Winning an election may create office. Sponsorship may create access. Inheritance may create a claim. Conquest may create enforceable control. None automatically creates competence, trust, legitimacy, moral right, or permanent acceptance.

### Advancement Opportunity

An Advancement Opportunity is a current chance to pursue, accept, negotiate, refuse, or reshape a Social Position change. It is not a compulsory quest or guaranteed result.

An opportunity should state:

- the position or change offered;
- who can confer, recognize, or enforce it;
- why it is available now;
- eligibility and evidence;
- actions, time, cost, risk, and competition;
- rights, access, duties, and dependencies;
- likely supporters and opponents;
- consequences of acceptance, failure, refusal, or withdrawal;
- what remains uncertain.

## Eligibility, Selection, and Appointment

Eligibility means a named process permits consideration. It does not guarantee selection or capability.

Selection may weigh:

- demonstrated capability;
- relevant Class Affiliation;
- Professional Competence;
- credentials or licences;
- seniority;
- service;
- reputation;
- trust;
- sponsorship;
- kinship or inheritance;
- wealth;
- political alignment;
- species or embodiment;
- ritual or divine recognition;
- availability;
- willingness;
- corruption, prejudice, or coercive power.

The GM should model what the structure actually values rather than assuming a fair meritocracy. An unjust selection can still be causally valid while remaining contested and harmful.

Appointment creates the position's actual rights and duties only if the appointing source has that authority and can make the appointment effective. It does not install capability or legitimacy in every audience.

## Recognition and Portability

A Status Portability Claim asserts that one audience should recognize some part of a Social Position established elsewhere.

Portability may concern:

- curriculum credit;
- class rank;
- professional credential;
- licence;
- institutional membership;
- office precedence;
- legal status;
- ritual standing;
- reputation;
- right of audience;
- access to tools or records;
- eligibility for consideration.

Assess:

1. the exact fact being transferred;
2. the original audience, procedure, evidence, and jurisdiction;
3. the receiving audience's purpose and standards;
4. actual present capability where relevant;
5. differences in law, culture, species, body, magic, Profession, and responsibility;
6. treaties, reciprocity, sponsorship, or political interests;
7. fraud, outdated records, mistaken identity, or changed institutions;
8. what rights, duties, and accountability would follow;
9. who may appeal or contest the decision.

Recognition for one purpose does not make positions universally equivalent. A foreign licence may permit an examination without authorizing practice. A famous title may open an audience without granting office.

## Reputation, Renown, and Audience Split

Public Reputation belongs to an audience and identity. Social Advancement may change when reputation affects access, selection, trust, scrutiny, prices, rivals, patronage, or danger.

Reputation can spread through:

- witnessed conduct;
- testimony;
- records;
- institutions;
- stories and art;
- trade networks;
- rivals and propaganda;
- magical communication;
- Soul Chronicles;
- mistaken identity;
- association with a group or office.

Spread changes information, not truth. One event can produce several reputations:

- a monster community remembers a rescuer;
- a human frontier calls the same person a traitor;
- a distant court knows only an exaggerated song;
- a guild disputes the professional account;
- close companions know the private cost.

There is no universal fame meter. Audience size does not prove accuracy, capability, legitimacy, or moral worth.

### Reputation and Position

Reputation may support an Advancement Opportunity, but the receiving structure still decides what evidence and procedure it accepts. Institutions may exploit fame, reject it, fear it, or grant ceremonial standing without practical authority.

## Sponsorship, Patronage, and Networks

Sponsors and patrons can provide:

- introduction;
- testimony;
- money;
- tools;
- protection;
- instruction;
- right of audience;
- legal support;
- access to records or sites;
- temporary responsibility;
- political backing.

That support belongs to the relationship and current resources. It may be conditional, revocable, self-interested, generous, coercive, corrupt, or misunderstood.

Patronage does not grant the patron's capability, legitimacy, or authority to the recipient. It can bypass a gate without proving the gate's standards were met. Refusing patronage remains possible, with real consequences.

Networks are relationships among agents, not a spendable social currency. Introductions, favors, obligations, trust, and information must have participants, history, expectations, and limits.

## Gatekeeping, Exclusion, and Inequality

Gatekeeping controls access to positions, teaching, tools, records, Professions, institutions, audiences, and recognition.

It may serve:

- safety;
- scarce resource allocation;
- confidentiality;
- accountability;
- cultural continuity;
- source consent;
- professional standards;
- monopoly;
- class or caste power;
- prejudice;
- exploitation;
- political control;
- fear of outsiders;
- protection from persecution.

The rule cannot infer capability or legitimacy from the existence of a gate. The GM should ask who designed it, what it tests, whom it excludes, what interests it protects, how it is enforced, and which alternatives exist.

Discrimination based on species, body, origin, sex, gender, lineage, disability, class, caste, religion, magical access, former life, or another status can create real barriers and consequences. It is not an objective progression limit unless an actual embodiment or source requirement independently exists.

Reform, accommodation, independent learning, migration, parallel institutions, solidarity, legal challenge, secrecy, resistance, and revolution may alter barriers through world causality. None is guaranteed or costless.

## Rights, Duties, and Accountability

Every meaningful position should specify what changes in practice.

Possible rights and access include:

- entering restricted places;
- using tools or records;
- voting;
- speaking in a proceeding;
- holding property or funds in trust;
- receiving protection;
- representing others;
- teaching or examining;
- making bounded decisions;
- calling on staff or supply;
- invoking a contract, rite, or law.

Possible duties include:

- service;
- care;
- confidentiality;
- reporting;
- stewardship;
- representation;
- obedience within a valid scope;
- disclosure of conflicts;
- maintaining standards;
- accepting review;
- protecting dependents;
- restitution or repair;
- succession planning.

Advancement that adds rights without obligations may still exist, especially under corrupt or unequal structures, but the asymmetry should be a world fact with consequences rather than an invisible character bonus.

## Decline, Loss, Removal, and Exit

Social position can change through:

- resignation;
- retirement;
- refusal;
- completion of service;
- term expiration;
- loss of election;
- revocation;
- disciplinary removal;
- broken trust;
- changed reputation;
- defeat;
- institutional collapse;
- legal reform;
- loss of sponsor or resources;
- identity exposure;
- relocation;
- succession;
- death;
- Reincarnation;
- audience change.

Name the lost layer. Removal from office can end Formal Authority while leaving Skill, relationships, reputation, or legitimacy. Exile can remove access while increasing symbolic influence elsewhere. Public disgrace can damage reputation while leaving legal office intact.

Loss should follow actual dependencies and procedures. It is not a generic de-leveling penalty. Likewise, preserving one relationship or capability does not negate the consequence of losing a position.

### Refusal and Renunciation

A character may reject a promotion, title, office, inheritance, licence, patron, ritual standing, or public identity. Others may still assign expectations, rumors, legal claims, or consequences, but the rules do not force personal identification, loyalty, or acceptance.

Renunciation may not instantly remove debts, law, public belief, material dependence, or institutional conflict. Agency governs the choice; causality governs what follows.

## Classes, Professions, and Institutions

### Class Advancement

A change in Class Affiliation or recognition may create curriculum access, peer standing, teaching permission, expectations, or obligations. It does not award Skills or make local rank universally portable.

### Professional Advancement

A change in Professional Role, scope, credential, licence, clientele, responsibility, or standing must state what work and accountability change. It does not grant Professional Competence or place the person above every practitioner.

### Institutional Advancement

Admission, membership, employment, committee role, office, tenure, command, or governance position depends on the institution's actual procedures and power. It creates only the access, resources, authority, and duties that structure can sustain.

Detailed institution and academy rules belong to the next Phase 5 task.

## Monster and Mixed-Society Advancement

Monster societies may organize position through:

- lifecycle or Evolution Stage;
- territory;
- age;
- kinship;
- contribution;
- ritual contest;
- migration role;
- scent, song, mark, or shared memory;
- ecological stewardship;
- species-specific capability;
- election;
- apprenticeship;
- divine or spirit relationship;
- distributed cognition;
- temporary crisis role.

No one pattern is universal. A body-linked role may reflect real current access without making social destiny biologically mandatory. A ritual victory may confer standing without granting leadership competence.

Mixed societies may need translated credentials, multiple legitimacy audiences, accessible procedures, shared offices, species-specific roles, and explicit limits on who can perform body-bound work. Human titles are not the default measure of advancement.

## Reincarnation and Soul Interactions

### Reincarnation

[Reincarnation](../soul/REINCARNATION.md) does not preserve current Social Position. Former names, roles, memberships, offices, licences, property, citizenship, reputation, audiences, followers, patrons, clients, rights, obligations, and authority remain with the former identity and world unless current causality reconnects them.

Retained Social Imprints may support judgment and faster learning. They do not restore standing.

### Soul Resonance

[Soul Resonance](../soul/SOUL_RESONANCE.md) may reopen meaningful history or make a connection recognizable within its Basis and Target. It does not prove identity, restore trust, or grant a position.

### Soul Echoes

[Soul Echoes](../soul/SOUL_ECHOES.md) may remember former institutions, relationships, and consequences. They cannot speak for current descendants, offices, cultures, or former allies.

### Retained Instincts

[Retained Instincts](../soul/RETAINED_INSTINCTS.md) may cue old etiquette, hierarchy, or social attention. Changed bodies, cultures, and institutions can make those responses misleading or harmful.

### Soul Constellations

[Soul Constellations](../soul/SOUL_CONSTELLATIONS.md) preserve recurring relational history without fixing present roles, trust, loyalty, status, or consent. Recognition may create an opportunity, not ownership.

### Soul Titles

[Soul Titles](../soul/SOUL_TITLES.md) may create Symbolic Authority, invitations, tests, fear, challenge, or narrative pressure where a receiving culture finds the Title Basis relevant. A Soul Title is not a social title, office, credential, licence, or automatic promotion.

### Soul Avatars

[Soul Avatars](../soul/SOUL_AVATARS.md) may synthesize the perspective of former rulers, workers, outsiders, and reformers. They do not grant votes, office, followers, or the right to decide for current people.

### Akashic Archive

[Akashic Archive](../soul/AKASHIC_ARCHIVE.md) Records may supply evidence about former deeds or institutions. Audiences remain free to interpret, verify, contest, or reject that evidence through present causality.

## Social Advancement Claim Resolution

When Social Advancement is proposed or asserted:

1. **Name the position change.** Identify role, access, responsibility, authority, legitimacy, reputation, relationship, rights, obligations, or support.
2. **Name the audience and structure.** Establish who can recognize, confer, contest, or enforce the change.
3. **Identify the route.** State appointment, election, service, examination, reputation, inheritance, sponsorship, coercion, reform, or another actual process.
4. **Check eligibility and evidence.** Distinguish stated criteria, actual selection interests, capability, status, bias, and uncertainty.
5. **Check authority and effectiveness.** Determine whether the conferring source can make the change real and where.
6. **State rights, access, duties, and dependencies.** Do not award a vague status bonus.
7. **Separate adjacent facts.** Resolve capability, class, Profession, office, reputation, legitimacy, relationships, material support, and Soul effects through their owners.
8. **Preserve agency.** Allow acceptance, negotiation, refusal, appeal, withdrawal, opposition, and divided loyalty where causally possible.
9. **Identify contest and portability.** Name rival claims, audiences, jurisdictions, enforcement, and external recognition.
10. **Apply consequences over time.** Let performance, accountability, institutions, factions, resources, relationships, and world events sustain or erode the position.

Do not resolve the claim with a social level, reputation total, influence point, class rank, follower count, or single persuasion check.

## Worked Examples

### Capable Apprentice, Exclusionary Guild

A four-armed monster apprentice demonstrates reliable metalwork and sound safety judgment. A human guild denies independent standing because its charter recognizes only citizens with two-handed examination forms.

The denial blocks licences, clients, tools, and legal protection. It does not erase capability. Reform, an adapted examination, a rival guild, independent practice, patronage, or migration may change the position through different costs and consequences.

### Connected but Incompetent Promotion

A noble patron appoints an inexperienced relative as workshop overseer. The appointment grants salary, staff access, and authority to schedule work. It does not grant technical Skill, Professional Judgment, peer trust, or legitimacy.

The overseer may learn, delegate well, conceal weakness, cause harm, lose the office, or become a symbol of institutional corruption. The position is real even though the competence claim is false.

### Local Healer, Split Recognition

A community trusts an unlicensed healer whose capability is well evidenced locally. A state medical body refuses recognition, while a neighboring monster settlement recognizes only part of the healer's body knowledge.

The healer has three different Social Positions, not one reputation score. Expansion requires specific translation, evidence, law, relationships, and practice rather than accumulating fame.

### The King Reborn Unknown

A former king reincarnates as an unknown child centuries later. Social Imprints support unusual judgment, and an Archive Record may eventually support the identity claim.

No former office, property, citizenship, subjects, or right to command returns automatically. Recognition may create protection, exploitation, ceremonial status, political crisis, or an Advancement Opportunity, each decided by current agents and institutions.

### Friend of Dragons at a Human Court

The Soul Title Friend of Dragons causes a court to invite its bearer to a diplomatic hearing after dragons close the mountain passes.

The title creates symbolic relevance and an audience. It does not grant diplomatic Skill, dragon trust, official appointment, correct information, or authority to negotiate. What follows depends on current capability, relationships, and the dragons' own choices.

### Ritual Victor Without Governance Skill

A monster society grants territorial standing to the winner of a ritual contest. The victor gains a recognized claim to one nesting ridge and a duty to defend its water.

The victory does not grant coalition, logistics, mediation, or governance capability. Other members may comply with the territorial custom while refusing broader leadership.

### Lateral Professional Move

A magical-school researcher leaves a prestigious academy rank to become an independent ward-failure inspector. The move reduces institutional access and public prestige but increases professional autonomy, field responsibility, and trust among frontier settlements.

Calling it a promotion or demotion without naming the audience and dimensions would hide the actual change.

### Refused Office

A respected river warden is offered command of a regional flood authority but refuses because the office requires permanent residence away from the communities the warden serves.

The refusal preserves player agency. It may disappoint sponsors, alter reputation, inspire trust, close access, or lead the institution to appoint someone else. It is not failed progression.

## Safeguards

### No Universal Social Ladder

Do not combine class rank, Profession, office, wealth, fame, legitimacy, followers, authority, trust, Soul Titles, and institutional access into one status level.

### No Status-to-Capability Conversion

Promotion, election, appointment, inheritance, credential, office, wealth, title, rank, reputation, or patronage does not grant Skills, Development, Professional Competence, or leadership judgment.

### No Capability-to-Authority Conversion

Expertise and power may influence selection or enforcement. They do not automatically confer office, legal rights, legitimacy, consent, or obedience.

### No Advancement Farming

Gift counts, favors, rescues, kills, victories, followers, speeches, examinations, years served, wealth spent, quests completed, and public displays matter through current people and procedures. They are not social XP.

### No Meritocracy Assumption

Structures may reward capability, service, wealth, lineage, politics, prejudice, ritual, luck, coercion, or corruption. Represent the actual process and consequences rather than treating selection as objective truth.

### No Permanent Unlocks

Access, office, reputation, patronage, licences, and authority depend on current identities, relationships, institutions, resources, law, and audiences. They can change without erasing earned capability.

### No Agency Theft

Advancement cannot force acceptance, identity, loyalty, belief, employment, oath, marriage, office, or political alignment. Social and material pressure may create consequences while the deliberate choice remains with the character unless another explicit control rule applies.

### No Universal Portability

Status recognized by one audience is not automatically valid in another. Every portability claim names its purpose, evidence, receiving structure, and limits.

### No Reincarnated Standing

Former office, rank, reputation, relationships, property, rights, obligations, followers, and legitimacy do not follow the soul. Soul systems can create evidence or symbolic pressure, not restoration by default.

### No Human Default

Monster and mixed societies define position through their own cultures, bodies, ecologies, institutions, and agents. Human titles and hierarchies are not universal.

### Consequences Are Not Punishment for Refusal

Refusing or leaving a position may cause believable social consequences. The GM must not invent unrelated harm merely to force an upward path or punish a player for choosing another life.

## Deferred Ownership

This document does not fully define:

- institution and academy governance;
- elections, succession, inheritance, property, law, wages, prices, or taxation;
- faction and political simulation;
- exact reputational spread procedures;
- dialogue checks or mind control;
- universal class or professional ranks;
- class transformation;
- final limits of human progression.

Those remain with later Phase 5 and World Engine tasks. Use the [Alpha Playtest Rules](../gm/ALPHA_PLAYTEST_RULES.md) for missing campaign procedures.
