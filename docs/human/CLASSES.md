# Human Classes

## Status

This document defines the canonical structure, recognition, use, and adjudication of Human Classes. Read [Human Class Philosophy](HUMAN_CLASS_PHILOSOPHY.md) first. That document owns the distinction between class, capability, Profession, institution, recognition, and identity.

## Core Rule

A Human Class exists when a society has developed a persistent framework for organizing, transmitting, interpreting, and recognizing a path of expertise.

A class is real because people use and reproduce that framework through teaching, practice, memory, criticism, and social response. It is not made real by a menu, a metaphysical slot, or a universal system declaration.

## The Class Validity Test

A proposed Human Class should answer all of the following questions:

1. **Domain:** What recurring problem, responsibility, discipline, or field of inquiry does the class organize?
2. **Transmission:** How can another person learn its methods, language, standards, or perspective?
3. **Interpretation:** What shared ideas make its practices part of one recognizable path rather than an accidental list of Skills?
4. **Practice:** What meaningful present-life activity develops the capabilities associated with it?
5. **Recognition:** Which people or groups can identify a relationship to the class, and what evidence do they accept?
6. **Continuity:** What allows the framework to persist, change, divide, or be reconstructed across more than one practitioner?

If the proposal lacks a coherent domain, transmissible structure, shared interpretation, or social continuity, it is probably better represented as a Skill, Technique, Profession, office, title, nickname, personal style, or temporary campaign label.

Formal institutions are not required. An oral lineage, travelling fellowship, neighborhood practice, family tradition, persecuted network, or distributed scholarly correspondence can sustain a class when the validity test is met.

## Class Profile

A Class Profile is the smallest useful canonical or campaign record of one Human Class. It should contain enough detail to adjudicate access, learning, recognition, and consequences without duplicating capability rules.

### Required Fields

| Field | What it records |
| --- | --- |
| **Name and scope** | The class's current name, the culture or audience using it, and any important translations or disputed labels. |
| **Domain** | The recurring field, problem, responsibility, or inquiry the framework organizes. |
| **Provenance** | How the class formed and which people, conditions, or earlier traditions shaped it. |
| **Interpretive core** | Shared principles, language, values, models, or questions that make the class coherent. |
| **Transmission routes** | Apprenticeship, school, service, oral teaching, texts, ritual, peer practice, examination, or other ways it is learned. |
| **Expected practice** | The current-life exercises, work, experimentation, and real use through which capability develops. |
| **Capability references** | Links to relevant Skills and Development Tracks; never an automatic grant list. |
| **Embodiment assumptions** | Senses, anatomy, cognition, lifespan, magical access, tools, language, or environment the usual teaching presumes. |
| **Recognition audiences** | The communities, peers, employers, institutions, or publics that recognize relationships to the class. |
| **Evidence and labels** | What those audiences use to distinguish students, practitioners, teachers, reformers, or other local relationships. |
| **Access and obligations** | Resources, communities, permissions, duties, codes, costs, taboos, or risks causally associated with participation. |
| **Related structures** | Associated Professions, institutions, offices, traditions, schools, laws, or rival frameworks, each kept as a separate fact. |
| **Variants and disputes** | Living differences in curriculum, interpretation, recognition, naming, and authority. |
| **Current condition** | Whether the class is flourishing, fragmented, suppressed, obscure, lost, reconstructed, or changing in the relevant world scope. |

### Optional Fields

A profile may also record:

- common misconceptions;
- known blind spots;
- characteristic teaching tools;
- historical schisms;
- relations with nonhuman traditions;
- circumstances that make the class difficult to practise;
- public stereotypes;
- evidence needed to verify a disputed claim;
- which facts are hidden, uncertain, or known only to particular audiences.

Optional details must remain useful. A Class Profile is not an inventory of every practitioner, exercise, Technique, or historical event.

## Class Curriculum

A Class Curriculum is the organized teaching route used by one version of a class. It may include:

- foundational language and models;
- ordered exercises;
- supervised practice;
- safety rules;
- case comparison;
- ethical or ritual obligations;
- tool use;
- collaborative methods;
- reflection and correction;
- practical trials;
- peer criticism;
- independent work;
- teaching responsibilities.

A curriculum creates **Current Access** to learning. It does not guarantee learning, capability, reliability, judgment, or mastery.

### Curricula Are Selective

Every curriculum reflects choices. It may prioritize speed over breadth, reliability over novelty, orthodoxy over experimentation, individual excellence over coordination, or public service over private gain. It may preserve excellent methods alongside outdated assumptions.

A student can learn from a curriculum without accepting all of its values. A reformer can remain recognizably connected to a class while rejecting part of its inherited structure. Whether the surrounding society recognizes that relationship is a separate social question.

### Multiple Curricula, One Class

One class may support several curricula when their practitioners still share enough domain, interpretation, continuity, and mutual recognition to treat them as variants of one framework. Different curricula may produce different capability patterns even when they share a class name.

### One Curriculum, Several Classes

An institution may teach a common foundation to students later recognized through different class frameworks. Shared exercises do not erase differences in interpretation, responsibility, or social recognition.

## Relationships to a Class

Class Affiliation is not one binary state. A person may have one or more fact-specific relationships to a class.

Common relationships include:

- **observer** - has encountered the framework without undertaking its teaching;
- **candidate** - seeks or is being considered for access;
- **student** - participates in a recognized teaching route;
- **practitioner** - uses relevant methods in meaningful current conditions;
- **peer-recognized practitioner** - is accepted by a named practitioner community;
- **teacher** - is trusted by a named audience to transmit some or all of the framework;
- **custodian** - maintains records, rites, tools, standards, or continuity;
- **reformer** - deliberately changes the framework while claiming continuity with it;
- **former affiliate** - retains some history but no longer participates or is no longer accepted;
- **outsider-classified** - is assigned the label by others without necessarily accepting it;
- **self-identified** - claims the relationship without necessarily being recognized by others.

These are descriptive relationships, not a mandatory ladder. A custodian may not be the strongest practitioner. A brilliant practitioner may refuse teaching. A former affiliate may retain capability. A self-identified founder may have no peers yet.

Every relationship claim should name:

- the class version involved;
- the relevant audience;
- the supporting evidence;
- the current rights or expectations, if any;
- whether the person accepts the relationship;
- which claims remain disputed.

## Admission and Access

Classes do not have universal entry requirements. Access follows current world causality.

Possible constraints include:

- teacher availability;
- language;
- tuition or service;
- sponsorship;
- geography;
- secrecy;
- legal restriction;
- social prejudice;
- religious obligation;
- bodily compatibility;
- tool or resource scarcity;
- safety standards;
- demonstrated preparation;
- trust built through prior conduct.

These constraints may be unjust, mistaken, or politically motivated. They still affect access while the responsible people and institutions can enforce them. They are not metaphysical class locks: theft, translation, migration, reform, independent discovery, defection, diplomacy, and institutional collapse may change them through actual consequences.

Admission creates opportunity and obligation, not capability. Refusal of admission does not erase capability learned elsewhere.

## Instruction and Development

Classes teach through the existing [Development System](../progression/README.md) and [Skill Engine](../skills/README.md).

Instruction can improve growth by providing:

- a valid route into unfamiliar practice;
- timely feedback;
- safer progression of difficulty;
- examples of failure and recovery;
- access to equipment and environments;
- shared vocabulary;
- peers and collaborators;
- accumulated case knowledge;
- methods for recognizing errors;
- opportunities for meaningful use.

Those advantages remain causal. Poor instruction can instill brittle Habits. A learner can misunderstand a sound lesson. An unsuitable body can require adaptation. A curriculum can omit a crucial case. A capable student can outgrow a teacher without automatically inheriting the teacher's status.

### Capability References, Not Benefit Lists

A Class Profile may reference:

- Skill Roots commonly introduced;
- Branches often explored;
- Development Tracks heavily exercised;
- tools and group structures commonly required;
- Conceptual Skills that advanced practitioners may eventually pursue;
- Adaptive Skills known to have emerged from recurring problems.

These references describe likely routes and dependencies. They are not granted abilities, starting packages, fixed builds, or guaranteed outcomes. The same curriculum can produce different capability because learners differ in embodiment, choices, practice, teachers, circumstances, and retained history.

## Local Ranks, Grades, and Names

A class may use ranks, grades, circles, colours, honorifics, examinations, masks, cords, seals, or other local recognition. Every label must define:

1. who issues or recognizes it;
2. what evidence it claims;
3. what rights or responsibilities follow;
4. where the claim is accepted;
5. how it may be challenged, suspended, or lost.

A rank may primarily indicate curriculum completion, practical trust, teaching permission, institutional seniority, ritual standing, examination success, or political office. It need not indicate combat capability or deeper mastery.

Ranks from different classes are not automatically comparable. Even identical labels may mean different things in two branches of the same class.

## Class Variants

A Class Variant is a recognizably related form of a class whose curriculum, interpretation, practice, or recognition differs without requiring a wholly separate class identity.

Variation can arise through:

- regional conditions;
- different embodiments;
- translation into another language;
- institutional competition;
- new tools or magic;
- migration;
- political reform;
- doctrinal disagreement;
- contact with monster societies;
- loss of archives or teachers;
- changed threats and responsibilities.

Create a variant when the distinction has practical consequences but shared provenance, domain, interpretive continuity, or mutual recognition still makes one class record useful. Create a separate class only when the difference in social framework is meaningful enough that combining them would hide how people teach, interpret, or recognize the path.

Detailed transformation, schism, synthesis, succession, and decline belong to the later Phase 5 Class Evolution task.

## Class Equivalence Claims

A Class Equivalence Claim asserts that two class relationships should be treated as comparable for a particular purpose.

Equivalence is always scoped. It may concern:

- admission into advanced study;
- recognition of prior instruction;
- permission to attempt an examination;
- eligibility for a Profession;
- legal reciprocity;
- access to archives or tools;
- coordination through shared methods.

To adjudicate equivalence, compare:

1. the purpose of the comparison;
2. actual present capability;
3. curriculum overlap and omissions;
4. embodiment assumptions;
5. standards and evidence;
6. practical judgment and experience;
7. recognition by the relevant audience;
8. political, cultural, or institutional interests that may distort the decision.

Equivalence for one purpose does not imply total sameness. Refusal of equivalence may reflect a genuine safety gap, a different ethical obligation, ignorance, prejudice, protectionism, or political conflict. The GM should distinguish those causes.

## Informal, Lost, Suppressed, and Emerging Classes

### Informal Classes

An informal class can persist through shared practice, oral teaching, peer correction, and community recognition without a centralized name or institution. Outsiders may not realize that the framework exists.

### Lost Classes

A class may become lost when no living community can transmit enough of its interpretive and practical structure to reproduce it. Surviving Skills, texts, ruins, tools, titles, or reincarnated memories are evidence, not automatic restoration.

Reconstruction requires present interpretation, experimentation, correction, and use. A reconstructed class may differ honestly from its predecessor.

### Suppressed Classes

Law, persecution, conquest, economic displacement, religious conflict, or institutional capture may force a class underground. Concealment affects recognition and access; it does not turn the class into a Hidden Skill.

### Emerging Classes

A novel personal method is not yet a class. It may become one when others can learn, interpret, practise, criticize, adapt, and socially recognize a coherent framework. The originator may contribute crucial capability without controlling every later meaning.

## Reincarnation and Retained History

Reincarnation preserves no class slot or standing.

A reincarnated person may carry:

- retained potential relevant to former practice;
- Soul Echoes with perspectives from prior class relationships;
- Retained Instincts that orient attention toward familiar cues;
- Soul Resonance with meaningful methods or communities;
- Soul Titles that shape symbolic reactions;
- memories or records sufficient to begin reconstruction.

The current incarnation still needs:

- a compatible embodied route;
- present training and calibration;
- current tools and conditions;
- evidence sufficient for any recognition claim;
- new relationships with living people and institutions;
- present consent to resume, reject, or reinterpret the former path.

A former teacher may return as a novice, an unrecognized outsider, a historical source, or the founder of a variant. No outcome is automatic.

## Interactions With Professions and Institutions

A class can prepare a person for work, but it does not create a Profession. A Profession additionally requires integrated responsibility, standards, judgment, and social function under [Profession Development](../progression/PROFESSION_DEVELOPMENT.md).

An institution can preserve and police a class, but membership in that institution is a separate relationship. An institution may:

- teach several classes;
- recognize practitioners it did not teach;
- employ class members in several Professions;
- deny recognition to capable outsiders;
- hold legal control over a class name without controlling all relevant knowledge;
- collapse while the class survives elsewhere.

Detailed Profession and institution procedures belong to later Phase 5 documents.

## Interactions With Monster Societies

Monster societies may have traditions that satisfy the same social functions as Human Classes while remaining shaped by monster embodiment, lifecycle, ecology, and culture. Do not rename them as Human Classes merely for convenience.

When a human class and monster tradition interact:

- compare actual domains and methods rather than names;
- identify which capabilities each embodiment can receive;
- preserve distinct social meanings;
- treat translation and accommodation as real work;
- avoid granting human Skills through status alone;
- avoid granting Species Traits or Monster Skill routes through human affiliation;
- allow shared or hybrid frameworks to emerge through sustained social causality.

## Class Claim Resolution

When a player, NPC, institution, or rule invokes a class, resolve the claim in this order:

1. **Identify the claim.** Is it about affiliation, instruction, capability, rank, authority, access, identity, or reputation?
2. **Identify the class version.** Name its culture, lineage, institution, region, or historical period.
3. **Identify the audience.** Determine who recognizes or disputes the claim.
4. **Check provenance.** Establish how the claimant gained the relevant relationship or evidence.
5. **Check the actual owner.** Resolve Skills, Development, Profession, office, institutional access, Soul facts, and Species facts through their own rules.
6. **Check present expression.** Apply current embodiment, practice, tools, conditions, and reliability.
7. **Apply scoped consequences.** Grant only the access, expectations, obligations, opportunities, risks, or reactions established by the current world.
8. **Record uncertainty.** Keep mistaken recognition, hidden evidence, disputed equivalence, and provisional rulings distinct from world truth.

The result should explain what the class relationship changes socially and what it does not change mechanically.

## Worked Examples

### The River-Warden Class

Several floodplain communities recognize River Wardens as practitioners of a framework joining water observation, crossing safety, rescue coordination, boat handling, and obligations to warn downstream settlements.

The Class Profile references relevant perception, navigation, communication, rescue, and tool-use Skills. It does not grant them. One curriculum teaches through seasonal service under an experienced warden; another uses a city academy and controlled canal exercises. Both are recognized for some civic work, but only the city issues a legal seal. The seal grants authority in city waterways, not universal mastery or command over village wardens.

### Two Classes With Similar Results

The Lantern Anatomists and the Quiet Palm lineage can both diagnose certain internal magical injuries. Anatomists teach explicit models, instruments, and comparative records. Quiet Palm teachers use touch, breath timing, oral metaphor, and long mentorship.

A hospital may recognize both for diagnosis but only Anatomists for operating a regulated device. That limited equivalence does not make the classes identical, and it does not prove that every member of either class is competent.

### One Name, Different Frameworks

Three kingdoms use the name "Ranger." One means a border office with legal authority. One means a forest-tracking class taught through family lineages. One means any paid wilderness scout.

The shared word establishes no automatic equivalence. The GM records the office, class, and Profession claims separately and asks which audience is speaking.

### The Unrecognized Founder

A courier develops a reliable method for navigating storms by combining local wind observation, route memory, and messenger coordination. The capability may qualify as an Adaptive Skill before any class exists. Years later, several couriers can learn, test, criticize, and transmit the method through a shared framework. Their social continuity creates an emerging class; it does not retroactively turn every past user into a member.

### The Reincarnated Custodian

A soul once maintained the records of a class destroyed two Ages ago. In the current life, Soul Echoes help interpret a surviving text, but the new body cannot perform several assumed gestures and the modern language lacks key concepts. Reconstruction requires translation, experimentation, adapted practice, and other people's criticism. The prior custodianship supplies evidence and perspective, not current rank or exclusive ownership.

### A Monster Student

An intelligent mantis-like person enters a human glassworker class. Its vision distinguishes stresses human teachers miss, but its forelimbs cannot use standard tools. The class provides concepts, safety practice, peers, and access to furnaces. New tools and present practice create a viable curriculum variant. The student does not gain human hand Skills, and the human class does not absorb the student's Species Traits as class benefits.

## Safeguards

### No Label-to-Capability Conversion

Never resolve "I am this class" as "I can therefore perform every associated action." Identify and test the actual capability.

### No Class Slots or Locking

There is no universal number of class slots. Breadth is limited by present time, access, embodiment, obligations, memory, practice, and tradeoffs. Social exclusivity is enforced by people and consequences, not metaphysical menus.

### No Automatic Bundles

Class Profiles reference capability routes but never award starting packages, progression picks, passive bonuses, or guaranteed Skill Evolution.

### No Rank Inflation

Do not create a new rank for every small improvement. Use a local label only when a relevant audience makes a consequential distinction and can explain its evidence, rights, or responsibilities.

### No Class Inflation

Use one class with variants when shared continuity remains the more truthful and useful representation. Use separate classes when combining them would erase meaningful differences in transmission, interpretation, or recognition.

### No Automatic Authority

Class recognition, teaching trust, professional licence, legal office, institutional membership, and social influence each require their own source.

### No Inherited or Reincarnated Mastery

Bloodline, name, Soul history, title, archive, teacher memory, or cultural prophecy may affect access and expectations. None bypasses current embodiment, practice, and Development.

### No Human Supremacy by Taxonomy

Human Class rules do not downgrade monster traditions, mixed frameworks, or self-organized expertise. Compare actual social function and capability ownership.

### Preserve Agency

A class can impose expectations and causal consequences, but it cannot decide a person's beliefs, loyalty, identity, or future choices. Oaths, conditioning, magic, coercion, and social pressure must use their own established rules and allow appropriate resistance or consequence.

## Deferred Ownership

This document does not fully define:

- Profession structures and practice;
- martial-tradition procedures;
- magical-school procedures;
- social advancement;
- institutional and academy governance;
- class transformation, merger, fracture, and succession;
- final limits on human progression.

Those remain later Phase 5 tasks. Class Profiles may point toward them without deciding their rules.
