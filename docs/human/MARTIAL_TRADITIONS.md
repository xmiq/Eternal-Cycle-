# Martial Traditions

## Status

This document defines the canonical structure and adjudication of Martial Traditions for Phase 5. Read [Human Class Philosophy](HUMAN_CLASS_PHILOSOPHY.md) and [Human Classes](CLASSES.md) first.

Martial Traditions are a focused kind of Human Class. They organize how communities prepare people to understand, survive, use, limit, and take responsibility for force and conflict. They do not own bodily capacity, learned combat Skills, weapons, military office, magical power, or combat outcomes.

## Core Rule

A Martial Tradition is a socially sustained class framework that organizes doctrine, training, interpretation, practice, and recognition around conflict, protection, force, or resistance under particular embodied and world conditions.

Its central question is:

> How does this community prepare people to perceive, decide, act, coordinate, and accept consequence when force or danger matters?

A tradition can be oriented toward duelling, war, policing, self-defence, monster hunting, hunting, bodyguard work, ritual contest, civil protection, nonlethal restraint, escape, resistance, or another recurring conflict domain. Lethality is neither required nor proof of seriousness.

## What a Martial Tradition Owns

A Martial Tradition may own:

- a coherent Martial Doctrine;
- a Class Curriculum for conflict-related learning;
- culturally specific training methods;
- shared language for timing, distance, initiative, threat, formation, restraint, and consequence;
- ethical and social interpretations of force;
- accepted Pressure Practice and safety procedures;
- local recognition, ranks, lineages, and teaching relationships;
- characteristic relationships among weapons, movement, roles, and environments;
- traditions of maintenance, preparation, recovery, review, and accountability;
- recurring tactical assumptions and known blind spots.

It does not own the effects of the things it organizes.

| Claim | Canonical owner |
| --- | --- |
| Strength, speed, endurance, balance, recovery, and bodily calibration | [Physical Development](../progression/PHYSICAL_DEVELOPMENT.md) |
| Weapon handling, movement, perception, tactics, coordination, and other learned capabilities | [Skill Engine](../skills/README.md) |
| Military, guard, instructor, hunting, or protection work | [Professions](PROFESSIONS.md) and [Profession Development](../progression/PROFESSION_DEVELOPMENT.md) |
| Rank, office, command authority, loyalty, morale, and reputation | [Social and Leadership Development](../progression/SOCIAL_AND_LEADERSHIP_DEVELOPMENT.md) and current institutions |
| Natural weapons, anatomy, senses, and Species Traits | [Species Development](../progression/SPECIES_DEVELOPMENT.md) and current form |
| Spells, mana, magical reinforcement, and source-specific supernatural effects | [Magical Development](../progression/MAGICAL_DEVELOPMENT.md) and later Magic rules |
| Soul Weapon personhood, form, awakening, and capabilities | [Soul Weapons](../soul-weapons/README.md) |
| Symbolic identity carried across lives | [Soul Titles](../soul/SOUL_TITLES.md) |

## What a Martial Tradition Is Not

### Not a Combat Skill Tree

A Martial Tradition may teach routes through several Human Skill Trees. It is not itself a Skill Tree, and membership never grants every associated Skill or Technique.

### Not a Weapon Category

No tradition owns all uses of a sword, spear, bow, shield, natural weapon, spell focus, vehicle, or improvised tool. Different traditions may use the same tool for different purposes, and one tradition may support several tools.

### Not a Combat Profession

A tradition organizes learning and interpretation. A Profession organizes work, responsibility, service relationships, and accountability. A soldier, guard, duellist, hunter, instructor, or bodyguard may draw from one or several traditions, and a tradition may be practised without becoming employment.

### Not Military Rank or Command

Martial recognition does not create a commission, office, legal authority, unit, obedience, supply, or command relationship. An excellent fighter may be a poor commander, and a capable commander may rely on others for direct combat.

### Not a Personality or Moral Alignment

Training may cultivate Habits, values, rituals, and social pressure. It does not automatically make a person disciplined, loyal, fearless, honorable, cruel, calm, obedient, or willing to kill.

### Not a Power Tier

Tradition names and ranks cannot place combatants on one ladder. Combat remains contextual across embodiment, capability, information, tools, environment, preparation, objective, allies, opposition, fatigue, injury, and chance.

## Martial Tradition Validity Test

A proposed Martial Tradition should answer:

1. **Conflict domain:** What recurring danger, use of force, protective duty, contest, or resistance does it prepare for?
2. **Doctrine:** What shared model connects perception, decision, action, restraint, and consequence?
3. **Transmission:** How can another person learn and test the framework?
4. **Training ecology:** Which partners, spaces, tools, exercises, pressures, feedback, safety measures, and recovery practices sustain learning?
5. **Embodiment:** Which bodies, senses, movement modes, weapons, armor, magic, and environments does the ordinary curriculum assume?
6. **Opposition:** What kinds of opponents, uncertainty, objectives, and counterplay does the tradition meaningfully address?
7. **Ethics and authority:** How does it interpret justified force, restraint, surrender, duty, consent, and responsibility?
8. **Social continuity:** Who preserves, recognizes, disputes, reforms, or rejects the tradition?

If the proposal is only one Technique, weapon preference, famous fighter's style, military unit, battle record, exercise regimen, title, or magical attack, it is not yet a distinct Martial Tradition.

## Martial Tradition Profile

A Martial Tradition Profile extends the [Class Profile](CLASSES.md#class-profile) with conflict-specific fields.

### Required Fields

| Field | What it records |
| --- | --- |
| **Name and scope** | Culture, lineage, region, institution, period, translations, and disputed names. |
| **Conflict domain** | The dangers, contests, protective duties, or uses of force the tradition addresses. |
| **Social purpose** | Why the tradition is preserved and whom it claims to protect, serve, govern, challenge, or prepare. |
| **Martial Doctrine** | Shared principles for perception, initiative, positioning, decision, action, restraint, coordination, and consequence. |
| **Operational contexts** | Terrain, range, visibility, scale, formation, law, equipment, magic, objectives, and common uncertainty. |
| **Embodiment assumptions** | Bodies, senses, natural weapons, reach, mobility, endurance, injury tolerance, and accommodations assumed by teaching. |
| **Arsenal and tools** | Weapons, armor, shields, vehicles, restraints, focuses, maintenance tools, training equipment, and their actual access requirements. |
| **Capability references** | Relevant Skills and Development Tracks; never granted techniques or bonuses. |
| **Training Ecology** | Teachers, partners, exercises, sparring rules, scenarios, facilities, review, safety, recovery, and material costs. |
| **Pressure Practice** | How learners encounter uncertainty, resistance, fear, fatigue, surprise, coordination, and consequence without treating harm as currency. |
| **Use-of-Force Standard** | The tradition's stated boundaries for escalation, restraint, surrender, protection, and responsibility, including disputes. |
| **Group model** | Roles, signals, formations, command assumptions, and dependencies where collective action matters. |
| **Recognition and evidence** | Who recognizes students, practitioners, teachers, or custodians and what demonstrations or conduct support those claims. |
| **Failure and review** | How injuries, tactical defeat, misconduct, brittle Habits, obsolete doctrine, and near misses are examined. |
| **Known counters and blind spots** | Contexts, opponents, strategies, bodies, tools, or assumptions against which the tradition is weak or uncertain. |
| **Related structures** | Professions, institutions, units, laws, rituals, rival traditions, monster societies, or Soul Weapon relationships. |
| **Variants and current condition** | Living branches, schisms, adaptations, suppression, decline, reconstruction, or changed social use. |

The profile records a learning and interpretation framework. It does not need to list every stance, strike, spell, drill, or historical practitioner.

## Martial Doctrine

Martial Doctrine is the tradition's shared model for interpreting conflict and choosing action. It may address:

- what counts as danger;
- what must be protected;
- when to seek, avoid, delay, contain, or end conflict;
- how to use terrain, distance, timing, information, and initiative;
- how individuals and groups divide attention and roles;
- how tools and bodies are matched to tasks;
- when escalation or withdrawal is justified;
- how surrender, prisoners, bystanders, wounded people, and defeated enemies are treated;
- how mistakes and consequences are reviewed.

Doctrine guides decisions; it is neither objective truth nor mind control. It may be insightful, narrow, obsolete, propagandistic, internally disputed, or designed for conditions that no longer exist.

Two practitioners can understand the same doctrine and choose differently because their values, information, responsibilities, capability, or circumstances differ.

## Training Ecology

A Training Ecology is the network of people, places, tools, constraints, exercises, opposition, feedback, safety, recovery, and culture that makes martial learning possible.

It may include:

- solo movement and control practice;
- forms or patterned sequences;
- tool handling and maintenance;
- paired drills;
- controlled sparring;
- wrestling, grappling, restraint, or escape practice;
- formation and signal exercises;
- terrain and visibility scenarios;
- tactical games and case review;
- observation of experienced practitioners;
- supervised field duties;
- physical conditioning and recovery;
- first aid and injury recognition;
- ethical discussion and consequence review;
- equipment fitting and adaptation;
- cross-tradition exchange;
- teaching and correction.

No single method proves a healthy ecology. Forms can refine control while hiding reactions to resistance. Sparring can build timing while overfitting to rules. Live conflict can expose uncertainty while teaching little if no one survives, reflects, or receives correction.

### Forms and Drills

Patterned practice can isolate mechanics, preserve sequences, build vocabulary, and make errors visible. It does not prove performance against resisting opposition or unfamiliar conditions.

### Sparring and Controlled Opposition

Sparring supplies resistance, timing, adaptation, and emotional pressure within stated safety rules. Every ruleset creates omissions. A tradition should know which actions, weapons, injuries, allies, terrain, magic, or objectives its sparring does not represent.

### Scenario Practice

Scenarios can test information, judgment, communication, protection, withdrawal, nonlethal control, and changing objectives. A rehearsed scenario is not the same as an unknown crisis.

### Field Practice

Real duty can create meaningful experience when it includes relevant variation, feedback, responsibility, and review. Patrol count, kills, wounds, victories, and years served do not generate mastery automatically.

## Pressure Practice

Pressure Practice is controlled or supervised training that introduces meaningful resistance, uncertainty, consequence, or physiological and social stress while preserving a learning purpose.

Useful pressure may involve:

- an uncooperative partner;
- incomplete information;
- time pressure;
- fatigue within safe limits;
- noise or restricted visibility;
- conflicting objectives;
- protecting another person;
- coordinating with unfamiliar allies;
- equipment failure;
- choosing restraint;
- recognizing when to stop.

Pressure must be proportionate to the learner, purpose, safeguards, and available recovery. Terror, humiliation, hazing, deliberate maiming, forced killing, and preventable trauma do not become superior teaching merely because they are intense.

Hardship can cause real adaptation or Skill Development when meaningful learning occurs. Suffering itself is not Development currency.

## Use of Force, Ethics, and Agency

A Use-of-Force Standard states how one tradition or responsible authority interprets escalation, restraint, protection, surrender, and accountability in a defined context.

Possible considerations include:

- immediacy and severity of threat;
- available alternatives;
- duty to protect;
- consent and lawful authority;
- risk to bystanders;
- ability to withdraw or contain;
- proportionality;
- treatment of surrender and incapacitation;
- obligations after force is used;
- disclosure and review.

No standard is universal. A duelling code, wartime doctrine, community self-defence practice, hunting ethic, guard procedure, sacred vow, and resistance movement may disagree sharply.

The GM should preserve those disagreements as social and moral facts. Tradition membership cannot decide a player's ethics, compel obedience, remove fear, force violence, or excuse conduct automatically. Oaths, coercion, magical control, law, and command authority each require their own valid sources and resistance rules.

## Weapons, Armor, and Tools

A tradition can structure access, maintenance, safe handling, tactical use, and social meaning around equipment. It cannot create equipment or make every practitioner proficient.

For each tool, distinguish:

- possession and legal access;
- physical compatibility;
- maintenance and supply;
- learned handling;
- tactical understanding;
- integration with armor, allies, terrain, and objectives;
- current condition;
- cultural recognition;
- magical or Soul-owned properties.

Weapon quality matters through actual reach, mass, balance, material, durability, edge, ammunition, enchantment, concealment, and maintenance. There is no universal weapon rarity or superiority ladder.

### Soul Weapons

A Soul Weapon is a person-linked weapon relationship governed by Soul Weapon rules. A Martial Tradition may teach ways to communicate, train, coordinate, or negotiate with one, but:

- tradition membership does not awaken a Weapon Soul;
- a Soul Weapon does not grant the entire tradition;
- the weapon's consent and agency remain relevant;
- prior-life coordination still requires current embodied calibration;
- a school's claim of ownership cannot erase the Weapon Soul's personhood;
- dramatic weapon names do not create conceptual or magical effects.

## Individual and Group Practice

Some traditions primarily train individual conflict. Others depend on formations, paired roles, crews, mounts, familiars, fortifications, supply, healers, scouts, commanders, or civilian coordination.

Group capability belongs to:

- members' actual Skills and Development;
- shared signals and doctrine;
- trust and communication;
- practised role coordination;
- leadership and decision structures;
- equipment and logistics;
- information;
- current morale and condition;
- terrain and objective.

It does not belong to a leader aura, formation bonus, uniform, unit title, or tradition label. Breaking one dependency can change a group's effectiveness without changing every member's personal capability.

## Matchups, Counters, and Tactical Context

Martial effectiveness is always assessed for a specific objective and context.

Relevant factors include:

- what each side knows or believes;
- preparation and surprise;
- initiative and distance;
- terrain, visibility, weather, and escape routes;
- body, reach, mobility, senses, size, and current condition;
- weapons, armor, magic, restraints, and maintenance;
- Skill breadth, specialization, reliability, and adaptation;
- allies, formations, command, supply, and communication;
- objectives such as delay, capture, protection, escape, demonstration, or survival;
- willingness to accept injury, collateral harm, exposure, or political consequence;
- known counters and unfamiliar threats.

A specialist can defeat a broader practitioner when the matchup favors the specialist's preparation and narrow mastery. A weaker combatant can prevail through terrain, information, tools, allies, timing, deception, or a better objective. A famous master can lose without the rules declaring the tradition false.

### Counterplay

Every repeatable martial approach should expose some combination of:

- preparation requirements;
- range or timing limits;
- resource costs;
- predictable commitments;
- environmental dependencies;
- coordination needs;
- recovery windows;
- information assumptions;
- legal or social consequences;
- opponents or methods it handles poorly.

A claim with no meaningful counterplay belongs under the owning Skill, magic, species, Soul, or artifact rules and must still satisfy that system's limits.

## Recognition, Ranks, and Demonstration

Martial ranks remain local Class Recognition. They may indicate:

- completion of a curriculum segment;
- safe participation in certain exercises;
- permission to carry or teach a tool;
- trust to supervise Pressure Practice;
- doctrinal knowledge;
- conduct under a code;
- service or seniority;
- political or lineage standing;
- demonstrated capability in a bounded context.

Every rank must state what it actually evidences. A ceremonial title may be socially powerful and tactically irrelevant. A low-ranked outsider may be highly capable. Tournament victory may demonstrate one ruleset and little about battlefield leadership, restraint, monster hunting, or unfamiliar weapons.

Useful assessment includes:

- technical control;
- perception and decision quality;
- adaptation under resistance;
- safety and restraint;
- performance across relevant contexts;
- recognition of limits;
- coordination;
- equipment care;
- recovery and review;
- conduct under the tradition's stated responsibilities.

No single duel, kill, survival, examination, or display proves universal martial mastery.

## Traditions Across Bodies and Species

A Martial Tradition's ordinary curriculum may assume human proportions, hands, vision, balance, lifespan, injury response, and tools. Those assumptions are local, not metaphysical.

Translation to another body may require:

- new guard geometry;
- different balance and locomotion;
- alternate grips or interfaces;
- changed distance and timing;
- sensory translation;
- different conditioning and recovery;
- revised safety rules;
- new team roles;
- abandonment of incompatible Techniques;
- creation of Adaptive Skills or Class Variants through present practice.

Monster societies may possess martial traditions organized around flight, burrowing, venom, web structures, collective bodies, territory, migration, or other nonhuman conditions. Do not classify them as crude versions of human schools.

A human may learn within a monster tradition and a monster may learn within a human one when communication, access, embodiment, consent, and a valid receiving route exist. Social affiliation grants neither Species Traits nor the other progression tree.

## Reincarnation and Soul Interactions

### Reincarnation

[Reincarnation](../soul/REINCARNATION.md) may preserve relevant Skill history, Stat XP, judgment, Soul Imprints, and meaningful memories. It does not preserve the former body, equipment, unit, rank, licence, opponents, teacher relationship, political authority, or current recognition.

The current incarnation must rebuild:

- bodily capacity and calibration;
- tool handling in the present body;
- timing under current conditions;
- Practised Reliability;
- knowledge of present weapons, magic, laws, species, and tactics;
- relationships and coordination;
- any current affiliation or authority.

### Retained Instincts

[Retained Instincts](../soul/RETAINED_INSTINCTS.md) may orient attention toward danger, spacing, balance, or familiar cues. They do not execute complete Techniques, distinguish ally from enemy perfectly, or override current choice. Untranslated defensive Habits can be dangerous in a new body or culture.

### Soul Echoes

[Soul Echoes](../soul/SOUL_ECHOES.md) may offer tactical memories, doctrinal disagreement, or warnings from prior conflicts. They remain source-bounded and cannot take responsibility for the current incarnation's choices.

### Soul Resonance

[Soul Resonance](../soul/SOUL_RESONANCE.md) may reveal meaningful continuity with a former tradition, teacher, battlefield, or weapon. It does not award current Skill, rank, objective truth, or victory.

### Soul Titles

[Soul Titles](../soul/SOUL_TITLES.md) may affect morale, challenge, fear, expectation, or symbolic legitimacy. King Killer may draw caution or provocation from a royal guard tradition, but it does not penetrate armor, defeat kings, or command rebels automatically.

### Soul Avatars

[Soul Avatars](../soul/SOUL_AVATARS.md) may synthesize several martial lives and expose conflicting doctrines. They cannot perform extra actions, supply missing anatomy, or remove the present self's agency.

## Skill Interactions

### Human Skill Trees

[Human Skill Trees](../skills/HUMAN_SKILL_TREES.md) map capability relationships that traditions may teach. One tradition may traverse several trees; several traditions may teach comparable outcomes through different branches.

### Adaptive Skills

[Adaptive Skills](../skills/ADAPTIVE_SKILLS.md) may emerge when practitioners repeatedly solve a genuine unresolved martial problem through variation, feedback, and integration. A new drill, nickname, victory, or institutional decree does not create one.

### Skill Evolution and Fusion

[Skill Evolution](../skills/SKILL_EVOLUTION.md) and [Skill Fusion](../skills/SKILL_FUSION.md) remain capability changes owned by the Skill Engine. A promotion, secret scroll, lineage initiation, weapon acquisition, or tradition merger cannot trigger them without their own evidence and requirements.

### Active and Passive Expression

[Active and Passive Skills](../skills/ACTIVE_AND_PASSIVE_SKILLS.md) governs guard Habits, reflexes, conditioned responses, maintained states, automatic defence, disruption, and Conscious Override. Tradition language cannot turn every trained response into costless passive protection.

### Conceptual Skills

[Conceptual Skills](../skills/CONCEPTUAL_SKILLS.md) may arise from deep, source-valid mastery of principles such as distance, momentum, rhythm, balance, or boundaries. A poetic doctrine or elite title does not create conceptual access or unlimited metaphysical force.

## Martial Tradition Claim Resolution

When a Martial Tradition is invoked in play:

1. **Name the claim.** Is it about affiliation, capability, rank, authority, doctrine, equipment, discipline, reputation, or outcome?
2. **Identify the tradition version.** Name its culture, lineage, institution, period, and relevant Class Variant.
3. **Name the conflict context.** Establish objective, opposition, information, environment, scale, law, and consequence.
4. **Check actual capability.** Resolve Physical Development, Skills, magic, Species Traits, equipment, and Soul effects through their owners.
5. **Check embodiment and condition.** Apply body, calibration, injury, fatigue, fear, tools, armor, and environment.
6. **Check training relevance.** Determine which doctrine, exercises, and pressure history actually address this situation.
7. **Check social facts.** Resolve recognition, rank, office, command, profession, authority, reputation, and obligations separately.
8. **Check opposition and counterplay.** Identify unfamiliar threats, commitments, dependencies, and tactical responses.
9. **Resolve the objective.** Judge success for the stated aim rather than assigning one combat power score.
10. **Apply consequence and review.** Preserve injury, resource use, exposure, legal response, moral consequence, learning opportunities, and uncertainty.

Do not resolve the claim by tradition rank, number of Techniques, kill count, title, or narrative reputation alone.

## Worked Examples

### Civic First-Blood Duelling

A republic's civic duelling tradition prepares advocates to settle certain public disputes under witness. Its doctrine emphasizes measure, visible intent, controlled escalation, and stopping at first blood. It teaches excellent one-on-one timing inside a bounded space and strong awareness of the duel's legal signals.

Its practitioners may be poorly prepared for ambush, armor, multiple attackers, monsters that do not read human surrender gestures, or protection of bystanders. The tradition remains valid and effective within its domain without becoming universal sword mastery.

### Mountain Oath-Guard

A mountain tradition trains small groups to protect travellers on narrow paths. It emphasizes stable footing, shields, short weapons, warning calls, controlled retreat, and holding enough time for civilians to pass a choke point.

One oath-guard is not automatically a legal guard, a loyal person, or a skilled open-field soldier. The doctrine's strength comes from terrain, coordination, and purpose; on flat ground against mobile archers its assumptions may fail.

### The Broad Veteran and the Narrow Specialist

A veteran has practised several weapons, formations, and campaign roles with sound general judgment. A younger specialist has spent years on close grappling in heavy clothing and confined rooms.

In an unarmed corridor capture, the specialist's narrow mastery and preparation can prevail. In a confused evacuation involving ranged threats and several allies, the veteran's breadth may be more effective. Neither can be assigned one truthful universal martial rank.

### Reincarnated Sword Instructor

A former instructor reincarnates into a short, four-armed body with unfamiliar joints and balance. Retained Instincts make distance and threat feel familiar, while old two-handed forms produce collisions and exposed angles.

The soul can diagnose some errors quickly but must rebuild strength, calibration, timing, and tool design through current practice. The result may become a new Class Variant rather than restored performance.

### Monster Tradition and Human Student

A flying monster society teaches a tradition of three-dimensional interception using wind cues, flock spacing, and controlled dives. A human rider studies its doctrine.

The rider can learn prediction, communication, and some tactical principles. Flight anatomy does not transfer. Effective participation depends on a mount relationship, equipment, current Skill, and translated team roles rather than affiliation alone.

### Soul Weapon Disagreement

A sword-bearing tradition teaches aggressive initiative, while an awakened Weapon Soul remembers generations of defensive bodyguard work and rejects one exercise as reckless.

The disagreement is a relationship and doctrinal conflict. Neither tradition rank nor weapon status compels obedience. The pair may negotiate a variant, separate, seek another teacher, or accept consequences from unresolved mistrust.

### Nonlethal Restraint Under Pressure

A city watch tradition trains de-escalation, team positioning, shields, restraint tools, and medical checks. One practitioner can win tournament bouts but repeatedly escalates too quickly in crowded streets.

Tournament success is relevant technical evidence, not proof of professional judgment or safe watch practice. Present scenario review and supervised duty may reveal what must change.

## Safeguards

### No Kill, Injury, or Survival Currency

Kills, wounds, battles, near-death events, duels, victories, losses, and years of service matter through actual learning and consequence. They never become automatic martial XP, class ranks, Skills, Soul growth, or immunity.

### No Technique Bundles

Joining, inheriting, naming, graduating, ranking, or reincarnating into a tradition does not award Techniques, passive effects, weapon proficiency, spells, or physical capacity.

### No Universal Combat Rank

Tradition grades, tournament records, military ranks, titles, reputation, and kill counts cannot replace contextual capability assessment.

### No Secret-Technique Bypass

A hidden manual, final form, lineage secret, forbidden stance, or dramatic name still requires valid Skill ownership, prerequisites, embodiment, practice, cost, and counterplay.

### No Weapon Superiority Ladder

Weapon categories and named artifacts remain contextual tools and persons. Rarity, age, size, material, enchantment, or fame does not make one universally superior.

### No Abuse as Best Training

Hazing, terror, humiliation, forced combat, preventable injury, deprivation, and trauma may cause real consequences. They are not intrinsically efficient learning and must not become the optimal route because intensity is mistaken for Development.

### No Automatic Discipline, Loyalty, or Courage

Training can develop capabilities and Habits, but character beliefs, fear, ethics, allegiance, and decisions remain subject to agency and their own causal histories.

### No Tradition Monopoly

A tradition's political claim to a weapon, method, title, or body of knowledge does not make independent discovery impossible or erase comparable traditions. Secrecy and enforcement remain causal world facts.

### No Reincarnated Mastery

Retained history can accelerate relearning and interpretation. It does not restore body, calibration, current reliability, equipment, rank, recognition, or authority.

### No Human Default

Monster and mixed-society martial traditions remain valid on their own embodied and cultural terms. Human taxonomy does not determine their sophistication.

### Preserve Consequence Without Predestination

Training choices, doctrine, violence, injury, reputation, law, and relationships should matter. A tradition does not guarantee victory, corruption, heroism, trauma, pacifism, or a predetermined life path.

- a universal combat engine;
- complete weapon lists or damage values;

- military organizations and Academy governance defined through [Institutions and Academies](INSTITUTIONS_AND_ACADEMIES.md), and legal systems owned by the World Engine;
- universal tournament rules;
- Soul Weapon awakening, evolution, compatibility, or manifestations;
- Monster Evolution routes defined in the [Monster Evolution rules](../monster-evolution/README.md);
