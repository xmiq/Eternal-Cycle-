# Professions

## Status

This document defines the canonical social structure and adjudication of Professions for Phase 5. It extends [Profession Development](../progression/PROFESSION_DEVELOPMENT.md), which remains authoritative for how Professional Competence, judgment, reliability, and retained professional progression develop.

Read [Human Class Philosophy](HUMAN_CLASS_PHILOSOPHY.md) and [Human Classes](CLASSES.md) first. A Profession is not a Human Class, even when one class is commonly used to prepare practitioners for one profession.

## Core Rule

A Profession is an organized or recognizable field of work and responsibility that serves a recurring social, economic, sacred, institutional, or ecological function through integrated knowledge, Skills, judgment, standards, tools, relationships, and accountability.

Professions answer:

> What work is being undertaken, for whom or what purpose, to what standard, with what responsibility, and under which social conditions?

They do not answer every question about how the practitioner learned, what the practitioner can currently do, what office the practitioner holds, or what the soul permanently is.

## Ownership Between Phase 2 and Phase 5

The distinction is deliberate:

| Question | Owner |
| --- | --- |
| How do knowledge, Skill, judgment, reliability, and professional mastery develop? | [Profession Development](../progression/PROFESSION_DEVELOPMENT.md) |
| What socially recognizable field of work exists, and how is it organized? | This document |
| Through what culturally situated framework is relevant expertise taught and interpreted? | [Human Classes](CLASSES.md) |
| What individual capabilities are actually present? | [Skill Engine](../skills/README.md) |
| What organization controls people, resources, procedures, or access? | [Institutions and Academies](INSTITUTIONS_AND_ACADEMIES.md) |
| What authority, reputation, relationship, or legitimacy exists? | [Social and Leadership Development](../progression/SOCIAL_AND_LEADERSHIP_DEVELOPMENT.md) and current world causality |

A Profession Profile may reference every adjacent fact, but it cannot grant or silently redefine them.

## The Profession Validity Test

A proposed Profession should answer all of the following:

1. **Recurring function:** What recurring need, service, responsibility, or kind of work does it address?
2. **Integrated practice:** Why does the work require a coherent combination of capability and judgment rather than one isolated task?
3. **Beneficiary or consequence:** Who or what relies on the work, and what can be helped, harmed, preserved, changed, or lost?
4. **Standards:** How do relevant people distinguish acceptable work, dangerous work, and unresolved uncertainty?
5. **Responsibility:** What is the practitioner expected to decide, disclose, refuse, protect, repair, document, or escalate?
6. **Social continuity:** How is the work recognized, requested, assigned, exchanged, transmitted, or reproduced across practitioners?
7. **Material context:** Which tools, resources, infrastructure, relationships, law, ecology, or magic make the work possible?

If the proposal is only a Skill, temporary task, office, product, employer label, social title, or dramatic event, it is not yet a separate Profession.

Payment and formal recognition are not required. A community-supported healer, household craft lineage, sacred caretaker, volunteer rescue practice, and monster migration guide may each be professional when the validity test is met. Being paid once does not make an activity a Profession.

## Profession Profile

A Profession Profile is the smallest useful record of one socially recognizable field of work. It supports adjudication without becoming a job roster, economy simulation, or capability package.

### Required Fields

| Field | What it records |
| --- | --- |
| **Name and scope** | The profession's names, translations, cultures, jurisdictions, and disputed classifications. |
| **Social function** | The recurring need, service, responsibility, or consequence the work addresses. |
| **Professional roles** | Distinct responsibilities within the field, kept separate from capability and office. |
| **Beneficiaries and affected parties** | Clients, patients, communities, institutions, ecosystems, descendants, spirits, or others who bear consequences. |
| **Work and outcomes** | Typical decisions, processes, products, services, records, maintenance, or interventions. |
| **Standards and uncertainty** | Accepted evidence, safety, quality, process, disclosure, and limits, including known disputes. |
| **Capability dependencies** | References to Skills, Development Tracks, tools, teams, and knowledge; never automatic grants. |
| **Class and training routes** | Common Human Classes, apprenticeships, schools, traditions, or informal routes that may prepare practitioners. |
| **Judgment boundaries** | Decisions requiring professional interpretation, collaboration, referral, refusal, or escalation. |
| **Tools and infrastructure** | Materials, workshops, records, supply chains, assistants, magical systems, protected places, or ecological conditions. |
| **Recognition and evidence** | Which audiences recognize practitioners and what those audiences believe credentials, work, testimony, or reputation establish. |
| **Jurisdiction and authority** | Any law, contract, rite, office, licence, community mandate, or territorial scope governing who may act. |
| **Accountability** | Who may question, review, sanction, compensate, dismiss, correct, or require repair, and through what process. |
| **Work arrangements** | Common patronage, exchange, wage, household, communal, military, guild, temple, state, itinerant, or independent structures. |
| **Embodiment and access** | Bodily, species, language, magical, cultural, legal, and material assumptions, plus known accommodations. |
| **Current condition** | Demand, scarcity, oversupply, fragmentation, regulation, suppression, obsolescence, reconstruction, or emergence. |

### Optional Fields

A profile may record:

- specializations and overlapping professions;
- common Class Equivalence Claims;
- ethical controversies;
- customary fees or exchanges where the World Engine establishes them;
- public misconceptions;
- common failures and referral boundaries;
- relations with monster and mixed societies;
- effects of war, migration, catastrophe, or changing magic;
- inherited inequalities in access;
- how professional records survive practitioners and institutions.

Particular current employees, clients, contracts, prices, vacancies, and assignments are campaign state and remain outside this repository.

## Professional Roles

A Professional Role is a bounded set of responsibilities within a Profession. Roles help distinguish what someone is expected to do without implying a universal rank.

Examples include:

- intake and initial assessment;
- technical specialist;
- field practitioner;
- safety reviewer;
- record keeper;
- team coordinator;
- supplier or maintainer;
- independent examiner;
- teacher or supervisor;
- emergency responder;
- public liaison;
- referral specialist.

Roles can overlap, rotate, disappear, or be divided differently by culture. One role may be an office in one institution, a rotating community duty in another, and an independent Profession elsewhere.

Assignment to a role creates responsibility and perhaps access. It does not create competence. Demonstrated competence does not automatically assign the role.

## Professional Status Facts

The following claims must be recorded separately:

- **trained** - has undertaken a relevant learning route;
- **capable** - can perform a stated responsibility under stated conditions;
- **practising** - currently performs relevant work;
- **employed or retained** - has a current work relationship with a named party;
- **commissioned** - has been assigned a bounded task or mandate;
- **credentialed** - an authority claims defined evidence was met;
- **licensed** - has permission within a jurisdiction;
- **recognized** - a named audience accepts a professional identity or standing;
- **office-holding** - occupies a role with institutionally sourced authority;
- **reputable** - is believed to be trustworthy, effective, prestigious, or notorious by an audience;
- **masterful** - demonstrates deep, integrated Professional Competence under the Development rules;
- **responsible** - currently owes defined duties to affected parties.

No one fact proves the others. A retired master may be capable but not practising. A licensed official may be weakly competent. A community healer may be practising and trusted without formal credentials. An employee may be assigned work beyond safe capability.

## Professional Service Relationships

Professional work occurs through relationships among practitioners, affected parties, employers, patrons, institutions, collaborators, regulators, suppliers, and communities.

A Professional Service Relationship should establish as much as the context requires:

- who requests, authorizes, or receives the work;
- what outcome or process is expected;
- what uncertainty has been disclosed;
- which standards apply;
- what resources and access are promised;
- who may make decisions;
- when consent is required;
- when confidentiality or public warning applies;
- when the practitioner must refuse or refer;
- how responsibility is shared;
- what review, compensation, repair, or sanction is possible.

These relationships may be contractual, customary, sacred, communal, familial, coerced, emergency-created, or implicit in an office. Their legitimacy and consequences depend on current law, culture, power, consent, and circumstance.

Coercion, enslavement, debt bondage, discriminatory exclusion, and exploitative apprenticeship may organize real work while remaining abuses. They do not grant competence, moral legitimacy, or permanent identity, and the GM must preserve affected people's agency and consequences rather than treating oppression as neutral professional flavor.

## Standards, Scope, and Uncertainty

A Professional Standard is local and fallible. It should identify:

- the responsibility or process covered;
- the audience or authority recognizing it;
- the evidence on which it is based;
- the bodies, materials, environments, and risks for which it was designed;
- required records, review, or disclosure;
- known exceptions and uncertainty;
- how the standard can be revised or challenged.

Standards can protect people, coordinate teams, preserve hard-won knowledge, and make responsibility reviewable. They can also become obsolete, exclusionary, ceremonial, corrupt, or dangerously overgeneralized.

Following a standard is relevant evidence, not automatic success or innocence. Departing from a standard may be negligent, innovative, necessary, culturally disputed, or irrelevant depending on the evidence and context.

### Scope of Practice

A Professional Scope of Practice is the set of responsibilities a named practitioner or recognized category is considered prepared, permitted, and supported to undertake in a stated context.

Scope may be limited by:

- present capability;
- credentials or licence;
- current tools and team;
- species or body compatibility;
- location and jurisdiction;
- case complexity;
- consent;
- institutional policy;
- available referral and emergency support;
- uncertainty or changed conditions.

Scope is not one permanent tier. A practitioner may be deeply capable in one responsibility and unsafe in an adjacent one.

## Professional Jurisdiction and Authority

Professional Jurisdiction is the social, legal, sacred, institutional, contractual, or territorial scope within which an authority recognizes, restricts, or assigns professional activity.

Jurisdiction can determine:

- who may sign a record;
- who may use restricted tools;
- which acts require a licence;
- whose diagnosis, inspection, or testimony has legal force;
- who carries liability;
- which standards apply;
- who may train or examine others;
- how disputes are heard.

Jurisdiction does not alter factual capability. A border can change legal permission without erasing Skill. An institution can revoke access while the practitioner retains knowledge. An occupying power can impose recognition without earning legitimacy.

Conflicting jurisdictions are world conflicts, not rule errors. The GM should identify which authorities can enforce which consequences and what each affected group believes.

## Accountability and Malpractice

Professional Accountability is the duty to answer for decisions, process, disclosure, foreseeable risk, records, and consequences under a stated responsibility.

Accountability may be enforced through:

- peer review;
- client or community judgment;
- guild discipline;
- court or administrative process;
- religious or ritual review;
- restitution or repair;
- loss of role, licence, access, or trust;
- public record;
- professional correction and retraining;
- refusal by collaborators or suppliers.

The [Profession Development](../progression/PROFESSION_DEVELOPMENT.md) rules own the distinction between failure, uncertainty, negligence, and growth. This document determines which relationships and institutions can review the work and what scoped consequences they can causally impose.

Accountability is not punishment for every bad outcome. Review should consider duty, information available at the time, reasonable alternatives, disclosure, process, scope, coercion, tools, collaboration, and unforeseeable change.

## Classes and Professions Are Many-to-Many

A Human Class may support several Professions. A Profession may receive practitioners from several Human Classes, informal apprenticeships, independent study, monster traditions, or mixed routes.

For each relationship, distinguish:

- which parts of a Class Curriculum are relevant;
- which professional responsibilities remain untrained;
- what practical experience is needed;
- whether an institution recognizes the route;
- which tools, standards, or laws differ;
- what Class Equivalence Claim is being made and for what purpose.

### Class Without Profession

A person may study a contemplative, martial, scholarly, artistic, or ritual class without making it a field of work. Class participation can shape capability and identity without clients, employment, professional responsibility, or economic function.

### Profession Without Human Class

A coherent Profession may be learned informally through household work, community necessity, peer correction, independent experimentation, monster tradition, or distributed practice. Lack of class affiliation does not prove lack of Professional Competence.

### Same Name, Different Fact

"Healer," "ranger," "scribe," "knight," or "priest" may name a Class, Profession, office, rank, reputation, or identity in different cultures. The GM must resolve what the speaker actually means rather than assuming one universal category.

## Specialization, Breadth, and Multiple Professions

A specialization narrows recurring professional responsibility within a broader field. It may deserve its own Profession Profile when it develops distinct integrated judgment, standards, affected parties, tools, and social continuity that the parent profile cannot represent clearly.

A task specialty or favored technique alone is not a Profession.

One person may practise several Professions when current capability, time, tools, relationships, obligations, and recognition support them. Multiple labels do not duplicate Skills or Development. Breadth creates coordination advantages and divided-practice costs through actual circumstances rather than a universal multiclass penalty.

Interdisciplinary work may be represented as:

- collaboration among several professions;
- one practitioner holding several scoped professional relationships;
- a hybrid Profession with genuinely integrated recurring problems and standards;
- a temporary team role for one unusual case.

Use the smallest truthful representation. Do not create a hybrid Profession merely by combining two titles.

## Work Arrangements and Exchange

A Profession's social function may be sustained through:

- wages;
- fees;
- patronage;
- guild allocation;
- household or kin obligation;
- reciprocal community service;
- temple support;
- military or state service;
- shares of production;
- subsistence and protection;
- itinerant exchange;
- communal provisioning;
- magical or ecological symbiosis;
- mixed arrangements.

Compensation affects access, incentives, dependence, bargaining power, and continuity. It does not define competence or whether a coherent Profession exists.

The later World Engine owns prices, labor demand, production, markets, taxation, scarcity, and economic change. Profession rules identify the relationships and dependencies that those systems affect.

## Formation, Change, and Disappearance

### Profession Formation

A new Profession forms when a recurring need produces integrated work, differentiated judgment, standards, responsibility, transmission, tools or relationships, and social continuity. One invention, crisis, title, or successful worker is insufficient by itself.

Formation may be gradual. Different audiences may disagree about whether a novel field is a Profession, a specialization, a scam, an office, a dangerous unregulated practice, or a temporary service.

### Obsolescence

A Profession can become obsolete when its social function disappears, another practice replaces it, required tools or materials vanish, law forbids it, ecology changes, or its standards become unusable.

Practitioners may retain valuable Skills and judgment. Obsolescence changes demand and organization; it does not erase capability.

### Loss and Reconstruction

A Lost Profession lacks enough living continuity to reproduce its integrated work and responsibility. Surviving Skills, tools, archives, Soul Echoes, or reincarnated memories may support reconstruction but do not restore the former Profession automatically.

Reconstruction needs present beneficiaries, methods, standards, materials, testing, transmission, and accountability. The result may be a transformed or novel Profession.

### Institutional Collapse

A Profession may survive the collapse of a guild, academy, temple, state, or company through independent practitioners, communities, records, successor institutions, or migration. Conversely, an institution may retain a professional title after losing the capability needed to perform the work safely.

Detailed institutional continuity is defined in [Institutions and Academies](INSTITUTIONS_AND_ACADEMIES.md).

## Reincarnation and Soul Interactions

### Reincarnation

[Reincarnation](../soul/REINCARNATION.md) may preserve legitimate professional knowledge, Skills, judgment, and Development history. It does not preserve current employment, clients, office, licence, jurisdiction, professional relationships, reputation, property, tools, institutional access, or the continued relevance of old work.

A reincarnated practitioner must establish:

- what retained capability is currently accessible;
- whether the new body can express it;
- which standards, tools, species, laws, and needs have changed;
- what present practice rebuilds reliability;
- whether any living audience recognizes the claim;
- what current responsibilities the person chooses to accept.

### Soul Resonance

[Soul Resonance](../soul/SOUL_RESONANCE.md) may align the soul with meaningful prior work, reveal the importance of an old method, or help reopen retained professional history. It cannot create a client relationship, current standard, licence, tool, or reliable practice.

### Soul Echoes

[Soul Echoes](../soul/SOUL_ECHOES.md) may supply case memories and prior judgment. Their advice remains source-bounded, potentially outdated, and subject to present verification. An Echo cannot accept responsibility in place of the current practitioner.

### Retained Instincts

[Retained Instincts](../soul/RETAINED_INSTINCTS.md) may orient attention toward familiar hazards or workflow cues. They do not supply a complete Profession, ethical judgment, authority, or recognized status.

### Soul Titles

[Soul Titles](../soul/SOUL_TITLES.md) may create symbolic trust, fear, expectation, or opportunity around work. They do not certify competence or force beneficiaries, peers, institutions, gods, or monsters to accept service.

### Soul Avatars

[Soul Avatars](../soul/SOUL_AVATARS.md) may synthesize perspectives from several lives, including conflicting professional values. They do not replace current evidence, tools, accountability, or consent.

## Monster and Mixed-Society Professions

[Intelligent Monster Societies](../monster-evolution/MONSTER_SOCIETIES.md) may organize Professions through bodies, lifecycles, ecologies, communication, and collective capabilities unlike human institutions.

A monster Profession may depend on:

- species senses or materials;
- migration stages;
- colony or pack coordination;
- molting, spawning, hibernation, or metamorphosis;
- territorial relationships;
- pheromone, song, movement, shared dream, or magical transmission;
- ecological obligations not recognized by humans.

Do not treat these as incomplete human jobs. Apply the same Profession Validity Test while preserving their actual social and embodied structures.

In mixed societies, professional access and standards may need translation, accommodations, multi-species teams, separate liability rules, and explicit treatment of who can perform which body-bound tasks. Shared work does not grant Species Traits or progression-tree access.

## Profession Claim Resolution

When a Profession matters in play:

1. **Name the claim.** Is it about capability, work, role, responsibility, employment, credential, licence, authority, reputation, or identity?
2. **Identify the Profession Profile.** Establish its culture, period, jurisdiction, and current condition.
3. **Name the responsibility.** State the actual work, affected parties, expected standard, and uncertainty.
4. **Check actual capability.** Use Profession Development, Skills, and the six capability layers.
5. **Check access and support.** Include body, tools, team, records, supply, language, law, consent, and infrastructure.
6. **Check recognition and authority.** Identify the audience, evidence, office, licence, and enforcement scope separately.
7. **Check the service relationship.** Establish who requested or authorized the work, what was disclosed, and who owes which duties.
8. **Apply accountability.** Resolve review and consequence through process, evidence, foreseeability, and current institutions.
9. **Apply world consequences.** Let demand, trust, prices, law, ecology, factions, and institutions respond through their owning systems.
10. **Record uncertainty.** Keep mistaken reputation, disputed standards, hidden conflicts, and provisional rulings separate from world truth.

Do not replace this procedure with a Profession score, level, XP total, or title check.

## Worked Examples

### One Class, Several Professions

The River-Warden class teaches water observation, crossing safety language, coordination, and public duty. Its affiliates may become ferry inspectors, rescue coordinators, canal surveyors, flood messengers, or remain nonprofessional community volunteers.

The shared class supports learning and coordination. Each Profession adds distinct beneficiaries, standards, tools, judgment, and responsibility. The class does not grant all five professions.

### One Profession, Several Classes

A city's injury-care Profession accepts practitioners from the Lantern Anatomists, Quiet Palm lineage, a military field-care apprenticeship, and an accredited monster brood-healing tradition. Each route contributes different strengths and omissions.

The hospital uses scoped examinations, supervised practice, language training, and role-specific standards. Recognition of one route for diagnosis does not establish equivalence for surgery, magical channel repair, or public-health authority.

### The Licensed but Unsafe Inspector

A bridge inspector holds a valid licence based on an examination passed twenty years ago. New living-stone construction has spread, but the inspector has not retrained and continues applying dead-stone assumptions.

The licence still grants legal access until the authority acts. It does not make the method safe. A failure triggers review of current knowledge, reasonable warning signs, institutional negligence, and the scope of the old standard rather than an automatic verdict based only on outcome.

### The Capable Unrecognized Midwife

A village midwife has extensive present practice, strong judgment, trusted relationships, and reliable results across several local bodies. A conquering state refuses recognition because the midwife lacks its academy credential and legal language.

The exclusion affects licence, supplies, referrals, and risk of sanction. It does not erase competence. Nor does local trust prove competence with unfamiliar species or complications outside the midwife's scope.

### The Obsolete Bell Listener

An old Profession once diagnosed mine instability by listening to enchanted warning bells bound into the rock. The bells fail after a change in magical law.

Bell Listeners retain geological knowledge, disciplined observation, evacuation judgment, and community trust, but their defining tool no longer works. Some become surveyors through retraining; some preserve a lost Profession; some resist evidence and endanger others. Obsolescence changes the field without deleting every capability.

### The Reincarnated Advocate

A soul that practised law in a former republic reincarnates under a hereditary temple court. It retains argument structure, evidence habits, and memories of institutional abuse. The old licence, clients, precedents, and right of audience are gone.

Present study may rebuild a useful legal Profession, expose injustice, or create a novel advocacy role. Acting as if the republican code still controls the court would be professionally irresponsible despite deep retained potential.

### A Mixed-Society Ventilation Profession

Human miners, burrowing monsters, and fungal cultivators jointly develop a Profession responsible for tunnel air, spores, heat, and evacuation. No one body can sense or perform every required task.

The Profession is sustained through a team, translated warning systems, shared standards, and role-specific accountability. Collective competence belongs to coordination and infrastructure, not to a leader aura or automatic transfer of senses.

## Safeguards

### No Profession XP Bar

Work count, years served, pay earned, clients seen, objects produced, emergencies survived, and titles accumulated are not universal Professional Development currency. Use the owning Development rules.

### No Profession Inflation

Do not create a new Profession for every task, specialty, tool, employer, rank, or combination of names. Require recurring integrated work, judgment, standards, responsibility, and social continuity.

### No Class-Profession Collapse

A class teaches and interprets expertise; a Profession organizes work and responsibility. Record their relationship instead of treating either label as the other.

### No Capability by Employment

Employment, assignment, commission, office, credential, licence, uniform, and reputation do not grant Skills or reliability.

### No Authority by Expertise

Professional Competence does not automatically grant legal, political, sacred, or institutional authority. Authority needs an actual source and jurisdiction.

### No Expertise by Authority

An officeholder, patron, noble, senior member, guild leader, or famous practitioner must still possess or obtain the capability required for the work.

### No Automatic Scope Expansion

Success in one role, body, material, culture, species, crisis, or jurisdiction does not establish safe competence everywhere.

### No Reincarnated Standing

Retained expertise may accelerate redevelopment. It cannot restore a job, office, licence, client, property, reputation, current standard, or right to command.

### No Status Shield

Prestige does not suppress evidence of error, and stigma does not erase demonstrated competence. Institutions and publics may behave otherwise, but those biases remain causal world facts rather than objective capability rules.

### Preserve Agency and Consent

Professional duty, oath, debt, expectation, or emergency can create pressure and consequence but cannot silently choose beliefs or actions for a character. Consent requirements, coercion, divided loyalty, refusal, and whistleblowing must remain playable.

## Deferred Ownership

This document does not fully define:

- martial traditions;
- magical schools;
- social advancement and status mobility;
- institutions, guilds, academies, licensing bodies, and governance defined in [Institutions and Academies](INSTITUTIONS_AND_ACADEMIES.md);
- class transformation and succession defined in [Class Evolution](CLASS_EVOLUTION.md);
- final limits defined in [Limits of Human Progression](LIMITS_OF_HUMAN_PROGRESSION.md);
- wages, prices, markets, taxation, or labor simulation;
- technical procedures for particular crafts, medicine, law, magic, or other work.

Those subjects remain with their linked owners, the remaining Phase 5 tasks, Phase 7, and World Engine tasks. Profession Profiles may reference them without inventing their rules.
