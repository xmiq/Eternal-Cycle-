# Retained Instincts

## Purpose

Retained Instincts preserve meaningful, body-shaped patterns of perception and response across reincarnation without granting the anatomy, automatic mastery, or compulsory behavior of a former body.

## Core Rule

A Retained Instinct is a Soul Imprint formed when a way of noticing, prioritizing, or responding becomes deeply integrated through lived embodiment or meaningful adaptation. It preserves a disposition toward a response, not the former body's organs, statistics, species traits, complete memories, or skills.

The current body supplies its own instincts and remains the only body acting in the world. A Retained Instinct may become relevant only through compatible sensation, present circumstances, Soul Resonance, and current-life development.

## Terms

### Current Instinct

An innate or developed response belonging to the present incarnation's body, species, upbringing, or current-life experience. Current Instincts do not need to persist from another life to be real or important.

### Retained Instinct

A persistent Soul Imprint carrying an integrated pattern of attention, urgency, valuation, or response from prior embodiment or adaptation. Use **Retained Instinct** on first reference and **instinct** thereafter when the meaning is clear.

### Instinct Origin

The incarnation, body, environment, relationship, or sustained adaptation that formed the Retained Instinct. An instinct retains its Origin even when later lives translate or reinterpret it.

### Instinct Cue

A present sensation, circumstance, emotion, bodily state, or pattern that makes a Retained Instinct relevant. An Instinct Cue may also serve as a Resonance Trigger when a genuine Resonance Link is involved.

### Instinct Access

The current relationship between an incarnation and one Retained Instinct: Quiescent, Cued, Available, or Naturalized. Access describes present expression, not power or permanent rank.

## What Retained Instincts Preserve

A Retained Instinct may preserve a body-shaped tendency to:

- notice a kind of motion, scent, vibration, posture, current, boundary, or threat;
- prioritize shelter, prey, kin, territory, altitude, escape, concealment, or formation;
- adopt timing, spacing, balance, pursuit, ambush, guarding, or avoidance patterns;
- read social distance, hierarchy, group movement, ritual rhythm, or cooperative roles;
- approach tools, materials, terrain, or repeated work through an integrated physical intuition;
- react emotionally or physically to a pattern that carried survival or identity-level meaning.

The instinct preserves the pattern as the source incarnation experienced it. It does not automatically preserve:

- the sensory organs needed to detect what the former body detected;
- claws, wings, venom, web glands, gills, additional limbs, or other anatomy;
- bodily statistics, mutations, species stages, or biological tolerances;
- a complete technique, profession, spell, language, or body of factual knowledge;
- an objective explanation of why the response once worked;
- authority over the current incarnation's deliberate choices.

## Distinctions

| Element | What it carries | Primary limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Current Instinct** | A response belonging to the present body or life | Normally ends with that body unless meaningfully imprinted |
| **Retained Instinct** | A persistent body-shaped pattern of attention or response | Requires compatible present expression and grants no missing anatomy |
| **Habit** | A repeated current-life behavior | Repetition alone does not make it persistent |
| **Practiced memory** | Intuitive access to something learned | Belongs to memory and skill rules rather than instinct alone |
| **Skill Imprint** | Retained development in a trainable capability | Still requires suitable means, practice, and system access |
| **Soul Echo** | The bounded perspective of one completed incarnation | May explain an instinct but does not own or replace it |
| **Compulsion** | A force that attempts to require behavior | Needs its own explicit cause, resistance, and consequence rules |

The same life may produce all of these in relation to one activity. A spider hunter might leave an Echo that remembers stalking, a Skill Imprint for trap construction, and a Retained Instinct that attends to tension changes. Keeping their functions separate prevents one useful memory from silently granting an entire former body and build.

## Formation

A Retained Instinct forms only when the relevant response becomes a meaningful part of how the soul engages with danger, work, relationship, environment, or identity.

Formation normally requires:

1. **Lived basis:** The soul actually experienced the relevant embodiment or adaptation.
2. **Meaningful use:** The pattern mattered under genuine uncertainty, responsibility, need, or consequence.
3. **Internalization:** The response became more than imitation or a temporary tactic; it shaped perception or decision-making.
4. **Integration:** The soul incorporated the response into a continuing way of engaging with the world.
5. **Persistence:** The pattern remained meaningful beyond one immediate reward or danger.

Sustained life within a body or environment is the most common route. One extraordinary event may reveal or complete an instinct that a life has already been building, but one isolated trivial reaction cannot reliably create a Retained Instinct. Intensity, pain, surprise, or Final Death does not substitute for integration.

Possible sources include:

- long survival within a monster's ecological niche;
- repeated use of an unusual sensory world until it changes attention;
- a protective role that makes monitoring others second nature;
- a craft practiced until material feedback becomes intuitive;
- migration, hunting, formation fighting, nesting, or territorial life that shapes priorities;
- adaptation to a disability, transformed body, hostile habitat, or nonhuman cognition;
- reconciliation of a former bodily response with a new identity.

Routine reflexes may end with the body. A thousand shallow reactions do not outweigh one genuinely integrated adaptation, and not every incarnation needs to form a new Retained Instinct.

## Instinct Access

Each Retained Instinct has its own current Access. These states are qualitative and may change with embodiment, Resonance, practice, injury, and context.

### Quiescent

The instinct persists but has no reliable present expression. The current body may lack a useful Cue, compatible sensation, or sufficient access.

### Cued

A relevant situation produces unease, attention, posture, emotion, imagery, or a brief impulse. The incarnation may not yet recognize the instinct or its Origin.

### Available

The incarnation can identify the pattern and deliberately account for it under suitable conditions. Expression remains effortful, partial, or dependent on tools and present-life practice.

### Naturalized

The instinct has been coherently translated into the current incarnation's own way of perceiving and acting. It can inform ordinary judgment without constant conscious effort, while remaining limited by the present body and situation.

Naturalized does not mean strongest, infallible, or permanently active. A Naturalized swimming instinct may become Quiescent in a body that cannot detect or survive water, and a Cued instinct may be extremely urgent during the one circumstance that activates it.

## Embodiment and Cross-Species Translation

The current body's instincts, senses, and limits are always real. Retained Instincts add historical influence; they do not overwrite the present species.

Expression falls into three broad cases:

- **Directly compatible:** The current body can detect and perform a closely related response. Prior instinct may become useful quickly, though present calibration is still required.
- **Analogically compatible:** The body cannot reproduce the old response literally but can translate its logic through current senses, movement, tools, magic, or training.
- **Incompatible:** No present means can detect or express the pattern. The instinct remains Quiescent or provides only symbolic and emotional context until circumstances change.

Translation preserves function without pretending bodies are interchangeable. A human who once sensed a web through eight legs may learn to read tension through fingers, feet, thread, instruments, or suitable magic. The human does not gain spinnerets or a spider's sensory resolution. A burrowing monster carrying a swordsman's spacing instinct might adapt distance and timing to horns or claws, but does not automatically gain the Swordsmanship skill.

Human and monster progression trees remain distinct. A Retained Instinct may reveal a promising crossover direction or support present experimentation only when Reincarnation, Development, skill, and evolution rules permit it. It cannot by itself unlock a class, evolution, mutation, or hidden branch.

## Activation, Response, and Agency

An Instinct Cue may produce an involuntary micro-response such as attention shifting, muscles preparing, balance changing, fear rising, or a familiar route feeling wrong. This is influence at the scale expected of an instinct, not permission for the GM to choose the character's deliberate action.

By default, a Retained Instinct cannot:

- make the incarnation attack, flee, obey, trust, feed, mate, or submit;
- take executive control of the body;
- speak as an independent personality;
- perform a complete skill or grant an extra action;
- override informed player choice by being described as irresistible.

A curse, frenzy, domination effect, species compulsion, or Soul Wound may create stronger pressure only under its own explicit rules, with fair warning and meaningful resistance. Calling such an effect an instinct does not waive those requirements.

Following an instinct can be wise, mistaken, or disastrous. It reflects adaptation to its Origin, not universal truth. The current incarnation decides whether and how to act on it and bears the consequences.

## Memory, Knowledge, and Provenance

A Retained Instinct carries orientation rather than a narrated recollection. It may signal that a surface is unsafe without recalling the cave where that lesson formed, or create comfort in a formation without identifying the former pack.

Specific memories, explanations, and historical facts require Soul Echo, memory, Resonance, or [Archive](AKASHIC_ARCHIVE.md) access. An Echo may identify an instinct's Origin and explain how it once functioned, but the instinct can remain accessible while that Echo is Dormant. Likewise, a clear Echo does not make every bodily response of its source life a Retained Instinct.

Later translation does not erase provenance. A flight instinct adapted into ship handling still originates in an aerial life. Recording Origin helps the GM distinguish useful analogy from invented knowledge and reveals why similar instincts may disagree.

## Conflict and Reconciliation

Current Instincts and several Retained Instincts may recommend incompatible responses. A prey life's stillness may conflict with a predator life's pursuit; a solitary apex body may resist a former pack instinct; a human social taboo may oppose a monster's territorial reading.

Conflict may cause:

- hesitation, fixation, false alarms, or misread priorities;
- competing postures or impulses;
- intrusive fear, hunger, protectiveness, or aggression;
- an unreliable translation of a useful pattern;
- Dissonance, fatigue, or Soul Strain when several strong Links activate together.

Conflict does not prove that one instinct is false or that the oldest instinct is authoritative. The current incarnation may compare Origins, test responses, set boundaries, practice a compatible synthesis, or accept that different circumstances call for different patterns.

Successful reconciliation may Naturalize a new expression, support a Deepening Event, or create a new Skill Imprint through real practice. It does not merge all instincts into a perfect answer or award development every time the same contradiction is revisited.

## Persistence, Suppression, and Harm

Legitimately formed Retained Instincts normally persist through Final Death, Life Reconciliation, Reincarnation, Interlife, and World Resets.

Access may recede because of:

- incompatible embodiment or absent Cues;
- changed identity or priorities;
- insufficient Soul Resonance, Depth, or Integrity;
- Dissonance among active instincts;
- a Soul Wound, sealed Region, damaged Link, or hostile effect;
- deliberate restraint while the incarnation develops a safer expression.

Reduced Access does not erase the Soul Imprint. Permanent removal or rewriting requires an explicit soul-harm rule proportionate to persistent progression, with an established cause, fair warning, meaningful resistance, and discoverable consequences.

An instinct may change through later integration. Its expression can broaden, narrow, become contextual, or combine with current-life development, but its historical Origin cannot be retroactively replaced for convenience.

## System Interactions

- **[Soul Fundamentals](SOUL_FUNDAMENTALS.md):** Retained Instincts are Soul Imprints and remain subject to embodiment, current agency, Integrity, and meaningful integration.
- **[Reincarnation](REINCARNATION.md):** Life Reconciliation recognizes qualifying instincts, and new embodiment determines their initial compatibility and Access.
- **[Soul Depth](SOUL_DEPTH.md):** Depth supports holding, distinguishing, and reconciling many body-shaped responses without granting them.
- **[Soul Resonance](SOUL_RESONANCE.md):** Reflexive Resonance can identify an instinct, activate a valid Cue, or support translation through a genuine Link.
- **[Soul Echoes](SOUL_ECHOES.md):** An Echo may contextualize its source life's instincts but neither owns nor automatically grants them.
- **[Soul Space](SOUL_SPACE.md):** Instincts may appear symbolically for examination and reconciliation without becoming residents or independent beings.
- **[Soul Constellations](SOUL_CONSTELLATIONS.md):** Familiarity or caution may act as a Trigger, but instinct alone cannot identify another soul or create a recurring relationship.
- **[Soul Titles](SOUL_TITLES.md):** Instinct may influence present Title Expression, while behavior without integrated identity and consequence cannot create a title by itself.
- **Soul Weapons:** A Weapon Soul may learn an incarnation's cues and cooperate with a compatible response, but remains a distinct person and does not inherit the instinct automatically.
- **[Akashic Archive](AKASHIC_ARCHIVE.md):** A valid Archive reading may verify or contextualize an Origin; an instinct does not provide general access or objective history.
- **Development and skills:** Present-life practice turns useful orientation into reliable capability. Retained Instincts do not replace Skill Imprints, training, or bodily development.
- **Monster evolution:** An instinct may become evidence for compatibility or a hidden direction only when the evolution system recognizes it; it is never an automatic unlock.

## Exploit Limits

- Repeating safe reflex drills does not engrave or strengthen a Retained Instinct indefinitely.
- Suicide, rapid reincarnation, manufactured fear, and disposable bodies do not create useful instincts by themselves.
- Killing, eating, dissecting, copying, possessing, or observing a creature does not transfer its instincts.
- A large collection of shallow lives does not produce an all-purpose danger sense or perfect answer to every situation.
- Instinct cannot reveal facts, enemies, traps, souls, or future events that its Origin and current senses could not support.
- Retained Instincts cannot grant missing anatomy, species traits, bodily statistics, skill mastery, extra actions, or progression-tree access.
- Deliberately provoking conflict or Dissonance does not guarantee Soul Depth, skill fusion, mutation, or evolution.
- Naming an impulse as instinct cannot bypass player agency, consent, resistance, or another system's costs.

## GM Procedure

When a Retained Instinct may become relevant:

1. identify the instinct and its specific Origin;
2. confirm that meaningful adaptation and integration justified its formation;
3. identify the present Instinct Cue;
4. compare the old response with the current body's actual senses and capabilities;
5. classify expression as direct, analogical, or incompatible;
6. determine the current Instinct Access and any relevant Resonance State;
7. present attention, urgency, posture, emotion, or intuition without choosing the character's deliberate action;
8. distinguish orientation from memory, knowledge, skill, and compulsion;
9. resolve conflict, Dissonance, fatigue, or Strain when established causes warrant it;
10. record meaningful translation or reconciliation without assigning universal instinct points.

The GM may conceal an Origin while it remains a fair mystery, but the Cues and later evidence must be consistent. An instinct should make prior embodiment matter without answering unrelated plot questions.

## Examples

### Web Sense Without Spinnerets

A human incarnation carries a cavern spider's instinct for tension changes. Footsteps on an ordinary stone floor do not become supernatural radar. When the human rigs fine cords across a campsite and practices reading them through fingers and feet, the instinct becomes Available and later Naturalized as a trapper's intuition. Skill still develops through present work.

### The Solitary Pack Leader

A young wyvern carries a wolf life's pack-coordination instinct. The wyvern's current territorial impulses initially treat nearby creatures as intruders, while an Instinct Cue draws its attention to spacing, shared movement, and isolated stragglers. Choosing to build a cooperative hunting group could translate the old pattern; the instinct cannot force loyalty from either the wyvern or its companions.

### The Smith in a Monster Body

A former human smith reincarnates as a heat-resistant salamander monster. The soul retains an instinct for changes in material under heat, but the new body lacks hands and smithing tools. The salamander may learn to arrange ore with its jaws and regulate a furnace with its body, eventually developing a monster-compatible craft. The instinct supplies attention to feedback, not the entire profession.

### Predator and Prey

A soul has lived as both a stalking cat and a burrowing prey animal. In tall grass, one instinct urges stillness while the other highlights the advantage of immediate pursuit. The conflict creates hesitation until the current incarnation learns to distinguish whether it is hiding, hunting, or protecting someone. Neither former life becomes the true self by winning automatically.

### Wings Remembered

A goblin retains an aerial life's instinct for reading rising air. It feels a Cued sense of opportunity near a heated cliff but cannot perceive or ride the current accurately with an ordinary body. With a glider, training, and real sensory feedback, the instinct may support analogical flight judgment. Without a compatible means, it remains suggestive rather than functional.
