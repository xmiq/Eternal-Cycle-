# Reincarnation

## Purpose

Reincarnation allows a soul to continue after death through a new body, species, culture, location, or Age while preserving soul-level progression and the consequences of the completed life.

## Core Rule

Reincarnation begins only after an incarnation reaches Final Death. It carries the soul forward; it does not revive the body, rewind the world, transport ordinary possessions, or restore the life that ended.

Every new incarnation must be a valid meeting between the soul and the living world. The soul brings persistent potential. The new body brings its own anatomy, instincts, circumstances, and limits.

## Terms

### Final Death

The point at which the current incarnation can no longer return through ordinary bodily recovery or any currently applicable established revival effect. Reincarnation and revival cannot complete for the same incarnation.

### Life Reconciliation

The transition process that determines what ended with the incarnation, what remained in the world, and what became or remained part of the soul.

### Interlife

The interval between Final Death and embodiment in the next incarnation. It may be consciously experienced, partially remembered, or entirely absent from perception.

### Reincarnation Mode

The agreed method used to determine the next valid incarnation. A mode controls who chooses among possibilities; it does not bypass eligibility or world causality.

## Eligibility

Possessing a soul does not by itself guarantee reincarnation.

Capitalized **Reincarnation** is the soul-level capability that reliably carries continuity into another incarnation. Other beings may reincarnate through setting-specific laws, divine systems, [Soul Constellations](SOUL_CONSTELLATIONS.md), or unknown causes, but those possibilities do not grant them the named capability.

Reincarnation may improve through meaningful use and soul development. Improvement may provide greater transition stability, broader awareness during Interlife, more influence over valid candidates, or better access to retained imprints. It does not award power merely for dying.

## Transition Procedure

### 1. Confirm Final Death

Resolve currently applicable recovery, resurrection, substitution, or survival effects first. Once Reincarnation proceeds beyond severance, the completed incarnation cannot also return through an ordinary revival effect.

Final Death must preserve external consequences. The body, witnesses, enemies, dependants, institutions, and unfinished events remain part of the world.

### 2. Sever the Incarnation

Separate the soul from body-bound and identity-bound assets.

- The body and its ordinary statistics end with the incarnation.
- Ordinary items remain where the death and surrounding events leave them. They are lost to the reincarnating soul even when survivors can recover them.
- Wealth, office, legal identity, and social authority do not follow the soul.
- Body-bound mutations, species stages, conditions, and biological traits end with the body.
- An established Soul Weapon bond and Weapon Soul accompany the soul. The weapon's physical manifestation follows its detailed rules.

Severance does not erase the world's memory of the dead incarnation. Survivors may mourn, celebrate, investigate, inherit, retaliate, or reshape history in response.

### 3. Perform Life Reconciliation

Classify each relevant element of the completed life into one of three outcomes:

1. **Ends with the incarnation:** bodily statistics, temporary effects, body-bound abilities, and identity-dependent authority.
2. **Remains in the world:** ordinary possessions, consequences, creations, descendants, debts, institutions, reputation attached to the former identity, and physical remains.
3. **Persists with the soul:** established Soul Imprints, soul-level systems, and bonds whose detailed rules permit persistence.

During Life Reconciliation, resolve the following when applicable:

- form the incarnation's new Soul Echo under the Soul Echo rules;
- change the access, later context, relationship, or condition of an existing Echo when established events warrant it;
- recognize new Soul Imprints;
- preserve legitimately acquired skills, Skill XP, and all legitimately earned Stat XP as Soul Imprints;
- alter Soul Titles or Soul Constellations;
- reveal Soul Strain or a Soul Wound;
- resolve the state of a Soul Weapon bond.

Death is not itself an achievement. Reconciliation recognizes what the life meaningfully developed before death; it does not grant a completion bonus simply because the life ended.

### 4. Resolve Interlife

Determine how much time passes and whether the soul is aware during it.

Interlife may last moments, generations, or Ages. The world continues through that interval under the World Engine. Political change, migration, extinction, technological development, disaster, and World Resets may occur without the soul's participation.

Interlife awareness depends on established soul development and conditions. It may allow reflection or communication but does not provide unlimited training, effortless recovery, or freedom from external time.

### 5. Generate Valid Incarnation Candidates

A valid candidate must satisfy all applicable constraints:

- **Embodiment:** There is a plausible birth, hatching, spawning, construction, awakening, or other species-appropriate route into life.
- **World state:** The species, location, era, and circumstances are possible after the Interlife interval.
- **Soul compatibility:** The soul can inhabit the form without violating established capacity, integrity, bond, or progression rules.
- **Earned access:** Rare, ancient, hybrid, or otherwise exceptional forms meet their unlock conditions.
- **Causal placement:** The incarnation belongs to the world rather than appearing without history or consequence.
- **Variety and focus:** Candidate generation supports monster reincarnation as a major form of play and avoids making one familiar choice universally optimal.

Soul history may influence valid candidates through Soul Resonance, Soul Titles, retained instincts, prior species mastery, Soul Constellations, World Reset effects, or other explicit rules.

### 6. Apply the Reincarnation Mode

Use the mode established for the campaign or current Cycle:

- **Emergent:** The GM or world system selects one valid candidate from established causes and hidden rules.
- **Constrained Choice:** The player chooses among a limited set of valid candidates revealed by the GM or system.
- **Directed:** The player names a desired direction, species family, or known option; the world grants it only if it is valid and earned.

A mode may be changed by an explicit rule, major soul development, or a new Cycle agreement. It should not be changed after seeing an undesirable result merely to obtain a stronger body.

Unknown or concealed properties may remain part of a candidate when the relevant mystery is fairly discoverable. The GM must not hide information solely to invalidate an informed choice.

### 7. Embody and Awaken

Create the new incarnation with:

- a body and starting stage appropriate to its species;
- current bodily statistics based on that body rather than prior stat values;
- innate species instincts and capabilities;
- a real location, social context, and ecological situation;
- access only to Soul Imprints that meet their current conditions;
- any Soul Strain, Soul Wounds, bonds, or transition effects that genuinely persist.

The default entry point is the earliest stage at which meaningful play can begin. Earlier infancy, gestation, incubation, or formation may be summarized without granting free development. A mature entry requires a valid history, and that history must account for the body's existing development and consequences.

Once embodiment begins, the incarnation is final unless another established effect intervenes. Rejecting a body requires another genuine transition; Reincarnation is not a menu that can be reopened until a perfect result appears.

## Starting Memory

The new incarnation begins as the current active self, not as an empty body controlled by an archived personality.

Memory is divided by function:

- **Continuity:** The soul may retain awareness that it has lived before and that prior lives belong to it.
- **Personal memory:** Important people, choices, emotions, and events may return clearly, dimly, or through triggers.
- **Practiced memory:** Skills and habits may survive as intuition even when their original lessons cannot be recalled.
- **Body memory:** Biological reflexes and species instincts come from the new body unless retained-instinct rules provide an additional influence.
- **Sealed memory:** Soul Strain, Soul Wounds, insufficient access, or an explicit effect may temporarily block recall.

Total recall is not the default. The exact starting access is governed by Soul Resonance, Soul Depth, Soul Echoes, retained instincts, and the condition of the new incarnation. Memory limits must create discovery and identity development rather than erase earned progression arbitrarily.

## Reset and Persistence Rules

### Resets with the Body

- ordinary bodily statistics;
- biological age and lifespan;
- body-bound mutations and species evolution;
- injuries, diseases, and conditions that are purely physical;
- body-dependent abilities not preserved as Soul Imprints;
- ordinary equipment, money, and carried resources.

Losing a body-dependent capability does not erase a legitimately acquired skill. The skill persists as a Soul Imprint but remains dormant or requires a compatible new expression while the body lacks the necessary anatomy.

### Remains in the World

- the former body and ordinary possessions;
- creations, ruins, writings, descendants, and institutions;
- political, ecological, economic, and historical consequences;
- memories and relationships held by surviving people;
- reputation attached to the dead identity;
- unfulfilled promises, debts, enemies, and opportunities.

### Persists with the Soul

Subject to each system's access rules:

- the continuity of the soul;
- legitimately acquired skills and Skill XP, stored as Soul Imprints even when current expression is unavailable;
- all legitimately earned Stat XP;
- Soul Depth and Soul Resonance;
- Soul Echoes and retained instincts;
- Soul Titles and Soul Constellations;
- Soul Weapons and Weapon Echoes;
- Soul Wounds, soul-level curses, and other explicitly persistent conditions;
- access already established with the Akashic Archive.

When classification is uncertain, ask whether the element is embodied, worldly, identity-bound, or explicitly engraved into the soul. Nothing persists merely because it would be convenient.

## Species and Progression Crossover

Reincarnation can place the same soul into human, monster, spirit, constructed, or stranger forms when valid candidates exist.

Human and monster progression trees remain distinct. The Reincarnation capability can eventually help a soul express retained experience across that boundary, but it does not convert every skill automatically.

Cross-tree access requires:

- a retained Soul Imprint;
- a compatible means of expression in the current form;
- relevant present-life experimentation or development;
- any Soul Depth, Soul Resonance, or unlock conditions defined by later rules.

When literal expression is impossible, experience may remain dormant or contribute to a new compatible technique. A spider does not gain human hands from sword mastery, and a human does not grow a venom gland merely by remembering a venomous life.

## Time Skips, Ages, and World Resets

Reincarnation may bridge any amount of time needed for a valid new life and a healthy long-term story. A long Interlife is a consequence, not a deletion of history.

- Time skips advance causal world conditions.
- World Resets may change geography, species, magic, technology, and civilization.
- A World Reset is not automatically triggered by one death.
- The soul carries only what its persistence rules protect through the transition.
- Returning to a familiar place or society requires that it still exists and can be reached.

The GM should summarize enough of the interval to explain the new Age and preserve the consequences of the former one, while leaving discoverable history for play.

## Interference and Failure States

Reincarnation is reliable continuity, not guaranteed comfort. Established forces may cause:

- a longer or less conscious Interlife;
- a narrower candidate pool;
- impaired memory access;
- Soul Strain or a Soul Wound;
- separation from a Soul Weapon's active manifestation;
- an unexpected but still valid location, species, or social context;
- pursuit by a being or system capable of tracing the soul.

Interference must have an established cause, fair warning appropriate to the threat, and an opportunity for resistance or later response. It cannot silently erase protected progression or create total soul annihilation under the default rules.

## Exploit Limits

- Suicide, repeated weak lives, and manufactured deaths do not improve Reincarnation by themselves.
- Reincarnation cannot be activated before Final Death as an escape from danger or consequence.
- A candidate cannot be rerolled repeatedly without a new valid transition and its costs.
- Directed selection cannot create extinct, unique, or forbidden forms without a valid embodiment route and earned access.
- A later incarnation does not inherit property, authority, relationships, or consent from a former identity.
- Long Interlife periods cannot be used for unlimited training or automatic Soul Wound recovery.
- Reincarnation does not duplicate the soul, preserve two active bodies, or allow revival and embodiment to complete together.

## GM Procedure

Before beginning the next incarnation, the GM should establish:

1. why Final Death is complete;
2. the immediate consequences left in the world;
3. the result of Life Reconciliation;
4. the Interlife duration and relevant world changes;
5. the active Reincarnation Mode;
6. the constraints used to generate valid candidates;
7. the selected body's species, stage, capabilities, and limitations;
8. starting access to memories, skills, instincts, bonds, and Soul Weapons;
9. any fair complications caused by established interference;
10. the first playable situation of the new life.

The GM may keep hidden requirements secret, but should preserve enough reasoning that the result remains consistent and can later be explained through play.

## Examples

### Monster Rebirth

A human scout reaches Final Death after protecting an evacuation. Their tools and social rank remain behind. After Life Reconciliation, perception and survival imprints persist. Constrained Choice offers several valid monster births shaped by the changed frontier. The player chooses a newly hatched cavern spider, beginning with spider anatomy and instincts rather than human statistics.

### Dormant Mastery

A former dragon mage reincarnates as a goblin whose body cannot initially channel the same volume of mana. The magical imprints persist, but their strongest expressions remain inaccessible. Present-life practice may recover efficient principles, unlock a goblin-compatible method, or eventually satisfy crossover requirements.

### Age Transition

A monster sovereign dies as World Stability is already failing. The resulting Interlife lasts centuries while a Reset changes the continents and dominant magic. The next incarnation enters the new Age with protected soul progression, but the former empire exists only through ruins, descendants, distorted legends, and surviving consequences.
