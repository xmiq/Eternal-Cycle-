# Age Transition Procedure

## Purpose

This document defines how a Game Master determines whether established historical change supports an Age Transition, coordinates the affected canonical owners, records disputed or uneven boundaries, and returns the result to play.

It operationalizes [Ages and World Resets](../world-engine/AGES_AND_WORLD_RESETS.md). It does not create an Age, generate a desired genre, cause a World Reset, choose historical outcomes, or replace the World Engine.

## Core Rule

Classify the history that occurred; never manufacture history to justify the classification.

An Age Transition is established only when lasting causal change makes a successor [Age Claim](../world-engine/AGES_AND_WORLD_RESETS.md#age-claim) more useful and accurate for a stated scope, period, and audience than the former claim. The procedure may instead find ordinary historical change, an emerging but unestablished transition, several overlapping transitions, a disputed boundary, or no transition at all.

## Ownership

This document owns:

- preparing a bounded Age-transition inquiry;
- comparing a former Age Claim and its Age Signature with the changed world;
- assembling a Transition Basis from established causes, changes, continuities, and evidence;
- selecting a scoped Boundary Finding;
- coordinating specialist handoffs and targeted World Revalidation;
- preserving competing claims, uneven adoption, refuges, minorities, and historical uncertainty;
- presenting a successor Age Claim without turning it into a script for the future;
- returning the classification to ordinary play or a Time Skip.

It does not own:

- the definitions of Age, Age Claim, Age Signature, Age Boundary, Age Transition, World Reset, Reset Footprint, Reset Survivorship, or World Revalidation;
- the causes or mechanics of any World Event, faction decision, war, migration, disease, advancement, Dungeon, Gate, magic, Soul effect, Evolution, or Reincarnation;
- the resolution of player or NPC choices;
- a universal calendar, era name, genre, duration, threshold, or changed-domain count;
- live campaign records in this repository.

## Key Terms

### Age-Transition Brief

An **Age-Transition Brief** is the external preparation record that states the transition question, scope, assessment interval, relevant audience, former Age Claim, candidate successor claim, established evidence, uncertainty, canonical owners, and intended use of the finding.

The Brief authorizes an inquiry. It does not presume that a transition occurred.

### Transition Basis

A **Transition Basis** is the evidence-backed comparison showing how established causes changed, preserved, displaced, or complicated the influential arrangements in a former Age Signature and whether a candidate successor Signature now describes the stated scope more accurately.

A list of dramatic events is not a Transition Basis by itself. The comparison must include continuity, distribution, causality, duration, exceptions, counterevidence, and uncertainty.

### Boundary Finding

A **Boundary Finding** is the GM's scoped conclusion about whether an Age Transition is established, emerging, absent, overlapping, or disputed and how its Age Boundary can presently be described.

It identifies evidence and limits. It is not a metaphysical command that forces every culture, region, observer, or later historian to use the same periodization.

## What This Procedure Is

The procedure is a historical-classification and continuity workflow. It helps the GM answer questions such as:

- Does the former Age Claim still explain the materially influential world order?
- Has a coherent successor arrangement become durable enough to describe?
- Which places, populations, institutions, ecologies, or metaphysical conditions changed?
- Which retained old arrangements remain influential?
- Is the apparent transition regional, network-wide, domain-wide, or broader?
- Is the boundary an event, a dated interval, a gradual divergence, or still uncertain?
- Does a World Reset separately qualify?
- What must be revalidated before play continues?
- What do different observers know, call, celebrate, deny, or misunderstand?

The procedure may be used during ordinary play, campaign preparation, a long Time Skip, Interlife, aftermath review, historical research, or retrospective reclassification.

## What This Procedure Is Not

It is not:

- an Age generator;
- a genre selector;
- a world-rebalancing tool;
- a chapter break that acts on the world;
- a timer that expires an Age;
- a catastrophe quota;
- a civilization-level ladder;
- a permission to erase inconvenient continuity;
- a universal history accepted by every culture;
- a World Reset trigger;
- an award for player importance, survival, death, or Reincarnation;
- a substitute for specialist resolution;
- a campaign record stored in this repository.

## When to Use It

Begin an Age-transition inquiry when at least one of the following is true:

- the former Age Signature repeatedly fails to explain current material conditions;
- several established World Events have reorganized durable dependencies or institutions;
- a foundational world condition changed and its effects have propagated far enough to alter ordinary life;
- a long Time Skip crosses a plausible Age Boundary;
- a World Reset has been established and a successor Age Claim must be evaluated;
- different regions or cultures now use competing periodizations that matter to play;
- a returning soul, Weapon Soul, institution, or archive confronts a world whose current order materially differs from the order it remembers;
- historical classification affects present law, identity, legitimacy, research, negotiation, or danger;
- players or world actors investigate whether a claimed new Age is real, premature, or propaganda.

The inquiry may begin before a transition is established. In that case, its result remains emerging, disputed, or absent until later evidence supports another finding.

## When Not to Use It

Do not open a full inquiry merely because:

- a monarch changed;
- a war began or ended;
- a city fell;
- a catastrophe was spectacular;
- a World Gate opened;
- a new spell, technology, species, faith, or institution appeared;
- many years elapsed;
- a protagonist died or reincarnated;
- an old calendar reached a symbolic date;
- a prophecy named a new era;
- the campaign would benefit from a different aesthetic;
- participants want a clean power reset.

Any of these may contribute to lasting change. None proves by itself that a successor Age Claim is more useful.

## Required Separations

### World Truth and Age Claims

World truth consists of actual conditions, actors, events, causes, continuities, and consequences. An Age Claim is an observer's scoped historical interpretation of those facts.

The GM establishes enough world truth for consistency while allowing scholars, rulers, religions, monsters, spirits, institutions, and communities to disagree about periodization.

### Events and Historical Classification

A [World Event](WORLD_EVENT_GENERATOR.md#world-event) establishes one bounded material occurrence or transition. An Age Transition classifies a longer causal reorganization when the resulting order supports a successor Age Claim.

One event may contribute to a transition. A transition may emerge from many events. Neither relation is automatic.

### Age Boundary and Dramatic Moment

An Age Boundary may be:

- one well-supported event;
- a disputed event among several candidates;
- a bounded interval during which the former claim ceased to fit;
- different intervals in different regions;
- retrospective because no contemporary observer recognized it;
- unresolved because evidence or historical development remains incomplete.

The procedure never invents one decisive day solely for convenience.

### Names, Genre, and Causes

An Age name is a social or historical claim. Genre is a campaign-facing description of resulting conditions. Neither causes those conditions.

The GM may describe a successor Age as magitech, cultivation-oriented, post-collapse, oceanic, spirit-governed, industrial, or another mode only after actual world arrangements support that description. A name may be biased, celebratory, fearful, ironic, propagandistic, obsolete, or unknown outside one audience.

### Classification and Future Outcome

A successor Age Signature describes an influential current arrangement. It does not guarantee that arrangement will endure, spread, win conflicts, remain coherent, or produce one predetermined history.

Actors keep choosing. Counterforces continue. The world remains capable of recovery, divergence, reversal, reform, collapse, contact, and further transition.

## Valid Boundary Findings

### No Age Transition

The world changed, perhaps severely, but the former Age Claim remains the most useful description for the stated scope and period.

Record meaningful events and legacies without inflating them into a transition.

### Transition Pressure Present

Material changes are undermining the former Age Signature, but no coherent successor Signature is yet established.

This finding creates Review Points, not inevitability. Recovery, adaptation, reversal, fragmentation, or a different successor order remain possible.

### Established Age Transition

The former Age Claim no longer describes enough influential conditions, and a successor claim has become more useful through lasting causal change.

The finding states scope, evidence, boundary, exceptions, uncertainty, and revalidation rather than declaring global uniformity.

### Scope-Specific Transition

A transition is established for one region, network, population, institution family, ecological zone, Contact Domain, or other bounded scope while the former Age Claim remains useful elsewhere.

Do not widen it merely because the affected scope is important to the campaign.

### Overlapping Transitions

Different but interacting successor orders become useful for different scopes, audiences, or periods.

The GM preserves overlap rather than forcing one universal Age label. A maritime trade network, inland monster confederation, and divine jurisdiction may cross different boundaries during the same centuries.

### Disputed Classification

World truth supports several plausible historical interpretations, evidence is incomplete, or audiences weight influential arrangements differently.

The GM records what is established and why disagreement remains. A disputed finding is not permission to conceal a settled fact from the GM's Factual View.

### Transition With a World Reset

A World Reset has separately satisfied its canonical Preconditions, Trigger where relevant, Mechanism, Footprint, Survivorship, and revalidation requirements. The Reset participates in an Age Transition, but the successor Age still requires a scoped claim and may remain uneven or contested.

The Reset label does not erase earlier causes or settle every historical interpretation.

## Building the Transition Basis

### Start From the Former Age Claim

Record the actual former Age Claim rather than a vague memory of the setting. Identify:

- its stated scope;
- its period and any existing boundary;
- the audience for whom it was useful;
- the influential arrangements in its Age Signature;
- exceptions, excluded perspectives, and competing claims;
- legacies inherited from still earlier history;
- evidence that those arrangements continued into the inquiry interval.

If no former Age Claim was ever established, the GM may frame one retrospectively from available evidence. Do not invent false consensus or precision.

### Trace Established Causes

Load only changes supported by existing campaign truth and canonical owners. Useful inputs may include:

- World Events and their Event Boundaries;
- Causal Event Chains;
- changed World-State Claims;
- population and migration changes;
- resource, food, economic, ecological, disease, war, and faction consequences;
- technical or magical advancement and decline;
- Dungeon formation, collapse, or exchange;
- Gate contact and Gate Legacies;
- changes to metaphysical law established through a valid Reset Mechanism;
- destroyed, transformed, dormant, extinct, inaccessible, or newly connected systems;
- autonomous responses, Counterforces, delays, and Pending Consequences.

Do not fill a missing causal route by saying “the Age changed.” The Age label is the conclusion, never the cause.

### Test Influence Rather Than Novelty

Newness alone is insufficient. Ask whether changed arrangements materially affect:

- ordinary survival and reproduction;
- access to resources, routes, information, institutions, or sources;
- political, religious, economic, ecological, or legal coordination;
- common capabilities and their support systems;
- conflict, cooperation, migration, and settlement;
- relations among humans, monsters, spirits, gods, Dungeons, and other actors;
- assumptions on which older institutions or techniques depended;
- what actors can reasonably expect, maintain, teach, inherit, or contest.

Several changed domains may support a transition. One foundational condition with widespread durable dependencies may also be enough. No universal count applies.

### Test Durability Without a Fixed Duration

Durability means the successor arrangement can persist through supported causes, maintenance, reproduction, transmission, adaptation, or stable consequences. It does not require permanence.

Evidence may include:

- institutions that reproduce the new arrangement;
- infrastructure with maintenance routes;
- ecological succession or changed migration patterns;
- generations educated under different assumptions;
- durable law, custom, or authority relations;
- lost capabilities or routes that cannot simply resume;
- new dependencies and Counterforces;
- repeated actor decisions that now treat the changed order as ordinary;
- broad consequences of a foundational world change.

A brief craze, temporary occupation, unsupported miracle, unmaintained invention, isolated mutation, or single charismatic leader may be historically important without establishing a successor Age.

### Preserve Continuity and Counterevidence

For every claimed change, ask what remained:

- old institutions that adapted;
- minority practices that continued;
- refuges outside the footprint;
- inaccessible or isolated regions;
- persistent roads, languages, debts, archives, ruins, borders, and memories;
- long-lived actors and Soul-linked witnesses;
- technical, magical, ecological, or social routes that survived;
- restored or recovered arrangements;
- evidence that the supposed successor order is still fragile or local.

Continuity does not automatically disprove a transition. It determines the claim's scope, exceptions, legacies, and explanatory accuracy.

### Compare Explanatory Usefulness

The final comparison is qualitative:

1. What materially influential conditions does the former Signature still explain?
2. What current conditions does it now misrepresent or omit?
3. Does a candidate successor Signature explain those conditions coherently?
4. What does the successor claim misrepresent or omit?
5. Which claim better serves the stated scope, period, and audience?
6. Would using one claim hide important overlap, exception, or uncertainty?

The test is not which name sounds more dramatic. It is which scoped interpretation best fits established world truth.

## The Procedure

### Stage 1: Frame the Inquiry

1. **State the question.** Ask whether a particular former Age Claim still fits a stated scope and period.
2. **State the use.** Identify why classification matters to preparation or play now.
3. **Set scope.** Name the regions, networks, populations, institutions, ecologies, Contact Domains, or world conditions under review.
4. **Set the assessment interval.** Use the period needed to inspect causes and durability without assuming a boundary date.
5. **Identify audiences.** Record whose Age Claims, names, calendars, or disputes are relevant.
6. **Open the Age-Transition Brief.** Separate established facts, inferences, claims, and unknowns.

If the question cannot be stated without prescribing the answer, rewrite it.

### Stage 2: Establish the Former Order

7. **Load the former Age Claim.** Record its Signature, scope, period, exceptions, names, and legacies.
8. **Test continuing support.** Identify arrangements that still function and the routes that sustain them.
9. **Identify former exclusions.** Record perspectives or regions that the old claim never described well.
10. **Preserve evidence.** Separate contemporary records, later accounts, institutional narratives, Soul testimony, archaeology, and hidden truth.

The former Age need not have been stable, peaceful, just, homogeneous, or universally recognized.

### Stage 3: Assemble Established Change

11. **Load causes and World Events.** Use only established inputs and direct Event Boundaries.
12. **Propagate consequences.** Follow Causal Event Chains through actual receivers, delays, decisions, feedback, and Counterforces.
13. **Update specialist claims.** Route each physical, social, ecological, magical, Soul, Skill, Evolution, class, weapon, Dungeon, Gate, or other change to its owner.
14. **Map distribution.** Record where, when, for whom, and through which routes each change operated.
15. **Preserve autonomous responses.** Let affected actors interpret, resist, exploit, repair, migrate, adapt, organize, or refuse through their own information and agency.
16. **Mark uncertainty.** Identify missing evidence, unresolved actor choices, delayed consequences, and genuinely hidden facts.

Do not proceed from an intended successor Age backward to convenient causes.

### Stage 4: Form the Candidate Successor Claim

17. **Identify influential changed arrangements.** Select only changes that materially shape the stated scope.
18. **Test durability.** Establish maintenance, reproduction, transmission, stable loss, or another route beyond momentary novelty.
19. **Draft a candidate Signature.** Describe the smallest coherent set of arrangements that explains the changed order.
20. **Test exceptions.** Identify refuges, minorities, old institutions, conflicting regions, failed adoption, and alternative orders.
21. **Test explanatory value.** Compare the former and candidate claims against the same evidence.
22. **Preserve alternatives.** Keep overlapping or competing claims when one description would distort world truth.

A candidate Signature is discarded or narrowed when it requires unsupported uniformity.

### Stage 5: Resolve the World Reset Checkpoint

23. **Ask the separate Reset question.** Determine whether established change includes a proposed World Reset.
24. **Load Reset ownership.** Apply canonical Reset Preconditions, Trigger where one exists, Mechanism, Footprint, and Survivorship.
25. **Reject label inflation.** A prophecy, ritual intention, catastrophe, Gate opening, divine claim, or world-scale spectacle is not enough.
26. **Preserve separate outcomes.** A transition may qualify without a Reset; a failed Reset attempt may still produce ordinary change; every valid Reset participates in a transition without dictating one uniform successor Signature.

The Age-transition procedure does not improvise a Reset Mechanism.

### Stage 6: Select the Boundary Finding

27. **Choose the narrowest supported finding.** Use no transition, transition pressure, established, scope-specific, overlapping, disputed, or transition with a Reset.
28. **Describe the boundary.** State the best-supported event, interval, regional divergence, or uncertainty.
29. **State scope and audience.** Identify where and for whom the finding is useful.
30. **State evidence and counterevidence.** Explain the decisive causal and continuity facts.
31. **State exceptions and legacies.** Preserve what did not change and what the former Age left behind.
32. **Set Review Points.** Identify evidence, propagation, recovery, institutional change, or later hindsight that could justify reassessment.

The result may remain provisional as historical interpretation while the underlying world facts remain established.

### Stage 7: Revalidate and Return to Play

33. **Identify changed assumptions.** List only continuing claims whose supports or requirements materially changed.
34. **Perform targeted World Revalidation.** Ask each canonical owner whether the claim continues, adapts, transforms, becomes dormant, fails, ends, or remains uncertain.
35. **Update external continuity records.** Preserve causes, states, actors, choices, losses, survivors, obligations, evidence, and Pending Consequences.
36. **Separate information views.** Establish world truth, player knowledge, character knowledge, public accounts, institutional claims, and mistaken beliefs independently.
37. **Present the changed world.** Show perceivable consequences and credible explanations without delivering an omniscient history lecture.
38. **Return agency.** Resume before the next deliberate player-character choice unless an applicable Standing Instruction already covers it.
39. **Keep the future open.** Treat the successor Signature as a present historical model, not a mandatory trajectory.

## World Revalidation

Age classification alone changes no specialist claim. Revalidation is needed only where the underlying transition altered an assumption on which a continuing claim depends.

Examples include:

- a magical technique whose source relation changed;
- an institution whose jurisdiction or communication network no longer exists;
- a profession whose materials and clients disappeared;
- a monster route whose ecology changed;
- a Soul Weapon expression whose compatible medium became unavailable;
- a Dungeon Function whose sustaining process failed;
- a trade obligation whose parties survived under changed law;
- a Reincarnation Candidate Pool whose reachable domains changed;
- an Archive access route whose institution or language was lost.

Do not re-prove facts untouched by the transition. Do not preserve an affected claim merely because it was once canonical in the campaign.

## Scope, Distribution, and Uneven Change

### Geographic Scope

One continent may cross a boundary while another does not. An island network may enter a contact-driven Age before inland regions receive reliable evidence or infrastructure.

### Population Scope

Different species, communities, classes, professions, religions, or ecological participants may experience the same period through different material arrangements.

Do not mistake unequal exposure for absence. Do not mistake elite adoption for universal change.

### Institutional Scope

An Age Claim centered on one empire, church, academy network, or trade league may remain useful inside its institutions while becoming misleading beyond them.

### Metaphysical Scope

A World Reset may alter one class of magical relation, spatial law, divine jurisdiction, or soul-world interface unevenly. “World-scale” does not mean every process changed in the same way.

### Temporal Scope

An Age Boundary may be sharp for a capital and gradual for its hinterland. Later historians may use one symbolic date while the GM preserves the longer causal interval.

## Disputed Periodization

Disagreement can arise because observers:

- occupy different scopes;
- value different influential arrangements;
- lack the same evidence;
- inherit different calendars or archives;
- benefit politically from one boundary;
- define continuity through different institutions;
- experienced the transition at different times;
- remember a former Age through survival, loss, conquest, liberation, or exile;
- deliberately suppress or mythologize history.

Resolve the dispute at two levels:

1. **Factual View:** What actually happened, remains uncertain, or is hidden?
2. **Observer Views:** What does each actor know, infer, claim, fear, or deny?

The GM may know that a transition is established while cultures dispute its name or boundary. The GM may also preserve a genuinely open classification when available evidence does not support one answer.

## Player Agency

An Age Transition may alter the choices available to player characters. It does not choose for them.

The procedure must pause when:

- a player character can materially influence an unresolved event;
- a Standing Instruction no longer covers changed conditions;
- survival, allegiance, sacrifice, migration, bodily alteration, covenant, or another identity-defining decision is due;
- a former plan becomes impossible and meaningful alternatives remain;
- a new incarnation requires player selection under its Reincarnation Mode;
- the character receives enough information to reconsider a prior commitment.

Player action may contribute decisively to history. Narrative centrality does not make the player the only cause, witness, solution, or valid interpreter of an Age.

## Time Skips

The [Time Skip Procedure](TIME_SKIP_PROCEDURE.md) owns authorization, Skip Mandates, Standing Instructions, Interruption Triggers, Return Horizons, and the Causal Bridge.

When a Time Skip reaches a possible Age Transition:

1. pause at the relevant Review Point;
2. preserve the start state and all established Simulation Deltas;
3. open an Age-Transition Brief;
4. classify the transition without inventing missing history;
5. perform required World Revalidation;
6. return to the Time Skip only if every active Skip Mandate remains valid;
7. include the Boundary Finding and decisive evidence in the Causal Bridge.

The classification may occur retrospectively after the Return Horizon if no earlier player choice depended on it. The underlying causes still must have been resolved when they occurred.

## Historical Witnesses and Persistent Souls

Long-lived beings can preserve rare evidence across an Age Boundary. They do not become omniscient historians.

### Reincarnated Souls

A reincarnated soul may retain Echoes, Titles, Resonance, instincts, Archive access, or other bounded continuity. It remembers only what valid access provides and interprets the present through a new body, current knowledge, and possibly obsolete assumptions.

Former office, citizenship, property, expertise, allegiance, or authority does not return automatically.

### Soul Echoes and Soul Avatars

An Echo is source-bound testimony from one completed life. A Soul Avatar may coordinate several authentic prior-life perspectives through valid Triggers and current expression.

Neither can:

- know events outside its sources;
- erase contradiction among lives;
- establish a historical claim by assertion;
- compel current institutions or descendants;
- bypass present embodiment or information;
- turn personal continuity into control of an Age.

They may provide evidence, recognize patterns, expose forgotten assumptions, or become politically important witnesses subject to corroboration and dispute.

### Weapon Souls and Weapon Echoes

A Weapon Soul may be a distinct long-lived witness with memories and Weapon Echoes across many incarnations. Its testimony remains shaped by custody, senses, access, relationships, damage, dormancy, and personal interpretation.

The Weapon Soul owns its speech and consent. The Bonded Soul cannot use it as an automatic historical database.

### Akashic Archive

Archive Records may preserve durable evidence of earlier arrangements and transitions. Access, interpretation, provenance, source type, redaction, and Archive scope remain governed by the [Akashic Archive](../soul/AKASHIC_ARCHIVE.md).

An Archive Trace does not classify an Age by itself or grant complete current-world knowledge.

## World Gates and Contact-Driven Change

A [World Gate](../world-engine/GATES_AND_WORLD_CONTACT.md) may introduce sustained contact, transit, information, materials, species, Mana relations, disease, institutions, conflict, or migration. Each effect requires a supported channel and causal route.

Gate contact contributes to an Age Transition only when its resulting changes become sufficiently influential and durable for the stated scope. Possible findings include:

- contact occurred but no Age Transition followed;
- a coastal or network-specific transition occurred while other regions remained in the former Age;
- several contacted domains developed different successor claims;
- Gate closure ended exchange but left durable Gate Legacies;
- Gate effects contributed to a Reset Precondition or Mechanism without the opening itself being a Reset;
- public recognition came long after hidden contact had already changed material conditions.

The reusable Gate-event template remains a separate roadmap task. This procedure does not implement it.

## Cross-System Interfaces

### Soul Engine

Soul rules own identity, continuity, Echoes, Titles, Resonance, Depth, Constellations, Avatars, instincts, Reincarnation, and Archive access. An Age Transition creates none of them and grants no Soul progression.

### Development and Skills

Persistent Potential may help a reincarnated person relearn under the successor Age, but Current Access, Embodied Expression, Practised Reliability, materials, teachers, and current-world validity remain necessary. Classification grants no Skill, Stat XP, or mastery.

### Monster Evolution

Changed ecology, pressure, contact, extinction, and world law may alter available forms or routes. Monster Evolution owns every species, stage, adaptation, mutation, hybridization, and transition claim.

### Human Classes and Professions

Classes, Professions, institutions, credentials, offices, schools, and social authority may persist, adapt, fragment, or disappear through actual world change. An Age name cannot grant or revoke capability by itself.

### Soul Weapons

Weapon Souls, bonds, forms, Echoes, compatibility, Evolution, and Manifestations remain with Soul Weapon owners. World Revalidation may find a former expression inaccessible under changed materials or laws while preserving personhood and relationship.

### Magic

Magic owners establish Mana relations, sources, Affinities, Spell Patterns, Rituals, Enchantments, Alchemy, divine relations, restrictions, and Development. A different magical Age does not rewrite these by genre label.

### World Engine

The World Engine owns all actual world state, causes, actor responses, transitions, propagation, persistence, and historical change. This procedure compares and presents those results.

### GM Toolkit

Use:

- [World-Event Generation](WORLD_EVENT_GENERATOR.md) for bounded direct occurrences;
- [Consequence Resolution](CONSEQUENCE_RESOLUTION.md) for immediate outcomes and causal handoffs;
- [Uncertainty Handling](UNCERTAINTY_HANDLING.md) for evidence and unresolved questions;
- [Time Skip Procedure](TIME_SKIP_PROCEDURE.md) for compression and player authorization;
- [Reincarnation Generation](REINCARNATION_GENERATION.md) when a new embodiment becomes available;
- Encounter, NPC, monster, Dungeon, and faction generation only for their established scopes.

No generator may create historical significance simply because a transition would be interesting.

### Alpha Playtest Rules

Where a narrow campaign-specific mechanic remains unresolved, use the [Alpha Playtest Rules](ALPHA_PLAYTEST_RULES.md). A blank record format does not make the Age classification provisional or authorize a missing mechanic.

## Uncertainty and Randomness

Age classification normally follows evidence and qualitative judgment rather than a random roll.

Random resolution may be used only when [Uncertainty Handling](UNCERTAINTY_HANDLING.md) permits it for one genuinely unresolved underlying fact, such as whether a distant record survived or which of several supported routes a storm followed. Resolve that fact first, update world truth, and then reassess the Transition Basis.

Never roll for:

- whether the campaign “deserves” a new Age;
- whether history is dramatic enough;
- which genre comes next;
- whether a player action becomes historically important;
- whether every region adopts the same order;
- whether a World Reset occurs without an established Mechanism;
- which interpretation best fits already settled evidence.

A weak Transition Basis cannot be repaired by randomness.

## Presenting an Age Transition

Present the result through what the characters can perceive or learn:

- unfamiliar routes, laws, currencies, species relations, institutions, tools, or magical assumptions;
- ruins, repaired structures, absences, archives, monuments, scars, and reused infrastructure;
- conflicting calendars, names, ceremonies, taboos, propaganda, and scholarship;
- people whose ordinary expectations differ from remembered ones;
- surviving communities that reject the dominant successor narrative;
- long-lived witnesses with limited and contested testimony;
- active consequences and choices rather than a compulsory lore lecture.

State out-of-character classification when needed for fair adjudication. Do not expose hidden causes, mistaken public beliefs, or unknown future branches merely to make the era easy to summarize.

## Worked Examples

### Gradual Magitech Adoption

Across two centuries, reliable Enchantments, source agreements, technical schools, repair professions, and transport networks reorganize several coastal societies. Inland communities use older routes, and one spirit-protected valley deliberately rejects the new standards.

The Transition Basis shows durable changes in work, trade, institutions, infrastructure, and expectations. The Boundary Finding establishes a coastal-network Age Transition across a long interval while recording inland and valley exceptions. “Magitech Age” is useful campaign shorthand, not a force that grants universal devices or magical access.

### Imperial Collapse Without a New Age

An empire loses its dynasty and fragments into rival crowns. Most roads, irrigation works, legal offices, trade obligations, academies, and military institutions continue under successor rulers.

The collapse is historically consequential, but the former Age Signature still explains the material order. The finding is no Age Transition. New wars, debts, claims, and political legacies remain fully real.

### Gate Contact Produces a Regional Transition

A stable World Gate supports one-way material transit and delayed two-way communication between an island chain and another Contact Domain. New crops, navigation methods, diseases, migrant communities, translation institutions, and religious disputes reshape the islands over generations.

The far continent receives only rumors and a few costly artifacts. The finding establishes an island-network transition, not a global one. The Gate did not cause a transition by opening; supported contact consequences did.

### World Reset With Uneven Survivorship

A valid Reset Mechanism changes spatial magic across several connected continents. Teleportation institutions fail, some protected routes persist, Dungeons reconfigure differently, trade collapses unevenly, and communities build new regional networks.

The Reset separately qualifies. The successor Age Claim emerges only after Survivorship and World Revalidation establish what continued. Isolated refuges and old routes remain exceptions rather than errors in the new Age description.

### Returning Soul With Obsolete Knowledge

A soul reincarnates centuries after serving an ancient city. Echo access recognizes the street pattern and one surviving temple, but the language shifted, the former enemy became a protected minority, property law changed, and the remembered river moved.

The Echo is useful evidence about the former Age. It does not grant current citizenship, authority, map knowledge, or perfect historical truth. The GM presents recognition and disorientation together.

### Weapon Soul as a Disputed Witness

An awakened spear survived in several vessels through a dynastic transition. It remembers negotiations and battles from its own custody but spent sixty years sealed underground.

Historians value its first-person testimony while challenging its gaps and loyalties. The Weapon Soul may consent to speak, refuse, or change its interpretation. It cannot settle the Age Boundary by identity alone.

### Competing Boundaries

Maritime scholars date a new Age from the first reliable oceanic Mana route. Mountain communities date it from the later collapse of pilgrimage passes. Monster societies in the northern forest reject both and use an ecological succession as their boundary.

All three claims describe real but differently scoped arrangements. The GM records overlapping Boundary Findings rather than selecting one universal calendar.

### Peaceful Ecological Restoration

Generations of negotiated grazing limits, river restoration, monster-human land councils, revived keystone species, and changed settlement design transform a degraded basin without war or catastrophe.

The new arrangement becomes durable and materially influential. A successor Age Claim is justified even though no dramatic founding event exists. The boundary remains an interval, and restoration can still fail in the future.

### Transition Pressure Reverses

A magical transport monopoly spreads rapidly and appears likely to replace roads, ports, and caravan institutions. Source withdrawal, maintenance failures, local resistance, and successful route diversification halt the change.

The earlier finding was transition pressure, not destiny. The final finding remains within the former Age, now carrying new ruins, laws, distrust, technical knowledge, and economic legacies.

### Two Ages Across One City

A capital's elite district uses Gate-linked logistics, standardized Enchantments, and distant source contracts. Its floodplain districts depend on inherited canals, local spirit agreements, and older craft networks that remain materially autonomous.

The city cannot be accurately assigned one homogeneous Signature. The GM may use overlapping scope-specific claims until later integration, displacement, or separation supports another finding.

## Human and AI GM Use

Human and AI GMs follow the same evidence, ownership, agency, and continuity rules.

An AI GM should:

- search [Ages and World Resets](../world-engine/AGES_AND_WORLD_RESETS.md) and relevant specialist owners before classifying;
- state the exact scope, period, audience, and former Age Claim;
- distinguish world truth, historical interpretation, public belief, and player knowledge;
- cite established causes and avoid backfilling a desired successor order;
- preserve “no transition,” emerging, overlapping, and disputed outcomes as valid;
- keep regional exceptions and minority continuities visible;
- route World Reset qualification separately;
- stop at Player Agency Checkpoints during compressed history;
- treat Soul, Echo, Avatar, Weapon Soul, institutional, and Archive testimony as source-bound;
- identify uncertainty rather than invent precise dates or universal consensus;
- store every populated Brief and finding outside the repository;
- ask for clarification when campaign history, scope, audience, or player authorization is materially ambiguous.

The ability to summarize centuries does not grant authority to invent them.

## Safeguards

- **No scripted transition:** Do not choose an Age and generate backward to justify it.
- **No transition quota:** Quiet continuity and failed transitions are valid history.
- **No universal timer:** Duration alone neither ends nor begins an Age.
- **No event inflation:** Catastrophe, war, contact, invention, death, or prophecy does not automatically establish a transition.
- **No genre causality:** A desired theme or aesthetic cannot change world truth.
- **No global flattening:** State scope, distribution, exceptions, overlaps, and uncertainty.
- **No elite substitution:** Institutional or elite adoption does not prove population-wide change.
- **No player centrality:** Player action may matter without becoming the sole cause, witness, or solution.
- **No agency theft:** Pause before unresolved deliberate player choices and preserve autonomous NPC and faction decisions.
- **No Reset shortcut:** Apply Preconditions, Trigger where relevant, Mechanism, Footprint, Survivorship, and World Revalidation.
- **No clean slate:** Preserve survivors, absences, ruins, records, ecology, relationships, obligations, damage, and other World Legacies.
- **No witness omniscience:** Persistent souls, Echoes, Avatars, Weapon Souls, gods, spirits, and archives retain only supported perspectives and access.
- **No authority inheritance:** Historical memory does not restore office, property, rank, citizenship, consent, capability, or institutional control.
- **No free progression:** Crossing a boundary grants no Development, Skill, Evolution, Soul growth, magic, class, or Soul Weapon benefit.
- **No successor destiny:** A current Signature does not predetermine future spread, victory, stability, or collapse.
- **No random history repair:** Randomness cannot replace missing causes or evidence.
- **No duplicate ownership:** Resolve each material effect once through its canonical owner.
- **No repository campaign state:** Keep Briefs, findings, timelines, Age names, current claims, witnesses, and transition records external.

## Scope Boundaries

This document does not define:

- one canonical setting history, current Age, successor Age, timeline, calendar, or genre;
- a universal transition threshold, duration, changed-domain count, score, or table;
- Age or World Reset mechanics owned by [Ages and World Resets](../world-engine/AGES_AND_WORLD_RESETS.md);
- World Stability supports, strain, forecasts, or interventions;
- World Events or consequences before classification;
- specialist system transitions or progression;
- a Time Skip's player authorization or Simulation Passes;
- a Gate-event template;
- encounter, monster, NPC, Dungeon, faction, settlement, species, Skill, character, Soul Weapon, or Soul Avatar templates;
- campaign Age names, live world truth, current actors, histories, or playthrough results.
