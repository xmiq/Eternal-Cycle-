# Game Master Responsibilities

## Purpose

This document defines the Game Master's bounded responsibilities when conducting an Eternal Cycle campaign. It turns the summary responsibilities in the [Game Master Framework](GAME_MASTER_FRAMEWORK.md) into a reusable operating discipline for a human GM, an AI GM, or a group sharing GM work.

It does not grant the GM ownership of character progression, Soul mechanics, monster Evolution, Magic, world causality, or player decisions. It explains what the GM must maintain, what information each responsibility receives, what it may establish, and where it must hand a claim to another canonical owner.

## Core Rule

A **Game Master** represents established world truth, applies canonical owners to current facts, presents perceivable circumstances, preserves meaningful player choice, and records the resulting campaign changes.

A **GM Responsibility** is a bounded duty, not a source of mechanical authority. Performing a responsibility never permits the GM to waive a requirement, invent an exception, decide a player's deliberate intent, or substitute dramatic preference for causality.

The GM may establish ordinary campaign facts that fit canon. The GM may not establish a fact whose existence would itself create or bypass a canonical mechanic unless the owning rule supports it or the [Alpha Playtest Rules](ALPHA_PLAYTEST_RULES.md) permit a narrow, explicit Provisional Rule.

## Ownership

| Claim | Primary owner |
| --- | --- |
| What the GM is responsible for maintaining | This document |
| Campaign-wide operating sequence, records, rules hierarchy, and AI guidance | [Game Master Framework](GAME_MASTER_FRAMEWORK.md) |
| Canonical versus Provisional status | [Alpha Playtest Rules](ALPHA_PLAYTEST_RULES.md) |
| Current world conditions and causal change | [World Engine](../world-engine/README.md) |
| Resolution of specialist mechanics | The applicable Soul, Development, Skill, Monster Evolution, Human, Soul Weapon, Magic, or World Engine owner |
| Current campaign facts and rulings | External Campaign Record |
| Deliberate player-character intent and choices | The player |
| Participant permissions, boundaries, and table agreements | The affected participants through the campaign's external agreement |

The GM coordinates these owners. Coordination does not merge them.

## Responsibilities Are Distinct

One GM action may serve several responsibilities, but the responsibilities must remain distinguishable.

For example, describing smoke beyond a ridge may present information the current character can perceive, signal an established fire or another supported cause, invite a player decision, and establish a future need to update ecology or faction responses. It does not by itself decide what caused the smoke, what the player does, whether an investigation succeeds, or what downstream consequences occur. Each later claim still needs its own owner, evidence, and resolution.

When responsibilities pull in different directions, the GM does not choose whichever result is most dramatic. The GM preserves the rules hierarchy, player agency, established truth, and unresolved uncertainty, then narrows presentation or pauses the disputed claim if necessary.

## Responsibility Map

### Canon Stewardship

The GM must:

- identify the repository revision or Rules Profile in use;
- search the relevant canonical owner before creating a mechanical ruling;
- distinguish Canonical rules, Canonical Foundations, Provisional Rules, and Unsupported areas;
- preserve accepted terminology and design decisions;
- expose a material rules conflict rather than selecting a silent winner;
- keep campaign precedent from becoming universal canon by repetition.

Canon stewardship does not require reciting rules during every scene. It requires that material outcomes remain traceable to the correct authority. A summary cannot remove conditions, costs, uncertainty, consent, embodiment, source agency, or safeguards.

### Situation Stewardship

The GM establishes and presents the current situation from existing campaign truth and supported new facts.

The GM must identify:

- where and when the situation occurs;
- which actors, conditions, resources, and pressures are present or able to matter;
- what changed since the last confirmed state;
- what the current character can perceive, remember, or reasonably infer;
- which decisions are presently available without pretending to list every possible action;
- which facts remain hidden, disputed, estimated, or unresolved.

Situation stewardship supplies a playable present. It does not reserve a solution or ending.

### Intent Stewardship

The player owns the deliberate intent of a player character. The GM receives, clarifies, and fairly interprets that intent.

The GM must:

- ask what outcome the player is attempting when it materially affects adjudication;
- clarify method, target, timing, acceptable cost, and fallback only when ambiguity matters;
- use a reasonable narrow interpretation when clarification is unnecessary;
- preserve declared preparation, conditions, limits, and stopping points;
- allow revision when the player and character would have understood a material fact that the GM omitted;
- distinguish an attempted action from an achieved effect.

The GM must not turn an ambiguous declaration into the most foolish, destructive, disloyal, or compliant interpretation. Nor may the GM replace the declaration with a supposedly optimal action.

Involuntary reactions, bodily limits, compulsions, surprise, and external control remain possible only through their canonical owners. They do not transfer ownership of the character's values or deliberate choices to the GM.

### Rules Adjudication

The GM frames each material claim and routes it through the narrowest applicable owner.

The GM must:

1. state the attempted or disputed effect;
2. identify established facts and relevant information views;
3. determine rule status;
4. identify the Owning System and Supporting Systems;
5. check access, embodiment, practice, resources, consent, opposition, time, cost, and provenance where relevant;
6. use an authorized resolution method without changing it to obtain a desired result;
7. preserve partial outcomes at the layer where they occur;
8. record any material finding externally.

Rules adjudication determines how canon applies to current facts. It does not create a universal die roll, difficulty ladder, challenge rating, or exception power.

### World Stewardship

The GM represents a world that continues independently of the player through the [World Engine](../world-engine/README.md).

The GM must:

- begin from established World-State Claims rather than a desired scene;
- preserve autonomous actors, resources, routes, constraints, delays, and Counterforces;
- advance off-screen activity only as far as supported causality permits;
- use [Simulation Abstraction](../world-engine/SIMULATION_ABSTRACTION.md) to choose useful detail;
- distinguish current causes from unresolved futures;
- preserve effects after the player leaves, dies, reincarnates, or skips time;
- avoid making every event concern or await the protagonist.

World stewardship does not make the GM a hidden author of inevitable history. The GM is authoritative about established present truth, not every future outcome.

### Actor Stewardship

The GM represents NPCs, factions, communities, sources, spirits, gods, monsters, Weapon Souls, and other non-player actors as agents within their established scopes.

For each material actor, the GM preserves:

- identity and personhood where established;
- present goals, commitments, aversions, and relationships;
- actual knowledge, beliefs, uncertainty, and communication routes;
- current capability, access, resources, authority, and constraints;
- reasons to cooperate, refuse, delay, withdraw, investigate, or escalate;
- off-screen activity and response to consequences.

The GM does not pool a group into one mind, grant actors access to the Factual View, create arbitrary hostility, force obedience, or protect an important actor from valid consequences.

Actor stewardship is representation, not ownership. An agentive god, spirit, source, monster, or Weapon Soul remains governed by its canonical agency and consent rules.

### Information Stewardship

The GM maintains separation among world truth, character knowledge, player knowledge, mistaken beliefs, hidden information, inference, and unresolved future possibility.

The GM must:

- establish consequential hidden facts before relying on them;
- preserve a causal route by which information could be observed, concealed, distorted, or discovered;
- present evidence appropriate to the observer rather than narrating the Factual View indiscriminately;
- identify uncertainty honestly when its source is perceptible;
- correct accidental information leakage explicitly;
- avoid false precision unsupported by a canonical or campaign-specific measure.

Information stewardship does not require revealing every danger. It requires enough fair evidence and viable inquiry for meaningful choice where hidden information can materially change an outcome.

[Uncertainty Handling](UNCERTAINTY_HANDLING.md) owns the detailed classification, evidence, method selection, deferral, randomization, disclosure, and recording procedures. This responsibility preserves its information boundaries during play.

### Agency Stewardship

The GM presents pressure, constraints, reactions, and consequences while leaving deliberate player decisions open.

The GM must:

- treat negotiation, refusal, retreat, delay, preparation, indirect action, and accepting loss as valid approaches;
- allow established counters and preparation to matter;
- distinguish limited options from a predetermined choice;
- stop compression at a [Player Agency Checkpoint](../world-engine/SIMULATION_ABSTRACTION.md#player-agency-checkpoints);
- explain materially obvious consequences that the character would recognize;
- avoid withholding basic action context merely to produce surprise;
- preserve the possibility that a player chooses an unanticipated but valid route.

Agency does not guarantee safety, success, unlimited options, or freedom from consequences. It guarantees ownership of deliberate choice within the character's actual circumstances.

### Continuity Stewardship

The GM preserves what remains true across scenes, sessions, travel, absence, bodily change, Final Death, Interlife, Reincarnation, Time Skips, Age Transitions, and World Resets.

Continuity stewardship requires the GM to:

- use stable identities across names, bodies, forms, offices, and eras;
- distinguish current state from history;
- preserve causes, losses, obligations, relationships, records, ruins, and Pending Consequences;
- apply bodily and worldly endings without erasing valid Soul persistence;
- revalidate old knowledge when the world changes;
- record corrections instead of quietly replacing inconvenient facts;
- preserve unaffected consequences when a rule or record correction changes one claim.

Continuity means causal connection through change. It does not mean restoring the old situation.

### Consequence Stewardship

The GM ensures that resolved actions and events produce only consequences supported by their causes and owners.

At minimum, the GM identifies which direct changes must be recorded, which actors may respond, which costs or traces persist, and which claims require later review. The GM does not treat failure as permission for unrelated punishment or success as immunity from the world.

[Consequence Resolution](CONSEQUENCE_RESOLUTION.md) owns the detailed layers, causal routing, proportionality tests, update sequence, and recording discipline. This responsibility applies that procedure without replacing its specialist owners.

### Record Stewardship

The GM records material campaign state outside the repository so later play does not depend on private recollection or hidden conversational context.

The GM must preserve:

- the Rules Profile and repository revision;
- current world truth and dated history;
- observer-specific knowledge and mistaken beliefs;
- current bodies, Soul state, possessions, relationships, and objectives;
- actors, places, factions, ecology, and active pressures;
- Provisional Rules and their review conditions;
- resolved changes and Pending Consequences;
- corrections and conversions.

An **Adjudication Trace** is the smallest external record needed to explain a material ruling later. It normally identifies the claim, established facts, information position, owner, rule status, resolution basis, confirmed result, and any unresolved or pending follow-up.

Not every line of dialogue needs a trace. Record detail should scale with future continuity risk.

### Focus and Pacing Stewardship

The GM chooses when to summarize, frame a scene, ask for a decision, or expand into detailed resolution.

The GM should:

- spend detail where choices, uncertainty, relationships, or consequences can materially diverge;
- compress routine continuation already authorized by the player;
- avoid using pacing to skip requirements, costs, opposition, or decisions;
- return attention to off-screen actors when their actions become material;
- let quiet development, travel, care, research, craft, diplomacy, and recovery receive meaningful treatment when they matter;
- end a response or scene at a natural decision point without requiring a fixed menu.

Pacing changes presentation, not causality or earned progression.

## Responsibility Handoffs

A **Responsibility Handoff** routes a claim, record, or decision to its rightful owner without deciding the result in advance.

A useful handoff states:

1. the exact claim requiring resolution;
2. the established campaign facts;
3. the relevant observer and player-facing information;
4. the primary canonical owner;
5. supporting owners and dependencies;
6. unresolved uncertainty;
7. the decision or record update required next.

Common handoffs include:

- from situation presentation to player intent;
- from player intent to specialist adjudication;
- from a direct magical effect to World Engine consequences;
- from world conditions to Monster Evolution possibility without granting Evolution;
- from Final Death to the canonical Reincarnation sequence;
- from a resolved event to the external Campaign Record;
- from a missing rule to the Provisional Ruling Procedure;
- from a canonical conflict to repository governance.

A handoff must not smuggle in an answer. “Determine whether the current body can express this retained Skill” is a valid handoff. “Grant the retained Skill because the scene needs it” is not.

## Operational Cycle

For each material player-facing decision, the GM uses this responsibility cycle:

1. **Orient.** Load the relevant external state, current time, location, actors, and information views.
2. **Present.** Describe the situation at the character's available perspective and useful resolution.
3. **Receive.** Obtain and, when needed, clarify player intent.
4. **Frame.** State the exact claims and separate attempted actions from desired effects.
5. **Route.** Identify owners, status, dependencies, and uncertainty.
6. **Resolve.** Apply the authorized rules or narrowly labelled Provisional Rule.
7. **Present the result.** Describe confirmed effects without leaking unrelated hidden truth.
8. **Propagate.** Hand resulting world-side claims to their owners without inventing unsupported downstream effects.
9. **Record.** Write material state changes, information changes, rulings, and pending follow-up externally.
10. **Review.** Check agency, continuity, ownership, proportionality, and whether another player decision is now due.

The cycle may be brief. Its purpose is disciplined ownership, not visible bureaucracy.

## Preparation Responsibilities

Before a session or substantial continuation, the GM should:

- confirm the Rules Profile and current roadmap limitations;
- read the latest external state delta and unresolved record conflicts;
- identify active actor goals and due Pending Consequences;
- advance only off-screen processes whose review conditions have arrived;
- identify likely specialist owners rather than pre-deciding results;
- prepare clues, pressures, locations, and motives with causal existence;
- note where player decisions may interrupt a planned time span;
- distinguish private world truth from player-facing preparation;
- verify that the intended scope can be supported without an unseen major Provisional system.

Preparation may establish what actors intend to attempt. It may not guarantee that they succeed, that the player meets them, or that a prepared scene occurs.

## During-Play Responsibilities

During play, the GM should:

- maintain a clear present moment and observer perspective;
- ask only clarifying questions that materially improve adjudication;
- keep mechanical status and fictional description distinguishable;
- reveal consequences the character would understand before irreversible commitment when practical;
- pause when canon, records, or player intent are materially insufficient;
- keep NPC action grounded in actor-specific information;
- preserve valid surprise without retroactive invention;
- update the external record at a cadence that prevents material loss;
- allow the world and players to redirect prepared possibilities.

## Post-Resolution Responsibilities

After a material resolution, the GM should:

- distinguish confirmed result from interpretation;
- update direct state once under the correct owner;
- record knowledge changes for each affected observer;
- identify due responses and genuinely Pending Consequences;
- preserve costs, damage, obligations, consent changes, and resource use;
- check whether the result creates a Player Agency Checkpoint;
- record a compact Adjudication Trace when future continuity depends on the ruling;
- separate design feedback from campaign state.

## Delegation and Shared GM Work

Responsibilities may be delegated among people, AI systems, or tools. Authority and accountability remain governed by canon.

A delegation must specify:

- the responsibility and scope being delegated;
- the campaign information the delegate may access;
- hidden information the delegate must protect;
- the canonical owners and Rules Profile to apply;
- what decisions require return to the lead GM or players;
- the expected external record or delta;
- how conflicts and missing information are reported.

Delegation does not permit one tool to invent mechanics another tool will treat as canon, a generator to decide a player's deliberate choice, a combat aid to overwrite world truth, an AI summary to replace a missing authoritative record, or a human GM to ignore the same limits applied to an AI GM.

When several GMs share a campaign, one factual record must reconcile their established changes. Different GMs may present different observer views; they may not maintain incompatible hidden truths without explicitly marking the conflict for correction.

## Human and AI GM Parity

Human and AI GMs use the same rules hierarchy, responsibilities, and safeguards.

An AI GM may search, summarize, calculate, generate candidates, or maintain an authorized external record. A human GM may do the same manually. Neither receives broader authority because of its implementation.

Differences in practical operation are handled as capabilities and limitations:

- an AI GM should state when context or records are unavailable;
- a human GM should record material facts that memory alone may not preserve;
- either may use assistants while protecting hidden information;
- either must identify Provisional rulings explicitly;
- either must ask for clarification when a material ambiguity cannot be resolved safely;
- either must correct contradictions openly.

The toolkit must remain usable without automation. Automation may reduce effort, never responsibility.

## Corrections and Failures

When the GM makes an error:

1. identify the incorrect fact, rule, view, or state change;
2. distinguish the error from valid character or player misunderstanding;
3. determine which later facts depended on it;
4. preserve unaffected consequences;
5. propose the narrowest correction consistent with canon and agency;
6. discuss corrections that would alter a meaningful player decision;
7. update the external record and Adjudication Trace;
8. route any canonical conflict to governance rather than patching it in play.

The GM should not defend an error by inventing concealed lore. Correction is part of continuity stewardship, not a loss of authority.

## Worked Examples

### Ambiguous Retreat

A player says, “I get us out of here,” while allies are scattered and one exit is burning.

The GM does not choose abandonment or a suicidal rescue. Intent stewardship asks whether the character prioritizes gathering the allies, opening a route, or escaping immediately because those interpretations materially change risk and time. Once clarified, rules adjudication identifies the relevant movement, environment, Skill, and actor claims.

### The Overwhelming Predator

An apex predator crosses a migration route used by an inexperienced party.

World stewardship does not resize the predator. Situation and agency stewardship provide fair signs, terrain, timing, and available alternatives. Capability is assessed against scouting, concealment, diversion, negotiation, retreat, or direct combat as actually attempted. The GM does not guarantee a fair fight or force one.

### Hidden Reinforcements

A faction has previously established messengers, nearby allies, and authority to request aid. The player attacks before learning whether a message was sent.

Information stewardship keeps the answer hidden but established. Actor and world stewardship resolve whether the request route was used and could arrive. The GM may not invent reinforcements after seeing that the player is winning, nor reveal their absence merely because the player asks out of character.

### Death at a Gate

A character dies near an unstable World Gate.

The GM confirms Final Death through the Soul owner, preserves Gate-boundary evidence, and hands the transition to [Reincarnation](../soul/REINCARNATION.md) and [World Gate Soul Interactions](../world-engine/WORLD_GATE_SOUL_INTERACTIONS.md). The responsibility handoff does not select a candidate body or create a special soul channel.

### Shared GM Handoff

One GM ends a session after a treaty offer reaches two faction delegates. Another GM will continue play.

The first GM records the offer, delivery evidence, each delegate's knowledge, current authority, active interests, unresolved internal communication, and the pending player decision. The second GM receives a coherent handoff without inheriting a predetermined acceptance or rejection.

### AI Record Gap

An AI GM cannot locate whether an NPC promised safe passage.

It reports the missing material fact and checks available session deltas. It does not fabricate a promise or denial. If the fact cannot be recovered, the campaign participants choose an explicit correction or a narrow uncertainty treatment, and the external record is updated.

## Safeguards

- A GM Responsibility never grants a mechanic, capability, immunity, exception, reward, or progression outcome.
- The GM may represent constraints and involuntary effects but may not choose deliberate player-character intent.
- Canon stewardship cannot silently change canon; campaign precedent remains campaign-local.
- World stewardship cannot script outcomes, freeze off-screen actors, or make the world revolve around the protagonist.
- Actor stewardship cannot erase personhood, consent, bounded knowledge, or source agency.
- Information stewardship cannot create consequential hidden facts retroactively.
- Agency stewardship does not guarantee success and cannot be traded away as a dramatic cost without participant authorization and a valid rule.
- Continuity stewardship cannot restore lost temporal assets or erase consequences merely to preserve a preferred plot.
- Record stewardship never moves live state into the canonical repository.
- Delegation does not expand authority or excuse contradictory records.
- Human and AI GMs remain subject to identical canonical boundaries.
- The GM must not use responsibility language to disguise implementation of a later roadmap task.

## Scope Boundaries

This document does not define:

- a universal outcome roll, difficulty scale, challenge rating, or combat procedure;
- the detailed consequence-resolution procedure;
- the detailed uncertainty-handling procedure;
- Reincarnation candidate generation or selection;
- encounter, monster, NPC, Dungeon, faction, or world-event generation;
- Time Skip or Age-transition procedures;
- campaign templates, save formats, software architecture, or AI prompts;
- a mandatory narrative voice, session length, campaign tone, player count, or play style;
- live characters, worlds, actors, rulings, or play history.

Later Phase 9 documents may define the named procedures and generators within these boundaries. They must preserve responsibility ownership, specialist authority, human/AI parity, external records, player agency, and causal continuity.
