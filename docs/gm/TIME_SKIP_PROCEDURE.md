# Time Skip Procedure

## Purpose

This document defines how a Game Master compresses narration across elapsed campaign time while preserving causes, player agency, autonomous actors, specialist ownership, uncertainty, and continuity.

It operationalizes the Time Skip rules in [Simulation Abstraction](../world-engine/SIMULATION_ABSTRACTION.md). It does not make time a cause, grant downtime rewards, generate events from nothing, reset the world, or permit the GM to choose a player character's life.

## Core Rule

Skip narration, never causality.

A Time Skip moves play from one established moment toward a later Return Horizon through as many causal Simulation Passes and player-agency pauses as the interval requires. Every material change still needs time, access, action, conditions, ownership, and a surviving record.

## Ownership

This document owns:

- establishing a bounded Skip Mandate;
- collecting player goals, Standing Instructions, delegations, limits, and Interruption Triggers;
- selecting a provisional Return Horizon;
- coordinating linked Simulation Frames through Review Points;
- detecting Player Agency Checkpoints and returning to play;
- assembling the external Causal Bridge from the start state to the return state;
- presenting the changed world without leaking hidden information.

It does not own:

- the Simulation Pass mechanics defined by Simulation Abstraction;
- World Events, consequences, World-State Claims, or specialist transitions;
- player-character intent beyond explicit instructions;
- training, recovery, Development, Skills, Evolution, Classes, Soul growth, Soul Weapons, or magic;
- death, Reincarnation, candidate selection, or Interlife mechanics;
- Age Transition or World Reset classification;
- live campaign records in this repository.

## Key Terms

### Time-Skip Brief

A **Time-Skip Brief** is the external preparation record stating the start moment, reason for compression, proposed Return Horizon, Skip Mandate, active Simulation Frames, Resolution Anchors, Material Exceptions, known Pending Consequences, information boundaries, and canonical owners.

It is preparation rather than proof that the full interval can be compressed.

### Skip Mandate

A **Skip Mandate** is the player-authorized scope within which the GM may summarize a player character's routine conduct during a Time Skip.

It records goals, priorities, Standing Instructions, delegation, accepted ordinary variation, limits, disclosure preferences, and Interruption Triggers. It grants no authority beyond those bounds and no guarantee of success.

### Standing Instruction

A **Standing Instruction** is a bounded player decision made in advance for a recurring situation or known range of conditions during compressed time.

It remains limited by the character's information, embodiment, access, capability, resources, relationships, consent, and any stated stop condition.

### Player Agency Checkpoint

A **Player Agency Checkpoint** is a Review Point at which a material deliberate player-character choice is due and no valid Standing Instruction, delegation, or accepted range settles it.

Compression must pause there and return the choice to the player.

### Interruption Trigger

An **Interruption Trigger** is a player-specified or canonically required condition that ends or pauses compression so the affected choice, danger, discovery, transition, or relationship change can return to ordinary play.

### Return Horizon

A **Return Horizon** is the intended latest time or condition at which compressed narration returns to ordinary play.

It is a target rather than permission to cross an earlier Player Agency Checkpoint, specialist transition, unresolved material branch, or required Review Point.

### Causal Bridge

A **Causal Bridge** is the external continuity account connecting the established start state to the established return state through decisive causes, choices, World Events, specialist transitions, changes, continuities, information, uncertainty, and surviving legacies.

It need not narrate every day. It must be sufficient to explain every material present claim.

## What a Time Skip Is

A Time Skip is a change in narrative presentation supported by one or more Simulation Passes.

It may compress:

- routine travel;
- recovery and maintenance;
- training, study, work, or research;
- seasonal or institutional cycles;
- construction and projects;
- quiet residence or relationship continuity;
- imprisonment, exile, dormancy, or isolation;
- Interlife and waiting for Reincarnation;
- off-screen regional change;
- generational or century-scale history.

The same rules apply at every duration. A night may require several Review Points during a crisis. A stable decade may require one broad pass.

## What a Time Skip Is Not

A Time Skip is not:

- a World Event;
- a Causal Link;
- proof that a process finished;
- a montage that grants capability;
- permission to age, heal, train, earn, build, discover, marry, rule, travel, or reconcile automatically;
- an inactivity field around the world;
- a clean reset of problems, resources, injuries, relationships, or records;
- an Age Transition or World Reset;
- a way to rebalance characters or setting by assertion;
- a substitute for a player decision;
- a fixed amount of progress per day, month, or year.

Time permits processes to operate. Their owners determine what they do.

## When to Use a Time Skip

Compression is appropriate when:

- the interval's likely activity is understood well enough to frame;
- ordinary detail would repeat without changing a material decision;
- players authorize how their characters approach the interval;
- the GM can identify Review Points and uncertainty;
- active processes can be advanced under existing owners;
- skipped detail can be restored from a sufficient causal record;
- the intended return condition is useful to play.

Compression may be proposed by the GM or a player. No player must accept compression through a period they want to play when their character has meaningful available choices.

## When Not to Use a Time Skip

Do not begin or continue a Time Skip when:

- a material player decision is already due;
- the character's intended conduct is unclear;
- the proposed interval hides stakes the character plainly perceives;
- a contested action needs Focused resolution;
- an identity-defining choice lacks authorization;
- a specialist transition must be adjudicated now;
- relevant campaign records are too incomplete to establish a start state;
- the desired end state is being used to invent its causes;
- participants disagree about the intended scope or return condition;
- compression would conceal that a live route has become impossible.

The GM may compress one Frame while leaving another in ordinary play.

## Skip Mandate Procedure

### Confirm the Player's Goal

Ask what the player wants the character to attempt, preserve, avoid, prioritize, or learn during the proposed interval.

"Train," "work," "wait," "travel," or "manage the kingdom" is a topic, not yet a complete instruction. Clarify enough to identify time allocation, methods, resources, limits, and interruption conditions where those details can change results.

### Separate Goal From Outcome

A player may authorize an attempt and describe priorities. The player does not dictate world response or guaranteed success unless the group is explicitly establishing a premise under campaign authority.

Likewise, the GM cannot reinterpret a goal as permission for unrelated sacrifices, loyalties, relationships, methods, or risks.

### Establish Standing Instructions

Useful Standing Instructions identify:

- triggering conditions the character can perceive;
- intended response or priority;
- acceptable methods;
- resource and risk limits;
- who may act or decide;
- when to seek new information;
- when the instruction expires;
- which conditions require a pause.

Examples:

- Continue sword practice while the teacher judges the form safe; pause if the injury worsens or a new permanent treatment is proposed.
- Maintain the trade route while escorts remain willing and losses stay within the stated reserve; pause before retaliation or a route change through sacred territory.
- Accept ordinary repair substitutions within budget; return to play before selling the workshop, binding a spirit, or taking political patronage.

### Record Delegation

Delegation identifies the actual delegate, scope, authority, information routes, resources, accountability, and conditions for referral.

Delegation permits another actor to choose. It does not let the GM treat the delegate as the player's remote body or guarantee that the delegate remains loyal, informed, capable, or available.

### Identify Nondelegable Choices

Unless explicitly and validly authorized, pause for choices involving:

- allegiance, betrayal, oath, covenant, or faith;
- sacrifice of a person, relationship, Soul, Weapon Soul, or irreplaceable value;
- marriage, parenthood, intimate consent, or permanent relationship definition;
- acceptance of office, rulership, servitude, or binding obligation;
- irreversible body alteration, Evolution, Soul change, or forbidden practice;
- Gate crossing, World Reset participation, or Reincarnation candidate acceptance;
- abandoning a central project, companion, dependent, or protected community;
- any other identity-defining choice material to that character.

A player may provide an explicit bounded instruction for some of these. Ambiguity never expands the Mandate.

## Interruption Triggers

Triggers may be:

- **player-defined:** a danger, offer, discovery, threshold, loss, visitor, message, relationship change, or choice the player wants to handle;
- **canon-required:** a transition whose owner demands direct adjudication or consent;
- **causal:** a Material Exception, Pending Consequence, Counterforce, failure, or branch that invalidates the current plan;
- **record-based:** a contradiction or missing fact that prevents trustworthy continuation;
- **return-based:** the Return Horizon itself.

Write triggers in observable or adjudicable terms where possible.

"Pause if something interesting happens" is too broad. "Pause if the expedition learns the pass is occupied, loses return supplies, or receives a credible request to cross the Gate" is usable.

The GM may pause for an unforeseen material choice even when it was not listed. The trigger list limits compression; it does not waive player agency elsewhere.

## Return Horizons

A Return Horizon may be:

- a date or duration;
- arrival at a place;
- completion or failure of a project stage;
- recovery to a stated functional condition;
- receipt of information;
- availability of a Reincarnation decision;
- a season, ceremony, hearing, election, migration, or institutional cycle;
- an event becoming decision-relevant;
- the earliest material deviation from the Standing Instructions.

Use the earliest supported return condition when several apply.

The intended endpoint may move because travel slows, a project stalls, a source refuses, a body changes, an actor dies, or another causal condition changes. The Causal Bridge explains the actual return point rather than forcing the calendar target.

## Preparation Procedure

### Step 1: Confirm Compression Is Wanted

State what ordinary play would omit and why compression is useful. Confirm the affected players agree to the proposed scope.

### Step 2: Freeze the Start State

Record the exact start moment and material current facts:

- embodiments, conditions, locations, access, resources, and obligations;
- relationships, factions, Institutions, dependents, and current activities;
- World-State Claims and local conditions;
- Active Causes, Pending Consequences, open branches, and uncertainty;
- player and character knowledge;
- current Soul, Skill, Development, Class, Profession, Weapon, Magic, and Evolution facts under their owners.

No later summary may silently revise this baseline.

### Step 3: Build Each Skip Mandate

Collect goals, Standing Instructions, delegated authority, acceptable routine variation, limits, and Interruption Triggers separately for each player character.

Characters in the same place do not automatically share one Mandate.

### Step 4: Set the Provisional Return Horizon

Choose the intended endpoint and the facts play will need there. Record conditions that can force an earlier return.

### Step 5: Establish Simulation Frames

Use [Simulation Abstraction](../world-engine/SIMULATION_ABSTRACTION.md) to define one or more Frames with independent scope, interval, resolution, owners, and Review Points.

Use mixed resolution when a personal project, war, relationship, Dungeon, Gate, or vulnerable minority needs more detail than the surrounding region.

### Step 6: Preserve Anchors and Exceptions

List every Resolution Anchor and Material Exception whose omission could alter:

- a player decision;
- a specialist transition;
- a duty or relationship;
- an irreversible loss;
- a vulnerable or exceptional group;
- a future branch;
- the credibility of the return state.

### Step 7: Map Active Processes and Actors

Identify:

- processes that can advance through elapsed time;
- necessary maintenance, supply, recovery, and feedback;
- autonomous actors and their current information;
- standing plans, procedures, and likely decision points;
- Counterforces and dependencies;
- cross-Frame exchanges.

### Step 8: Map Specialist Checkpoints

Identify possible points where Soul, Reincarnation, Development, Skill, Monster Evolution, Human systems, Soul Weapons, Magic, World Gates, or another owner must resolve a material transition.

Do not resolve those transitions in advance merely because they may occur.

### Step 9: Choose the First Review Point

Choose the earliest material time or condition among:

- player Interruption Triggers;
- due Pending Consequences;
- actor decisions;
- resource exhaustion or renewal;
- project milestones;
- bodily or recovery checkpoints;
- specialist transitions;
- threshold approaches;
- information arrival;
- relevant World Event candidates;
- the Return Horizon.

Begin there rather than jumping directly to the endpoint.

## Advancement Procedure

Repeat this procedure until compression pauses or reaches the Return Horizon.

### Step 10: Advance Only to the Review Point

Use the coarsest truthful resolution. Advance travel, work, maturation, maintenance, depletion, recovery, transmission, ecological change, construction, and other processes only as far as current conditions support.

### Step 11: Resolve Due World Events

Use [World-Event Generation](WORLD_EVENT_GENERATOR.md) for due or emerging material occurrences. An event may be prevented, delayed, redirected, misidentified, or remain unresolved.

The skip does not require something dramatic to happen.

### Step 12: Resolve Autonomous Actor Choices

NPCs, factions, Institutions, spirits, gods, monsters, Weapon Souls, sources, and other actors act from their own information, motives, relationships, procedures, capability, access, and constraints.

Do not optimize them with GM knowledge or freeze them because the player is absent.

### Step 13: Apply Standing Instructions

Apply a Standing Instruction only when:

- its trigger is present and perceivable;
- the instruction remains within scope;
- the character retains access, ability, resources, time, and authority;
- no listed limit or Interruption Trigger has occurred;
- no changed condition makes the interpretation materially ambiguous.

The instruction authorizes an attempt, not a result.

### Step 14: Resolve Specialist Work

Hand each material claim to its owner. Examples include:

- Development from current practice and feedback;
- Skill change from meaningful use and correction;
- Profession work, research, and production;
- bodily recovery, conditioning, injury, aging, and rust;
- magical practice, source response, and maintenance;
- Soul Weapon relationship, consent, use, damage, and change;
- Monster adaptation and Evolution eligibility;
- Reincarnation stages and candidate availability.

Summarize repeated valid practice only after the owner establishes its structure and relevant evidence.

### Step 15: Apply Consequences and State Changes

Use [Consequence Resolution](CONSEQUENCE_RESOLUTION.md), [Causal Event Chains](../world-engine/CAUSAL_EVENT_CHAINS.md), World-State Variables, and specialist domains.

Record each changed fact once with cause, distribution, evidence, uncertainty, and persistence.

### Step 16: Test for Player Agency Checkpoints

Pause immediately when a material deliberate player choice is due and no valid Mandate settles it.

Do not decide first and ask the player to approve afterward. Return to the last established moment before commitment, present available information and stakes, and resume ordinary play.

### Step 17: Record the Simulation Delta

For each pass, record externally:

- elapsed interval;
- decisive causes and actor choices;
- World Events and consequences;
- specialist results;
- changed and unchanged conditions;
- Resolution Anchors and Material Exceptions;
- information changes;
- uncertainty and unresolved branches;
- Pending Consequences and legacies;
- the next Review Point.

### Step 18: Reassess the Frames

Expand, compress, split, merge, suspend, or retain Frames according to current material needs. A personal crisis may become Focused while a stable region remains Regional.

### Step 19: Choose the Next Review Point

Use changed conditions rather than the original estimate. Do not extrapolate one trend across the remaining interval without rechecking its supply, maintenance, actors, opposition, and feedback.

### Step 20: Continue, Pause, or Return

- Continue if Mandates remain valid and no earlier pause is required.
- Pause if a Player Agency Checkpoint, specialist need, contradiction, or uncertainty requires ordinary play.
- Return when the Return Horizon is reached or the requested purpose is no longer served by compression.

## Return Procedure

### Step 21: Establish the Return State

Record the actual return moment and current facts relevant to renewed play.

The return state must be derivable from the start state plus recorded Simulation Deltas. It cannot contain a convenient unexplained capability, relationship, asset, disaster, institution, map change, or historical fact.

### Step 22: Audit Continuity

Confirm:

- every material change has a cause and owner;
- every continuity has a persistence route;
- player Mandates were not exceeded;
- agency checkpoints were preserved;
- specialist transitions were resolved under their owners;
- distributions and Material Exceptions survived compression;
- hidden truth, actor knowledge, player knowledge, and public history remain distinct;
- irreversible changes and absences were not averaged away;
- unresolved branches remain unresolved;
- no Age Transition or Reset was declared by elapsed time alone.

### Step 23: Build the Causal Bridge

Summarize:

- how much time elapsed;
- what the player characters attempted and how their plans changed;
- decisive World Events and actor choices;
- important continuities and why they persisted;
- material gains, losses, failures, transformations, and unfinished work;
- relationship and institutional changes;
- world changes and uneven effects;
- surviving uncertainties, Pending Consequences, and legacies;
- the immediate situation at return.

The bridge explains change. It is not a reward list or omniscient history lecture.

### Step 24: Present Information by View

Tell players:

- what their characters directly experienced during authorized routine;
- what reached them through established information routes;
- what they can observe at return;
- what remains uncertain, disputed, hidden, forgotten, or mistaken.

Do not reveal every off-screen cause merely because it was simulated.

### Step 25: Resume Ordinary Play

Present the first material situation or decision at the return point. Use an Encounter only if [Encounter Generation](ENCOUNTER_GENERATOR.md) finds a valid Encounter Source and player decision.

## Personal Projects and Training

A compressed project still requires:

- a clear objective;
- current embodiment and access;
- time allocation;
- a method or procedure;
- materials, teachers, subjects, tools, or sources where needed;
- repetition with feedback;
- recovery and ordinary needs;
- interruptions and competing duties;
- evidence for the owner's result;
- Plateaus, diminishing returns, risk, failure, and maintenance.

Do not convert duration directly into mastery. "Five years of training" establishes available elapsed time only after actual practice, quality, interruptions, body limits, and owner rules are known.

Retained progression may accelerate redevelopment through its existing rules. It does not replace rebuilding, practice, suitable embodiment, or current-life effort.

## Recovery, Aging, and Bodily Change

Time alone does not guarantee recovery. Advance:

- healing conditions and treatment;
- sleep, nutrition, safety, and care;
- infection, stress, pain, and reinjury;
- rehabilitation and changed function;
- aging, maturation, senescence, and species life cycle;
- rust, conditioning, adaptation, and maintenance;
- magical or environmental effects under their owners.

A body may recover, plateau, deteriorate, transform, or die through valid causes. Compression cannot preserve it unchanged for convenience or kill it merely to reach Reincarnation.

## Relationships and Social Life

Relationships continue through contact, absence, care, conflict, obligations, shared activity, information, and interpretation.

A Standing Instruction may authorize routine letters, visits, support, or negotiation. It cannot decide another person's response or silently establish intimacy, forgiveness, loyalty, betrayal, marriage, parenthood, leadership, reputation, office, or legal standing.

Material relationship choices create Player Agency Checkpoints. NPC choices remain autonomous.

## Resources, Work, and Projects

Income, production, construction, research, travel, maintenance, and administration require their actual routes.

Track what can change the result:

- inputs and supply;
- labor and Skill;
- access and authority;
- tools and infrastructure;
- demand and exchange;
- upkeep, waste, failure, and replacement;
- interruptions and competing claims;
- distribution, custody, and ownership;
- actor decisions and opposition.

Do not award a standard downtime income, completed invention, building, faction, or institution merely because time passed.

## Death, Interlife, and Reincarnation

### Death During a Skip

A possible death requires an actual causal route, appropriate resolution, fair information, and the relevant consequence and Soul procedures.

If a deliberate player choice could materially alter the risk and no Standing Instruction settles it, pause before the choice. Do not kill a character off-screen through an invented event or protect them because death would interrupt the plan.

### Interlife

During Interlife, the world continues through normal Simulation Passes. The continuing soul follows [Reincarnation](../soul/REINCARNATION.md) and other Soul owners.

The player's absence from a body does not pause:

- relationships and grief;
- succession and institutions;
- populations and ecosystems;
- debts, promises, ownership disputes, and political change;
- Weapon Soul agency and custody;
- World Events and legacies.

### Candidate Decisions

Compression pauses when the player receives a Reincarnation candidate choice, mode choice, meaningful Gate-related option, or other deliberate Soul decision not already validly instructed.

The GM cannot use the Return Horizon to preselect or skip past that choice.

### New Incarnation

After Reincarnation, a new Skip Mandate is normally required. The new body, circumstances, age, dependencies, information, access, and agency can invalidate prior Standing Instructions.

No old instruction transfers automatically across death.

## Multiple Player Characters

Each player character has an independent Skip Mandate and may require a different Frame or resolution.

Possible outcomes include:

- all characters reach one shared Return Horizon;
- one character pauses early while others continue under valid Mandates;
- ordinary play resumes for one Frame while another remains compressed;
- the group chooses a common earlier return point;
- synchronized events affect several Frames with different information and consequences.

Never average incompatible goals or let one player's Mandate authorize another character's choices.

## Long and Generational Skips

Centuries require unequal Review Points, succession, maintenance, replacement, reproduction, knowledge transmission, ecological processes, and specialist handoffs.

Preserve:

- exceptional and long-lived beings;
- minorities and refuges;
- institutional succession and schism;
- records, copying, language change, and forgetting;
- infrastructure and maintenance;
- Soul, Weapon Soul, Archive, Dungeon, Gate, and other protected continuity;
- extinction, absence, ruins, obligations, and World Legacies;
- unresolved branches whose requirements have not matured.

Long duration does not guarantee progress, decline, unification, diversification, technological advancement, magical saturation, Age Transition, or World Reset.

If the evolving state may meet the canonical criteria for an Age Transition, stop at the relevant Review Point and hand classification to the [Age Transition Procedure](AGE_TRANSITION_PROCEDURE.md). Do not prelabel the interval.

## Uncertainty and Randomness

Compression does not change [Uncertainty Handling](UNCERTAINTY_HANDLING.md).

For each material unresolved branch:

1. state the exact question;
2. apply established facts and owners;
3. remove impossible branches;
4. preserve actor choices;
5. declare any random method before resolution;
6. resolve only among supported possibilities;
7. record the result and scope;
8. continue from the changed state.

Do not roll once for an entire season, project, relationship, war, life, or civilization. Do not reroll because the Return Horizon would be more convenient under another result.

## Cross-System Interfaces

### Soul Engine

Time Skips preserve Soul identity, continuity, wounds, relationships, Echoes, Titles, Resonance, Constellations, Soul Space, Avatars, and Archive conditions through their owners. Duration and narrative importance grant no Soul change.

### Development and Skills

Compressed practice uses current embodiment, access, meaningful effort, feedback, reliability, Plateaus, rust, and diminishing returns. Retained progression accelerates only through existing bounded mechanisms.

### Monster Evolution

Environmental pressure and generations may change eligibility, but each Adaptation, Mutation, Hybrid, transition, and extinction claim remains with Monster Evolution. Time is not an Evolution currency.

### Human Classes and Professions

Work, training, institutions, offices, credentials, recognition, and Class Evolution require actual current-life participation and social routes. A skipped career is not a capability package.

### Soul Weapons

Weapon Souls retain personhood, consent, memory, custody, condition, compatibility, and independent action. Time, use, danger, separation, or fame guarantees no Awakening or Evolution.

### Magic

Mana, sources, costs, maintenance, Affinity, practice, Drift, failure, and source agency continue. Long operation does not imply unlimited supply, permanent consent, or universal access.

### World Engine

Simulation Abstraction advances Frames; Causal Event Chains connect change; specialist domains own their claims; World-State Variables record current conditions; World-Event Generation establishes bounded occurrences.

The Time Skip procedure selects pauses and presentation. It owns none of those transitions.

### GM Toolkit

Use Consequence Resolution, Uncertainty Handling, Reincarnation Generation, World-Event Generation, and other completed generators only within their scopes. Encounter Generation applies when play returns to a material player decision.

### Alpha Playtest Rules

Use a Provisional Rule only for one narrow missing mechanic supported by completed foundations. Use the [Age Transition Procedure](AGE_TRANSITION_PROCEDURE.md) for historical classification, and do not use Time Skip to implement Phase 10 templates by implication.

## Worked Examples

### Winter Sword Practice

A reincarnated master wants to spend winter rebuilding sword technique in an unsuitable adolescent body. The Skip Mandate specifies safe daily practice, instruction from a local teacher, continued household duties, and a pause if pain worsens or the teacher proposes permanent magical alteration.

Simulation Passes preserve food, rest, injury, teacher availability, body growth, corrective feedback, Retained progression, and Plateaus. The Causal Bridge may establish improved reliability and clearer access without restoring former-life mastery or awarding progress from three months alone.

### Two Centuries of Interlife

A soul remains in Interlife while the world advances. There is no current body to train, hold office, own property, or issue worldly instructions.

Regional and Epochal Frames advance populations, factions, institutions, ecology, technology, magic, Dungeons, Gates, and surviving relationships through uneven Review Points. The procedure pauses when a valid Reincarnation decision becomes available. The Causal Bridge explains the new world without treating it as a clean reset.

### Expedition Interrupted by a Closed Route

The player authorizes routine travel, trade within a stated reserve, and retreat from ordinary danger. The Mandate requires a pause before entering sacred territory or abandoning stranded companions.

A landslide closes the planned pass. Alternate routes include sacred territory and a dangerous return. The changed choice exceeds the Mandate, so compression stops at the blockage rather than deciding the expedition's morality off-screen.

### Quiet Decade of Workshop Work

A craftsperson operates a stable workshop for ten years. Demand, supply, health, apprentices, taxes, maintenance, relationships, and technical practice are advanced through a few broad passes because no crisis requires annual resolution.

The result may include reliable production, some Development, trained apprentices, worn tools, changed markets, and unfinished experiments. It does not grant legendary mastery, wealth, an Institution, or a new invention without evidence.

### Split Party, Mixed Resolution

One character studies at an academy, one escorts a seasonal migration, and one waits in Interlife. Each receives a separate Mandate and Frame.

The migration Frame pauses at a negotiation requiring player choice. The academy and Interlife Frames can remain prepared but do not advance beyond synchronized facts that depend on that unresolved choice. One timeline does not erase separate agency.

### Recovery Does Not Finish on Schedule

A player proposes a six-month skip to full recovery. Treatment, nutrition, rest, and rehabilitation support improvement, but infection and reinjury create a Plateau before the target date.

The Return Horizon was a goal, not a guaranteed body state. Play resumes early at the treatment decision specified by the Mandate. The GM neither declares full healing nor stretches the calendar until the desired outcome appears.

### Century Crosses a Possible Age Boundary

Several long-term changes make the old Age description increasingly misleading. The Time Skip records the actual events, transitions, survivals, and uncertainty through Review Points.

It does not declare the new Age. Compression pauses at the material classification point for the dedicated [Age Transition Procedure](AGE_TRANSITION_PROCEDURE.md).

### World Gate Signal During Routine Study

A student authorizes a year of ordinary study but asks to return for any credible World Gate contact. Six weeks in, an established signal reaches the academy through valid instruments.

The Interruption Trigger fires. The World Event and information route are recorded, and play resumes before the character chooses whether to join the investigation.

## Human and AI GM Use

Human and AI GMs use the same authorization, simulation, agency, ownership, and record rules.

An AI GM should:

- quote or summarize each Skip Mandate before advancing;
- separate player goals from guaranteed outcomes;
- search campaign records and canonical owners before every material transition;
- identify the earliest Review Point rather than jump to the requested endpoint;
- pause before any deliberate choice outside a Standing Instruction;
- keep player characters, NPCs, factions, Weapon Souls, sources, and other actors independently agentive;
- maintain separate Frames when scopes or resolutions differ;
- preserve hidden truth, Observer Views, and player knowledge separately;
- record Simulation Deltas and build an evidence-backed Causal Bridge;
- state when a desired endpoint was delayed, prevented, redirected, or made impossible;
- avoid filling skipped years with twists or rewards merely because it can generate them;
- ask for clarification when a Mandate, limit, return condition, or established fact is materially ambiguous.

Compression speed does not increase creative authority.

## Safeguards

- **No causality skip:** Every material return-state fact needs a route from the start state.
- **No endpoint scripting:** A Return Horizon is not a guaranteed condition.
- **No agency laundering:** Ambiguous goals do not authorize identity-defining decisions.
- **No retroactive consent:** The GM cannot resolve a choice and seek approval afterward.
- **No one-plan party:** Each player character retains an independent Mandate.
- **No frozen world:** Autonomous actors and processes continue off-screen.
- **No optimized world:** Compression grants no omniscience, coordination, or ideal strategy.
- **No time currency:** Duration alone grants no progress, recovery, wealth, Evolution, Soul growth, magic, or authority.
- **No montage mastery:** Training and work retain access, effort, feedback, limits, and Plateaus.
- **No automatic project completion:** Supply, maintenance, labor, failure, opposition, and changed conditions matter.
- **No automatic relationship outcome:** Contact and time do not dictate another person's agency.
- **No protected protagonist:** Valid danger and death remain possible through fair causes.
- **No off-screen execution:** A material unprovided player choice forces a pause.
- **No universal turn size:** Review Points follow causality rather than calendar increments.
- **No averaged exception:** Minorities, exceptional beings, bottlenecks, and irreversible losses remain explicit when material.
- **No random life roll:** One roll cannot resolve a life, project, relationship, war, or civilization.
- **No hidden-history license:** Expansion cannot invent decisive causes that would invalidate earlier choices.
- **No Age by duration:** Centuries do not automatically create an Age Transition or Reset.
- **No information leak:** The Causal Bridge is presented through valid views.
- **No campaign data in canon:** Briefs, Mandates, records, Deltas, bridges, and timelines remain external.
- **No automation supremacy:** The procedure coordinates judgment rather than replacing it.

## Scope Boundaries

This document does not define:

- a universal downtime action, training rate, income rate, recovery rate, project clock, or random table;
- campaign-specific Standing Instructions, histories, projects, events, or return states;
- Simulation Resolution mechanics;
- World Event, consequence, or specialist mechanics;
- player-character choices beyond explicit authorization;
- Age-transition or World Reset procedure;
- a campaign template or live record stored in the repository.
