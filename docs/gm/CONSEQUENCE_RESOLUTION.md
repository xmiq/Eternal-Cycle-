# Consequence Resolution

## Purpose

This document defines how a Game Master turns a resolved action or event into bounded, causal, persistent consequences. It coordinates immediate specialist outcomes with later actor and World Engine responses without replacing any system that owns the underlying change.

Consequence Resolution is not a punishment table, reward table, universal success ladder, dramatic-complication license, or substitute for uncertainty handling. It answers what follows from an established result, who or what receives that change, which owner resolves each later claim, and what must remain in the external Campaign Record.

## Core Rule

**Resolve the attempted effect under its owning system first. Then apply each supported consequence once, to the subjects and scopes it can actually reach, preserving delay, distribution, agency, uncertainty, and counterforces.**

A successful action may create costs, exposure, opposition, obligations, or harmful externalities. A failed action may still create information, displacement, partial effects, learning opportunities, or altered relationships. Neither success nor failure authorizes an unrelated benefit or punishment.

## Ownership

| Claim | Primary owner |
| --- | --- |
| Procedure for identifying, routing, recording, and reviewing consequences | This document |
| Immediate mechanical or fictional result of an attempted effect | The applicable Soul, Development, Skill, Monster Evolution, Human, Soul Weapon, Magic, World Engine, or Provisional owner |
| Causal propagation, branches, feedback, Counterforces, Pending Consequences, and Causal Horizon | [Causal Event Chains](../world-engine/CAUSAL_EVENT_CHAINS.md) |
| Current world conditions changed by a consequence | The relevant [World Engine](../world-engine/README.md) domain |
| What a character or actor knows about a consequence | Campaign information views under the [Game Master Framework](GAME_MASTER_FRAMEWORK.md) |
| How genuine uncertainty is represented or resolved | [Uncertainty Handling](UNCERTAINTY_HANDLING.md) |
| Material campaign outcomes and follow-up | External Campaign Record |
| Reusable canonical change | Repository governance, never ordinary campaign play |

Consequence Resolution coordinates these owners. It cannot use a downstream consequence to rewrite the immediate result or use an immediate result to pre-decide an autonomous response.

## Key Distinctions

### Attempt, Effect, and Outcome

An **attempt** is what an actor tried to do. An **effect claim** is the exact change sought or disputed. An **Immediate Outcome** is what the owning system establishes at the end of that attempt's current resolution.

The Immediate Outcome may confirm:

- the intended effect;
- a narrower or partial effect;
- an effect redirected to another valid target or form;
- an interruption before completion;
- no intended effect;
- an established failure effect owned by the same process.

These are descriptive possibilities, not a universal result table. The owning system determines which distinctions are meaningful.

### Outcome and Consequence

The Immediate Outcome closes the current effect claim. A consequence is a further state change, exposure, response, obligation, opportunity, or persistence claim caused by that outcome or by costs already incurred during the attempt.

Breaking a gate's lock is an Immediate Outcome. The noise heard by nearby guards, loss of access control, repair demand, and later institutional response are consequences. Whether a guard investigates remains an actor claim, not part of the broken lock by implication.

### Cost and Consequence

A **cost** is a requirement, expenditure, burden, exposure, sacrifice, or tradeoff attached to an attempt or effect by its owner. A consequence is what changes because the attempt, cost, or outcome occurred.

Mana consumed to shape a spell is a cost. Local Mana disturbance caused by that consumption may be a consequence. A promised favor offered during negotiation is a cost or obligation; the creditor's later decision to call it in is a possible response.

Costs do not buy guaranteed success unless a rule explicitly makes that relationship. Consequences do not retroactively become costs merely because they are painful.

### Stakes and Consequence

A **Stakes Declaration** is a player-facing summary of materially foreseeable kinds of gain, loss, exposure, or commitment before an irreversible decision. It preserves meaningful choice without promising an exhaustive outcome.

Stakes may include:

- obvious bodily or material danger;
- known resource use or opportunity cost;
- likely exposure, delay, or escalation;
- duties, consent changes, or relationships visibly at risk;
- uncertainty whose existence the character can recognize;
- consequences already made likely by established facts.

A Stakes Declaration does not reveal hidden truth, guarantee exact severity, list every remote effect, or turn an honestly unforeseen development into unfair play. The GM corrects the framing when the character would have understood a material risk that was accidentally omitted.

### Direct and Downstream Consequence

A **Direct Consequence** follows from an established outcome without requiring a new unresolved decision or intervening process.

Examples include:

- a severed rope no longer supporting its load;
- a spent reagent no longer being available;
- a witnessed declaration entering the witness's observation;
- a damaged bridge changing immediate passage conditions;
- Final Death ending the current embodiment.

A downstream consequence requires another route, process, actor, delay, or owner. A merchant hearing of the damaged bridge, choosing a detour, and changing prices are separate claims.

### Response Opportunity and Response

A **Response Opportunity** exists when an actor or process has enough access, information, time, capability, authority, motivation, or operating conditions to respond.

An opportunity does not guarantee a response, its timing, or its form. Actors may ignore, misunderstand, refuse, delay, compete, cooperate, or be prevented. Non-agent processes remain limited by their own conditions and Counterforces.

### Trace and Knowledge

A physical, magical, social, spiritual, legal, ecological, or informational trace may exist without being observed. Observation may occur without correct interpretation. Interpretation may spread without being true.

Consequence Resolution therefore records separately:

- what trace exists in world truth;
- who could encounter it;
- who actually observes it;
- what each observer can infer;
- what beliefs or reports result;
- which uncertainty remains.

## Consequence Layers

Review the following layers only where the established outcome can reach them.

### 1. Immediate State

Record the direct change under its owner:

- current body or condition;
- object, structure, route, or terrain;
- resource, Mana, tool, or access;
- relationship, agreement, authority, or consent;
- Skill, Development, Evolution, Class, Soul, or Soul Weapon state when its owner grants a change;
- location, timing, or active process.

Do not record the same change again as a later consequence.

### 2. Cost and Burden

Confirm costs actually incurred, not costs merely threatened. Preserve:

- consumed or committed resources;
- fatigue, injury, stress, maintenance, or recovery demands established by an owner;
- time, position, concealment, opportunity, or flexibility surrendered;
- promises, debts, duties, risks, and dependencies accepted;
- collateral burdens borne by other subjects.

Paid cost does not imply earned Development, Skill, Soul growth, Title, Evolution, or success.

### 3. Information and Trace

Identify signals created by the attempt and outcome:

- visible, audible, magical, spiritual, ecological, documentary, economic, or social evidence;
- concealment changed or identities exposed;
- knowledge gained, lost, distorted, or made obsolete;
- witnesses and communication routes;
- false attribution or competing interpretations with causal sources.

Do not promote a trace into universal knowledge.

### 4. Relationship and Authority

Determine whether the outcome directly changes an agreement, trust basis, consent state, office, claim, obligation, legitimacy, fear, grievance, or expectation.

Most attitudes are actor responses rather than automatic meters. A witnessed betrayal may alter a relationship because the participant's commitments and interpretation support it. A stranger elsewhere does not become hostile merely because the event was dramatic.

### 5. Spatial and Environmental Conditions

Route material, ecological, Mana, infrastructure, access, hazard, and habitat changes to their World Engine owners. Preserve scale, distribution, duration, renewal, repair, displacement, and affected populations.

A local fire is not automatically regional collapse. A contained magical release is not automatically harmless. Follow actual routes.

### 6. Actor Response

For each materially affected actor or group:

1. determine what they can know;
2. identify what matters to them;
3. check capability, access, authority, resources, and competing priorities;
4. identify available responses and Counterforces;
5. resolve only when the actor must or can act;
6. preserve disagreement and distributed agency.

The player character's deliberate response returns to the player.

### 7. Institutional and Systemic Response

When effects cross a broader scope, hand them to the relevant population, resource, economy, ecology, faction, conflict, disease, advancement, Dungeon, Stability, Age, Reset, or world-contact owner.

Institutional response requires people, procedure, communication, authority, capacity, and time. A law does not enforce itself; a market does not possess one mind; a religion does not interpret every event uniformly.

### 8. Delay, Persistence, and Legacy

Determine what:

- ends immediately;
- persists while a maintaining cause remains;
- decays, heals, repairs, adapts, or transforms through an owned process;
- awaits a stated delay or trigger as a Pending Consequence;
- survives as evidence, memory, absence, obligation, altered ecology, institution, ruin, tradition, or other legacy.

Elapsed time alone neither erases nor worsens a consequence. A causal process must do so.

## Resolution Procedure

Use this procedure after the current effect claim has been resolved.

### Step 1: State the Immediate Outcome

Write the narrowest true statement of what the owner established.

Include:

- attempted effect;
- confirmed result and layer;
- subjects and scope;
- timing and duration already established;
- costs already incurred;
- unresolved portions that remain unresolved.

Avoid labels such as “critical failure,” “victory,” or “catastrophe” unless their concrete meaning is also stated.

### Step 2: Freeze the Resolution Boundary

Separate facts settled by the immediate owner from claims that require another decision, process, observer, or system.

This prevents two errors:

- smuggling downstream punishment into the initial failure;
- treating an intended downstream benefit as part of immediate success.

### Step 3: Identify Direct Consequences

For each established subject, ask what changes without another unresolved link. Confirm the relevant owner and apply the change once.

If a change depends on a new actor choice, communication route, environmental process, compatibility test, or passage of time, it is not yet direct.

### Step 4: Map Affected Subjects and Distribution

Identify who or what bears each effect, cost, exposure, or opportunity. Consider:

- intended and unintended targets;
- bystanders and absent dependents;
- unequal access to protection or recovery;
- linked environments and infrastructure;
- agentive sources, spirits, Weapon Souls, monsters, institutions, and communities;
- subjects who benefit while others bear the burden.

Do not average away a Material Exception or move every consequence onto the protagonist.

### Step 5: Establish Traces and Information Routes

Record what evidence exists and which observers can encounter it. Keep world truth, observation, interpretation, report, belief, and player knowledge separate.

If a consequential hidden fact was not established before it mattered, do not invent it retroactively to intensify the result.

### Step 6: Create Consequence Handoffs

A **Consequence Handoff** routes an established change to the next owner without deciding that owner's result.

A handoff states:

1. originating outcome and cause;
2. affected subject and scope;
3. transmitting route;
4. receiving owner;
5. known constraints and Counterforces;
6. information available to relevant actors;
7. timing or trigger;
8. what remains unresolved.

“The temple observed the forbidden mark and can begin its own inquiry” is a valid handoff. “The temple declares war” is unsupported unless its decision process has also resolved.

### Step 7: Classify Follow-Up

At the current moment, a downstream claim may be:

- **established:** its owner has resolved it;
- **pending:** requirements are met but delay, an operating process, or a stated review condition remains;
- **possible:** a supported branch exists but requirements or resolution remain incomplete;
- **closed:** a necessary route failed or a Counterforce ended it;
- **unknown to an observer:** world truth may be settled while that observer lacks access;
- **genuinely unresolved:** even the Factual View does not yet contain a settled result.

These categories preserve state; they do not define the later uncertainty-resolution method.

### Step 8: Test Proportionality and Causal Fit

For every material consequence, test:

- **cause:** what established event or condition produces it;
- **route:** how it reaches this subject;
- **scope:** why it is local, distributed, regional, or broader;
- **magnitude:** which capability, quantity, intensity, or authority supports it;
- **duration:** what maintains or ends it;
- **distribution:** who bears or avoids it and why;
- **foreseeability:** what signs were available to the acting character;
- **counterplay:** what could prevent, reduce, redirect, repair, negotiate, or accept it;
- **ownership:** which system establishes each change;
- **independence:** whether it has already been counted elsewhere.

“More dramatic” is not a causal answer.

### Step 9: Stop at the Causal Horizon

Resolve only far enough to preserve:

- the next meaningful decision;
- a foreseeable material consequence;
- a future dependency;
- a due Response Opportunity;
- a Player Agency Checkpoint;
- a required specialist handoff.

Remote branches remain possible rather than becoming facts. Use Simulation Review Points for delayed or large-scope continuation.

### Step 10: Present and Record

Present what affected characters can perceive and reasonably infer. Record world truth separately.

The external Adjudication Trace should preserve, where material:

- Immediate Outcome;
- direct state changes and costs;
- affected subjects and distribution;
- traces and information changes;
- Consequence Handoffs;
- established and Pending Consequences;
- possible or closed branches worth retaining;
- Counterforces and repair routes;
- next review time or condition.

The trace is campaign state and never belongs in this repository.

## Outcome Integrity

### Apply Each Change Once

One cause may matter to several owners, but the same state change cannot be charged repeatedly under different labels.

Examples:

- Mana already consumed as a spell cost is not consumed again as a generic consequence;
- one injury is not separately multiplied as bodily failure, combat failure, and dramatic failure;
- lost equipment is not also removed from Soul persistence when it was never Soul-persistent;
- a faction's response does not automatically duplicate every member's response;
- one witness does not become universal reputation.

Distinct consequences from the same cause remain valid when they change different subjects or states.

### Partial Outcomes Stay at Their Layer

A failure in access does not imply failed intent, bodily collapse, public exposure, or moral corruption. A failure in precision may still produce a broad effect. A failure to persuade may still reveal information or clarify disagreement.

Name the failed or successful relationship. Then derive only consequences supported by what actually occurred.

### Prevention Is an Outcome

A valid defense, refusal, interruption, evacuation, cure, ward, counterargument, or negotiated concession may prevent a threatened consequence. Record the prevention's costs and traces without secretly restoring the prevented harm for drama.

Prevention may leave residual risk, displacement, debt, or adaptation where supported; it does not require a consolation penalty.

### Recovery Requires a Cause

Injury, damaged trust, depleted resources, disrupted ecology, institutional harm, and magical residue do not clear at scene end. Recovery proceeds through the relevant owners, access, effort, time, support, and constraints.

Recovery also need not mean restoration. A repaired bridge may use a new route; reconciliation may establish different terms; an ecosystem may stabilize in another state.

## Death, Reincarnation, and Temporal Boundaries

Final Death produces a strict ownership split.

The Soul owner determines:

- end of the current embodiment;
- Interlife transition;
- Soul-persistent reconciliation;
- later Reincarnation eligibility and sequence.

Consequence Resolution preserves world-side changes such as:

- bodily remains and physical evidence;
- lost temporal possessions and access;
- unfinished duties and relationships;
- witnesses, reports, grief, blame, celebration, or uncertainty;
- succession, ecological, factional, economic, and political responses;
- the continuing fate of locations, dependents, enemies, and allies.

Reincarnation does not cancel these consequences. A later incarnation encounters them only through valid time, place, identity, information, recognition, and access routes.

Time Skips, Age Transitions, and World Resets change simulation resolution or world conditions, not causal accountability. Preserve Resolution Anchors, Material Exceptions, legacies, and still-valid Pending Consequences through their respective owners.

## Cross-System Handoffs

### Soul Engine

Route Final Death, Soul persistence, Echo formation, Title relevance, Resonance, Depth, Constellations, Archive Traces, and Avatar claims to their owners. Meaningful consequences may become evidence for those systems; they do not automatically grant Soul change.

### Development and Skills

An outcome may provide feedback, practice, hardship, reflection, or a meaningful use context. Development and Skill owners decide whether learning or integration occurred. Success, failure, injury, repetition, killing, or survival alone grants nothing.

### Monster Evolution

Consequences may establish pressure, adaptation history, altered ecology, mutation exposure, or route conditions. Evolution still requires its own candidate, eligibility, transition, cost, agency, and continuity rules.

### Human Classes and Professions

Consequences may change recognition, access, office, certification, responsibility, institutional support, or professional evidence. They do not automatically grant a Class, Profession, rank, or mastery.

### Soul Weapons

Shared danger, care, conflict, trust, damage, Awakening pressure, and legacy may become bond or evolution evidence. The Soul Weapon owners retain personhood, consent, compatibility, Vessel, Manifestation, Skill, and evolution authority.

### Magic

Resolve spell, ritual, enchantment, alchemical, divine, forbidden, source, affinity, cost, residue, and failure claims within Magic. Then hand established material, Mana, ecological, social, institutional, or contact effects to the World Engine.

### World Engine

The World Engine receives established inputs. It does not magnify them merely because the originating action involved the player. Causal Event Chains govern propagation; domain owners govern the receiving state.

## Communication Principles

Before a material irreversible choice, communicate:

- what the character is trying to change;
- obvious requirements and constraints;
- materially foreseeable stakes;
- important uncertainty the character can recognize;
- whether the declaration commits, begins a process, or can still be revised.

After resolution, distinguish:

- what visibly happened;
- what the character can infer;
- what cost was confirmed;
- what remains unresolved;
- when another decision is required.

Do not narrate every causal branch. Do not conceal the basic nature of an obvious risk to manufacture shock.

## Corrections

When a consequence was applied without support, applied twice, assigned to the wrong subject, or based on leaked information:

1. identify the invalid state change;
2. identify later claims that depended on it;
3. preserve unaffected outcomes and consequences;
4. restore unresolved branches where appropriate;
5. return any materially altered player choice for discussion;
6. update the external record openly;
7. route a canonical conflict to governance.

Do not repair one unsupported consequence by inventing another.

## Worked Examples

### Interrupted Ritual

A ritual is interrupted after its boundary is established but before the intended transformation stabilizes.

The Ritual owner determines which roles, Pattern, links, costs, and failure layer were actually reached. Consequence Resolution records consumed materials, the still-existing boundary, exposed participants, residue, witnessed signals, and any direct damage. It hands Mana disturbance and environmental residue to Magic and the World Engine. It does not declare an explosion, curse, or Development gain merely because the ritual failed.

### Successful Negotiation, Uneven Effects

A scholar persuades a city council to open protected grain stores.

The social owner confirms the agreement, scope, authority, and obligations. Direct consequences include lawful access under the agreed terms and the council's recorded commitment. Distribution, transport, spoilage, excluded districts, merchant response, public interpretation, and future political pressure require economy, resource, faction, and actor handoffs. “Success” neither feeds everyone instantly nor requires betrayal to keep the story tense.

### Retreat from an Apex Predator

A party diverts an apex predator with prepared scent lures and escapes.

The immediate outcome is successful separation under current conditions. Costs include lures, time, and abandoned position. Traces include the party's route and altered scent field. The predator's later behavior depends on its senses, needs, territory, learning, and ecology. The GM does not resize the predator, force a pursuit, or award combat progression for avoiding combat.

### Soul Weapon Protects Its Partner

A Weapon Soul uses a valid Manifestation Route to interpose its Vessel during an attack.

The Soul Weapon and attack owners resolve protection, damage, capacity, consent, and expression. Consequence Resolution records Vessel damage, changed position, witnessed manifestation, any bond-relevant interaction, and repair demands. Public fear or reverence requires observers and interpretation. The event may become evidence for Resonance or evolution, but grants neither automatically.

### Final Death During a Siege

A character dies while holding a gate long enough for civilians to withdraw.

The current body ends and the Soul transition proceeds under Reincarnation. Consequence Resolution preserves the evacuation actually achieved, the gate's condition, witnesses, remaining attackers, lost equipment, unfinished promises, and actor responses. A Soul Title, Echo significance, institutional legend, or historical turning point requires its own owner and evidence. The next incarnation does not inherit the gate, office, or public authority.

### Hidden Sabotage Misattributed

A mill fails because an established saboteur damaged a concealed bearing. Villagers witnessed only the breakdown after a public argument with the miller.

World truth records sabotage and the physical trace. Observer records preserve the argument, breakdown, and differing interpretations. Consequence Resolution may establish loss of milling capacity and a supported rumor route. It cannot make the rumor true, give every villager the same belief, or reveal the saboteur to players without discovery.

### Prevented Dungeon Collapse

Explorers reinforce a failing support before a Dungeon chamber collapses.

The Dungeon and relevant Skill owners establish whether the support holds. If it does, the threatened collapse is prevented. Consequences may include consumed materials, blocked passage during work, altered load paths, noise, and a maintenance dependency. The GM does not collapse another chamber as a consolation complication unless a separate causal route supports it.

## Safeguards

- Resolve the immediate owner before deriving consequences.
- Apply each state change once.
- Match partial success and failure to the layer that resolved.
- Do not turn consequence into a generic penalty, reward, currency, or escalation quota.
- Success grants no immunity; failure grants no license for unrelated punishment.
- Suffering, killing, sacrifice, injury, repetition, death, and elapsed time do not automatically grant progression.
- A paid cost does not guarantee success or permit a larger unrelated effect.
- A Response Opportunity does not pre-decide an autonomous actor.
- Hidden consequences need prior causes, fair evidence where choice depends on them, and valid information routes.
- Consequences follow actual subjects, distribution, routes, delays, and Counterforces rather than narrative spotlight.
- Compression cannot erase a material consequence or resolve it by omission.
- Reincarnation, Time Skips, Age Transitions, and World Resets do not clean the world state.
- Current consequences, traces, outcomes, and Pending Consequences remain outside the repository.
- Human and AI GMs use the same procedure and authority boundaries.

## Scope Boundaries

This document does not define:

- a universal roll, difficulty, success tier, damage model, challenge rating, or encounter budget;
- the mechanics owned by any completed specialist system;
- a detailed method for representing or resolving genuine uncertainty;
- Reincarnation candidate generation;
- encounter, monster, NPC, Dungeon, faction, or world-event generation;
- Time Skip or Age-transition procedure;
- campaign templates or live records.
