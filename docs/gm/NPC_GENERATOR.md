# NPC Generator

## Purpose

This document defines how a Game Master creates and maintains non-player characters for Eternal Cycle campaigns without reducing persons to plot functions, stat blocks, species stereotypes, or automatic allies and enemies.

The generator produces external actor profiles from established bodies, histories, relationships, institutions, information, capability, and world placement. It does not create campaign data inside the canonical repository.

## Core Rule

Generate an NPC as a causally situated person with independent agency, bounded knowledge, real relationships, current activity, capabilities, constraints, and capacity to change.

An NPC exists in the world before becoming useful to a scene. Generation may establish the actor's present state and decision context; it cannot script future choices, guarantee a role in the player's story, or make the NPC know the GM's Factual View.

## Ownership

This document owns:

- selecting the necessary NPC detail scope;
- validating a Person Basis;
- building current-life identity and history;
- separating personal, social, institutional, faction, capability, Soul, and embodiment facts;
- defining actor-specific information, motives, constraints, and current activity;
- producing external NPC Profiles and Continuity Cores;
- a bounded NPC decision procedure;
- handoffs to Encounters, factions, consequences, and specialist systems.

It does not own:

- personhood rules or the creation of Souls;
- species, bodies, Monster Evolution, or Reincarnation;
- Development, Skills, Classes, Professions, magic, or Soul Weapons;
- faction decisions, institutional rules, law, economies, or world state;
- player-controlled character intent;
- Encounter selection or resolution;
- scripted story roles;
- campaign records in this repository.

## NPC Is a Campaign Role

An **NPC** is an established person or agentive being whose deliberate choices are represented by the GM rather than a player at the current moment.

NPC is not:

- a species;
- a lesser kind of person;
- a metaphysical category;
- a statement of importance;
- proof of a Soul;
- proof of human-like cognition;
- an enemy designation;
- a permanent control assignment.

Humans, monsters, spirits, gods, constructs, distributed minds, Weapon Souls, Soul Avatars, and other established persons may occupy the NPC role. Each keeps the rules of its actual nature.

Control may change by agreement. A former NPC may become player-controlled, and a temporarily unattended player character may be represented under explicit boundaries. Transfer of control does not rewrite identity, capability, memory, relationships, or prior choices.

## Key Terms

### NPC Generation Brief

An **NPC Generation Brief** is the external input for one generation pass, stating why an actor profile is needed, what Person Basis and world context exist, which information views matter, how much detail is justified, and which claims remain outside scope.

### Person Basis

A **Person Basis** is the validated embodiment, continuity, personhood, origin, identity, and world-placement foundation that permits one person or agentive being to exist.

The Basis may reference a human body, Monster Profile, spirit, construct, Weapon Soul, Soul Avatar, deity, distributed person, reincarnated embodiment, or another canonically supported form. NPC generation cannot declare personhood or occupancy merely to fill a role.

### NPC Seed

An **NPC Seed** is an unvalidated preparation possibility combining a Person Basis, current-life history, relationships, information, motives, capability, and placement.

It remains noncanonical preparation until ownership, causality, continuity, and placement tests pass.

### NPC Profile

An **NPC Profile** is an external Campaign Record for one NPC, separating identity, Person Basis, history, capability, relationships, information views, present objectives, constraints, activity, decision tendencies, and continuity.

It is not a complete personality model or a script for every future choice.

### NPC Continuity Core

An **NPC Continuity Core** is the minimum set of durable facts needed to preserve an NPC through changing scenes, relationships, injuries, roles, time, death, and possible Reincarnation.

It normally contains identity, Person Basis, current location or route, important relationships, commitments, values, established knowledge, major consequences, present objectives, and due Review Points.

### NPC Decision Frame

An **NPC Decision Frame** is the bounded current context used to represent one NPC choice from that NPC's Observer View, perceived options, values, relationships, obligations, capability, costs, uncertainty, and time pressure.

The Frame supports a decision. It does not convert personality into a deterministic algorithm.

## Generation Scopes

Use the least detail that can preserve causality and agency.

### Passing Contact

A person relevant to one narrow exchange may need only:

- Person Basis;
- current role and activity;
- immediate objective;
- Observer View;
- relevant capability or authority;
- one or two constraints;
- disposition arising from context;
- continuity note if consequences persist.

Do not create an elaborate biography solely because the NPC received a name.

### Recurring Local Actor

Add durable relationships, routine, history, resources, commitments, information channels, development, and possible off-screen action.

### Institutional or Faction Representative

Separate personal identity from office, membership, mandate, jurisdiction, instructions, discretion, accountability, and private beliefs.

The NPC cannot speak for an entire institution or faction beyond actual authority and information.

### Companion, Dependent, Rival, or Other Close Relationship

Add independent goals, boundaries, attachments, disagreements, needs, growth, and reasons to remain, leave, refuse, or renegotiate.

Relationship importance does not transfer control to the player or guarantee narrative permanence.

### Exceptional or Soul-Connected Person

Use specialist owners for Reincarnation, Echo relationships, Soul Constellations, Soul Titles, Soul Avatars, gods, spirits, or Soul Weapons. Add only the NPC-facing current access and perspective.

Exceptional history does not make the NPC omniscient, central to every event, or immune to ordinary consequence.

## Person Basis Requirements

### Personhood and Agency

Use established canon and evidence. Human appearance, speech, intelligence tests, tool use, sapience labels, Soul detection, species, legal status, obedience, silence, and unfamiliar communication are not interchangeable proofs.

If personhood is unresolved, preserve the question and apply precaution appropriate to possible agency and harm. NPC generation cannot settle it for convenience.

### Embodiment

Reference the actual current body or manifestation:

- species, form, Stage, age, structure, senses, movement, communication, and needs;
- health, injury, disease, suppression, adaptation, alteration, and current condition;
- access and expression limits;
- sustaining sources and dependencies;
- occupancy and continuity.

For monsters, use [Monster Generation](MONSTER_GENERATOR.md). For Reincarnation, use the established current embodiment after [Reincarnation Generation](REINCARNATION_GENERATION.md). For Weapon Souls, Soul Avatars, and other special persons, use their own owners.

### Origin and Current-Life History

Establish how the NPC came to exist and enough lived history to support present identity, capability, relationships, beliefs, and placement.

History should include actual time, access, care, education, labor, travel, social context, choices, and consequences. A mature NPC cannot receive decades of mastery, contacts, wealth, or authority without causal years and institutions.

### Identity

Identity may include:

- names, self-names, titles, pronouns, kinship, lineage, or collective identity;
- body, culture, language, faith, work, place, community, and chosen affiliation;
- memories, values, commitments, aspirations, grief, shame, pride, and uncertainty;
- ways the NPC understands or disputes labels others apply.

Do not reduce identity to species, occupation, Class, faction, Soul Title, trauma, relationship to the player, or one personality adjective.

### World Placement

The NPC needs a valid place, time, route, sustenance, shelter or support, relationships, and current activity.

They may be absent, traveling, busy, asleep, ill, detained, working, caring for someone, pursuing another objective, or changed before player contact. Placement is not a promise of availability.

## NPC Generation Procedure

### Step 1: State the Exact Need

Name the world-side or play-facing question requiring an NPC.

Good questions include:

- Who currently holds this office and what authority do they actually have?
- Which established resident witnessed the river change?
- Who maintains the ward during this shift?
- Which member of the monster delegation can communicate here?
- Who has a causal relationship to the missing courier?

"We need a quest giver," "the party needs a healer," and "this scene needs a villain" are not sufficient foundations.

### Step 2: Search Existing Campaign State

Before creating a person, check whether an established actor already occupies the relevant relationship, role, place, or information route.

Do not duplicate residents, relatives, officers, experts, witnesses, enemies, or allies because their prior profile is inconvenient. Reuse only when the existing actor's location, knowledge, capability, motives, and availability genuinely fit.

### Step 3: Select or Validate a Person Basis

Reference an existing Person Basis or create one through the applicable body, monster, spirit, construct, Soul, or other owner.

Confirm personhood, occupancy, continuity, embodiment, origin, and current placement without using NPC generation to bypass them.

### Step 4: Choose Detail Scope

Use passing, recurring, representative, close, or exceptional scope according to likely causal persistence.

Increase detail only when a relationship, decision, capability, secret, or continuity dependency becomes material.

### Step 5: Establish Current-Life Timeline

Place birth or formation, maturation, education, work, travel, relationships, major changes, and current activity on a plausible timeline.

The timeline may remain concise. It must still support age, Development, Skills, authority, wealth, knowledge, and social ties.

### Step 6: Establish Culture, Language, and Self-Identification

Use actual societies, communities, households, institutions, and embodied communication. Define names and identifiers through those routes or through the NPC's own choices.

Avoid one culture per species, one worldview per settlement, and names selected only to signal morality or importance.

### Step 7: Establish Relationships

Record the few relationships that materially shape the NPC:

- care and dependence;
- family or chosen kin;
- friendship, affection, rivalry, resentment, trust, grief, or obligation;
- teachers, students, colleagues, clients, patrons, debtors, and neighbors;
- institutional and faction ties;
- Soul-connected relationships where canon supports them.

Every relationship has two or more perspectives. One person's loyalty, love, fear, debt, or claim does not establish the other's consent or interpretation.

### Step 8: Establish Work, Roles, and Social Position

Separate:

- actual Skills and Development;
- Human Class or other learning framework;
- Profession;
- employment or service relationship;
- office and mandate;
- credential or rank;
- Institution membership;
- faction relationship;
- reputation and public belief;
- legal rights, duties, and restrictions.

No label proves the others.

### Step 9: Establish Capability Contextually

Record only capabilities likely to affect current decisions or continuity. Use the six capability layers and [Capability Assessment](../progression/CAPABILITY_ASSESSMENT.md).

Include access, embodiment, reliability, tools, support, preparation, limits, and uncertainty. Do not create a complete ability list or one universal rating.

### Step 10: Establish Resources and Dependencies

Identify what the NPC controls, may request, borrows, maintains, owes, consumes, protects, or cannot access.

Distinguish personal property, shared resources, office resources, faction assets, institutional infrastructure, Soul Weapon partnership, and temporary custody. Position does not make every connected resource personally available.

### Step 11: Build the NPC Observer View

Record what the NPC:

- directly observed;
- learned through trusted or untrusted sources;
- infers;
- believes incorrectly;
- doubts or disputes;
- has forgotten;
- cannot perceive;
- chooses to conceal;
- has not yet had a route to learn.

Expertise improves interpretation within its scope. It does not provide the Factual View.

### Step 12: Establish Values, Needs, and Commitments

Define several decision-relevant concerns rather than one alignment label.

Possible concerns include:

- survival, care, belonging, autonomy, duty, justice, faith, craft, status, curiosity, safety, revenge, repair, legacy, community, truth, wealth, or freedom;
- promises, contracts, taboos, vows, office, kinship, debt, and chosen principles;
- fears, needs, vulnerabilities, and unacceptable costs.

Values may conflict. A person can be generous and proud, loyal and exhausted, truthful and secretive about one harm, brave in work and fearful for family.

### Step 13: Establish Present Objectives

Record:

- what the NPC is trying to accomplish now;
- why it matters to them;
- what they currently plan to do;
- what they will not do under ordinary conditions;
- what could make them reconsider;
- which pressures or deadlines are due;
- what they are doing if the player never arrives.

Objectives are current state, not permanent scripts.

### Step 14: Establish Decision Tendencies

Use history, values, relationships, habits, capability, and risk tolerance to describe tendencies such as cautious verification, direct confrontation, consensus seeking, concealment, ritual observance, generosity, avoidance, or improvisation.

Tendencies shape perceived options and likely judgment. They do not predetermine every action or function as numerical personality traits.

### Step 15: Add Internal and External Constraints

Constraints may include:

- embodiment, health, fatigue, fear, grief, or incomplete Development;
- lack of information, time, tools, permission, supply, or support;
- relationships, dependents, obligations, law, custom, office, or accountability;
- faction orders and discretion;
- magical, Soul, environmental, or communication limits;
- opposition and foreseeable consequence.

Constraints make choices meaningful. They should not exist only to force the player's preferred interaction.

### Step 16: Separate Information Views

Maintain:

- Factual View;
- NPC Observer View;
- relevant institutional, faction, or community views;
- player-facing evidence;
- hidden established truth;
- mistaken beliefs;
- disputed claims;
- genuinely unresolved questions;
- Record Gaps.

Do not use an NPC as an exposition channel for facts they cannot know.

### Step 17: Establish the Continuity Core

Record the minimum facts that must survive compression, time, and changing focus:

- identity and Person Basis;
- current place or route;
- important relationships and commitments;
- established knowledge and major mistaken beliefs;
- current objectives and activity;
- material resources, injuries, obligations, and consequences;
- due Review Points and Pending Consequences.

### Step 18: Validate Placement and Autonomy

Confirm the NPC has a causal reason to be present and an independent activity if contact never occurs.

Remove any assumption that the NPC waits indefinitely, knows when the player needs them, changes location to preserve a prepared scene, or adopts hostility or trust without cause.

### Step 19: Create the External NPC Profile

Write the profile at the chosen detail scope. Mark inferred, hidden, disputed, and unresolved material explicitly.

### Step 20: Hand Off Without Scripting

If routes intersect a player decision, provide the NPC as an established [Encounter Source](ENCOUNTER_GENERATOR.md#encounter-source). The Encounter generator frames contact; the NPC Decision Frame represents choices as they become due.

Do not pre-write the complete conversation, betrayal, alliance, romance, death, redemption, or refusal.

## NPC Decision Procedure

Use this procedure whenever an NPC choice becomes material.

### 1. State the Decision

Name the exact choice and current time available. Do not ask what the NPC does "in general."

### 2. Load the NPC Observer View

Use only information the NPC possesses or can currently infer. Preserve errors, uncertainty, concealment, and communication delay.

### 3. Identify Perceived Options

List options the NPC can recognize through culture, experience, capability, and current conditions. The GM's preferred or objectively best option may not be visible.

### 4. Apply Values and Relationships

Identify which commitments, needs, attachments, fears, duties, and prior consequences materially pull among those options.

No single tag automatically wins. Resolve the particular conflict.

### 5. Apply Capability and Constraints

Remove impossible options and distinguish possible, accessible, sustainable, legal, socially permitted, costly, and uncertain actions.

### 6. Consider Expected Consequences

Use what the NPC expects, not what the GM knows. A mistaken forecast can support a poor but coherent decision.

### 7. Determine Whether the Choice Is Settled

If established commitments and conditions clearly settle the action, apply it. If several materially live options remain, use bounded qualitative judgment or [Uncertainty Handling](UNCERTAINTY_HANDLING.md) for the exact question.

Randomness may resolve hesitation among supported options. It cannot erase identity, consent, a binding decision already made, or evidence that settles the choice.

### 8. Commit the Choice

Record the choice before learning later outcomes. Do not revise it because the result becomes inconvenient.

### 9. Resolve the Attempt Through Its Owner

Choosing an action does not guarantee its desired effect. Apply capability, opposition, environment, timing, and specialist systems.

### 10. Update the NPC

Record observed results, changed beliefs, relationships, resources, injuries, obligations, confidence, objectives, and Pending Consequences. Experience may change tendencies through a causal bridge.

## Goals, Contradictions, and Change

NPC depth comes from connected causes, not a quota of quirks.

Useful internal tensions include:

- two sincere values that cannot both be protected;
- duty conflicting with care;
- public office conflicting with private belief;
- expertise paired with uncertainty outside its scope;
- loyalty surviving after trust is damaged;
- desire for change constrained by dependence;
- old identity conflicting with a changed body or Age;
- an accurate fear supporting a harmful strategy;
- a mistaken belief maintained by credible evidence.

Do not add contradiction solely to make an NPC unpredictable. Change should follow new information, consequence, reflection, Development, relationship, altered embodiment, social pressure, or time.

Consistency means continuity of causes, not repeating the same behavior forever.

## Relationships and Player Agency

An NPC may admire, fear, love, resent, trust, depend on, owe, recognize, or oppose a player-controlled character. These states:

- require their own histories and evidence;
- belong separately to each participant;
- may be mixed or asymmetric;
- create opportunities and pressures rather than control;
- can change through choices and consequences;
- do not guarantee access, forgiveness, loyalty, romance, rivalry, or betrayal.

Soul Constellations, Soul Resonance, Soul Titles, prior-life relations, authority, reputation, gifts, rescue, intimidation, and social capability do not make an NPC property or erase consent.

The player controls their character's deliberate intent. The GM controls the NPC under the same agency protections.

## Factions, Institutions, and Representation

An NPC's relationship to a group may be:

- member;
- employee;
- officeholder;
- representative;
- contractor;
- dependent;
- beneficiary;
- captive;
- dissenter;
- rival claimant;
- former member;
- external ally or opponent.

Record actual mandate, jurisdiction, information, discretion, resource access, reporting route, and accountability.

An NPC can:

- misunderstand orders;
- disagree while complying;
- exceed authority and face consequences;
- refuse an unlawful or unacceptable command;
- conceal information;
- lose office;
- represent only one internal coalition;
- act personally rather than institutionally.

The [Faction Behaviour](../world-engine/FACTION_BEHAVIOUR.md) owner determines faction-scale decisions. An influential NPC does not become the entire faction unless its valid decision route actually grants that authority.

## Off-Screen Activity and Continuity

NPCs continue within [Simulation Abstraction](../world-engine/SIMULATION_ABSTRACTION.md).

At each relevant Review Point:

1. load the NPC Continuity Core;
2. advance ordinary bodily needs, work, travel, communication, recovery, and obligations;
3. resolve information arriving through actual routes;
4. represent due NPC choices through the Decision Procedure;
5. apply capability and Counterforces;
6. record consequences and changed objectives;
7. stop at the Causal Horizon or next material choice.

Do not simulate every minute. Do not freeze the NPC because the player is elsewhere.

### Ageing, Injury, and Death

Bodies age, develop, recover, regress, become injured, and die through their owners. Importance grants no Plot Immunity.

Final Death ends the current body's activity and worldly claims. Preserve relationships, grief, property, office succession, consequences, and valid Soul continuity separately.

### Reincarnation

An NPC Soul may Reincarnate only through the canonical sequence. The new incarnation receives no automatic office, property, reputation, legal identity, faction membership, Profession, Class standing, relationships, or current capability.

Recognition by others remains an observer-specific claim and does not restore control over the former life.

## Information, Secrets, and Lies

A secret must identify:

- the established truth;
- who knows, suspects, or misunderstands it;
- how each learned or concealed it;
- motives and capability for concealment;
- traces and possible discovery routes;
- consequences of disclosure or continued secrecy.

Not every NPC needs a secret. Privacy, uncertainty, ignorance, shame, tactical withholding, institutional confidentiality, false belief, and deliberate deception remain distinct.

A lie is an NPC action with intent and information. The generator cannot label a mistaken statement a lie merely to make the NPC suspicious.

## Capability and Importance

Narrative importance is not capability. Capability is not authority. Authority is not legitimacy. Recognition is not truth.

A minor NPC may possess exceptional narrow expertise. A ruler may lack practical Skill. A renowned veteran may be injured or obsolete. A child may have decisive information. A monster courier may hold lawful mandate in one jurisdiction and no standing elsewhere.

Use [Capability Assessment](../progression/CAPABILITY_ASSESSMENT.md) for an exact objective. Do not scale NPCs to player progress or assign one overall rank.

## Selection and Randomization

Randomization may select among supported ordinary details after the Person Basis and world context are established.

Possible bounded fields include:

- a culturally supported name or name component;
- age within a plausible role range;
- ordinary appearance or inherited variation;
- which established neighborhood, household, teacher, shift, or work route applies;
- one of several supported current activities;
- a minor habit grounded in embodiment or culture;
- which witness noticed an event when several were genuinely present;
- hesitation among several materially live choices.

Randomization cannot decide:

- personhood or Soul status;
- morality, consent, loyalty, hostility, love, betrayal, or obedience;
- Development, Skills, magic, Soul history, Soul Weapons, office, wealth, or authority without history;
- membership in a faction that has no access route;
- a secret after seeing the player's plan;
- a universal power level;
- future choices beyond the committed question;
- Encounter occurrence or outcome.

State the supported options and weighting before resolving. Do not reroll until the NPC becomes more useful, favorable, dramatic, diverse, or surprising.

## Cross-System Interfaces

### Soul Engine

Soul systems may affect identity, memory access, relationship, symbolic authority, internal perspective, or exceptional expression only through their owners. NPC generation cannot assign Soul Depth, Resonance, Echoes, Titles, Constellations, Retained Instincts, Soul Space, or Soul Avatars as personality decoration.

### Reincarnation

Use [Reincarnation Generation](REINCARNATION_GENERATION.md) for candidate sourcing and placement. A mature reincarnated NPC needs an actual current-life history rather than a restored former biography.

### Development and Skills

Capabilities require access, embodiment, current-life experience, practice, reliability, and maintenance. A biography does not award mastery merely because it mentions training or danger.

### Monster Evolution

Use [Monster Generation](MONSTER_GENERATOR.md) for monster Species References, embodiment, ecology, and individual variation. NPC generation adds actor-specific identity and decision continuity without humanizing the monster by default.

### Human Classes and Professions

Classes, Professions, schools, traditions, institutions, offices, credentials, ranks, Social Position, and actual capability remain separate. NPC generation records their current relationships; it does not grant bundles or standing by label.

### Soul Weapons

Weapon Souls are distinct persons and partners where established. An NPC Profile may describe one in the NPC role, but weapon identity, awakening, compatibility, trust, refusal, evolution, and manifestations remain under Soul Weapon rules.

### Magic

Magical capability requires Mana relations, access, Development, Skills, sources, costs, and current conditions. An NPC called mage, priest, spirit, or god does not receive unrestricted effects.

### World Engine

Populations, resources, economies, factions, war, disease, advancement, Dungeons, Stability, Ages, Gates, law, and institutions supply changing conditions. NPCs respond through their Observer Views and actual access; profiles cannot overwrite world state.

### Encounter Generator

An NPC becomes an Encounter Source only through current location, activity, route, and decision relevance. The NPC generator cannot guarantee contact or script the result.

### Consequence and Uncertainty

Use [Consequence Resolution](CONSEQUENCE_RESOLUTION.md) for results and [Uncertainty Handling](UNCERTAINTY_HANDLING.md) for genuinely unsettled facts or choices. NPC importance grants no outcome protection or permission to alter random results.

## Worked Examples

### Capable but Unrecognized Midwife

A village midwife learned through apprenticeship and decades of practice but lacks the regional license introduced after her training. Her Professional Development and local trust are strong, her legal standing is weak, and she distrusts inspectors after a prior humiliation.

The profile separates capability, Profession, credential, reputation, law, and motive. She may cooperate, refuse, conceal a patient, or seek recognition according to the current Decision Frame. "Healer NPC" does not grant magical restoration.

### Monster Diplomat as NPC

A [Monster Profile](MONSTER_GENERATOR.md#monster-profile-template) establishes a metamorphic adult's body, culture, communication, and current capability. The NPC Profile adds personal history, a narrow water-sharing mandate, relationships inside the delegation, private doubts, Observer View, and current plan.

The diplomat is neither a species spokesperson nor automatically friendly. The faction retains collective decisions, and the monster retains nonhuman embodiment rather than being rewritten as a human in another shape.

### Reincarnated Founder Without Office

A soul once founded an academy and now lives in a young body centuries later. A Soul Echo and Retained Instinct support recognition of old practices, while current embodiment, incomplete memory access, and changed language constrain expression.

The NPC Profile records the current-life identity and relationships. The academy may doubt, exploit, welcome, or reject the claim through its own actors. Former office and property do not return automatically.

### Honest but Mistaken Witness

A ferry worker saw colored light near a Gate during heavy rain and believes a known smuggler caused it. The Factual View establishes a natural Gate fluctuation; the NPC's conclusion follows partial sight and prior suspicion.

The worker is not lying. Further evidence may correct or reinforce the belief. The GM does not use the NPC's confidence as proof.

### Officer in a Divided Faction

An officer receives an ambiguous order from one council bloc while owing personal loyalty to a community the order may harm. The profile records mandate, information, discretion, values, relationships, resources, and accountability.

The NPC Decision Frame resolves what the officer attempts. It does not treat faction intent as one command or guarantee rebellion for dramatic effect.

### Weapon Soul Refuses a Social Role

A Weapon Soul is publicly treated as a dynastic emblem but identifies as a retired partner and refuses ceremonial manifestation. Its NPC Profile records its own values, memory, relationships, current access, and Observer View.

The officeholder's claim of ownership does not erase Weapon Soul agency. Soul Weapon rules own manifestation and partnership; NPC generation owns only current representation and continuity.

### Passing Traveler

A traveler shares a shelter for one night. The GM records a valid Person Basis, destination, trade, immediate concern, knowledge of the road, and current willingness to exchange news.

No hidden royal lineage, special Soul, combat package, or future relevance is added. If no consequence persists, the profile remains minimal.

### NPC Becomes Player-Controlled

A participant takes control of an established recurring NPC by group agreement. The handoff transfers deliberate future intent to the player while preserving the NPC Continuity Core, body, information, capabilities, relationships, obligations, and prior choices.

The GM does not keep steering the character through old tendencies, and the player does not rewrite inconvenient history through the control change.

## Human and AI GM Use

Human and AI GMs use the same procedure.

An AI GM should:

- search campaign records before creating a duplicate person;
- retrieve Person Basis and specialist owners before assigning capabilities;
- maintain separate Factual, NPC, institutional, and player-facing views;
- use concise Continuity Cores to survive context changes;
- distinguish present objectives from fixed personality scripts;
- stop before player-controlled intent and consent;
- preserve NPC refusal, absence, competing priorities, and off-screen action;
- label inferences and Provisional Rules;
- update profiles after material information and consequences;
- ask for clarification when identity, prior promises, personhood, control, or continuity records conflict materially.

An AI's complete prompt context is not the NPC's knowledge. A human GM's familiarity with the plot is not the NPC's motive.

## Safeguards

- **No plot-role generation:** Begin from world need and Person Basis, not quest giver, villain, healer, romance, rival, or boss slots.
- **No NPC inferiority:** NPC status changes control, not personhood or capability.
- **No universal stat block:** Capability remains contextual and evidence-based.
- **No player scaling:** NPC age, training, resources, and placement follow causal history.
- **No instant biography rewards:** Backstory does not grant unsupported mastery, wealth, authority, contacts, or Soul progression.
- **No species personality:** Species, body, ecology, or progression tree does not dictate one identity or morality.
- **No class package:** Class, Profession, rank, credential, office, and actual capability remain distinct.
- **No faction mind:** Membership does not provide complete faction knowledge or automatic compliance.
- **No omniscience:** NPCs use their own Observer Views.
- **No exposition possession:** NPCs do not reveal facts they cannot know because players need them.
- **No arbitrary hostility:** Aggression requires motive, information, means, trigger, and current choice.
- **No automatic obedience:** Authority, magic, fear, reputation, titles, relationships, and rescue do not erase agency.
- **No relationship ownership:** Affection, debt, Soul connection, prior lives, and loyalty never make a person a reward.
- **No scripted betrayal or redemption:** Change requires choices, information, and consequences.
- **No plot immunity:** Importance does not prevent injury, loss, failure, death, or displacement.
- **No frozen actors:** NPCs continue at appropriate simulation resolution when off-screen.
- **No quantum availability:** Prepared NPCs do not appear wherever the player seeks them.
- **No retroactive secrets:** Hidden truth and lies require prior existence, motive, and traces.
- **No random morality or consent:** Randomization cannot decide identity-defining agency claims by category.
- **No compulsory complexity:** Passing contacts need no elaborate trauma, secret, twist, or future hook.
- **No campaign data in canon:** Populated briefs, profiles, relationships, histories, and decisions remain external.
- **No automation supremacy:** The Decision Procedure supports judgment rather than replacing personhood with an algorithm.

## Scope Boundaries

This document does not define:

- named campaign NPCs or histories;
- player-character generation;
- bodies, species, Monster Evolution, or Reincarnation mechanics;
- factions, institutions, Classes, Professions, Skills, magic, or Soul systems;
- Encounter, Dungeon, faction, or world-event generation;
- dialogue scripts, alignment systems, personality scores, or universal social mechanics;
- romance, loyalty, morality, or consent outcomes;
- campaign state in the repository.
