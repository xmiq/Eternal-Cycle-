# Uncertainty Handling

## Purpose

This document defines how a Game Master identifies, preserves, communicates, resolves, defers, and records uncertainty in Eternal Cycle. It provides one procedure usable by human and AI GMs without imposing a universal die roll, probability scale, or outcome table.

Uncertainty Handling does not replace capability assessment, specialist resolution, Causal Event Chains, player intent, investigation, or campaign records. It first asks what is uncertain, to whom, and why. Only then does it select an authorized way to continue.

## Core Rule

**Uncertainty belongs to an exact question and information view. Do not randomize what canon and established facts already settle, reveal what is merely hidden, or invent what a missing record cannot support.**

The GM may be authoritative about established world truth while remaining uncertain about a future actor decision, an operating process, a hidden observer's interpretation, or an outcome not yet resolved. Authority is disciplined state ownership, not omniscience.

This procedure selects how to settle a supported question. It cannot create missing access, capability, cause, authority, consent, source, or exception.

## Information Views

### Factual View

The **Factual View** is the GM's working representation of established world truth. It includes hidden facts and dated state records but not every future outcome, every unspecified trivial detail, or perfect knowledge of another agent's eventual choice.

The Factual View may itself contain:

- settled facts;
- supported estimates;
- explicitly unknown present conditions;
- genuinely unresolved processes;
- record conflicts;
- open details that have never mattered;
- Pending Consequences and possible branches.

Calling something part of the Factual View does not make an estimate exact or a future predetermined.

### Observer View

An **Observer View** contains what one actor, character, institution, group, or sensing process can access from its own position, senses, records, memories, communication, expertise, beliefs, and constraints.

Different observers may:

- witness different parts of one event;
- hold incompatible but sincere beliefs;
- possess evidence of unequal quality;
- use different conceptual languages;
- infer differently from the same trace;
- know a fact without being able to prove it;
- be unaware that uncertainty exists.

A collective has no automatic shared mind. Information moves through actual routes.

### Player-Facing View

The player-facing view contains what the GM has presented to a player, including any out-of-character rule or safety information. It may exceed or differ from the current character's knowledge.

The GM must not demand impossible forgetfulness from a player. The campaign may use open information, private information, or mixed presentation by agreement, but character action still distinguishes accessible character knowledge from player knowledge.

## Sources of Uncertainty

Every material **Uncertainty Question** records the exact unsettled claim, relevant view, source of uncertainty, stakes, owners, supported alternatives, and resolution condition.

### Hidden Established Truth

The world truth is settled, but a specified observer lacks access.

Examples:

- a messenger was intercepted, but the recipient does not know;
- a monster has an established concealed adaptation;
- a Weapon Soul privately refused a proposed act;
- a faction cell already chose a route unknown to outsiders.

This is uncertainty for the observer, not for the Factual View. Resolve discovery, concealment, inference, or mistaken belief. Do not randomize the underlying fact again.

### Incomplete Observation

An observer receives partial, noisy, delayed, damaged, translated, or context-poor evidence.

The uncertainty concerns what the evidence supports. Better senses, Skill, tools, access, corroboration, or time may improve the view. Expertise cannot recover information that left no accessible trace.

### Uncertain Interpretation

Evidence exists, but its meaning, source, classification, or consequence is not settled for an observer.

Interpretation depends on relevant knowledge, models, assumptions, bias, vocabulary, and alternatives. A confident interpretation remains a belief until support establishes more.

### Mistaken Belief or Disputed Claim

One or more actors already hold claims inconsistent with world truth or with each other.

Their beliefs may still cause real decisions and consequences. Adjudication preserves provenance, conviction, incentives, and available evidence rather than selecting the loudest or most narratively useful claimant.

### Genuinely Unresolved Outcome

The present facts support more than one future result, and no result has yet occurred or been selected by an authorized method.

Examples include:

- whether an autonomous actor accepts a new offer;
- whether a strained structure fails before repair reaches it;
- which supported route migrating animals take;
- whether a contested technique succeeds under closely balanced conditions.

The GM must not secretly write one branch as historical fact before the relevant action, process, or method resolves it.

### Model or Forecast Uncertainty

The underlying present may be partly known, but prediction depends on incomplete data, simplifying assumptions, changing actors, feedback, or sensitivity to conditions.

Forecasts state scope, date, basis, assumptions, confidence limits, alternatives, and what evidence would revise them. An exact percentage is used only when a valid local method genuinely supports it.

### Record Gap

A **Record Gap** exists when material campaign state is absent, contradictory, corrupted, or too vague to recover a fact safely.

A Record Gap is not an in-world mystery by default and not permission to choose the most dramatic answer. First search records and dependencies. If the fact remains unrecoverable, use explicit correction, participant clarification, a narrow agreed reconstruction, or a bounded prospective treatment that does not invalidate prior choices.

### Open Detail

An **Open Detail** is a previously unspecified fact whose earlier value was immaterial to established decisions, capabilities, consequences, and continuity.

The GM may establish an ordinary Open Detail when needed, constrained by canon and known context. It cannot become a retroactive trap, rescue, cause, immunity, authority, capability, treasure, relationship, or secret plan that would have changed an earlier meaningful decision.

### Rules Uncertainty

The group may be unsure what a rule says, which owner applies, or whether canon covers a claim.

This is resolved by repository search, authority classification, ownership analysis, and, where allowed, the Provisional Ruling Procedure. Randomness cannot decide what canon means.

### Intent Ambiguity

The GM may be uncertain what the player means by a declaration.

Clarify when interpretations materially differ in target, method, commitment, risk, consent, cost, timing, or consequence. Do not roll to discover the player's intent or exploit ambiguity against them.

## Evidence States

Evidence may be described qualitatively as:

- **demonstrated:** directly established within the relevant scope;
- **corroborated:** supported by independent compatible routes;
- **inferred:** supported through a stated model or causal interpretation;
- **claimed:** asserted by a source whose basis may be unknown or contested;
- **outdated:** once supported but not safely current;
- **disputed:** materially incompatible evidence or interpretation remains;
- **unknown:** usable support is absent for the stated observer and question.

These are not probability ranks. One direct observation may be misleading; several reports may share one source; an old record may remain accurate. Preserve provenance, scope, date, and limitations.

## Resolution Methods

Choose the narrowest method that preserves causality, ownership, agency, and useful uncertainty.

### Deterministic Application

Use deterministic application when established facts and canonical rules settle the question.

Examples:

- an ordinary temporal item is lost on Final Death;
- an actor cannot use a route that is physically absent;
- a spell requiring a source cannot form without one;
- a character cannot deliberately answer before the player chooses.

Do not roll merely to create suspense or give an unsupported attempt a chance.

### Clarification

Ask the relevant participant when the uncertainty concerns:

- player intent;
- an external table agreement;
- a missing preference only that participant owns;
- permission for secrecy, correction, or content boundaries;
- campaign setup not inferable from canon.

Clarification is not asking the player to choose hidden world truth for convenience unless the campaign explicitly delegates that authorship.

### Investigation and Evidence

Use observation, inquiry, experiment, research, communication, or testing when the uncertainty belongs to an Observer View.

Resolve:

1. what access and tools the observer has;
2. which traces or sources exist;
3. what the method can discriminate;
4. interference, concealment, translation, and error;
5. what evidence is actually obtained;
6. what conclusions that observer can support.

A failed inquiry may leave the fact hidden, narrow alternatives, produce misleading evidence through a causal source, or reveal that the method was inadequate. It does not prove that nothing exists.

### Bounded Qualitative Judgment

Use bounded judgment when canon supplies the relevant relationships, the outcome matters, exact quantification would add no truth, and one result is sufficiently supported without a separate random method.

The GM records:

- the exact question;
- decisive conditions and Counterforces;
- why the selected result follows;
- what remains uncertain;
- consequences and review conditions.

Judgment cannot override a close contest merely because one result would be more convenient. If materially supported branches remain and their distinction matters now, use another valid method or defer.

### Declared Randomization

Use randomization only for a genuinely unresolved question with at least two supported branches when:

- no owner already settles the result;
- the current decision requires resolution now;
- the campaign authorizes the chosen random method;
- branch inclusion and weighting reflect established conditions;
- the method is fixed before its result is known;
- the resulting scope can be recorded and honored.

Randomness resolves uncertainty among supported branches. It cannot create a missing cause, capability, authority, source, consent, access route, immunity, or exception.

### Deferred Resolution

Defer when:

- the relevant actor has not yet chosen;
- an operating process has not reached a material Review Point;
- required evidence, input, or player choice is not available;
- deciding now would reveal hidden information without benefit;
- the branch does not yet affect a meaningful decision;
- the Causal Horizon does not require settlement.

Record a **Resolution Trigger**: the time, condition, observation, actor opportunity, threshold, or decision that makes resolution necessary. Deferral preserves branches; it does not secretly choose one.

### Explicit Correction or Reconstruction

Use correction when the uncertainty comes from a Record Gap, contradiction, accidental leak, or prior adjudication error rather than from the fictional world.

Prefer, in order:

1. recover the authoritative record;
2. preserve the last compatible established fact;
3. infer only what dependencies require and clearly support;
4. obtain participant agreement for a narrow reconstruction;
5. restore a meaningful choice if the gap changed its basis;
6. mark any remaining fact explicitly unresolved going forward.

Do not disguise correction as a surprise revelation.

### Provisional Ruling

Use a Provisional Rule only when the uncertainty is actually a narrow design gap and the Alpha Playtest Rules authorize one. The ruling defines campaign-local mechanics; it does not resolve unrelated world uncertainty or become canon through repetition.

## Uncertainty Procedure

### Step 1: Frame the Question

State one exact claim.

Weak framing: “What happens?”

Useful framing: “Before the repair crew reaches the eastern support, does the established fracture propagate enough to close the lower passage?”

Name the subject, scope, time, relevant effect, and why the answer matters.

### Step 2: Identify the View

Ask whether the question concerns:

- established Factual View;
- one or more Observer Views;
- player-facing information;
- a future not yet settled;
- a missing or conflicting campaign record;
- a rules interpretation;
- player intent.

The same topic may contain several questions with different views.

### Step 3: Name the Source

Classify the uncertainty as hidden truth, incomplete observation, uncertain interpretation, mistaken belief, genuinely unresolved outcome, model uncertainty, Record Gap, Open Detail, rules uncertainty, or intent ambiguity.

If several sources coexist, resolve them in ownership order. A character's uncertain interpretation of a genuinely unresolved future is not one undifferentiated roll.

### Step 4: Establish Supported Branches

A **Supported Branch Set** records only alternatives with a valid causal route and relevant owner support.

For each branch, identify:

- enabling conditions;
- opposing conditions and Counterforces;
- affected subjects;
- information dependencies;
- timing;
- what would close or favor it.

The set need not list immaterial variants. It must not include impossible outcomes merely to appear balanced.

### Step 5: Remove Settled or Unsupported Branches

Apply deterministic facts. Close branches lacking necessary causes, access, authority, consent, embodiment, or other requirements. Preserve unusual branches when they are genuinely supported.

Do not give every attempt a minimum chance or every dominant capability a failure chance.

### Step 6: Assess Decision Relevance

Resolve now only if the answer affects:

- a current player or actor decision;
- an Immediate Outcome;
- a due Consequence Handoff;
- a material state update;
- a Review Point;
- a continuity dependency that must be recorded.

Otherwise set a Resolution Trigger and defer.

### Step 7: Commit the Method

A **Resolution Commitment** records, before the result is known:

- question and scope;
- current evidence and conditions;
- branch set;
- selected method;
- any qualitative weighting or deterministic exclusions;
- whether resolution is open or concealed and why;
- who or what performs it;
- how the result will be recorded.

Do not switch methods, weights, inputs, or interpretation after seeing an inconvenient result.

### Step 8: Resolve Once

Apply the committed method once unless the method itself specifies a valid repeat, staged process, or interruption. A reroll, redraw, reinterpretation, or concealed override requires a cause and prior rule, not dissatisfaction.

The result settles only the framed question. It does not imply unrelated quality, knowledge, intent, collateral effect, or future response.

### Step 9: Apply Consequences

Pass the Immediate Outcome to [Consequence Resolution](CONSEQUENCE_RESOLUTION.md). Keep direct changes, costs, traces, observers, Response Opportunities, Pending Consequences, and future uncertainty separate.

### Step 10: Present the Appropriate View

Present evidence and outcome from the relevant observer's access. The player may receive:

- a confirmed fact;
- a visible result without its hidden cause;
- an inference with stated basis;
- competing reports;
- recognizable uncertainty;
- no new information when nothing accessible changed.

Do not reveal the branch set or concealed result if doing so would leak protected world truth. Do reveal enough rules and stakes for fair participation.

### Step 11: Record and Review

The external Adjudication Trace records:

- Uncertainty Question and view;
- uncertainty source;
- evidence and branch set;
- Resolution Commitment;
- result and scope;
- information revealed to each observer;
- consequence handoffs;
- remaining uncertainty;
- Resolution Trigger for anything deferred.

## Randomization Discipline

### Branch Weighting

Weights may be qualitative unless a specialist system or campaign method supplies valid numbers. Consider actual capability, preparation, environment, compatibility, opposition, scale, reliability, access, and Counterforces.

Equal weighting is not neutral when conditions strongly favor one branch. Precise percentages are not honest when evidence supports only a rough relationship.

### Open and Concealed Resolution

Use open resolution when knowing the method and result does not compromise hidden information. It strengthens shared verification.

Concealed resolution is valid when exposure would reveal whether a hidden actor, trap, route, or state exists. The GM still records the commitment and result externally. Concealment protects information; it does not authorize alteration.

### No Fudging

Once committed, a random result is not changed to preserve a planned scene, protect an important NPC, punish an unexpected tactic, prolong a fight, or manufacture drama.

If the branch set or method was invalid, correct it openly and preserve unaffected choices. Do not call an override “interpretation.”

### Repeated Trials

Repeated attempts require a changing or continuing process. Ask what each attempt consumes, changes, reveals, risks, or learns.

Do not permit unlimited rerolls against an unchanged state when failure has already settled the question. Do not forbid a new attempt when preparation, method, access, time, target, or conditions materially changed.

### Random Tables and Generators

A table or generator supplies candidates or resolves a declared branch only within its scope. It does not establish canon, bypass eligibility, replace actor choice, or guarantee suitability. Later Phase 9 generator documents preserve this rule.

## Fair Hidden Information

Hidden truth may create surprise when it has:

- prior causal existence;
- a valid reason for concealment;
- effects consistent with that existence;
- discoverable traces where meaningful choice depends on them;
- no retroactive immunity from a valid counter;
- observer-specific access and interpretation.

Fair evidence need not reveal the answer. It should preserve the ability to notice danger, investigate, prepare, withdraw, seek expertise, accept uncertainty, or knowingly take risk where those options are plausible.

The GM may keep exact identity, location, capability, motive, or timing concealed. The GM should not conceal the basic category of a danger the character plainly perceives merely to force commitment.

## Capability and Opposition

Use [Capability Assessment](../progression/CAPABILITY_ASSESSMENT.md) before randomizing a contested result.

- If one side clearly satisfies the exact objective and no material Counterforce remains, resolve deterministically.
- If the objective is impossible under current access or embodiment, do not roll.
- If both sides can materially affect the result and the interaction remains unsettled, identify the exact contested layers.
- If capabilities are incomparable, resolve the actual sequence, environment, preparation, information, and objectives rather than inventing one power score.
- If an authorized specialist method already governs uncertainty, use it.

Uncertainty does not make all opponents equal and certainty does not require universal superiority.

## Autonomous Actors

An actor's future choice may remain unresolved even when the GM knows their current motives and constraints.

Resolve through:

- established commitments or procedures that clearly settle the choice;
- actor-specific qualitative judgment grounded in current information;
- a declared random method when supported alternatives remain materially live;
- deferral until the actor receives information or reaches a decision point.

Do not use randomness to erase identity, values, consent, relationships, fear, duty, or recent consequences. Do not treat them as deterministic scripts either.

## Temporal and Off-Screen Uncertainty

Off-screen simulation uses the same procedure. Compression does not grant certainty.

Preserve:

- Resolution Anchors and Material Exceptions;
- actor-specific information and choices;
- maintenance, delay, travel, communication, and succession;
- supported branches and Resolution Triggers;
- Pending Consequences;
- player agency checkpoints.

Do not pre-resolve a century of contingent history because the player is absent. Resolve only to the current Causal Horizon at the coarsest truthful resolution.

## Cross-System Interfaces

### Soul Engine

Soul Echo testimony, Resonance, Titles, Archive Traces, Constellations, and Avatar expressions remain source-bound and fallible where their owners say so. Soul significance does not convert inference into truth or grant future knowledge.

### Development and Skills

Skill and Development affect what evidence can be obtained, interpreted, tested, expressed, and repeated. They do not guarantee unrelated knowledge or remove all uncertainty. Outcomes still depend on scope and current embodiment.

### Monster Evolution

Hidden routes and conditions remain established or genuinely unresolved under Evolution ownership. Observer ignorance does not let the GM retroactively add an adaptation. Random generation cannot waive eligibility or transition requirements.

### Human Classes and Professions

Expertise, office, certification, and institutional access shape evidence and authority within scope. They do not grant universal truth, perfect forecasts, or automatic obedience.

### Soul Weapons

Weapon Souls preserve their own views, secrets, consent, memory limits, and interpretations. A Bond does not merge Factual Views or authorize the GM to speak a partner's unchosen intent.

### Magic

Magical sensing, divination, prophecy, Divine Responses, Archive access, and source communication reveal only what their canonical routes support. Symbolic or divine evidence does not establish every interpretation or moral conclusion.

### World Engine

World-State Claims distinguish truth, observation, estimate, and belief. Causal Event Chains define supported branches; Simulation Abstraction preserves unresolved futures through compression.

### Consequence Resolution

An uncertainty result becomes one bounded Immediate Outcome. Consequence Resolution determines its supported direct and downstream effects without expanding the random result's scope.

## Corrections

If uncertainty was mishandled:

1. identify whether the error affected truth, view, branch support, method, result, presentation, or record;
2. preserve unaffected established facts;
3. remove unsupported branches or restore branches closed without cause;
4. disclose a compromised method or accidental information leak;
5. revisit materially affected player choices;
6. re-resolve only if the original result lacks valid authority;
7. update the external trace;
8. route canonical conflicts to governance.

An inconvenient but valid result is not an error.

## Worked Examples

### The Cracked Bridge

The bridge's current damage, load, weather, and repair history are established. The party considers crossing with a heavy cart.

Capability and structural conditions first remove impossible branches. If the bridge clearly supports or cannot support the load, resolve deterministically. If failure timing remains genuinely unresolved, frame that exact question, declare the method before crossing, and communicate the visible stakes. A concealed check may protect exact damage knowledge, but its commitment and result are recorded. Failure affects only the supported structural outcome and later consequences.

### The Missing Promise

An NPC's old promise of safe passage is absent from the records, and participants remember it differently.

This is a Record Gap, not an in-world random event. Search session deltas and dependent facts. If unrecoverable, agree on the narrowest reconstruction or restore the affected decision. Do not roll to decide whether the NPC “really” promised it and then pretend the result was always history.

### The Concealed Adaptation

A monster's heat-shedding adaptation was established before the characters chose fire as their main tactic. Characters had access to unusual habitat signs but did not investigate.

The adaptation is hidden established truth, not a random branch. Resolve the attack against it under the relevant owners. Present visible evidence as it appears. The GM cannot add the adaptation after seeing the fire tactic, nor must the GM reveal its exact function before contact.

### The Divided Council

A council has current interests, members, procedures, and disagreement, but no final response to a new offer.

The future decision is genuinely unresolved. Established rules may make some positions deterministic; others remain live. The GM can resolve member actions through actor judgment and procedure, randomize only a supported tie or uncertain participation if authorized, or defer until debate occurs. “The council” does not receive one mood roll.

### The Unreadable Gate Signal

A World Gate emits a repeating signal. Its physical pattern is established, but local scholars disagree whether it is language, navigation, warning, or machine noise.

World truth distinguishes the signal from interpretations. Investigation can improve recordings, compare repetitions, test responses, and seek compatible expertise. Randomness may resolve a supported experimental outcome, not define an entire foreign civilization or make the preferred translation true.

### The Dominant Swordsman

A rested master with suitable embodiment, reach, footing, and awareness faces an untrained opponent in an ordinary exchange with no hidden Counterforce.

The narrow contest may be deterministic. The GM need not roll to create a failure chance. This does not make the master universally superior: ambush, poison, politics, numbers, terrain, conflicting objectives, restraint, or another domain can create different questions.

### Echo Testimony

A Soul Echo recalls a route through a city from a prior Age.

The memory is source-bound historical evidence. Current streets remain uncertain until observed or corroborated. Treating the Echo as sincere does not make the map current; treating it as old does not make it useless.

## Safeguards

- Frame one exact uncertainty question and information view at a time.
- Hidden truth, observer ignorance, and genuinely unresolved futures remain distinct.
- Missing records do not grant creative authority over material history.
- Open details cannot retroactively invalidate preparation, counters, consent, or decisions.
- Canon and established facts are applied, not rolled against for suspense.
- Impossible branches receive no minimum chance; dominant branches receive no mandatory failure chance.
- Randomness selects only among supported branches and cannot create missing causality.
- Resolution methods and inputs are committed before results are known.
- Concealed resolution remains recorded and cannot be altered secretly.
- One result settles only the framed question.
- Player intent is clarified, never randomized.
- Fair hidden information requires prior existence and causal evidence where choice depends on it.
- Expertise improves scoped evidence and judgment; it grants no omniscience.
- Compression preserves unresolved futures and agency checkpoints.
- Human and AI GMs share the same truth, method, disclosure, and record duties.
- Current questions, branches, commitments, results, secrets, and records remain outside this repository.

## Scope Boundaries

This document does not define:

- one universal die, deck, oracle, probability scale, difficulty ladder, success tier, or failure table;
- specialist capability, effect, or progression mechanics;
- player-character intent;
- Reincarnation candidate generation;
- encounter, monster, NPC, Dungeon, faction, or world-event generation;
- Time Skip or Age-transition procedure;
- campaign-specific secrecy agreements, random tools, or live state.
