# Profession Development

## Purpose

This document defines how crafts, trades, scholarship, medicine, law, administration, military service, priesthood, exploration, magical work, monster practices, and other professions develop. It distinguishes practical competence from employment, social rank, certification, and reputation while preserving meaningful professional learning across Reincarnation.

## Core Rule

A Profession is an integrated body of practice that combines relevant knowledge, Skills, judgment, tools, standards, responsibilities, relationships, and an economic or social role. Profession Development occurs when a practitioner becomes more capable of producing, evaluating, and taking responsibility for work in that domain.

Holding a job, class label, guild rank, title, licence, office, or reputation does not create Professional Competence. Likewise, a capable practitioner may remain unrecognized, unlicensed, politically excluded, or unable to work because tools, institutions, language, law, or embodiment are incompatible.

Reincarnation may preserve integrated knowledge, Skills, and judgment through legitimate Soul Imprints. It does not preserve the former identity's certification, legal authority, clients, reputation, institutional access, tools, or assumption that old methods remain correct in a new body or Age.

## Terms

### Profession

An organized or recognizable field of work and responsibility combining knowledge, Skills, judgment, standards, tools, relationships, and a social or economic function.

### Professional Competence

The demonstrated ability to perform a defined professional responsibility to an appropriate standard under stated conditions.

### Professional Judgment

The ability to interpret incomplete situations, choose among valid methods, recognize limits, weigh consequences, and decide when to act, adapt, seek help, refuse, or escalate.

### Professional Standard

A stated expectation for process, evidence, safety, quality, responsibility, or outcome within a profession. Standards may be cultural, institutional, legal, technical, spiritual, ecological, or self-imposed and remain fallible.

### Credential

Documented or socially recognized evidence that an authority believes a person met defined requirements. A Credential creates World Recognition and may support access without guaranteeing present competence.

### Licence

Permission from an authority to perform a defined professional activity within its jurisdiction. A Licence grants legal or institutional access rather than Professional Competence.

### Apprenticeship

A structured relationship in which a learner develops through instruction, observation, practice, correction, increasing responsibility, and participation in real work.

### Professional Stage

A local description of demonstrated responsibility or standing, such as apprentice, journeyman, master, novice, keeper, full member, sworn healer, or another cultural equivalent. Stages are profession-specific and not universal levels.

### Institutional Access

Permission and practical ability to use an institution's tools, records, facilities, clients, networks, authority, or protected methods.

### Informal Profession

A coherent field of work and responsibility practised without formal recognition, standardized credentials, or a controlling institution.

### Lost Profession

A profession whose practitioners, institutions, tools, knowledge network, materials, or social function no longer survive in usable continuity.

### Novel Profession

A newly integrated field created in response to new tools, species contact, magic, law, ecology, knowledge, or social need.

## Professions and the Six Layers

| Layer | Professional question |
| --- | --- |
| **Persistent Potential** | What integrated knowledge, Skills, judgment, and professional Soul Imprints survived prior lives? |
| **Current Access** | What can this incarnation recall, interpret, relearn, or reach through present teachers, language, records, and institutions? |
| **Embodied Expression** | What work can the current body, senses, magic, tools, materials, law, and environment actually perform? |
| **Practised Reliability** | What responsibilities can the practitioner discharge consistently across real cases, pressure, uncertainty, and consequence? |
| **Contextual Effectiveness** | How useful is the practice for this client, material, culture, crisis, ecology, objective, and Age? |
| **World Recognition** | What do guilds, clients, courts, temples, communities, monsters, states, and peers believe or authorize? |

These layers can diverge sharply. A reincarnated master may have deep Persistent Potential and no current licence. A prestigious official may possess World Recognition but weak judgment. An independent practitioner may be highly effective locally while failing a distant guild's formal examination.

## The Components of Professional Capability

### Knowledge

Professional knowledge includes facts, theories, classifications, precedents, procedures, hazards, histories, and causal models relevant to the field.

Knowledge may be accurate, partial, obsolete, culturally bounded, deliberately restricted, or dependent on laws that changed. Knowing a procedure does not establish that the practitioner can perform or judge it safely.

### Technical Skill

Technical Skills are the bounded learned capabilities used within the profession. A smith may use heat judgment, hammer control, joining, finishing, and tool maintenance. A healer may use examination, sanitation, dosing, surgery, magical channel work, or communication.

Possessing one impressive technique does not establish the whole profession. Professional work often requires selecting, sequencing, and declining techniques responsibly.

### Professional Judgment

Judgment interprets cases that do not match an ideal exercise. It includes:

- identifying the real problem and relevant uncertainty;
- distinguishing urgent from merely visible issues;
- selecting a method appropriate to the body, client, material, law, and consequence;
- recognizing when evidence is insufficient;
- balancing risk, quality, time, resources, and obligations;
- noticing when a familiar method has become unsafe or obsolete;
- seeking collaboration, referral, permission, or withdrawal when required;
- accepting responsibility for foreseeable consequences.

Judgment develops through varied cases, feedback, reflection, mentorship, and responsibility. It is not downloaded with theory or awarded by seniority.

### Experience

Experience is lived exposure to relevant work. It creates opportunities to encounter variation, failure, consequence, and tacit patterns.

Time served is not equivalent to learning. Repeating one narrow task for decades may create reliable specialization while leaving broader Professional Competence undeveloped. Experience contributes when it is interpreted and integrated.

### Certification and Credentials

Credentials communicate that an authority evaluated defined evidence. They may reduce uncertainty, protect clients, allocate responsibility, and open access to restricted work.

They may also be outdated, corrupt, discriminatory, politically controlled, forged, overly narrow, or irrelevant outside their jurisdiction. A valid Credential should state what it tested and what it did not.

### Reputation

Reputation is what others believe about a practitioner. It affects clients, prices, trust, scrutiny, rivals, referrals, and access.

Reputation can lag behind current competence, attach to the wrong cause, survive after death, or be destroyed by politics. It remains in the world with the professional identity rather than following the soul automatically.

### Legal and Formal Authority

Legal authority permits decisions or actions within a jurisdiction: prescribing medicine, commanding troops, judging disputes, certifying structures, performing rites, or signing public accounts.

Authority may exist without expertise and expertise without authority. Acting without authority may still be effective while creating legal, ethical, political, or spiritual consequences.

### Tools and Institutional Access

Professional capability often depends on workshops, laboratories, libraries, courts, temples, supply networks, assistants, records, secure sites, magical infrastructure, or specialized tools.

Knowing how to use unavailable infrastructure does not make it present. Access may depend on money, membership, law, trust, species, size, language, location, or political alignment.

### Responsibility

Professional work affects clients, communities, ecosystems, institutions, and later practitioners. Greater responsibility can reveal judgment and create Development because consequences are real.

Responsibility is not a reward currency. Taking reckless control of lives or resources does not create mastery merely because the stakes were high.

## Forms of Profession

### Crafts and Trades

Crafts and trades transform materials, maintain systems, construct objects, provide services, and preserve practical standards. Examples include smithing, masonry, weaving, cooking, transport, farming, repair, brewing, and toolmaking.

Their work may be informal, household-based, itinerant, industrial, guild-regulated, species-specific, or magical.

### Scholarship

Scholarship develops methods for inquiry, evidence, preservation, interpretation, comparison, and teaching. Knowing many facts is not sufficient; scholarly competence includes source criticism, uncertainty, argument, ethics, and the ability to revise conclusions.

### Medicine and Care

Medicine includes diagnosis, prevention, treatment, recovery, pain management, public health, species knowledge, magical healing, and decisions about limits or referral.

Healing output alone does not establish medical judgment. Different bodies, souls, diseases, undead states, constructs, and magical channels may require distinct professional traditions.

### Law and Administration

Law and administration organize decisions, records, rights, obligations, resources, processes, and accountability. They depend on current institutions and authority as well as transferable analysis and communication.

Remembering a former legal code can help identify patterns while being dangerous if the present jurisdiction changed.

### Military Roles

Military professions include logistics, reconnaissance, engineering, medicine, command, training, intelligence, fortification, supply, and specialist combat roles.

Combat skill is only one component. Rank, command authority, strategic judgment, and the ability to sustain an organization remain separate.

### Priesthood and Sacred Service

Priesthood may combine doctrine, ritual, pastoral care, law, community leadership, divine relationship, scholarship, and institutional office.

Devotion, divine favor, ritual Skill, ordination, and legal authority are distinct. A god may grant access without making the recipient a capable priest, and an experienced priest may lose divine access while retaining professional judgment.

### Exploration

Exploration integrates navigation, logistics, observation, documentation, survival, negotiation, risk assessment, and responsibility toward companions and encountered peoples or ecologies.

Reaching an unknown place once does not establish the profession. Reliable exploration includes preparation, return, evidence, and adaptation when assumptions fail.

### Monster Professions

Monster professions arise from monster bodies, ecologies, societies, materials, communication, magic, and needs. Examples may include tunnel-memory keeper, brood healer, migration guide, web architect, carrion assessor, scent archivist, lair engineer, venom mixer, or territorial mediator.

They need not imitate human guilds or written schools. A profession may be transmitted through pheromone, movement, communal memory, ritual hunt, molting pattern, song, or another valid cultural method.

### Magical Professions

Magical professions use magic within a sustained practice of judgment, tools, standards, and responsibility. Examples include enchanter, ritual engineer, ward inspector, alchemist, spirit negotiator, magical physician, or mana-infrastructure maintainer.

Magical power does not replace professional practice. The later magic system will define mana, spells, rituals, and affinities; this document governs their use as work.

### Hybrid Professions

Hybrid professions integrate domains whose interaction creates a distinct responsibility, such as magical engineering, battlefield medicine, ecological warding, legal divination, monster-human interpretation, or soul-safe artifact restoration.

Combining titles does not create a hybrid profession. The integrated field must have recurring problems, methods, standards, and consequences not adequately handled by one parent profession alone.

### Informal and Guild-Regulated Professions

An Informal Profession may have coherent methods and community accountability without licences or centralized standards. A guild-regulated profession may protect safety and knowledge while also restricting competition or excluding bodies and cultures it was not designed to assess.

Neither form is automatically more competent. The GM should model what each structure actually teaches, tests, protects, controls, and overlooks.

## Professional Development

Professional Development occurs through integrated change in the ability to understand, perform, judge, and take responsibility for work.

Useful routes include:

- structured instruction and apprenticeship;
- supervised real work with increasing responsibility;
- deliberate practice of technical Skills;
- exposure to varied cases, materials, clients, bodies, failures, and environments;
- comparison with standards and peer review;
- experimentation with documented consequences;
- study of theory, history, precedent, and failure;
- reflection, correction, and revision of obsolete assumptions;
- teaching, explanation, and mentoring where the practitioner can do so;
- collaboration across specialties and species;
- independent practice that receives honest external feedback;
- invention responding to a genuine new need.

Routine employment creates no automatic Development. The practitioner must encounter and integrate relevant variation, feedback, judgment, or responsibility.

## Apprenticeship and Increasing Responsibility

An Apprenticeship should provide more than unpaid repetition or proximity to a master.

A sound progression may include:

1. observation with explanation and safety boundaries;
2. isolated practice of Foundational Skills;
3. supervised contribution to real work;
4. correction with reasons and alternative methods;
5. responsibility for bounded tasks;
6. exposure to failure, variation, and client consequence;
7. independent decisions with review;
8. recognition of limits and when to seek help;
9. eventual responsibility for complete work or for teaching others.

Abusive hardship, secrecy for its own sake, dangerous initiation, and years served do not prove quality. A harsh apprenticeship may create real capability despite poor conditions, but suffering is not the source of competence.

## Professional Stages and Institutions

### Apprentice and Cultural Equivalents

An apprentice is learning under structured guidance and normally carries limited responsibility. Equivalent roles may be novice, hatchling-helper, listener, shadow, junior keeper, uninitiated hand, or another culturally grounded stage.

The label does not imply age, species, intelligence, or lack of prior-life potential.

### Journeyman and Independent Practice

A journeyman or equivalent has demonstrated enough breadth and reliability to perform defined work independently, travel between contexts, or accept ordinary responsibility.

Independence may be recognized by a guild, community, patron, lineage, clients, or the practitioner's demonstrated work. It remains scoped to the profession and jurisdiction.

### Mastery

Professional Mastery involves deep judgment, reliable work across meaningful variation, diagnosis of difficult failure, responsibility for consequences, and the ability to adapt standards when circumstances change.

Teaching, owning a workshop, holding office, producing a masterpiece, surviving a famous crisis, or receiving a title may be evidence but is not a universal requirement.

### Schools

Schools organize curriculum, teachers, exercises, records, peers, and assessment. They may accelerate learning and preserve knowledge across generations.

A school cannot guarantee that every graduate is competent or that unaffiliated practice is inferior. Its methods may become obsolete after changes in materials, magic, species contact, or law.

### Guild Ranks

Guild ranks may combine competence, membership, contribution, dues, politics, seniority, patronage, and legal authority. Their meaning must be stated locally.

A high rank does not automatically grant broad practical superiority. A low-ranked specialist may outperform a guild master in one narrow technique.

### Licences and Titles

Licences grant permission. Professional titles communicate role or recognition. Neither becomes a Soul Title merely because the words match.

Revocation may remove legal access while leaving competence intact. Fraudulent practice may display credentials without capability. A reincarnated practitioner must establish a current identity and qualify again under present law unless a valid institution creates another route.

### Independent Practitioners

Independent practitioners may develop through mentorship, self-study, community work, experimentation, inherited tradition, or necessity. Their competence must be assessed through evidence rather than absence of institutional status.

They may lack legal protection, peer review, supplies, referral networks, or access to restricted tools even when highly capable.

## Lost and Novel Professions

### Lost Professions

A Lost Profession may survive through fragments of theory, tools, Archive Records, ruins, Soul Echoes, or reincarnated practitioners. Recovering it requires more than remembering a label.

The current world must reconstruct:

- language and conceptual categories;
- materials, tools, infrastructure, and supply;
- body or magical compatibility;
- standards and methods for testing results;
- a present social, ecological, or economic function;
- safe transmission to new practitioners.

Reconstruction may create a transformed profession rather than a perfect copy.

### Novel Professions

A Novel Profession forms when new problems and methods become coherent enough to require integrated practice, standards, responsibility, and transmission.

One clever invention is not automatically a profession. Repeated need, teachable methods, differentiated judgment, supporting tools, and consequences help establish a field.

New professions may arise from World Gates, new species contact, magical-law changes, technology, ecological collapse, Soul Engine research, or institutions responding to earlier mistakes. Those later systems own the events that create the need.

## Reincarnation and Professional Continuity

Professional persistence is component-specific. Legitimately integrated theory, Skills, judgment, and practice may persist through Soul Imprints and related memory systems. The former profession label does not create one indivisible package.

### Remembered Theory

Theory may return clearly, through an Echo, as partial intuition, or only after a Trigger. It remains a claim to investigate rather than proof that current conditions match.

### Outdated Knowledge

An old method may be obsolete because:

- natural or magical laws changed;
- species and diseases evolved;
- materials, tools, measurements, or language changed;
- later scholarship corrected an error;
- social values, ethics, or accepted risks changed;
- former infrastructure no longer exists;
- the profession's economic or legal function disappeared.

Retained expertise should help recognize patterns and design tests, not force the world to validate old conclusions.

### Changed Tools and Materials

Tool familiarity may transfer by principle, while actual use requires present body calibration and material knowledge. A smith trained in iron cannot assume that living crystal responds to the same heat, force, or repair.

### Changed Laws and Authority

Legal codes, guild jurisdictions, military structures, temple authority, and professional liability remain with the world. Former office and licences do not follow the soul.

A reincarnated judge may understand procedure while having no right to hear a case. Acting anyway creates current legal and political consequences.

### Lost Languages and Cultural Categories

Knowledge may remain inaccessible because the current incarnation cannot read its old notation or because the present culture divides the field differently. Translation requires language access, interpretation, and evidence.

### Accelerated Retraining

Persistent Potential may accelerate:

- recognition of Foundational Skills and useful curricula;
- diagnosis of novice errors;
- understanding of professional responsibility;
- comparison between old and current standards;
- tool calibration where functional correspondence exists;
- recovery of case patterns and judgment after present validation.

It cannot provide a mature body, current tools, clients, licence, institutional trust, updated knowledge, or Practised Reliability in the new context.

## Professional Assessment

Assess a professional for a stated responsibility rather than assigning one total rank.

Consider:

1. relevant knowledge and its currency;
2. demonstrated technical Skills;
3. case variety and Professional Judgment;
4. current body, tools, materials, language, and magical access;
5. standards used and evidence of quality;
6. reliability under expected pressure and consequence;
7. ability to recognize limits, errors, and need for collaboration;
8. certification, licence, jurisdiction, and Institutional Access;
9. reputation and possible bias or misinformation;
10. team, infrastructure, supply, and environmental support;
11. uncertainty and hidden dependencies.

Useful comparisons are scoped:

> The guild novice is licensed and demonstrably reliable with one newly standardized sealant. The unaffiliated veteran lacks current certification but has broader judgment across unfamiliar structural failures.

Neither is universally the better professional.

## Failure, Malpractice, and Obsolete Expertise

Professional failure may result from:

- insufficient knowledge or Skill;
- poor judgment under uncertainty;
- outdated assumptions;
- unavailable or unsuitable tools;
- communication or cultural failure;
- institutional pressure, corruption, or unsafe standards;
- overconfidence and failure to seek help;
- fatigue, injury, magical suppression, or resource shortage;
- an unfamiliar species, material, disease, law, or environment;
- a valid method overwhelmed by circumstances beyond its scope.

Failure does not always prove incompetence. The GM should distinguish unavoidable uncertainty from negligence, and a bad outcome from a bad process.

Malpractice requires a relevant duty, foreseeable risk, available alternatives, and a failure of care or responsibility under the applicable culture or law. It should not be used merely to punish experimentation or hindsight.

Professional Development may follow honest review and changed practice. Repeating preventable harm does not generate competence simply because the consequences are severe.

## Anti-Grinding and Anti-Status Rules

- Time employed, clients served, items produced, battles survived, rites performed, or cases processed do not grant automatic Professional Development.
- Repeating one solved task develops narrow reliability and then reaches diminishing returns.
- Credentials, guild dues, patronage, seniority, wealth, fame, and office do not substitute for competence.
- Performing one spectacular technique does not grant the entire profession.
- Delegating work does not develop the delegator's technical Skill, though real supervision may develop judgment or leadership.
- Manufactured emergencies and preventable harm do not create mastery through stakes alone.
- Reading an Archive Record, consulting an Echo, or recalling a former life provides information, not current professional reliability.
- Reincarnation cannot preserve licences, clients, legal authority, institutional access, or reputation as soul-owned assets.
- A profession label cannot bundle unrelated Skills, statistics, magic, tools, and authority into one package.
- Hybrid-profession names do not create integration without recurring problems, methods, standards, and practice.
- Lost professions cannot be restored by one memory without present testing, tools, transmission, and social function.
- Institutional exclusion should create real barriers and consequences without being mistaken for proof of incapacity.

## Scope Boundaries

This document does not define:

- a universal profession list, class system, or Profession XP currency;
- exact curricula, examinations, rank ladders, wages, prices, licences, or guild laws;
- complete human, monster, magical, divine, military, legal, medical, or craft institutions;
- technical rules for smithing, medicine, scholarship, ritual, administration, exploration, or other work;
- automatic persistence of jobs, offices, credentials, reputation, clients, tools, or legal authority;
- the social structure, roles, service relationships, jurisdiction, and accountability of Professions defined in [Professions](../human/PROFESSIONS.md);
- the complete magic, social, species, economy, technology, or World Engine systems;
- a universal ethical code imposed on every culture or profession.

Later systems may define local structures. They must state their jurisdiction, evidence, access, responsibilities, and limits without turning Professional Stage into an overall character level.

## GM Procedure

When professional capability or Development matters:

1. name the profession, responsibility, client or objective, and current context;
2. separate knowledge, technical Skill, judgment, experience, standards, Credential, reputation, authority, and Institutional Access;
3. identify the body, tools, materials, language, infrastructure, magic, team, and law involved;
4. establish what practice, instruction, responsibility, feedback, or reflection produced Development;
5. assess current reliability across relevant cases rather than time served or title;
6. identify obsolete knowledge, cultural assumptions, missing data, and unfamiliar bodies or materials;
7. apply licences, guild ranks, titles, and reputation only through their actual institutions;
8. preserve integrated Imprints across Reincarnation without restoring worldly standing;
9. require current validation and retraining before old expertise governs high-consequence work;
10. resolve failure through evidence, process, uncertainty, and responsibility rather than outcome alone;
11. record local stages only with their scope and authority;
12. defer complete professions, institutions, technical procedures, and economies to their later systems.

## Worked Examples

### The Smith in a Different Metallurgy

A master steel smith reincarnates in an Age where tools are grown from living crystal. The soul remembers heat, stress, grain, workflow, and responsibility, but crystal responds to resonance and pruning rather than forging temperature.

Old judgment helps the smith form useful questions and avoid careless assumptions. Present experimentation, local instruction, different tools, and real work are required. The result may become a new hybrid profession rather than restored steel mastery.

### The Healer in Another Species

A human physician reincarnates as an insect monster whose colony treats illness through scent, shedding, shared heat, and fungal symbiosis. Human anatomy and bedside language are not directly applicable.

Diagnostic caution, sanitation principles, observation, and responsibility persist as useful potential. The healer must learn colony biology, communication, tools, and ethics before treating others reliably. Former medical prestige creates no authority in the brood.

### The Monster Architect

A web-building monster designs suspended settlements for several species. Its profession integrates tension mapping, material choice, wind behavior, evacuation, accessibility, communal ritual, and negotiation over shared space.

Human masons may not recognize the title **architect**, but the work has coherent standards and responsibility. The monster's profession is not merely a collection of innate web traits.

### The Dragon Historian

A long-lived dragon remembers oral accounts, ruin sequences, migration records, and several scholarly methods. Longevity provides sources but does not make every recollection accurate.

Professional competence appears in provenance, comparison, correction, preservation, and willingness to distinguish memory from evidence. A younger scholar may outperform the dragon in one script family while lacking its broad historical context.

### The Magical Engineer

A magical engineer maintains a city's levitation network. The profession combines structural analysis, ritual timing, mana-flow control, inspection standards, public safety, supply, and team coordination.

High magical output alone does not qualify a caster to repair the network. A weak caster with excellent diagnostic judgment, tools, and collaborators may be the more effective engineer.

### The Certified Novice and Uncertified Veteran

A guild-certified novice has recently trained in a new damp-stone sealing method and passed a practical examination using its reactive compounds. An uncertified veteran from an excluded tunnel species has decades of broader structural experience but has never used those compounds.

The novice outperforms the veteran at that narrow sealing technique because the training, tools, and current standard match the task. The veteran remains more reliable at diagnosing unfamiliar pressure fractures and coordinating a complex repair. Certification is meaningful evidence within its tested scope, but it does not make either practitioner universally superior.

### The Reincarnated Administrator

A former imperial administrator enters a village monster life. The soul remembers ledgers, supply chains, delegation failures, and tax abuse. The village has no writing, central treasury, or legal office.

Old knowledge may inspire better food storage and duty rotation only after the incarnation learns local trust, seasonal needs, and nonverbal records. Former office grants no command, and importing the imperial system unchanged may reproduce its harms.
