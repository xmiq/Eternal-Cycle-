# Species Development

## Purpose

This document defines Development tied to a current species, form, lifecycle, biology, metaphysics, ecology, and body-specific possibility. It establishes the boundary between ordinary maturation, traits, learned capability, transformation, mutation, and later monster evolution rules.

## Core Rule

Species Development is growth or change available because of what the current incarnation is. It includes maturation, expression of inherited structures, body-specific adaptation, lifecycle transitions, and access to valid species pathways.

Species Development belongs to the current form. Reincarnation does not carry a former species stage, anatomy, natural weapon, sense, Magical Organ, inherited trait, mutation, or evolution branch into the new body. Prior lives may preserve Retained Instincts, Skill XP, Stat XP, knowledge, and other legitimate Soul Imprints through their own rules, but those records do not become portable species traits.

Humans and monsters begin with distinct progression trees. Neither is the default version of the other, and social sophistication does not make a monster biologically human. Bounded crossover may become possible only through Reincarnation, functional correspondence, present development, body compatibility, and an explicit receiving rule.

## Terms

### Species Development

Growth or change belonging to a current species or form through maturation, trait expression, body-specific adaptation, lifecycle, environment, and valid transformative pathways.

### Species Potential

The range of maturation, trait expression, adaptation, transformation, and evolution routes presently available to a body under its species, form, lifecycle, condition, environment, and established alterations.

### Species Trait

An anatomical, physiological, sensory, cognitive, magical, spiritual, material, reproductive, or lifecycle characteristic belonging to a current species or form.

### Inherited Trait

A Species Trait supplied through the current body's valid origin, lineage, construction, spawning process, or other established inheritance route.

### Trait Expression

The present degree and manner in which an existing Species Trait manifests under maturation, health, environment, regulation, and current form.

### Maturation

Expected growth and change within a current form's lifecycle without necessarily becoming a distinct evolved form.

### Metamorphosis

A structured lifecycle transition that substantially reorganizes the current body or form according to an established species route.

### Transformation

An established temporary or lasting change in form, structure, material, function, or metaphysical state. Its source owns its duration, access, costs, maintenance, and reversibility.

### Mutation

A change in a body's inherited or current structure outside ordinary maturation, caused through an established biological, environmental, magical, corruptive, artificial, divine, or other route.

### Evolution

A lasting transition into a distinct species form or stage through a valid evolution system. Phase 4 owns branches, requirements, rarity, and resulting forms.

### Body Compatibility

The degree to which a current body and form possess the structures, regulation, scale, senses, materials, cognition, magic, and lifecycle needed to support a proposed capability or change.

### Ecological Role

The current relationships through which a species or population affects and depends on habitats, resources, prey, predators, competitors, symbionts, disease, decomposition, and environmental change.

### Artificial Modification

A body or form change produced and maintained through constructed components, procedures, programming, tools, or designed systems rather than ordinary species inheritance.

### Divine Alteration

A body or form change produced through a deity or divine system under its own authority, purpose, costs, limits, and persistence.

### Corruption

A transformative process that imposes, spreads, or develops changes through an established harmful, alienating, coercive, unstable, or value-laden source. The source must define what makes it corruptive rather than relying on appearance alone.

### Purification

A process that removes, transforms, stabilizes, or reclassifies a defined corruptive influence. Purification does not automatically restore an earlier body or erase consequences.

## Species Development and the Six Layers

| Layer | Species question |
| --- | --- |
| **Persistent Potential** | What Retained Instincts, Stat XP, Skill Imprints, knowledge, and other legitimate history from prior species remain available through their own systems? |
| **Current Access** | Which current species traits, lifecycle routes, adaptations, and possible changes can this incarnation presently reach or activate? |
| **Embodied Expression** | What can the current form, anatomy, senses, organs, materials, health, maturity, and environment actually express? |
| **Practised Reliability** | Which controllable species capabilities can be used consistently after present calibration and meaningful practice? |
| **Contextual Effectiveness** | How useful is this form for the current objective, habitat, opposition, ecology, tools, allies, and conditions? |
| **World Recognition** | How do cultures, scholars, gods, monsters, institutions, and observers classify, value, fear, regulate, or misunderstand the species and form? |

Persistent Potential does not mean that a former species remains hidden inside the current body. It records prior Development through the systems that actually own it. Current Species Potential begins from the new incarnation's valid form and pathways.

## Distinguishing Species Development

### Species Trait and Physical Development

A Species Trait supplies a structure or route. [Physical Development](PHYSICAL_DEVELOPMENT.md) owns the conditioning, regulation, recovery, and present bodily adaptation built through use.

Wings may be a Species Trait. Wing strength, endurance, balance, and recovery are Physical Development. Flight technique is Skill Development. A transformation that creates wings does not grant the conditioning or technique required to use them safely.

### Species Trait and Skill

Possessing anatomy does not establish reliable learned use. Claws, venom glands, web organs, echolocation structures, gills, distributed senses, and Magical Organs may create access to Skills without supplying those Skills in full.

Some simple responses may be Current Instincts expressed early in life. More adaptable, precise, or culturally transmitted methods require [Skill Development](SKILL_DEVELOPMENT.md). Species techniques remain in the species or monster tree where their prerequisites and identity belong.

### Species Trait and Retained Instinct

A [Retained Instinct](../soul/RETAINED_INSTINCTS.md) preserves a body-shaped pattern of attention or response. It does not preserve the former trait that once supported that response.

A spider life's attention to tension may survive. Spinnerets, web silk, leg-borne vibration sensing, venom, and spider maturation do not. The current body determines whether the old orientation can express directly, analogically, or not at all.

### Species Development and Monster Evolution

Species Development establishes the shared concepts and boundaries that any evolution system must respect. The completed [Monster Evolution](../monster-evolution/README.md) framework defines:

- evolution branches and stage structures;
- visible and hidden requirements;
- common, rare, hybrid, and exceptional forms;
- environmental influence on available branches;
- social, behavioral, ecological, and developmental evidence;
- branch-specific gains, losses, constraints, and consequences;
- how evolution changes populations and the World Engine.

This document does not select branches or provide universal evolution thresholds.

### Species Development and Reincarnation

Reincarnation ends the former body's species stage and creates a new valid incarnation. It may preserve history useful to relearning or crossover while never treating the new body as cosmetic.

Choosing, qualifying for, or being placed into a species does not grant every mature trait, cultural practice, hidden branch, or former-life ability at birth.

## Maturation and Lifecycle

### Growth

Maturation may alter size, proportion, tissue, material density, cognition, senses, reproductive state, Magical Organs, metabolism, social role, and available Species Potential.

Age alone does not guarantee healthy or complete maturation. Nutrition, environment, disease, care, activity, stress, magic, construction, and species-specific conditions may support, delay, redirect, or damage it.

### Lifecycle Stages

A species may recognize eggs, larvae, juveniles, adults, elders, molts, castes, seasons, modules, bodies, or other stages. Stage labels describe a local lifecycle and are not universal levels.

Different stages may open and close real capabilities. Loss is not automatically failure: a larval sense may disappear when an adult gains flight, and an artificial form may exchange modularity for stability.

### Metamorphosis

Metamorphosis reorganizes the current form through an expected lifecycle route. It may require stored resources, a safe site, time, environmental cues, social support, magical conditions, or temporary vulnerability.

Prior training may help preparation and recovery, but it cannot skip required structures or guarantee the outcome. The post-metamorphic body requires new Physical Calibration and Skill practice.

### Aging and Senescence

Some species decline, stabilize, transform, divide, renew, or grow stronger with age. No human aging curve applies universally.

Age may provide opportunity for experience while also changing expression, recovery, cognition, senses, role, or access. Time lived does not create Development without adaptation and integration.

## Species Traits

### Inherited Traits

Inherited Traits enter through the current body's valid origin rather than through the soul's collection of former forms. They may express at birth, during maturation, under an environmental condition, after Metamorphosis, or only when another established requirement is met.

Inheritance establishes a route, not guaranteed quality or destiny. Health, variation, suppression, lineage, environment, and present Development can alter Trait Expression, while learned control remains separate.

### Natural Weapons

Natural weapons may include claws, teeth, horns, tails, stingers, venom, constriction, electricity, breath organs, corrosive fluids, sonic structures, or body mass.

The trait supplies potential. Safe maintenance, targeting, dose control, range judgment, restraint, and use around allies require Physical and Skill Development. A natural weapon does not guarantee combat superiority.

### Sensory Systems

Species may perceive light, scent, vibration, pressure, heat, magic, electrical fields, currents, sound, memory traces, or other established signals.

Having a sense does not make its interpretation perfect. Range, resolution, noise, attention, fatigue, environment, unfamiliar signals, and learned discrimination still matter.

### Magical Organs

Magical Organs are Species Traits when they belong to the current form's biology or metaphysics. [Magical Development](MAGICAL_DEVELOPMENT.md) owns magical Access, perception, control, capacity, affinity, and learned use.

A species may inherit a working Channel without inheriting mature reserves or every spell associated with the species.

### Current Instincts

Current Instincts belong to the present species, body, development, and upbringing. They may orient feeding, movement, danger, care, migration, social distance, nesting, metamorphosis, or environmental response.

Instincts influence without determining personality, morality, culture, or deliberate choice. Stronger compulsory effects require their own explicit rules and agency safeguards.

### Cognition and Distributed Bodies

Species may think through centralized brains, distributed networks, colonies, artificial processors, magical cores, collective interfaces, or unfamiliar temporal and sensory structures.

The form's actual cognition matters for access, attention, memory, identity, communication, and action economy. Distributed structures do not automatically grant parallel turns, group minds, perfect coordination, or immunity to confusion.

## Ecology and Social Learning

### Ecological Roles

Species develop within habitats and causal relationships rather than isolated combat niches. Food, shelter, reproduction, symbiosis, predation, competition, disease, migration, waste, construction, and resource use shape which traits are useful and sustainable.

An apex predator is not universally strongest. It may be vulnerable to scarcity, disease, coordinated opposition, environmental change, or reproductive limits. Changing one species can alter many others through the World Engine.

### Environmental Adaptation

Current bodies may acclimate or develop within valid ranges for heat, cold, pressure, altitude, mana density, toxins, salinity, darkness, gravity, or other conditions.

Ordinary bodily acclimation belongs to Physical Development. Species Development owns inherited ranges, specialized structures, lifecycle responses, and established transformative pathways. Exposure alone does not create a new species trait or evolution.

### Social Learning

Species capability is not only biological. Groups may teach hunting, migration, nest construction, language, tool use, magic, profession, restraint, ritual, parenting, history, or governance.

Those learned capabilities remain Skills, Professions, or Social Development rather than becoming innate traits merely because most members learn them. A monster can develop sophisticated culture without becoming human.

### Species Identity and Variation

A species label does not prescribe one personality, morality, culture, class, diet choice, allegiance, intelligence, or destiny. Individuals and populations vary through lineage, environment, health, upbringing, mutation, Development, and history.

Species rules may establish real shared structures and constraints without turning every member into an interchangeable stereotype.

## Body-Changing Routes

### Transformation

Transformation is the broad category for a change of form. The source must define:

- what changes and what remains;
- whether the change is temporary, conditional, maintained, or lasting;
- which system owns access and reversal;
- how body, mind, identity, senses, and magic are affected;
- what calibration and recovery are required;
- which consequences persist after reversal.

Temporary transformation does not create permanent Species Development merely because the form was powerful.

### Mutation

Mutation changes current structure outside ordinary maturation. It may be beneficial, harmful, mixed, unstable, heritable, contagious, chosen, imposed, or environmentally induced according to its source.

Receiving a trait is not the same as developing its use. Mutations can close former routes, create maintenance needs, conflict with other structures, alter social recognition, or produce ecological consequences.

### Evolution

Evolution is a lasting transition into a distinct form or stage under a later evolution system. It should reflect compatible current development and meaningful life rather than only a level threshold or kill count.

Every branch must define gains, losses, changed needs, Body Compatibility, identity implications where relevant, and consequences. Powerful branches should create different possibilities and costs rather than one universally best ladder.

### Artificial Modification

Artificial Modification may add, replace, remove, reprogram, or connect structures through technology, craft, alchemy, magic, or constructed bodies.

The modification retains its maker, maintenance, resource, interface, failure, and compatibility rules. Installation does not make it inherited, soul-owned, or mastered. A sufficiently integrated constructed lineage may qualify as a species only through the applicable [Monster Evolution](../monster-evolution/README.md) and [World Engine](../world-engine/README.md) rules.

### Divine Alteration

Divine Alteration changes a form through a deity or divine system. The effect may be blessing, punishment, office, covenant, rescue, test, or creation according to that source.

Divine origin does not make the form morally correct, irrevocable, heritable, universally recognized, or exempt from Body Compatibility and current practice.

### Corruption

Corruption must identify its source, route, target, changes, agency conditions, warning, resistance, progression, consequences, and possible endings. Appearance, monster status, unfamiliar anatomy, or cultural disapproval is insufficient to define corruption.

Corruptive change may grant real capabilities while causing instability, dependence, altered values, harm, social response, or ecological spread. Suffering does not make the result earned Development automatically.

### Purification

Purification addresses a defined corruption rather than enforcing one culturally preferred body. It may remove a source, stabilize changes, restore some functions, transform the influence, or allow coexistence.

It does not automatically return the exact prior form, erase memories, undo harm, remove useful Development, or settle the person's identity. Lasting restoration needs a valid route and consequences.

## Human and Monster Separation

Human and monster progression trees begin separately because they organize different bodies, instincts, ecologies, cultures, institutions, Skills, and transformative possibilities.

Separation does not mean humans lack biology or monsters lack society. It means each tree owns its assumptions and neither receives the other's branches or labels automatically.

A human tree may emphasize learned classes, institutions, equipment, and stable form. A monster tree may emphasize body-bound traits, lifecycle, ecological practice, mutation, and branching form. Particular settings may vary, but crossover requires an explicit rule rather than treating all paths as one catalog.

## Reincarnation and Bounded Crossover

Prior species lives may contribute:

- Retained Instincts with clear Origins;
- Stat XP applicable through current Body Compatibility;
- Skill Imprints and Transferable Principles;
- memories and Echo guidance;
- knowledge of species ecology, senses, magic, and behavior;
- evidence relevant to a later hidden or hybrid route if its Owning System permits it.

They do not contribute the former traits themselves.

Bounded crossover requires:

1. genuine provenance in prior Development;
2. current access to the retained history;
3. a functional correspondence or explicit transformative route;
4. present-life experimentation, calibration, and meaningful use;
5. compatibility with the receiving human, monster, species, Skill, or evolution tree;
6. a defined result with costs, constraints, and provenance;
7. protection against duplicate traits, XP, actions, and progression rewards.

When no present route exists, former species potential remains latent history rather than active anatomy.

## Plateaus, Failure, and Regression

Species Development may Plateau when the current lifecycle, environment, resources, health, form, or branch conditions no longer permit further change.

Failure may include incomplete maturation, unsafe metamorphosis, maladaptation, rejected modification, unstable mutation, transformed needs without resources, inaccessible traits, or a form poorly matched to its environment.

Regression must identify what changed. Injury may impair Trait Expression without removing the trait. Suppression may block a Magical Organ. Malnutrition may delay maturation. Reversal may end a transformation. Final Death ends the form entirely while protected learning is reconciled through its own systems.

## Species Assessment

Assess a species capability for a stated form, stage, objective, and environment. Consider:

1. current species, form, lifecycle, maturity, and origin;
2. Species Potential and actually expressed traits;
3. anatomy, senses, cognition, natural weapons, Magical Organs, and Current Instincts;
4. health, condition, Physical Development, Skill, and calibration;
5. habitat, resources, ecological relationships, tools, magic, and social support;
6. current transformation, mutation, modification, divine alteration, corruption, or purification;
7. Body Compatibility and dependencies for the attempted capability;
8. Practised Reliability rather than assumed species stereotype;
9. World Recognition, misclassification, prejudice, and hidden variation;
10. consequences for the body, population, ecology, and future pathways.

Useful comparisons remain contextual:

> The aquatic predator has superior underwater pursuit and pressure tolerance. The terrestrial humanoid has better tool access and overland endurance. Neither body is universally more developed.

## Anti-Farming and Anti-Stacking Rules

- Reincarnation does not collect former Species Traits, stages, organs, senses, mutations, or evolution branches.
- Killing, consuming, dissecting, observing, possessing, copying, mating with, or being harmed by a species does not grant its traits without an explicit route.
- Time alive, age, births, deaths, molts, kills, meals, pain, corruption, and repeated weak forms do not create automatic evolution progress.
- Deliberately choosing weak bodies does not multiply retained Development or guarantee a stronger later branch.
- A trait name cannot bypass absent anatomy, lifecycle, Body Compatibility, maintenance, or receiving-tree permission.
- Similar traits from several lives do not permit trait stacking into duplicate organs, actions, resistances, or bonuses.
- Temporary transformations and borrowed bodies do not become permanent Species Development through repeated use alone.
- Artificial or divine alterations retain external dependencies and do not become inherited or soul-owned automatically.
- Corruption cannot be farmed as cheap power by ignoring its source, agency conditions, instability, harm, and consequences.
- Purification cannot erase inconvenient costs, choices, identity, or world consequences by declaring a preferred baseline.
- Social Development, language, profession, morality, or intelligence does not change a monster into a biological human.
- Human and monster trees cannot be merged into one purchasable trait catalog through Reincarnation.

## Scope Boundaries

This document does not define:

- complete human or monster progression trees;
- specific species catalogs, statistics, diets, lifespans, reproductive systems, or cultures;
- evolution branches, hidden requirements, rare forms, hybrid forms, or final evolutions;
- universal level, kill, consumption, age, or XP thresholds for transformation or evolution;
- exact mutation, corruption, purification, divine, artificial, or shapeshifting procedures;
- trait costs, slots, points, inheritance formulas, or breeding rules;
- damage, combat actions, natural-weapon formulas, or threat ratings;
- a route for purchasing arbitrary Species Traits with retained progression.

The [Monster Evolution](../monster-evolution/README.md) framework defines evolution branches and requirements inside these boundaries. It preserves current embodiment, provenance, human and monster distinction, ecological consequence, meaningful tradeoffs, anti-farming rules, and the ownership of every supporting Track.

## GM Procedure

When Species Development or a body-changing claim matters:

1. name the current species, form, lifecycle stage, origin, and proposed capability or change;
2. separate Species Trait, Trait Expression, Physical Development, Skill, instinct, magic, and external support;
3. identify Species Potential, Body Compatibility, environment, resources, health, and current conditions;
4. identify the valid maturation, metamorphosis, transformation, mutation, evolution, artificial, divine, corruption, or purification route;
5. apply that route's access, agency, cost, maintenance, reversibility, and failure rules;
6. require present calibration and meaningful practice for controllable expression;
7. route retained instincts, Stat XP, Skills, memory, and Echo knowledge through their own systems;
8. preserve human and monster tree ownership and require explicit permission for crossover;
9. test for duplicate traits, rewards, actions, and recursive reincarnation gains;
10. assess ecological, social, and world consequences as well as personal capability;
11. communicate uncertainty and variation without treating a species label as a complete profile;
12. route branches, hidden requirements, rare forms, and evolution generation to their [Monster Evolution](../monster-evolution/README.md) owners.

## Worked Examples

### Human Reborn as a Spider Monster

A human craftsperson reincarnates as a newly hatched spider monster. The new body begins with spider Species Potential, Current Instincts, immature spinnerets, multiple limbs, and the colony's actual lifecycle rather than human anatomy.

Human Skill Imprints may support planning and tool concepts, but web production, vibration sense, movement, venom control, and maturation require present structures and practice. The spider does not begin as a tiny human with extra limbs.

### Aquatic Predator Reborn as a Terrestrial Humanoid

An aquatic predator's soul enters a terrestrial humanoid. Retained current-reading instincts and compatible Stat XP may support balance, timing, and environmental observation.

Gills, pressure tolerance, fins, lateral-line senses, and aquatic evolution routes ended with the former body. Swimming knowledge may remain useful, but running and breathing air develop through the current form.

### Dragon Soul in a Physically Weak Species

A dragon soul reincarnates as a small, physically weak monster. It remembers aerial scale, breath timing, territory, and magical pressure but has no wings, dragon mass, mature Channels, or breath organ.

Those histories may guide training or qualify as evidence for a compatible route under [Hidden Evolution Conditions](../monster-evolution/HIDDEN_EVOLUTION_CONDITIONS.md). They do not make the current monster secretly dragon-strength or guarantee dragon evolution.

### Monster Gains Social Development

A territorial monster learns language, mediation, law, empathy, and coalition building while living among several peoples. Its Social Development becomes sophisticated, and it may govern a mixed settlement effectively.

None of that changes its biology into a human. Its body, Species Traits, lifecycle, instincts, and monster-tree ownership remain real while its culture and choices expand.

### Artificial Body Without Inherited Instincts

A soul awakens in a newly constructed body with modular limbs, sensors, and a magical core. The design supplies structures and Species Potential, but no biological childhood, ancestral instincts, or automatic understanding of its interfaces.

The incarnation must calibrate movement, interpret sensors, regulate the core, and develop a current identity. Former Retained Instincts may translate where functional correspondence exists; the artificial body does not inherit them as factory settings.

### Purification Without Restoration

A forest monster is altered by a corruptive mineral that creates luminous armor and dependence on contaminated water. Purification removes the spreading source and stabilizes the monster, but some armor remains and its former metabolism cannot be restored fully.

The result preserves consequences and current identity. Further recovery, adaptation, and social response continue rather than resetting the body to a preferred earlier state.
