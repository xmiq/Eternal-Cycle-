# Capability Assessment

## Purpose

This document defines how GMs, players, characters, institutions, and other observers assess capability without a universal character level, combat rating, power score, or mandatory total.

A Capability Assessment is always made **for a stated question under stated conditions from a stated information position**. It may compare one character with a task, hazard, opponent, institution, team, or another character. Its conclusion is useful only within that scope.

## Core Rule

Assess capability through descriptive Capability Profiles covering relevant Development, access, embodiment, reliability, context, support, and evidence. Do not add unlike qualities into one authoritative number.

The central assessment question is:

> What result is being attempted, what can each relevant participant currently bring to it, under which conditions, and how certain is that conclusion?

Power can matter greatly without answering every question. A character may be physically dominant, professionally unqualified, magically suppressed, politically powerless, perfectly prepared for one opponent, or incomparable with someone pursuing a different objective.

## Terms

### Capability Assessment

A scoped, evidence-based judgment about what a character or group can presently attempt, express, sustain, or accomplish under specified conditions.

### Assessment Scope

The objective, participants, place, time, constraints, available preparation, and relevant consequences to which an assessment applies.

### Capability Profile

A concise description of the dimensions relevant to an Assessment Scope, including strengths, limits, dependencies, conditions, and uncertainty. It is not a complete biography or universal rating.

### Evidence State

How well a claim is supported. Useful states are **demonstrated**, **corroborated**, **inferred**, **claimed**, **outdated**, **disputed**, and **unknown**. They are descriptions rather than numerical confidence values.

### Known Capability

A capability for which the assessor has usable evidence within the present scope. Knowledge may still be incomplete or wrong about limits and conditions.

### Hidden Capability

A real capability, dependency, weakness, resource, or condition unavailable to a particular observer. Hidden from one observer does not mean hidden from the GM or unknowable in principle.

### Comparative Finding

A scoped conclusion about the relationship between capabilities for a stated objective. It is not a permanent rank between people.

### Institutional Measure

A rank, certification, class, grade, stage, licence, threat category, or test result created for a defined institutional purpose. It remains useful within its evidence, jurisdiction, and assumptions and imperfect outside them.

## Three Assessment Views

### Factual View

The GM records what is actually established by canon and current conditions, including hidden capabilities and uncertain mechanics. Even the Factual View should distinguish known facts from unresolved or probabilistic outcomes. It is not permission to predetermine a result.

### Observer View

An in-world person or institution assesses from available signs, records, testimony, tests, biases, models, and incentives. Observer assessments may be honestly mistaken, manipulated, outdated, culturally narrow, or correct for the wrong reason.

### Player-Facing View

Players receive information their characters can reasonably obtain, plus clear framing needed for meaningful choice. The GM should communicate evidence, visible risks, and uncertainty without revealing every secret. Later outcomes should be explainable by prior clues and consistent causality.

These views must not be silently conflated. An institution's bronze rank is not the GM's declaration of total ability, and the GM's knowledge of a hidden counter is not automatically available to a character.

## Start With Scope

An assessment should name:

1. **Objective:** defeat, survive, escape, delay, rescue, persuade, diagnose, build, govern, research, travel, or another concrete result.
2. **Participants:** individuals, teams, institutions, creatures, hazards, and third parties whose actions matter.
3. **Conditions:** terrain, weather, law, magic, time pressure, distance, visibility, social setting, and available information.
4. **Preparation:** what may be selected, learned, built, negotiated, positioned, or rehearsed beforehand.
5. **Resources and support:** tools, allies, infrastructure, authority, supply, contracts, and environmental advantages.
6. **Current state:** injury, fatigue, morale, suppression, obligations, damaged equipment, and expended resources.
7. **Success and consequence:** what counts as success, what costs are acceptable, and what happens after the immediate result.

Changing one of these may change the finding without changing anyone's Development.

## The Six Layers in Assessment

Every profile should separate the shared [six capability layers](DEVELOPMENT_SYSTEM.md#the-six-layer-capability-model).

| Layer | Assessment question |
| --- | --- |
| **Persistent Potential** | What relevant retained capacity or familiarity genuinely exists? |
| **Current Access** | What can the present incarnation reach, recall, invoke, or relearn now? |
| **Embodied Expression** | What can the current body, species, health, environment, and available route perform? |
| **Practised Reliability** | What remains consistent under pressure, fatigue, variation, disruption, and consequence? |
| **Contextual Effectiveness** | How useful is that expression for this objective, matchup, preparation, toolset, and team? |
| **World Recognition** | What do relevant observers believe, certify, permit, fear, reward, or misunderstand? |

A high value in one layer cannot stand in for another. Extensive retained mastery with no current access is not immediate capability. Spectacular one-time expression is not proof of reliability. Public recognition is not proof of accuracy.

## Profile Dimensions

The following dimensions are a menu, not a mandatory scorecard. Use only those relevant to the Assessment Scope and describe them qualitatively.

### Potential

What routes could be developed or restored with time, access, compatibility, and effort? Potential matters for training and long horizons, but not as current performance.

### Access

Which relevant Skills, knowledge, memories, powers, permissions, senses, tools, and routes can be reached now? Note triggers, suppression, consent, and activation requirements.

### Expression

What output can presently be produced, at what scale, through which embodiment, and with which constraints? Separate ordinary capability from temporary enhancement and external support.

### Reliability

How consistently can the capability be used under relevant pressure, fatigue, uncertainty, variation, or interference? Distinguish rehearsed conditions from adaptable use.

### Adaptability

How well can the participant diagnose change, improvise, transfer principles, recover from error, and choose a new route? Adaptability is context-specific and does not make every specialist obsolete.

### Endurance

How long or how often can the relevant activity be sustained, recovered, supplied, and repeated? Use the appropriate physical, magical, cognitive, social, logistical, or institutional form rather than one universal stamina pool.

### Preparation

What information, positioning, planning, rehearsal, construction, diplomacy, or contingency work has actually been completed? Potential preparation is not current preparation.

### Compatibility

How do body, Skill, tool, environment, magic, culture, authority, objective, and opposition correspond? Name counters and incompatibilities rather than applying a vague matchup bonus.

### Support

Which people, tools, institutions, resources, environments, and agreements contribute? Record agency, availability, interruption, maintenance, and ownership.

### Consequence

What costs, exposure, injury, strain, obligation, collateral change, political response, ecological effect, or future vulnerability follows success or failure? Capability to achieve a result is not capability to absorb every consequence.

Other dimensions may be added when a narrow system needs them. They must remain scoped and cannot be summed into a mandatory total.

## Evidence and Uncertainty

### Evidence Sources

Evidence may include:

- direct observation under relevant conditions;
- repeated demonstrations across variation;
- credible records or witnesses;
- physical traces, tools, injuries, and environmental effects;
- formal tests with known scope;
- verified Skill, profession, species, magical, or institutional records;
- self-report, rumor, reputation, prophecy, or hostile intelligence;
- Soul Echo, Archive, Resonance, Title, or other supernatural information under its own limits.

The source affects what the evidence can establish. A crater demonstrates destructive effect but may not reveal preparation time, cost, accuracy, repeatability, source, or who caused it.

### Evidence Quality

- **Demonstrated:** directly shown under substantially relevant conditions.
- **Corroborated:** supported by independent evidence that agrees on the material claim.
- **Inferred:** a reasonable conclusion from signs, analogies, or partial tests.
- **Claimed:** asserted by an interested or unverified source.
- **Outdated:** once supported but possibly changed through injury, training, Reincarnation, equipment, law, or time.
- **Disputed:** credible evidence or interpretations conflict.
- **Unknown:** available evidence does not support a responsible conclusion.

Evidence states may differ by dimension. A fighter's force may be demonstrated while endurance is inferred and hidden magic remains unknown.

### Communicating Uncertainty

The GM should name the source of uncertainty:

- missing information;
- unreliable witness;
- hidden capability or weakness;
- unfamiliar species or magic;
- changing environment;
- untested pressure or duration;
- unclear objective;
- uncertain consent, morale, or institutional response;
- several plausible causal models.

Useful uncertainty gives players something to investigate, prepare for, or choose around. False precision conceals assumptions; arbitrary surprise invalidates informed choice.

## Comparative Findings

Use a finding only for the stated objective and conditions.

### Clearly Superior

Available evidence shows one side dominates the relevant capability with no known practical counter, bottleneck, or uncertainty likely to reverse the comparison under the stated conditions. This does not imply superiority outside the scope.

### Generally Advantaged

One side has several material advantages and is favored across ordinary variations, but preparation, mistakes, hidden information, costs, or a specific counter could change the result.

### Situationally Advantaged

One side is favored because of a particular environment, objective, tool, matchup, timing, support arrangement, or prepared condition. Removing that condition may reverse or dissolve the finding.

### Uncertain

Evidence, reliability, hidden information, conditions, or interacting variables do not support a responsible directional conclusion. Uncertain does not mean equal.

### Mismatched

The participants apply capability through sharply asymmetric routes, objectives, ranges, timescales, or domains. Direct comparison obscures the actual interaction, so the assessment should identify which side can impose its preferred conditions.

### Incomparable

No shared objective or meaningful common dimension has been stated. The assessor must refine the question rather than rank unlike capabilities.

Comparative findings are not probabilities, encounter levels, moral judgments, or guarantees. They summarize current evidence and causal advantage.

## Matchups, Preparation, and Teams

### Matchups and Counters

A narrow counter may overcome broader Development when it interrupts a dependency, prevents expression, changes the objective, or exploits a real incompatibility. The counter must exist causally and still be delivered, sustained, and protected.

A victory through a counter demonstrates that interaction. It does not prove the victor is universally superior or that the defeated character lacks mastery.

### Preparation and Information

Preparation can change conditions before direct conflict begins. Maps, research, diplomacy, traps, rituals, legal orders, supply, evacuation, equipment choice, and timing may be the decisive capabilities.

Preparation must be actually completed and remains vulnerable to bad information, interruption, cost, exposure, and changing conditions. Intelligence is not automatically correct merely because it is detailed.

### Team Composition

Assess a group through [Group Capability](DEVELOPMENT_INTERACTIONS.md#group-capability): role coverage, communication, trust, leadership, tools, supply, timing, and member condition. Do not total personal ranks.

A team of modest specialists may be generally advantaged for a complex objective while vulnerable to separation. One exceptional individual may dominate a narrow task while leaving other dependencies uncovered.

### Morale and Objectives

Willingness, fear, loyalty, legitimacy, desperation, rules of engagement, and acceptable cost affect what participants choose and sustain. They do not rewrite physical or magical capability, and social influence does not become mind control.

An opponent may retreat despite being capable of winning, or succeed at delay while losing a duel. The assessment must use the real objective.

## Institutional Measures

Institutional ranks are useful when they state what they measure.

A guild grade may summarize tested procedures, a licence may establish legal permission, a military rank may establish command office, a spell circle may describe one school's curriculum, and a threat category may guide evacuation. None is a universal character level.

For every Institutional Measure, record:

- issuing authority and jurisdiction;
- intended decision or audience;
- tested evidence and conditions;
- dimensions not tested;
- date and current validity;
- assumptions about species, tools, teams, environment, and law;
- whether the measure grants access or merely describes evidence;
- known incentives, biases, and error patterns.

Institutions may preserve imperfect measures because they are fast, administratively useful, politically convenient, or adequate for common cases. Their errors should follow their methods rather than existing only to surprise players.

## Reusable Capability Profile

Use this concise format when an assessment matters:

```markdown
### Capability Profile: <subject>

Scope: <objective, participants, conditions, preparation, success>
Perspective: <factual, observer, or player-facing>

Relevant capability:
- <Track or source>: <current expression and evidence state>

Access and embodiment:
- <available routes, conditions, incompatibilities, suppression>

Reliability and endurance:
- <pressure, variation, duration, recovery>

Preparation and support:
- <tools, allies, institutions, information, environment>

Key dependencies and bottlenecks:
- <what limits or could interrupt the result>

Uncertainty:
- <unknown, hidden, disputed, or outdated information>

Comparative finding: <label, scope, and causal reason>
Consequences: <costs and likely follow-on effects>
```

Omit irrelevant headings in routine play. Never fill the template merely to create administrative completeness.

## Assessment Procedure

1. State the Assessment Scope and real objective.
2. Choose the Factual, Observer, or Player-Facing View.
3. Identify relevant Development Tracks and effect owners.
4. Separate the six capability layers.
5. Select only profile dimensions that can affect the objective.
6. Record current state, preparation, support, tools, environment, and team composition.
7. Identify dependencies, bottlenecks, counters, incompatibilities, and possible substitutions.
8. Assign Evidence States to material claims.
9. Name hidden, disputed, outdated, and unknown information from this perspective.
10. Choose a Comparative Finding only if a meaningful comparison exists.
11. Explain the finding through causal advantages rather than a total score.
12. Reassess when conditions, information, participants, injuries, resources, or objectives materially change.

## Worked Examples

### One Duel Does Not Establish Universal Superiority

A spear specialist defeats a broadly accomplished veteran in a narrow courtyard. The specialist controls range, has practised that geometry, and carries the suitable weapon. The result demonstrates a Situationally Advantaged matchup and successful execution.

It does not prove the specialist is the better scout, commander, survivor, negotiator, or fighter in every terrain. The veteran's defeat does not erase broad mastery, and a rematch in dense woodland may have a different finding.

### Hidden Counter

An armored fire monster appears Clearly Superior to an exhausted hunting party in open ground. The party has no visible weapon capable of penetrating its shell. One hunter secretly carries a single cold-binding seal learned from a local shrine.

The observers' conclusion is reasonable from their evidence; the GM's Factual View records the hidden counter and its delivery requirements. If the seal is revealed, readied, and applied to a cracked joint, the matchup may become Situationally Advantaged rather than retroactively proving the first assessment foolish.

### Injured Master and Healthy Novice

A sword master with a damaged shoulder retains deep Skill and judgment but cannot safely generate normal reach or repeated force. A healthy novice has better current condition and endurance but poor timing.

For teaching, diagnosis, and one controlled exchange, the master is Generally Advantaged. For prolonged fighting with the injured arm, the finding may be Uncertain or favor the novice. Skill mastery and current expression remain separate.

### Ritual Mage and Ambush Predator

A ritual mage can protect a village from a migrating leviathan with a day of preparation, assistants, survey markers, and stored power. The same mage is vulnerable to a fast predator at close range before the ritual is established.

Calling either participant "stronger" hides the decisive timescale and dependencies. The matchup is Mismatched until the objective and starting conditions are stated.

### Combat and Political Capability Differ

A dragon can destroy a fortress gate but lacks legal standing, local language, trusted intermediaries, and knowledge of succession law. A physically frail minister cannot survive the dragon's attack but can assemble a coalition, redirect supply, and invalidate a claimant's authority.

The dragon is Clearly Superior at direct destruction. The minister is Generally Advantaged at the defined coalition objective. The two are Incomparable without a shared task.

### Team Composition Beats a Personal Total

A rescue team combines a durable burrower, a healer, a mapmaker, and a quiet coordinator. None is individually exceptional in combat. In a collapsed mine, their senses, access routes, triage, navigation, and communication cover the objective's dependencies.

A famous duelist is individually more dangerous but lacks excavation sense, medical competence, and route knowledge. The team is Generally Advantaged for rescue; the duelist may be Clearly Superior in a duel. Neither conclusion is contradictory.

### Guild Rank Is Useful and Imperfect

A guild's Silver Smith examination tests standardized alloys, tool safety, documentation, and three common repairs. A self-taught monster smith fails the written portion and receives no rank, yet has extraordinary judgment with living chitin that the exam never tests.

Silver rank remains useful evidence for regulated human workshops. It is weak evidence for chitin craft and no proof of universal superiority. The monster's specialty likewise does not establish knowledge of standard bridge fasteners or guild law.

### Threat Category Misleads Outside Its Purpose

A city classifies monsters by expected evacuation area after uncontrolled attack. A large territorial beast receives a higher category than a tiny mimic. The category is useful for emergency logistics.

For infiltration, assassination, or detection, the mimic may be more dangerous. The measure was not false; using it as a universal combat or intelligence rank was the error.

### Reputation Error With Real Consequences

A reincarnated ruler carries a feared Soul Title but has no current office, army, or demonstrated command. Witnesses interpret the title as proof that kingdoms inevitably obey the child.

World Recognition may open doors, provoke assassination, or attract followers. The Capability Profile records the reputation and symbolic pressure while marking current authority and leadership reliability unknown. Belief changes the world without making the belief factually accurate.

## Safeguards

- Never sum profile dimensions into a mandatory overall score.
- Never compare without naming an objective and conditions.
- Never treat Potential, Access, Expression, Reliability, Effectiveness, and Recognition as interchangeable.
- Never let an institutional rank silently become canonical truth outside its scope.
- Never infer all capability from one victory, defeat, spectacle, title, reputation, or test.
- Never use hidden information to invalidate fair evidence retroactively; preserve clues and causality.
- Never reveal hidden capability merely because the GM possesses a Factual View.
- Never treat Uncertain as equal or use it to avoid a conclusion supported by evidence.
- Never let combat capability stand for professional, social, political, magical, or moral worth.
- Never make a counter automatic; assess access, delivery, cost, and reliability.
- Never treat group success as one leader's personal power.

## Scope Boundaries

This document does not define:

- universal combat math, encounter budgets, probabilities, or challenge ratings;
- a mandatory character sheet or complete inventory of every capability;
- the exact tests, ranks, licences, grades, or threat categories of future institutions;
- action resolution, damage, morale, reputation, or information-detection formulas;
- permission for players or characters to know hidden GM information;
- guaranteed outcomes from Comparative Findings.

Later systems may introduce local measures for narrow purposes. Every measure must state its scope, evidence, assumptions, and omissions and may not become a universal character level by convenience.
