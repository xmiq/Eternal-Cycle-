# Skill Development

## Purpose

This document defines how learned capabilities are acquired, practised, adapted, retained, relearned, and assessed across different bodies and progression trees. It establishes the shared Development rules for skills without implementing the complete human and monster Skill Engine reserved for Phase 3.

## Core Rule

A Skill is a bounded learned capability that becomes reliable through meaningful practice, feedback, adaptation, and use. A skill label records a developed pattern; it does not create the capability, provide a universal package of statistics, or waive embodiment, knowledge, tools, access, and context.

Skill XP and qualifying Skill Imprints may persist through Reincarnation as Persistent Potential. Current Access, Embodied Expression, and Practised Reliability must still be established in the present life. Human and monster skill trees begin separately, and retained experience crosses that boundary only through an explicit, body-compatible translation route.

## Terms

### Skill

A bounded learned capability organized around a recognizable function, method, or family of related actions. A Skill has evidence of acquisition, requirements for expression, limits, and conditions under which it remains reliable.

### Technique

A specific method or execution pattern used within a Skill. Several techniques may serve one Skill, and one technique may depend on several supporting capabilities.

### Habit

A repeated behavior or response that has become easy to initiate. A habit may be useful, harmful, automatic, or context-blind and is not necessarily a Skill or persistent Soul Imprint.

### Discipline

A sustained practice that organizes methods, standards, training, judgment, identity, and often a community or tradition around related capabilities.

### Competency

A practical capability demonstrated to a defined standard and context. Competency may be narrow, institutionally assessed, or current-life only without representing deep mastery.

### Mastery

Deep, adaptable, and reliable understanding within a bounded Skill or technique, demonstrated across meaningful variation. Mastery is contextual and does not imply universal superiority, perfection, or authority outside its scope.

### Specialisation

A deliberately narrowed area within a Skill or Discipline in which concentrated practice creates unusual precision, efficiency, judgment, or adaptation at the cost of narrower transfer.

### Skill XP

Persistent developmental familiarity earned within a specific Skill through legitimate adaptation and meaningful use. Skill XP is not a spendable currency, universal experience pool, current proficiency score, or substitute for present expression.

### Skill Imprint

The persistent Soul Imprint that preserves a Skill's legitimately earned structure and Skill XP after Final Death. It may remain inaccessible or incompatible in a later body.

### Adaptive Skill

A Skill that emerges when repeated meaningful behavior develops a stable, distinct capability not adequately represented by an existing Skill in the current progression tree.

### Composite Skill

A coordinated capability that depends on several developed Skills or Tracks while retaining a distinct purpose and practice. Its components do not disappear merely because their use is integrated.

### Foundational Skill

A capability that supplies methods, perception, control, or judgment required by one or more later Skills.

### Dependent Skill

A Skill whose reliable expression requires a named foundational capability, body structure, tool, resource, permission, or other established prerequisite.

### Transferable Principle

An abstract relationship or method that can support relearning in another body, toolset, culture, or progression tree when a valid functional correspondence and present practice exist.

### Body-Dependent Technique

A technique whose expression relies on particular anatomy, senses, proportions, natural weapons, magical organs, or movement modes.

## Skills and the Six Layers

| Layer | Skill question |
| --- | --- |
| **Persistent Potential** | What Skill XP, Skill Imprints, principles, and source history legitimately survived? |
| **Current Access** | What methods, memories, instincts, or training routes can this incarnation presently reach and understand? |
| **Embodied Expression** | What can the current body, progression tree, tools, language, magic, and environment actually execute? |
| **Practised Reliability** | What can the incarnation perform consistently under pressure, fatigue, uncertainty, variation, and consequence? |
| **Contextual Effectiveness** | How useful is the Skill for this objective, matchup, team, environment, and preparation? |
| **World Recognition** | What ranks, certificates, reputations, traditions, or observers recognize the capability, accurately or otherwise? |

A former master may have high Persistent Potential but no Current Access. A knowledgeable student may have Current Access and poor execution. A reliable technique may be ineffective against one counter. An institution may certify a Skill that has rusted or refuse to recognize a capable outsider.

## Distinguishing Related Forms of Capability

### Skill and Technique

A Skill organizes a bounded capability; a technique is one way of expressing it. Swordsmanship may contain cuts, guards, footwork, distance judgment, maintenance habits, and tactical transitions. One named cut is not the whole Skill.

Learning a technique does not automatically develop every supporting element. A copied motion may remain fragile until perception, timing, correction, and pressure testing are integrated.

### Skill and Habit

Habits reduce initiation effort through repetition. They may support reliability, but repetition can also automate an error or response that fails outside familiar conditions.

A habit becomes evidence of Skill Development only where it serves a capability and survives relevant variation. A habit becomes a persistent Skill Imprint only when the Skill system recognizes meaningful integration.

### Skill and Discipline

A Discipline may include many Skills, values, training practices, tests, institutions, and bodies of theory. It is broader than one technique and may overlap Profession, Social, Magical, or Species Development.

Belonging to a Discipline grants no automatic mastery. A tradition may preserve good instruction while also preserving obsolete assumptions or cultural bias.

### Skill and Competency

Competency is a demonstrated threshold for a stated purpose. A novice medic may be competent to clean a wound while unqualified to diagnose a magical disease. A pilot may be competent in calm weather and unreliable in a storm.

Competency labels must state the task, conditions, evidence, and authority assigning them. They do not form a universal ladder across unrelated Skills.

### Skill and Mastery

Mastery combines refined execution, understanding, adaptability, diagnosis, efficiency, and Practised Reliability within a bounded scope. It is shown by handling meaningful variation, not by repeating one ideal demonstration.

A master can fail through poor information, a counter, incompatible embodiment, an unfamiliar tool, injury, or an objective outside the Skill's scope. One failure does not erase mastery, and one victory does not prove it.

### Knowledge Without Execution

A character may understand terminology, principles, sequences, risks, and likely outcomes without being able to perform the Skill. Archive Records, books, observation, Echo testimony, and instruction often begin here.

Knowledge can improve practice and error correction. It does not supply physical calibration, magical access, species structures, tacit judgment, or reliability under pressure.

### Execution Without Theory

A character may perform a learned pattern reliably without explaining why it works. Embodied apprenticeship, imitation, species instinct, cultural transmission, or survival can create such capability.

Limited theory may make transfer, diagnosis, teaching, improvisation, and response to unfamiliar failure more difficult. It does not make the demonstrated execution unreal.

## Skill Structure

### Foundational Skills

Foundational Skills supply capabilities on which other Skills rely. Examples include balance for acrobatics, heat judgment for smithing, pulse control for a ritual tradition, or spatial mapping for aerial hunting.

Foundational does not mean universally basic. A spider's web-tension reading may be foundational in one monster tree and inaccessible in another. A written magical notation may be foundational to one school and irrelevant to an oral tradition.

### Dependent Skills

A Dependent Skill names its prerequisites. These may include:

- a Foundational Skill;
- a Physical Dimension or species structure;
- a tool, material, language, teacher, or safe environment;
- magical sensitivity, affinity, authority, or channel;
- legal, social, religious, or institutional access;
- another Skill at a demonstrated local standard.

Losing a prerequisite may suppress expression without erasing the Skill Imprint. A surgeon without hands retains knowledge and potential while needing an accommodation, translated method, or different role.

### Composite Skills

A Composite Skill coordinates several capabilities toward one recurring function. Ritual casting might combine notation, timing, material preparation, magical control, group coordination, and error diagnosis.

The composite exists only when the coordination itself has been meaningfully practised. Possessing every component does not automatically create the integrated Skill, and developing the composite does not duplicate XP into every component for the same adaptation.

### Specialisations

A Specialisation narrows context in exchange for refinement. A swordsman may specialize in fighting within tunnels, a tracker in aerial spoor, or a diplomat in oath-bound monster councils.

Specialisation may create exceptional Contextual Effectiveness without increasing every use of the parent Skill. Its limits must be visible enough that a narrow specialty does not become a disguised universal bonus.

### Transferable Principles

Transferable Principles may include distance, rhythm, leverage, feedback, sequence, information control, energy conservation, threat prediction, material response, or social framing.

A principle supports a new learning route only when:

1. it was genuinely understood or integrated in the source Skill;
2. it addresses a comparable function in the present task;
3. the current body or system has a valid means of expression;
4. differences and translation loss are identified;
5. present experimentation and practice establish reliability.

Analogy is evidence for a learning hypothesis, not proof that two Skills are interchangeable.

### Body-Dependent Techniques

Body-Dependent Techniques require their actual structures. Web-building with spinnerets, venom metering through glands, wing-stall recovery, echolocation through specialized organs, and a human sword grip cannot be copied literally into every body.

Persistent Potential may preserve timing, pattern, or judgment. The present incarnation must either possess a compatible route, use a valid tool or magical substitute, develop a translated technique under its current tree, or leave the old expression latent.

## Acquiring Skills

Skill acquisition is demonstrated through changed capability. Different sources contribute different things.

### Practice

Deliberate practice repeats a relevant operation with feedback, correction, recovery, and variation. Useful practice targets an actual limitation rather than merely accumulating repetitions.

### Instruction

Teachers, texts, schools, traditions, system interfaces, and Soul Echoes may supply explanations, demonstrations, exercises, safety, and correction. Instruction improves the learning route; it does not perform the adaptation for the learner.

### Observation

Observation may reveal sequence, timing, tools, errors, intent, or a candidate principle. What can be learned depends on viewpoint, senses, prior knowledge, speed, concealment, and whether the crucial work is externally visible.

Witnessing a technique once may create a clue or imitation attempt. It does not award the Skill, its XP, hidden prerequisites, or reliable execution.

### Experimentation

Experimentation develops Skill when the learner forms a useful question, varies a method, observes consequences, and integrates the result. Random variation without interpretation may produce accidents rather than capability.

### Imitation

Imitation can provide an initial motor, social, magical, or procedural pattern. It is limited by the model's visibility and the imitator's body, understanding, and feedback.

Successful imitation in one rehearsed condition does not prove adaptability or Mastery.

### Survival and Hardship

Danger may demand fast adaptation, expose hidden errors, and create meaningful consequences. Survival does not automatically grant a Skill. Panic, luck, allies, or raw physical capability may carry the event without the learner integrating a repeatable method.

### Reflection

Reflection compares intention, action, feedback, and result. It can improve understanding and prepare correction. Reflection without later expression may develop knowledge while leaving execution unchanged.

### Repeated Meaningful Use

Using a capability toward real goals introduces variation, responsibility, and consequences. Repetition remains meaningful while it continues to demand judgment, adaptation, or reliable performance rather than replaying a solved action.

### Mentorship

Mentorship combines instruction with sustained observation of the learner, individualized correction, staged responsibility, and access to a practice community. It may accelerate development without guaranteeing loyalty, institutional rank, or identical style.

### Cultural Transmission

Songs, stories, rituals, games, work routines, apprenticeships, communal hunts, and species practices may transmit Skills without formal schools. Cultural familiarity can hide how much training a capability required; it does not make the Skill innate or universal.

### Soul Echo Guidance

A Soul Echo may provide personal context, warnings, demonstration memory, or an Overlay under its own rules. The Echo remains limited by its source life, Presence, consent, and the current body's expression.

Letting an Echo perform through Delegation does not automatically develop the current incarnation. Skill growth requires the present self's participation, feedback, understanding, and integration.

## Skill XP and Qualitative Proficiency

Skill XP records legitimately earned developmental familiarity within a specific Skill. It may be recognized when practice or use produces a durable change in one or more of these dimensions:

- **understanding:** the learner can explain, predict, or diagnose more of the capability;
- **execution:** the learner can perform a broader or more demanding expression;
- **efficiency:** the learner uses less time, effort, material, risk, or attention for comparable results;
- **precision:** the learner controls outcome and variation more closely;
- **reliability:** performance remains stable under relevant pressure and disruption;
- **adaptability:** the learner can respond when conditions, tools, bodies, or opponents change;
- **integration:** the Skill coordinates coherently with relevant bodies, tools, other Skills, and responsibilities.

These dimensions are evidence, not seven universal sub-scores. A Skill can be deep in one and weak in another. A brilliant improviser may lack consistent precision; a reliable craftsperson may have narrow theoretical language.

Skill XP is not awarded for labels, kills, time, suffering, observation, or repetition by themselves. It does not move freely between Skills or buy unrelated techniques.

Exact XP formulas, numerical progression curves, and tree-specific ranks belong to later systems. Qualitative records should state what changed and under what conditions.

## Local Skill Ranks and Mastery Evidence

Schools, species systems, guilds, gods, interfaces, and game subsystems may use local proficiency ranks. A valid rank states:

1. the Skill or technique being assessed;
2. the authority or process assigning the rank;
3. the evidence and conditions tested;
4. which proficiency dimensions it covers;
5. what tools, body, environment, or assistance were permitted;
6. when reassessment, maintenance, or loss of recognition applies;
7. what the rank does not claim.

Ranks may be useful without being metaphysically exact. One school may emphasize formal precision, another survival adaptability, and a monster lineage instinctive integration.

Mastery should be recognized when the practitioner repeatedly demonstrates deep understanding or tacit judgment, adaptable execution, reliable performance, diagnosis of failure, and control of consequences within a bounded scope. Teaching may be evidence but is not mandatory; some bodies or minds cannot teach through the observer's preferred language.

There is no universal number of repetitions, duels, kills, years, techniques, or students that establishes Mastery.

## Breadth, Depth, and Specialisation

### Skill Breadth

Breadth may mean many distinct Skills or wide coverage within one Skill. It supports flexibility and transfer but demands attention, maintenance, and opportunities for relevant practice.

Accumulating weak Skill labels does not create equivalent breadth. The GM should record only capabilities with meaningful evidence and surface only those relevant to play.

### Mastery Depth

Mastery Depth is refinement within a bounded capability. It may include subtle perception, efficient action, robust correction, adaptation under pressure, and an ability to recognize when the Skill should not be used.

Depth does not make the Skill universally applicable or remove dependencies.

### Specialisation and Generalisation

A specialist can defeat or outperform a broader practitioner inside the specialist's narrow conditions. A generalist can respond when the objective changes, tools fail, or several moderate problems interact.

Neither approach is a universal advancement path. Development follows what is actually practised and integrated.

## Improvisation and Reliability Under Pressure

Improvisation is not random novelty. It applies developed principles to a changed problem while monitoring feedback and consequence.

Reliable improvisation depends on:

- sufficient understanding or tacit pattern recognition;
- accurate perception of the changed condition;
- control of foundational techniques;
- time and resources appropriate to the risk;
- experience diagnosing failure;
- willingness to narrow or abandon an unsafe attempt.

Pressure may reveal Practised Reliability or expose a dependency that safe practice concealed. Pressure does not automatically improve performance or grant more Skill XP. Panic, fatigue, injury, social stakes, and divided attention may narrow expression.

## Plateaus, Maintenance, Rust, and Regression

### Plateaus

A Skill Plateau occurs when the current methods and problems no longer produce meaningful adaptation. Causes may include:

- solved exercises and predictable opponents;
- poor feedback or a misunderstood error;
- a weak Foundational Skill;
- a Physical, Magical, Species, Social, or tool bottleneck;
- avoidance of unfamiliar conditions;
- obsolete theory or a bad habit;
- insufficient recovery, attention, or meaningful use;
- the current body's actual limit.

Changing the problem intelligently may reopen growth. Merely making it lethal does not.

### Maintenance

Maintenance preserves calibration and reliability through suitable use. It may be necessary even when it creates little new Skill XP.

Maintenance needs differ. A language, surgical method, venom dose, flight maneuver, and oath ritual may decay in different ways and on different timescales.

### Rust

Rust reduces recall speed, timing, confidence, precision, or reliability through disuse or changed conditions. The Skill Imprint and much of its structure may remain.

Compatible retained potential may allow rapid recovery, but present rehearsal and calibration are still required.

### Regression

Regression is a material loss of current capability because supporting development, access, health, tools, language, magical law, or other prerequisites changed. It should be traced to its actual cause rather than treated as an arbitrary rank decrease.

Recovery may restore an old expression, create an accommodation, or develop a translated one. Repeated loss and restoration cannot be farmed for endless XP.

## Relearning Across Incarnations

Relearning uses the shared Recognition, Access, Calibration, Reconstruction, Stabilization, and Extension sequence from the [Development System](DEVELOPMENT_SYSTEM.md#retained-progression-and-relearning).

Retained Skill XP may:

- reveal useful principles and common errors;
- improve exercise selection and diagnosis;
- speed conceptual reacquisition;
- help recognize a compatible teacher, tool, or method;
- shorten calibration when the new body and tree are close;
- support a translated route after meaningful experimentation.

It does not:

- grant immediate current proficiency or a starting rank;
- perform practice through an Echo or Soul Space;
- restore missing anatomy, magical access, materials, language, or social permission;
- make obsolete knowledge correct in a changed Age;
- convert every technique into the present progression tree;
- guarantee that former Mastery can be expressed in one life.

Relearning can surpass the source Skill when the current incarnation tests old assumptions and develops a better present method. Prior lives are a starting map, not a command to reproduce the past.

## Human and Monster Skill Trees

Human and monster skill trees begin separately because they organize different bodies, instincts, cultures, systems, and developmental possibilities.

A human Skill may assume:

- hands, language, standardized tools, written theory, institutions, or deliberate curricula;
- human social categories and legal permissions;
- techniques shaped by human reach, senses, movement, and lifespan.

A monster Skill may assume:

- natural weapons, metamorphosis, nonhuman senses, ecological roles, inherited cues, or species communication;
- growth through hunting, migration, colony life, territorial practice, or transformation;
- cognition and culture that do not map cleanly onto human schools.

Neither tree is inherently more complex or more advanced. Intelligent monsters may have formal traditions, and humans may learn through embodied practice.

### Bounded Crossover Through Reincarnation

A crossover candidate requires:

1. a genuine retained Skill Imprint or integrated principle;
2. Current Access to enough of the source to guide development;
3. a present body and tree with a functional correspondence;
4. meaningful present-life experimentation and practice;
5. any required Species, Physical, Magical, Social, or evolution access;
6. an explicit result recognized by the receiving tree;
7. clear limits, dependencies, translation loss, and maintenance.

The result may be a close analogue, a hybrid Skill, an Adaptive Skill, a tool-mediated practice, or no available expression. Reincarnation does not convert the entire source tree or make every prior Skill universally purchasable.

Detailed human trees, monster trees, crossover unlocks, and hidden Skill requirements belong to Phase 3.

## Adaptive Skill Emergence

An Adaptive Skill may emerge when a character repeatedly combines behavior, context, feedback, and purpose into a stable capability that the current tree does not already represent adequately.

Emergence requires:

1. **Repeated meaningful behavior:** the action recurs under genuine use and variation rather than a staged loop;
2. **Coherent function:** the behavior solves a recognizable class of problems;
3. **Integrated method:** perception, execution, and correction have become more than an accidental combination;
4. **Distinct value:** the result is not merely a renamed existing Skill or one technique inside it;
5. **Tree compatibility:** the current human, monster, species, magical, or other progression structure permits the capability;
6. **Demonstrated reliability:** the result works often enough under relevant conditions to be more than a lucky improvisation.

Example: repeatedly throwing knives while shaping wind does not guarantee **Windknife**. If the practitioner develops a stable method for reading airflow, coupling release timing to magic, controlling spin, correcting drift, and using it under varied conditions, the current magical or Skill tree may later recognize an Adaptive Skill.

Adaptive emergence does not create an ability on demand from a desired name. Exact naming, interface behavior, rarity, evolution, and tree placement belong to Phase 3.

## Composite Development and Future Skill Fusion

Composite use is common. Skill fusion is exceptional and remains a later Skill Engine rule.

A future fusion candidate must at minimum have:

- developed component Skills with genuine provenance;
- a meaningful functional overlap rather than convenient labels;
- repeated integrated use under varied conditions;
- a new capability that cannot be represented adequately as ordinary coordination;
- compatible embodiment and progression-tree permission;
- retained dependencies, costs, tradeoffs, and failure states;
- no duplicated XP, benefits, actions, or mastery from counting the same adaptation twice.

Until Phase 3 defines fusion, component Skills remain distinct and coordinated use creates no fused label or automatic evolution.

## Anti-Grinding and Anti-Farming Rules

- Repeating a solved action produces maintenance and diminishing returns rather than unlimited Skill XP.
- Witnessing, reading, copying, consuming, or naming a Skill does not grant it.
- Killing or defeating a skilled being does not transfer its Skill XP, techniques, or tree access.
- Survival, pain, danger, or Final Death does not grant a Skill merely because it was intense.
- Practising an incompatible motion cannot create missing anatomy or force a tree to accept it.
- A Skill label never grants a package of statistics, equipment, knowledge, or supporting Skills.
- Several labels for the same capability do not multiply XP or effects.
- Letting an Echo, teacher, summoned being, or automated tool perform does not count as the learner's practice.
- Arranged low-risk opponents and scripted demonstrations cannot establish pressure reliability indefinitely.
- Repeated loss and relearning do not duplicate retained XP.
- Adaptive Skills cannot be farmed by inventing names for trivial combinations.
- Composite Skills and future fusions cannot count the same adaptation once per component.
- Breadth does not create unlimited attention, active Skills, or simultaneous actions.

## Scope Boundaries

This document does not define:

- complete human or monster skill trees;
- universal skill categories, slots, rarity, or numerical levels;
- exact Skill XP formulas or advancement curves;
- named active, passive, hidden, ultimate, or conceptual Skills;
- complete adaptive-skill generation procedures;
- skill evolution, fusion, inheritance, copying, or deletion mechanics;
- class, profession, school, guild, or species advancement structures;
- combat, crafting, social, research, ritual, or travel resolution;
- magical laws, spell construction, species evolution, or body mutation;
- one universal Mastery threshold shared by every capability.

Phase 3 may define tree-specific mechanics inside these boundaries. It must preserve current embodiment, present-life practice, provenance, human and monster distinction, and the non-fungibility of Development Tracks.

## GM Procedure

When a character attempts to acquire, improve, retain, or translate a Skill:

1. name the exact Skill, technique, function, and Owning progression tree;
2. identify prerequisites, body structures, tools, knowledge, access, and supporting Tracks;
3. identify the practice, instruction, observation, experimentation, use, or mentorship involved;
4. state the feedback, adaptation, and evidence of changed capability;
5. separate knowledge, execution, reliability, adaptability, and World Recognition;
6. check repetition, diminishing returns, Plateaus, maintenance, rust, and regression;
7. record Skill XP only for legitimate Skill-specific development;
8. preserve any Skill Imprint without assuming present access or expression;
9. require functional correspondence and current-tree permission for cross-species translation;
10. test Adaptive or Composite claims against distinct function, integration, and reliability;
11. communicate proficiency through scoped evidence rather than a universal rank;
12. defer tree generation, fusion, evolution, and numerical formulas to Phase 3.

## Worked Examples

### Swordsmanship

A reincarnated sword master remembers guards and distance through an Advisor Echo. The current body is shorter, weaker, and left-handed. Persistent Skill XP improves error recognition, but the incarnation must rebuild grip, footwork, timing, endurance, and pressure reliability.

Mastery remains Persistent Potential until present expression is calibrated. Winning one duel does not restore every former technique or prove universal combat superiority.

### Tracking

A tracker learns spoor, weather, animal behavior, concealment, and inference through field use and mentorship. Reading one perfect footprint is a technique; reliable tracking requires adapting when signs are partial, false, old, or made by an unfamiliar species.

A wolf life's scent instinct may support attention, but a human nose cannot reproduce the old sensory resolution. Tools and present practice may create an analogical method.

### Web-Building

A spider monster develops web-building through spinneret control, tension judgment, material management, anchoring, and environmental feedback. The Skill is not innate merely because the species has spinnerets; hatchlings may begin with Current Access to simple patterns and still lack reliable construction.

A later human incarnation retains principles and Skill XP but needs thread, tools, hands, and a receiving human or hybrid tree. It does not grow spinnerets through memory.

### Venom Control

A venomous monster learns to meter dose, preserve reserves, choose delivery depth, avoid contaminating allies, and account for prey. Venom production is a species trait; control is a Skill dependent on the current glands, delivery anatomy, and sensory feedback.

A nonvenomous later body may preserve toxicology knowledge and timing while the active venom-control technique remains inaccessible.

### Diplomacy

A diplomat develops listening, framing, negotiation, cultural inquiry, and agreement design. These Skills support but do not replace relationships, legitimacy, reputation, language, or formal authority.

A polished formula that succeeds in one court may fail in a monster council whose commitments are expressed through territory and shared hunting. Adaptation, not the label **Diplomacy**, determines usefulness.

### Smithing

A smith combines heat judgment, hammer control, material diagnosis, tool care, and process planning. Individual techniques are Skills; the full responsibility and standards of the trade belong to Profession Development.

A reincarnated master may recognize bad heat immediately while lacking local tools, materials, strength, or knowledge of an Age's alloys. Theory and retained Skill XP accelerate experiments without creating a finished profession.

### Aerial Hunting

A winged predator develops stall recovery, wind reading, prey prediction, dive control, and safe disengagement. The Composite Skill depends on flight anatomy, senses, physical conditioning, and hunting judgment.

A terrestrial reincarnation may retain transferable principles about approach angles and target prediction. It cannot execute the aerial Skill without a valid flight route.

### Echolocation

A cave species may possess organs capable of producing and hearing useful signals. Echolocation Skill develops through signal choice, filtering, spatial interpretation, motion correction, and practice in varied environments.

Hearing an echo once does not grant spatial mastery. A later body with ordinary hearing might use clicks and tools for a narrow analogue only after real calibration and training.

### Ritual Casting

Ritual casting may coordinate notation, materials, timing, magical control, participant roles, error detection, and recovery. Knowing the words is not the Composite Skill, and one successful scripted ritual does not establish reliability.

Changed magical laws, missing materials, or an incompatible body may leave the retained Skill historically clear but currently unusable.

### Leadership

Leadership may use communication, planning, delegation, morale reading, teaching, and decision-making Skills. It is relational and contextual, not a passive aura or one generic Skill that commands obedience.

A war leader may have excellent tactical direction and poor administration. A former command Skill does not restore rank, trust, or willing followers after Reincarnation.

### Adaptive Windknife

A rooftop hunter repeatedly throws knives while shaping crosswinds. Early success comes from luck and raw magic. Over time, the hunter develops airflow perception, release timing, spin correction, safe limits, and reliable use in several weather conditions.

Only then may the receiving Skill and magic systems recognize a distinct Adaptive Skill. The name does not create it, and the result still depends on knives, wind access, body calibration, and practice.
