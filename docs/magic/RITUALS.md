# Rituals

## Purpose

This document defines magical rituals as structured procedures that distribute magical work across preparation, time, roles, materials, place, authority, relationships, infrastructure, and coordinated action. It establishes participation, substitution, sequence, integrity, interruption, failure, persistence, and safeguards without imposing one universal ceremonial form.

Rituals integrate [Mana](MANA.md), [Magical Affinities](MAGICAL_AFFINITIES.md), [Spell Formation](SPELL_FORMATION.md), [Magical Development](../progression/MAGICAL_DEVELOPMENT.md), the [Skill Engine](../skills/README.md), sources, embodiment, social structures, and current world law. They do not replace those systems.

## Core Rule

**A magical Ritual is a bounded, prepared procedure that coordinates differentiated dependencies and contributions across a Ritual Structure toward one magical purpose.**

A Ritual can enable an effect no participant can produce alone by arranging real support. It does not make that capability personal, waive missing requirements, duplicate the full result into every participant, or become powerful merely because it is elaborate.

A Ritual is not:

- every repeated, sacred, formal, communal, or symbolic act;
- automatically a large or slow spell;
- a universal substitute for Mana, Access, authority, Skill, embodiment, consent, or world law;
- proof that every participant understands or can reproduce the whole procedure;
- a method for pooling people into one ownerless resource;
- a way to turn pain, death, devotion, or sacrifice into generic fuel;
- a permanent capability carried intact through Reincarnation;
- automatically safe, true, moral, divine, or socially legitimate.

## What Counts as a Magical Ritual

A procedure counts as a magical Ritual when:

1. it has a bounded magical purpose;
2. several differentiated dependencies or contributions are intentionally organized;
3. preparation or coordinated structure materially changes what can occur;
4. sources, roles, conditions, costs, authority, and consequences remain identifiable;
5. integrity, interruption, completion, and failure can be adjudicated;
6. the procedure is more than a social label or one ordinary action described ceremonially.

A Ritual may be solitary. A lone practitioner can coordinate time, site, materials, instruments, source permissions, sequential roles, and prepared states. A group gathering is not automatically a Ritual, and a Ritual need not be public, religious, spoken, or human.

## Ritual and Spell

Ritual and spell answer different questions.

| Structure | Primary question |
| --- | --- |
| **Spell** | How does one bounded repeatable Pattern specify, form, target, express, and end an effect? |
| **Ritual** | How are several dependencies and contributions prepared, coordinated, transferred, validated, and completed toward one magical purpose? |

A Ritual may:

- contain one or more spells;
- prepare conditions for a later spell;
- maintain or terminate an existing effect;
- negotiate with a source;
- transform a site or material;
- coordinate a non-spell species expression;
- test, diagnose, suppress, contain, or witness magic;
- produce an emergent effect through the whole arrangement.

A spell does not become a Ritual merely because it takes time or uses a tool. A Ritual does not become a spell merely because it has a repeatable outcome.

## Canonical Terms

### Ritual

A bounded, prepared procedure that coordinates differentiated dependencies and contributions across a Ritual Structure toward one magical purpose.

### Ritual Purpose

The exact magical objective, affected subjects, exclusions, success conditions, limits, and consequences toward which a Ritual Structure is organized.

### Ritual Structure

The complete causal arrangement of roles, sources, components, site, sequence, timing, authority, transfers, safeguards, and completion conditions that makes a Ritual attempt possible.

### Ritual Role

A bounded contribution, responsibility, authority, or monitoring function assigned to one participant, office, source, tool, collective, or other actor in a Ritual Structure.

### Ritual Contribution

The actual action, resource, authority, information, Skill, embodiment, consent, or support supplied through one Ritual Role.

### Ritual Anchor

A being, object, relationship, location, pattern, office, or other stable reference that keeps one part of a Ritual Structure connected, oriented, or continuous.

### Ritual Site

The physical, magical, relational, jurisdictional, or environmental context prepared or selected for a Ritual.

### Ritual Component

A material, symbol, instrument, pattern, record, substance, body state, offering, or other prepared element with one identified function in the Ritual Structure.

### Ritual Sequence

The required order, dependency graph, synchronization, or state transition through which Ritual Contributions become valid.

### Ritual Window

The bounded time, condition, alignment, lifecycle state, relationship state, source state, or opportunity during which a Ritual or one of its transitions can function.

### Ritual Load

The current demand placed upon participants, sources, Anchors, Channels, components, site, coordination, and safeguards by a Ritual Structure.

### Ritual Integrity

The degree to which the current Ritual Structure still preserves the relationships required for its purpose under change, error, interference, and loss.

### Ritual Transfer

A defined movement of Mana, authority, information, obligation, state, material, attention, or another owned contribution between parts of a Ritual Structure.

### Ritual State

The current phase and condition of a live Ritual attempt, including completed dependencies, active roles, accumulated Load, integrity, interruptions, and unresolved consequences.

### Ritual Residue

The persistent material, magical, relational, environmental, legal, informational, or spiritual aftermath left by a Ritual attempt.

### Ritual Profile

A qualitative external Campaign Record describing one Ritual Structure or live Ritual State without converting it into a universal stat block.

## Ritual Purpose

A Ritual begins with a bounded purpose rather than spectacle.

The purpose identifies:

- the intended magical change, preservation, diagnosis, contact, containment, or transition;
- affected subjects and protected exclusions;
- relevant scale, precision, duration, and completion;
- why a coordinated structure is needed;
- which parts are spells and which belong to other systems;
- success, partial success, refusal, and failure conditions;
- downstream ecological, social, material, and spiritual consequences.

**Seal the valley**, **summon the dead**, or **bless the kingdom** is incomplete unless the local rules supply exact meanings, targets, authority, boundaries, and effects.

## Ritual Structure

A Ritual Structure records how its purpose can be attempted.

### Sources and Mana Relations

Identify every magical source, current Mana State, transfer route, conversion, renewal process, owner, and downstream burden. Several participants supplying Mana does not create one anonymous pool.

### Roles and Contributions

Identify who or what performs each function, what it supplies, what authority it has, which Skills it uses, what it can observe, and what happens if it refuses or fails.

### Components and Anchors

Identify why each material, symbol, tool, body, relationship, object, or site feature matters. Ornament, tradition, redundancy, misinterpretation, and essential function must remain distinguishable.

### Site and Environment

Identify geographic, ecological, celestial, territorial, legal, source, and infrastructure conditions. A portable diagram cannot automatically carry a place-bound relationship.

### Sequence and Ritual Windows

Identify ordered dependencies, synchronized actions, parallel work, waiting periods, thresholds, and time-sensitive opportunities. Sequence may be linear, cyclical, branching, conditional, distributed, or responsive.

### Transfers and Load

Identify what moves among roles and structures, who owns it before and after, where Load accumulates, and how overload, loss, debt, or contamination is prevented.

### Validation and Safeguards

Identify checkpoints, instruments, witnesses, feedback, consent confirmations, quarantine, capacity margins, Abort Routes, and decision authority.

### Completion and Aftermath

Identify what closes, remains, must be maintained, is consumed, is owed, becomes visible, or changes in the world after success, interruption, or failure.

## Ritual Roles

A Role is functional rather than a universal hierarchy. Possible roles include:

- source negotiator;
- authority bearer;
- Mana supplier;
- Channel or conduit;
- Pattern former;
- target witness;
- Anchor keeper;
- site preparer;
- component maker or material handler;
- timekeeper or synchronizer;
- translator;
- boundary maintainer;
- instrument operator;
- error monitor;
- safety officer;
- protector;
- affected-party representative;
- recorder;
- termination lead;
- recovery or residue handler.

One participant may hold several compatible roles. One role may be distributed across a collective, tool network, colony, institution, or several shifts where the structure supports continuity.

Holding a role does not grant the capabilities of other roles. The authority bearer does not absorb the Mana supplier's Capacity. The timekeeper does not learn the Pattern. The lead does not own everyone's action.

### Role Substitution

A different participant, tool, office, or method may fill a role only when it supplies the same required function or a valid adapted function. Matching titles or costumes is not enough.

Substitution must recheck:

- capability and present reliability;
- Access, authority, and consent;
- embodiment and interface;
- communication and timing;
- compatibility with other roles;
- Load and failure handling;
- interpretation under the current Ritual Structure.

## Participation and Agency

Every person-like participant retains agency, perspective, consent, and ownership.

Participation may be willing, contractual, mistaken, coerced, compelled by an explicit hostile effect, or physically forced. These states have different facts and consequences. Calling an act **ritual consent** does not make coercion disappear.

Informed participation should identify:

- the role and expected contribution;
- foreseeable material risks and uncertainty;
- what information is withheld and why;
- who may change the procedure;
- when and how withdrawal is possible;
- consequences of withdrawal at different Ritual States;
- continuing obligations, traces, or exposure;
- who controls termination.

Consent can be withdrawn. Withdrawal may still have causal consequences once dangerous processes are active, but those consequences do not retroactively create permission.

Incapacitated people, children, prisoners, enthralled beings, summoned entities, Weapon Souls, animals, monsters, and collective persons require their actual agency and protection rules. An institution, god, family, office, owner, or ritual leader cannot consent for a person merely by claiming jurisdiction.

## Preparation

Preparation can provide:

- tested components;
- calibrated tools;
- source agreements;
- legal or magical authority;
- participant training;
- synchronized schedules;
- mapped targets and boundaries;
- suitable Mana State;
- protected sites;
- rehearsed communication;
- reserves and recovery arrangements;
- validated contingencies;
- public notice or affected-party consent.

Preparation creates dependencies as well as advantages. Components can age, permissions can expire, participants can change, sites can be disturbed, sources can deplete, and rehearsed assumptions can become false.

Time alone does not improve a Ritual. Meaningful preparation changes readiness or evidence.

## Sequence, Windows, and State

A Ritual Sequence defines dependencies rather than requiring theatrical steps.

Useful state questions include:

1. Which prerequisites are complete?
2. Which roles and sources are currently active?
3. Which Transfers have begun and remain reversible?
4. Where is Load accumulating?
5. Which Ritual Windows are open or closing?
6. Which Anchors and boundaries remain valid?
7. What can still be changed safely?
8. Which Abort Routes remain available?
9. What residue or obligation already exists?

A live Ritual State belongs in the external Campaign Record. Canon defines how to reason about it, not the state of a current ritual.

## Integrity and Interruption

Ritual Integrity is claim-specific. A Ritual may retain target identification while losing source stability, or preserve containment while losing its completion route.

Interruption may affect:

- one participant or role;
- communication or synchronization;
- a source or authority;
- an Anchor or component;
- a Transfer in progress;
- the site or Mana State;
- target relation;
- safety and termination;
- the procedure's secrecy or social legitimacy.

Possible responses include:

- pause at a stable checkpoint;
- replace a role;
- reduce purpose or scale;
- isolate one branch;
- redirect Load;
- open an Abort Route;
- complete a safe containment state;
- continue with explicit increased risk;
- terminate and manage residue.

Interruption does not always mean total failure. Nor may the GM preserve the intended result after a critical dependency is gone merely because the Ritual took a long time to prepare.

## Substitution and Scale

Rituals can exchange some personal output for:

- more time;
- specialized roles;
- multiple sources;
- rare materials;
- a prepared site;
- tools and infrastructure;
- social or magical authority;
- collective coordination;
- distributed Load;
- narrower conditions;
- greater visibility or consequence.

Every substitution states what requirement changes and what new dependency replaces it. A Ritual cannot substitute **effort** for an absent world law or **sacrifice** for a source that does not accept it.

### Scaling Limits

More participants, Mana, time, materials, or complexity do not guarantee a larger result.

Scale may be limited by:

- source throughput and renewal;
- Channel and Anchor Capacity;
- Pattern coherence;
- communication and synchronization;
- site geometry or jurisdiction;
- target relation;
- environmental absorption;
- authority and consent;
- component quality and compatibility;
- containment and termination;
- coordination overhead;
- ecological and political response.

Large rituals often become more fragile, visible, contested, slow, and consequential rather than simply more powerful.

## Components, Offerings, and Sacrifice

A Ritual Component matters through a defined function.

Possible functions include:

- carrying or filtering Mana;
- representing a valid target relation;
- storing information;
- confirming identity or authority;
- anchoring place or time;
- regulating sequence;
- absorbing Load;
- enabling communication;
- satisfying a negotiated exchange;
- recording consent or witness;
- containing residue.

An offering is a contribution presented to a source, participant, institution, law, ecology, or relationship under a recognized rule. Its effect depends on what the receiver can perceive, value, accept, refuse, or enforce.

Destruction does not create value automatically. Blood, death, pain, memories, souls, rare objects, vows, and public suffering are not universal fuels. A source that demands harm retains agency and moral consequence; its demand does not make exploitation necessary, true, or mechanically superior.

No Ritual may consume, split, bind, erase, duplicate, or transfer a Soul merely because participants describe the act as sacrifice. Any genuine Soul effect must follow completed Soul safeguards, informed agency, resistance, and its own receiving rule.

## Authority, Witness, and Recognition

A Ritual may require authority from a source, office, territory, law, relationship, Title, deity, spirit, institution, community, or affected party.

Keep separate:

- magical authority to invoke one effect;
- legal permission;
- social legitimacy;
- ritual office;
- technical competence;
- source consent;
- affected-party consent;
- public recognition;
- historical witness.

A king may have legal authority to order a ritual but no magical authority to open its source. A priest may hold ritual office but lack current Skill. A hidden witness may satisfy a magical relationship while the public believes no one attended.

Witness can provide observation, testimony, identity confirmation, shared intention, social legitimacy, source recognition, or an actual magical component only where the Ritual Structure says so.

## Skills, Knowledge, and Development

[Ritual Knowledge](../progression/MAGICAL_DEVELOPMENT.md#ritual-knowledge) is information and understanding. It is not performance.

The Skill Engine owns learned capabilities such as:

- preparing one component;
- performing one role;
- reading Ritual State;
- synchronizing participants;
- controlling a Transfer;
- maintaining an Anchor;
- translating doctrine;
- diagnosing integrity loss;
- adapting a sequence;
- opening an Abort Route;
- designing or reconstructing a Ritual Structure.

A Composite Skill may coordinate several of these functions for one recurring ritual role or family. No participant receives the full ritual's Skill or Development because the group succeeded.

Development applies to what each participant meaningfully practised, understood, integrated, and can perform more reliably. Repetition without variation, feedback, responsibility, or changed capability is not unlimited growth.

Institutional capability may preserve a Ritual across generations through records, roles, infrastructure, standards, and teaching. That Collective Capability does not reside in full within any leader or member.

## Embodiment, Species, and Monsters

Rituals may be designed around nonhuman bodies and ecologies.

Examples include:

- a spider colony weaving a distributed boundary;
- wolves synchronizing territorial song with a lunar Current;
- slimes exchanging state through contact and division;
- dragons regulating a volcanic source through breath and nesting geometry;
- fungal minds coordinating through seasonal fruiting bodies;
- spirits negotiating through names, places, and reciprocal witness;
- humans using tools and notation to compensate for limited senses.

Do not translate every Ritual into human circles, chants, hands, or individual cognition. Identify the actual participants, stable collective, body interfaces, lifecycle, communication, and ecology.

Monster Evolution may create structures suitable for a Ritual Role but does not grant Ritual Knowledge, every role, or automatic group coordination. Ritual participation does not award an Evolution Route or Mutation by itself.

## Affinities and Spell Formation

[Magical Affinity](MAGICAL_AFFINITIES.md) may support one source, method, component, site, environment, or role through an exact Affinity Expression. It does not confer the whole Ritual.

[Spell Formation](SPELL_FORMATION.md) governs each spell embedded in a Ritual. The Ritual owns coordination among spells and other contributions; Spell Formation owns each Pattern, target relation, expression, Sustainment, and spell failure.

A Ritual can establish conditions for a spell without becoming that spell's caster. A spell can support a Ritual without carrying the Ritual's authority, witness, or collective purpose.

## Soul-System Interactions

### Soul Resonance

[Soul Resonance](../soul/SOUL_RESONANCE.md) may align participants with a genuine place, relationship, object, history, source, or repeated ritual within its Basis and Target. A receiving rule must define whether that alignment helps recognition, translation, entry, coordination, or another bounded function.

It does not supply every role, force agreement, power the Ritual, or make historical repetition valid under changed law.

### Soul Echoes and Retained Instincts

[Soul Echoes](../soul/SOUL_ECHOES.md) may preserve role experience, procedures, failures, and social context. [Retained Instincts](../soul/RETAINED_INSTINCTS.md) may orient attention toward timing, danger, or familiar patterns.

They do not perform simultaneous external roles, restore former sites, or grant current participants' consent.

### Soul Titles

A [Soul Title](../soul/SOUL_TITLES.md) may satisfy a receiving rule for identity, witness, symbolic authority, or source recognition where its Basis is relevant. It does not provide technical competence, legal permission, Mana, or obedience.

### Soul Depth and Soul Avatars

[Soul Depth](../soul/SOUL_DEPTH.md) may support integration of complex ritual histories without becoming ritual capacity. A [Soul Avatar](../soul/SOUL_AVATARS.md) may synthesize accessible perspectives but cannot occupy several external roles, bypass current embodiment, or multiply actions.

### Soul Constellations

[Soul Constellations](../soul/SOUL_CONSTELLATIONS.md) may make recurring relationships relevant to a Ritual when a valid world route reconnects participants. The Constellation does not summon, obligate, coordinate, or consent for member souls.

### Akashic Archive

[Akashic Archive](../soul/AKASHIC_ARCHIVE.md) Records may preserve procedures, roles, perspectives, and former Ritual States as evidence. They do not restore missing participants, authority, source conditions, sites, components, interpretation, or continuity automatically.

## Soul Weapons

A Weapon Soul participates as a person, not a component merely because it has a vessel.

A Soul Weapon may serve as:

- a consenting participant in a Ritual Role;
- an Anchor through its actual continuity;
- a Pattern interface or instrument;
- a source negotiator;
- a target or protected subject;
- a holder of memories or evidence;
- a willing conduit under current form and Capacity.

Separate Weapon Soul action, bonded-soul action, vessel function, source, affinity, authority, Skill, and compatibility. Soul Intertwining does not merge roles or consent.

A Ritual cannot awaken, bind, evolve, manifest, inherit, or transform a Weapon Soul outside the completed Soul Weapon rules. Calling a person an offering, relic, key, or hereditary weapon does not suspend personhood.

## Human Classes, Professions, and Institutions

Human structures may preserve Ritual Knowledge, train roles, maintain sites, negotiate authority, regulate safety, certify competence, control access, or monopolize components.

A ritual office or credential can be socially real without proving current magical capability. Institutions may conduct a Collective Capability no member can perform alone, but turnover, funding, trust, infrastructure, succession, and accountability remain dependencies.

Religious, civic, familial, legal, professional, and political ceremonies may have no magical effect while still carrying social consequences. Conversely, a hidden solitary Ritual may be magical without recognition.

## Reincarnation

At Final Death:

- active Ritual States, accumulated Load, prepared bodily conditions, personal Reserves, and current Practised Reliability do not transfer;
- sites, components, infrastructure, institutions, offices, worldly authority, contracts, and living participants remain in the world;
- legitimate Ritual Knowledge may persist through Magical Imprints;
- bounded role and coordination Skills may preserve Skill Imprints;
- Soul Echoes and Archive Records may preserve perspectives and evidence;
- Titles, Resonance, relationships, and Soul Weapon passage follow their own rules.

A reincarnated ritualist must revalidate current world law, source, site, participants, bodies, materials, authority, sequence, and safety. Former expertise can accelerate reconstruction and reveal missing assumptions. It cannot recreate the old arrangement.

If a former Ritual no longer functions, reconstruction may yield a successor procedure with documented discontinuities. Reusing the old name does not establish continuity.

## World and Ecological Consequences

Ritual preparation and performance can affect:

- Mana sources, Currents, Reservoirs, Sinks, and local ecology;
- component harvesting and waste;
- settlements, infrastructure, labor, and trade;
- migration, breeding, dormancy, and habitat;
- faction goals, law, religion, legitimacy, and conflict;
- public trust, fear, secrecy, and misinformation;
- long-term obligations and source relationships;
- contamination, Ritual Residue, and dangerous sites;
- who controls specialized knowledge and authority.

Consequences persist through ordinary world causality. Annual ceremonies do not reset ecological cost, and interrupted Rituals do not vanish from history.

[Magic and the World Engine](WORLD_ENGINE_INTERACTIONS.md) defines the causal handoff for these consequences. Detailed variable simulation remains owned by Phase 8.

## Failure

### Purpose Failure

The intended outcome is vague, contradictory, impossible under current law, or different from what affected parties believe.

### Preparation Failure

Components, training, permissions, site, timing, sources, or contingencies are inadequate before activation.

### Role Failure

A participant cannot, will not, or does not perform the required contribution.

### Coordination Failure

Communication, synchronization, interpretation, handoff, or decision authority breaks down.

### Anchor Failure

A stable reference moves, breaks, changes identity, becomes inaccessible, or was never valid.

### Transfer Failure

Mana, authority, information, state, obligation, or another contribution moves incorrectly, incompletely, or to the wrong recipient.

### Load Failure

Demand exceeds a participant, source, Channel, component, site, or safeguard.

### Integrity Failure

Enough relationships fail that the current structure no longer supports its purpose even if individual components remain active.

### Window Failure

A required opportunity closes, was misidentified, or changes before the relevant transition.

### Completion Failure

The effect begins but cannot settle, terminate, transfer responsibility, or enter a safe maintained state.

Failure may produce partial effects, refusal, injury, debt, exposure, ecological damage, Residue, political consequences, or no magical result. Harm and repetition do not grant progress automatically.

## Safety and Counterplay

Ritual safeguards may include:

- staged activation;
- independent verification;
- role redundancy where valid;
- capacity margins;
- consent checkpoints;
- separated sources and containment;
- protected communication;
- role-specific abort authority;
- Load monitoring;
- quarantine and evacuation;
- backup Sinks and termination teams;
- public notice and affected-party review;
- residue handling and long-term observation.

Counterplay may target source, authority, participants, trust, timing, site, component supply, communication, Anchors, target relation, secrecy, legal permission, containment, recovery, or aftermath.

Opposition need not overpower the entire Ritual. It may expose a lie, persuade a participant to withdraw, redirect a supply route, alter a site, close a Window, or force the ritualists to choose a safer reduced purpose.

## Information and Secrecy

Keep separate:

- actual Ritual Structure and current Ritual State;
- each participant's role knowledge;
- leadership's model and hidden assumptions;
- public ceremony and magical function;
- written procedure and tacit practice;
- source testimony and institutional doctrine;
- false, incomplete, obsolete, or encoded records;
- player knowledge and character knowledge;
- Ritual Residue and observer interpretation.

No participant must know the whole Ritual for it to function, but ignorance creates dependence and potential exploitation. Secrecy does not erase evidence, risk, accountability, or the need for informed consent.

## Ritual Claim Procedure

When a Ritual matters:

1. confirm that the procedure is magically real and not solely a social ceremony;
2. state the Ritual Purpose, affected subjects, exclusions, and success conditions;
3. identify world laws, sources, Mana Relations, authority, and consent;
4. map Roles, participants, Contributions, Skills, embodiment, and agency;
5. map Components, Anchors, site, environment, Sequence, and Ritual Windows;
6. identify every Transfer, Load-bearing structure, integrity requirement, and safeguard;
7. resolve embedded spells and adjacent effects through their owning systems;
8. track current Ritual State, interruption, substitution, and withdrawal externally;
9. resolve failure at the correct layer and account for costs once;
10. establish completion, termination, maintenance, Residue, and world consequences;
11. award Development only for contributions actually performed and integrated;
12. apply Reincarnation and continuity component by component;
13. preserve information boundaries and store live state outside canon.

## Safeguards

### No Requirement Waiver

Ritual preparation can substitute named dependencies only through valid receiving rules. It cannot create absent world laws, sources, authority, consent, embodiment, or target relations.

### No Universal Group Pool

Participants retain separate Mana, Capacity, Skills, actions, authority, consent, and consequences. Transfers require explicit routes and losses.

### No Leader Ownership

A leader coordinates defined decisions and responsibilities. It does not own participants, absorb their capabilities, or receive the whole Ritual's Development.

### No Ritual XP

Duration, repetition, spectacle, danger, attendance, contribution size, success, failure, or casualty count does not create a ritual progression currency.

### No Sacrifice Currency

Pain, blood, death, souls, memories, vows, and destruction have no universal conversion value. Exploitation remains exploitation even when a source responds to it.

### No Automatic Authority

Tradition, office, lineage, title, prophecy, ownership, invitation, public acclaim, or ritual leadership grants only the authority recognized by a current rule.

### No Consent Fiction

Silence, incapacity, coercion, ignorance, legal ownership, inherited obligation, summoned status, Weapon Soul bonding, or collective need does not become informed consent.

### No Capability Duplication

Group success does not install the whole procedure, Skills, spells, affinities, authority, or reliability in every participant.

### No Infinite Scale

More participants, Mana, materials, time, complexity, Reincarnations, or sacrifice cannot bypass throughput, integrity, coordination, source, target, environment, termination, and consequence.

### No Reincarnation Setup Transfer

Ritual Knowledge may persist, but active state, sites, roles, participants, institutions, authority, components, and Practised Reliability remain current-life and world-bound.

### No Soul or Weapon Bypass

Rituals cannot consume, merge, compel, duplicate, bind, awaken, evolve, manifest, or transfer Souls and Weapon Souls outside their completed rules.

### No Mandatory Ritual Path

Rituals are one method of magical organization. Direct spells, species expressions, tools, institutions, nonmagical solutions, and refusal remain possible where current causality permits.

### No Campaign Data in Canon

Named current rituals, participant lists, prepared sites, active Ritual States, component inventories, live obligations, source agreements, and populated Ritual Profiles belong in external Campaign Records.

## Worked Examples

### Floodplain Boundary Ritual

A town prepares a seasonal Ritual to redirect a dangerous river Current away from inhabited ground. Surveyors map the floodplain, stoneworkers repair Anchors, spirit negotiators renew permission, farmers identify protected channels, and three casters maintain separate boundary spells.

No participant owns the whole effect. Heavy upstream mining changes the Current after rehearsal, so the safety officer reduces the protected area rather than forcing the old design. The smaller success preserves the town while flooding abandoned fields and creating a political dispute.

### Spider Colony Molting Ritual

A spider colony weaves a distributed cocoon around juveniles during a lunar Mana Window. Different castes produce structural silk, sense tension, carry mineral salts, and regulate humidity. The emergent Pattern supports safe metamorphosis.

The Ritual belongs to the colony's coordinated practice, not to a queen or individual spider. A predator breaking one outer line lowers integrity but does not erase the whole structure. Replacing the sensing caste with human observers fails because their instruments cannot perform the same live tension correction without adaptation.

### Solitary Source Petition

A hermit spends nine days preparing a spring, cleaning contamination, mapping its Mana Current, and renewing a relationship with its spirit. On the final morning the hermit offers stewardship records and requests permission to draw healing water.

The Ritual is solitary but coordinates site, time, service, evidence, source agency, and a bounded request. The spirit may refuse. Nine days of devotion does not compel it or become stored power.

### Soul Weapon as Anchor

A Weapon Soul that remembers an extinct city agrees to serve as an identity Anchor while historians open a bounded Archive-reading Ritual. The Weapon Soul confirms landmarks; its wielder maintains physical protection; a scholar translates records; another caster supplies the spell.

The Weapon Soul remains a participant and may withdraw if the Ritual exposes private memories. Its vessel continuity supports the Anchor role but does not make it an owned component or give every participant access to its past.

### Reincarnated Ritual Architect

A former ritual architect remembers a weather-balancing structure from an earlier Age. In the current Age, one source is extinct, the coast has moved, and legal authority belongs to several monster communities.

The architect's retained knowledge identifies likely functions and failure points. Years of negotiation, site study, translation, and testing produce a successor Ritual with different roles and a smaller purpose. The old name provides history, not automatic continuity.

### Public Coronation Without Magic

A coronation uses sacred language, ancestral objects, formal witnesses, and repeated gestures but no magical source or effect. It changes office, legitimacy, obligations, and public belief through social rules.

It is a ritual in ordinary language but not a magical Ritual under this document. A later god may recognize the office through a separate receiving rule; that does not retroactively make every coronation magical.

### Coerced Participant

A faction chains a river spirit into the Mana-supplier role and calls its resistance **ritual turbulence**. The Ritual may still produce magic through an explicit hostile binding, but it does not have consent.

The binding, resistance, source damage, ecological collapse, moral responsibility, and future retaliation remain real. The GM does not reward coercion with superior efficiency merely because the procedure is [forbidden](FORBIDDEN_MAGIC.md) or cruel.

### Interrupted Gate-Sealing Ritual

A team attempts to close a breach. Halfway through, the target witness discovers that closing it fully would trap refugees between worlds. The witness withdraws confirmation.

The termination lead can preserve a temporary containment state while negotiations continue. The Ritual does not finish as planned, but interruption creates an intelligible choice rather than arbitrary explosion or automatic success.
