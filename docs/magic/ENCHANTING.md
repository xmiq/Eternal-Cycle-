# Enchanting

## Purpose

This document defines enchanting as the design, installation, operation, maintenance, alteration, and removal of persistent magical configurations anchored to hosts. It establishes host compatibility, source ownership, functions, interfaces, layering, degradation, failure, custody, and safeguards without reducing enchanted things to item statistics.

Enchanting integrates [Mana](MANA.md), [Magical Affinities](MAGICAL_AFFINITIES.md), [Spell Formation](SPELL_FORMATION.md), [Rituals](RITUALS.md), [Magical Development](../progression/MAGICAL_DEVELOPMENT.md), the [Skill Engine](../skills/README.md), crafting, embodiment, world law, and Soul Weapon personhood. It does not replace any of them.

## Core Rule

**An Enchantment is a bounded magical configuration anchored to a Host so that a defined function can persist, recur, or become available beyond the moment of installation.**

Persistence always has a cause. An Enchantment may remain through stored Mana, ambient draw, a maintained source, a material pattern, a relationship, periodic renewal, a living process, an institution, or another valid route. It is never permanent merely because its label says so.

An Enchantment is not:

- an item stat, rarity, quality tier, or universal bonus;
- every magical object, creature, place, tool, curse, blessing, or Soul Weapon;
- a spell frozen without additional anchoring rules;
- a Skill, Class, Profession, Species Trait, Mutation, Evolution, Soul effect, or Weapon Form;
- proof of intelligence, awareness, personhood, memory, consent, or a Weapon Soul;
- a way to make borrowed power internal or Soul-persistent;
- automatically beneficial, voluntary, legal, stable, repairable, or removable;
- an inventory entry that survives Reincarnation.

## What Counts as an Enchantment

A magical configuration counts as an Enchantment when it has:

1. a specific Host;
2. a bounded magical function;
3. an anchoring relationship that outlasts initial installation;
4. a source and Mana Relation;
5. one or more interfaces through which the function operates;
6. conditions, limits, maintenance, degradation, and failure;
7. evidence that separates it from ordinary material behavior, active spells, spirits, constructs, Soul effects, and mistaken interpretation.

A Host may be an object, structure, site, territory, body, living tissue, plant, monster-grown material, vehicle, tool, garment, written medium, network, or other substrate recognized by current law.

Person-like Hosts and occupied vessels retain agency and consent. A Host category does not imply ownership.

## Canonical Terms

### Enchantment

A bounded magical configuration anchored to a Host so that a defined function can persist, recur, or become available beyond the moment of installation.

### Enchantment Host

The object, structure, site, territory, body, tissue, living system, network, or other substrate to which an Enchantment is causally anchored.

### Enchantment Function

The exact magical operation an Enchantment can perform, support, regulate, sense, prevent, transform, store, communicate, or make available under stated conditions.

### Enchantment Pattern

The organized magical relationships encoded into or around a Host that constrain an Enchantment's Function, source, interfaces, activation, persistence, and failure.

### Enchantment Anchor

The material, spatial, relational, symbolic, biological, legal, source-bound, or other valid relationship that keeps an Enchantment Pattern attached to its Host.

### Enchantment Interface

A defined route through which a Host, user, source, environment, tool, target, or maintainer can sense, activate, supply, direct, constrain, inspect, repair, or terminate an Enchantment.

### Enchantment Supply

The source and replenishment route that provide the Mana or equivalent magical support required for an Enchantment's current operation.

### Host Compatibility

The purpose-specific ability of a Host to receive, anchor, survive, and express one proposed Enchantment under current material, bodily, magical, environmental, and legal conditions.

### Enchantment Load

The current demand one or more Enchantments place upon a Host, source, Anchor, Interface, environment, user, or maintenance system.

### Enchantment Integrity

The degree to which an Enchantment still preserves the relationships required for its defined Function under wear, change, interference, and repair.

### Enchantment Layer

One independently sourced and adjudicable Enchantment configuration sharing a Host with other material, magical, spiritual, constructed, Soul, or living systems.

### Enchantment Trigger

The event or satisfied condition that causes an Enchantment Function to activate, change state, release a stored process, or permit use.

### Enchantment Maintenance

The work required to preserve or restore an Enchantment's source, Pattern, Anchor, Interfaces, Host condition, calibration, safety, and intended Function.

### Enchantment Drift

A causal change in an Enchantment's Function, Pattern, source relation, Trigger, Interfaces, Integrity, Host relationship, or failure behavior over time.

### Enchantment Residue

Persistent magical, material, informational, environmental, relational, or legal aftermath left when an Enchantment operates, degrades, is altered, or ends.

### Enchantment Profile

A qualitative external Campaign Record describing one Enchantment and its current relationship with its Host without treating it as a universal item stat block.

## Enchantment Function

Every Enchantment states one or more bounded Functions.

Possible Functions include:

- maintaining a temperature or pressure range;
- sensing one source-appropriate condition;
- warning through a defined signal;
- storing and releasing a bounded Mana State;
- regulating flow through a Channel or network;
- reinforcing a material relationship;
- changing friction, buoyancy, permeability, conductivity, or another property;
- preserving, masking, revealing, translating, or transmitting information;
- opening a valid interface to a source;
- supporting one Spell Pattern or Ritual Role;
- denying, filtering, redirecting, or containing one influence;
- maintaining a boundary or environmental condition;
- applying a defined magical treatment to a consenting body.

A Function must state:

1. what it acts upon;
2. what it changes or permits;
3. source and supply;
4. activation or continual-operation conditions;
5. target, boundary, range, and exclusions;
6. throughput, Capacity, and Load limits;
7. user and maintenance interfaces;
8. evidence, counters, and failure;
9. residue and world consequences.

**Flaming sword**, **perfect armor**, **truth bell**, and **infinite bag** are labels rather than complete Functions.

## Host Compatibility

Compatibility is assessed for one proposed Enchantment and Function.

Relevant factors may include:

- material composition and structure;
- size, geometry, continuity, porosity, flexibility, and movement;
- heat, pressure, corrosion, fatigue, healing, growth, and ordinary wear;
- existing Mana State, affinities, Channels, inscriptions, and sources;
- biological immune response, metabolism, lifecycle, and consent;
- existing Enchantment Layers, spirits, constructs, Soul effects, and ordinary mechanisms;
- environment, jurisdiction, ownership, custody, and access;
- available fabrication, tools, precision, and maintenance;
- how damage, repair, replacement, division, and combination affect identity.

Host Compatibility is not one score. A glass plate may hold a precise light Pattern but fail under impact. A living tree may heal around an Enchantment while gradually changing its geometry. A Soul Weapon vessel may be physically compatible while its Weapon Soul refuses installation.

### Host Preparation

Preparation may clean, shape, temper, inscribe, grow, tune, align, stabilize, protect, or expose a Host interface. It may require ordinary craft, magical Skills, ritual work, alchemy, medicine, negotiation, or several owners.

Preparation does not make the Host universally enchantable. It may weaken other functions, create maintenance burdens, alter ownership, produce waste, or make future repair harder.

## Enchantment Pattern

An Enchantment Pattern encodes the relationships necessary for continued or recurring operation.

It may include:

- a Spell Pattern adapted for anchoring;
- a material arrangement;
- a persistent Mana circuit;
- an environmental or site relationship;
- a source contract;
- a biological feedback loop;
- a symbolic or Conceptual constraint;
- a repeating Ritual Structure;
- a constructed controller;
- several separate layers with defined interfaces.

The Pattern is not identical to visible inscriptions. A blank surface may carry a material or relational Pattern; ornate runes may be decoration, instructions, or deception.

An Enchantment Pattern must remain within current world law. Anchoring an impossible effect does not make it possible.

## Anchors and Interfaces

### Enchantment Anchors

An Anchor explains why the Pattern remains associated with the Host.

Possible Anchors include:

- material lattice or shaped structure;
- bonded coating, thread, ink, crystal, or component;
- persistent spatial geometry;
- a living organ or symbiotic process;
- a source relationship or contract;
- territory, property, office, name, or identity recognized by a magical law;
- repeated maintenance or periodic Ritual;
- a linked external device or network;
- a Soul Title, Soul Resonance, or other identity fact only where a receiving rule recognizes it.

Every Anchor states what happens when the Host is repaired, divided, moved, transformed, copied, renamed, sold, inherited, or destroyed.

### Enchantment Interfaces

An Interface may be operated by touch, gesture, thought, tool, spoken command, source response, pressure, environment, credential, relationship, bodily signal, or another valid route.

Interfaces state:

- who or what can use them;
- what information they expose;
- which actions they permit;
- which Skills, authority, consent, or compatibility they require;
- how authentication and false positives work;
- what happens under damage, spoofing, interference, or misuse;
- whether the Host or another person can refuse.

An Interface does not grant ownership of the Host or source.

## Sources, Supply, and Persistence

An Enchantment's persistence class describes its actual support rather than its quality.

### Stored-Supply Enchantment

The Host contains a bounded Reservoir or equivalent state. Operation depletes it until replenished through a valid route.

### Ambient-Supply Enchantment

The Enchantment draws from a compatible local Field or Current. Movement, depletion, competition, pollution, or World Reset can alter operation.

### Linked-Source Enchantment

The Host remains connected to an external source, network, deity, spirit, institution, site, or device. Distance, authority, consent, rate, and interruption matter.

### Maintained Enchantment

People, institutions, rituals, organisms, or systems periodically restore Pattern, Supply, calibration, or Host condition.

### Living Enchantment

A biological or ecological process participates in persistence. Growth, healing, diet, lifecycle, disease, symbiosis, reproduction, and agency remain relevant.

### Self-Regulating Enchantment

The Pattern includes feedback that adjusts operation and maintenance within bounded conditions. Self-regulation does not mean sentience, infinite energy, self-repair under all damage, or freedom from entropy and world change.

Several forms may coexist. Each retains provenance and failure.

## Installation

Installation establishes the Enchantment-Host relationship.

### 1. Define the Function

State the intended operation, exclusions, activation, persistence, and acceptable failure.

### 2. Assess Host Compatibility

Test material, biological, magical, legal, environmental, and personhood conditions for this Function.

### 3. Establish Source and Supply

Identify Mana Relation, authority, consent, replenishment, throughput, and consequence.

### 4. Design the Pattern

Select or construct the magical organization, Anchors, Interfaces, Trigger, boundaries, termination, and safeguards.

### 5. Prepare Host and Components

Perform actual craft, treatment, inscription, growth, ritual, or assembly under the relevant owners.

### 6. Install in Bounded Stages

Establish the Anchor, connect Supply, activate Interfaces, and monitor Load rather than assuming one dramatic moment completes every layer.

### 7. Validate

Test intended Function, exclusions, false triggers, user access, interference, degradation, termination, and failure under representative conditions.

### 8. Settle and Record

Allow material and magical state to stabilize, establish maintenance, record provenance, and address residue, custody, and affected parties.

Installation may be a Spell, Ritual, craft process, biological treatment, source agreement, or combination. Those owners remain separate.

## Activation and Operation

An Enchantment may operate continuously, periodically, conditionally, deliberately, responsively, or through user request.

An Enchantment Trigger must rely on something the Pattern can actually detect. **Activate for the rightful owner**, **warn of evil**, or **protect the innocent** requires a valid receiving rule and bounded definitions.

During operation resolve:

- Trigger validity;
- source availability and authority;
- Supply rate and conversion;
- Host and Interface condition;
- user Skill and current action;
- target relation and resistance;
- Enchantment Load and other Layers;
- maintenance state;
- residue and consequences.

An Enchantment described as passive is still causal. It has sensing, supply, response, maintenance, and failure even if no deliberate activation is required.

## Layering and Interference

One Host may carry several Enchantment Layers only when each remains independently identifiable.

For every Layer record:

- Function;
- Pattern and Anchor;
- source and Supply;
- Host interfaces and occupied structures;
- Trigger and target;
- Load;
- maintenance;
- effect on other Layers;
- failure and removal.

Layers may:

- operate independently;
- share a Host while using different structures;
- compete for Mana, space, attention, heat, or maintenance;
- interfere through incompatible Patterns;
- alter one another's triggers or traces;
- require an explicit coordinating controller;
- make repair or removal more dangerous;
- remain socially grouped under one item name.

They do not stack into a total enchantment score or fuse because labels match. Ten weak wards do not become an invulnerable ward unless a valid structure coordinates them, and that structure adds dependencies.

## Maintenance, Repair, and Drift

Enchantment Maintenance may include:

- replenishing Supply;
- cleaning or repairing the Host;
- replacing components;
- restoring inscriptions or geometry;
- recalibrating Interfaces and Triggers;
- renewing permissions, contracts, or source relationships;
- managing residue and waste;
- testing safety and false activation;
- adapting to bodily growth or environmental change;
- documenting modifications and provenance.

Ordinary repair does not necessarily repair the Pattern. Magical repair does not necessarily restore material strength. Replacing a component may preserve function, create a successor, or end identity depending on the Anchor.

### Enchantment Drift

Drift may arise through wear, repair, repeated activation, source change, hostile interference, user adaptation, biological growth, environmental exposure, World Reset, or altered law.

Drift can narrow, broaden, distort, suppress, redirect, destabilize, or end a Function. It is not automatic improvement and does not become personality without evidence of personhood.

## Alteration, Suppression, and Removal

Changing an Enchantment requires identifying the affected Layer and owner.

Possible operations include:

- suppressing activation while preserving the Pattern;
- disconnecting Supply;
- disabling an Interface;
- replacing a Trigger;
- adapting a Pattern;
- moving or replacing an Anchor;
- separating one Layer;
- dispelling or unwinding magical structure;
- physically removing an anchored component;
- transforming the Host;
- allowing controlled depletion;
- terminating a source relationship.

Removal can leave damaged Hosts, unstable Supply, obligations, traces, blank interfaces, environmental discharge, or Enchantment Residue. **Dispel** is not a universal eraser.

If a person-like source, living Host, construct, spirit, or Weapon Soul is affected, consent, resistance, care, and agency remain independently resolved.

## Craft, Skills, and Professions

Enchanting may require distinct learned capabilities in:

- material preparation;
- Pattern design;
- Spell Construction;
- Ritual coordination;
- source negotiation;
- inscription or fabrication;
- diagnosis;
- Host Compatibility assessment;
- Layer integration;
- calibration and validation;
- maintenance and repair;
- safe removal;
- documentation and professional judgment.

The Skill Engine owns learned technique. Profession Development owns integrated work, standards, responsibility, clients, affected parties, and consequences. Magic owns the Enchantment.

A brilliant smith may lack Magical Access. An enchanter may rely on a smith for Host preparation. A noncasting inspector may diagnose dangerous Drift with calibrated instruments. Group success transfers none of these capabilities automatically.

## Persons, Living Hosts, and Consent

Enchanting a living body or person-like Host can alter health, sensation, movement, communication, vulnerability, identity expression, or dependency.

Before installation identify:

- whether the Host is a person;
- informed consent and ability to withdraw;
- medical, species, and lifecycle effects;
- who controls activation and termination;
- access to maintenance and removal;
- coercion, ownership claims, institutional pressure, and alternatives;
- what happens after growth, injury, Reincarnation, or loss of Supply;
- effects on offspring, symbionts, collective members, and ecology where relevant.

Consent to one Function is not blanket consent to modification, tracking, control, data extraction, repair, resale, inheritance, or future Layers.

A hostile enchantment may exist through a valid attack, curse, binding, or coercive system. Its existence does not create consent, legitimacy, or immunity from resistance and consequence.

## Species, Monsters, and Evolution

Monster craft may enchant lairs, resin, bone, webs, reefs, fungal networks, territories, tools, shed organs, or living bodies through species-appropriate methods.

Keep separate:

- Species Traits and Magical Organs;
- learned monster-native craft and Skills;
- Enchantment Host and Function;
- symbiosis and ecology;
- Mutation, Hybridization, and Evolution;
- temporary transformation or treatment;
- social claims about what the monster has become.

An Enchantment attached to a body does not become anatomy or an Evolved Form merely because it is useful, old, integrated, or difficult to remove. It may create Evolutionary Pressure or support a compatible route, but Monster Evolution retains ownership.

If an enchantment becomes genuinely reproduced through a species lifecycle, the transition to Species Trait requires an explicit biological or metaphysical inheritance route and the appropriate later evolution adjudication. Repetition or breeding labels are insufficient.

## Spell and Ritual Interactions

[Spell Formation](SPELL_FORMATION.md) governs Spell Patterns used to install, activate, maintain, diagnose, or remove Enchantments. The Enchantment Pattern owns persistence beyond the Spell Instance.

[Rituals](RITUALS.md) may coordinate sources, roles, site, materials, authority, installation, or maintenance. The Ritual does not become a permanent capability of the Host unless the Enchantment route actually anchors it.

An Enchantment may support a Spell Formation Component or Ritual Role through an Interface. It does not grant the user every Skill, Pattern, authority, or contribution associated with that procedure.

## Affinities

[Magical Affinity](MAGICAL_AFFINITIES.md) may affect Host Compatibility, source response, Pattern stability, material interaction, user Interface, or environmental operation through one exact Expression.

Separate:

- Host affinity;
- Enchantment Pattern affinity;
- source compatibility;
- user's affinity;
- maintainer's Skill;
- Weapon Soul or living Host affinity.

Installing an affinity-bearing material or Layer does not transfer its affinity to the user, Host soul, species, or bonded partner.

## Soul-System Interactions

### Soul Resonance

[Soul Resonance](../soul/SOUL_RESONANCE.md) may align a soul with a meaningful enchanted Host, maker, event, source, or Pattern within its Basis and Target. It may support recognition or interpretation only through a receiving rule.

Resonance does not install, power, repair, inherit, or own the Enchantment.

### Soul Echoes and Retained Instincts

[Soul Echoes](../soul/SOUL_ECHOES.md) may remember Enchantment use, construction, failure, and former Hosts. [Retained Instincts](../soul/RETAINED_INSTINCTS.md) may orient attention toward familiar Interfaces or warning signs.

Neither restores the item, Pattern, Supply, Host Compatibility, Access, or current Skill.

### Soul Titles

A [Soul Title](../soul/SOUL_TITLES.md) may be recognized by an Enchantment Trigger, Interface, source, or law only through a bounded receiving rule. Recognition can permit or deny one function without proving ownership, morality, or universal identity.

### Soul Depth and Soul Avatars

[Soul Depth](../soul/SOUL_DEPTH.md) may support integration of complex craft history without granting Enchantment capacity. A [Soul Avatar](../soul/SOUL_AVATARS.md) may synthesize accessible perspectives but cannot provide missing tools, materials, actions, or current reliability.

### Akashic Archive

[Akashic Archive](../soul/AKASHIC_ARCHIVE.md) Records may preserve procedures, provenance, former states, and failures. They do not recreate the Host, source, Pattern, components, legal rights, or current-law validity.

## Soul Weapons

Enchantments and Weapon Souls remain separate sources.

An Enchantment on a Weapon Vessel may:

- alter one material property;
- provide an Interface or Supply route;
- support a Spell or Ritual;
- protect or burden the vessel;
- communicate a fixed message;
- detect a bounded condition;
- interfere with Awakening, Bond communication, form, or Manifestation;
- support a later Weapon Evolution Route while retaining its external ownership.

It does not:

- prove or create a Weapon Soul;
- speak as the Weapon Soul merely because it produces words;
- become Weapon Soul personality or memory;
- transfer to the bonded soul through Soul Intertwining;
- become a Soul Weapon Form through repeated use;
- override Weapon Soul consent;
- Awaken, bind, evolve, manifest, inherit, or command the Weapon Soul.

After Awakening, installation, alteration, activation, and removal that materially affect the Weapon Soul or its vessel require the Weapon Soul's actual consent unless a separate hostile effect is being adjudicated. Custody and ownership do not replace personhood.

[Weapon Evolution](../soul-weapons/WEAPON_EVOLUTION.md) may integrate an external source only through its own Source Integration rules, preserving source, permission, maintenance, counters, and consequences. Enchanting alone cannot launder a Layer into soul-bound persistence.

## Custody, Ownership, and Recognition

Keep separate:

- physical ownership or custody of a Host;
- authorship of the Pattern;
- source ownership;
- legal right to use or alter it;
- user authentication;
- maintenance responsibility;
- intellectual secrecy;
- personhood of any occupant or Host;
- social reputation and market value;
- actual magical capability.

Buying an enchanted object does not transfer source consent, licence, safe operation, maintenance knowledge, or the right to modify a person-like Host. Theft may transfer physical possession while leaving every other layer unchanged.

## Reincarnation

At Final Death:

- ordinary enchanted equipment, bodily Enchantment Layers, active Interfaces, current Supply, prepared charges, and maintained states do not travel with the soul;
- Enchantments remain with their Hosts and sources according to world causality;
- legitimate enchanting knowledge may persist through Magical and Professional Imprints;
- bounded construction, diagnosis, use, and maintenance Skills may preserve Skill Imprints;
- Soul Echoes and Archive Records may preserve experience and evidence;
- Soul Weapons follow their own passage rules, while external Enchantments on vessels remain separately sourced.

A new incarnation must reacquire or create Hosts, establish current Access, validate materials and world law, rebuild Skills and reliability, and negotiate source permissions. Retained expertise can accelerate design and diagnosis but does not summon inventory or transfer Layers.

A body-bound Enchantment ends, remains with remains, transfers to another Host, or persists only through its actual Anchor and source. Death is not a universal dispel, and Reincarnation is not a universal carry route.

## Failure

### Host Failure

The Host cracks, corrodes, grows, rejects, deforms, dies, divides, moves, or otherwise stops supporting the Function.

### Anchor Failure

The Pattern loses its causal attachment to the Host or attaches to the wrong continuity.

### Pattern Failure

The encoded relationships are incomplete, corrupted, mistranslated, obsolete, or incoherent.

### Supply Failure

Mana or equivalent support depletes, is interrupted, is incompatible, exceeds rate, loses consent, or changes state.

### Interface Failure

Users, maintainers, sources, or targets cannot communicate with or control the Function correctly.

### Trigger Failure

The Enchantment activates late, early, falsely, continuously, for the wrong subject, or not at all.

### Load Failure

Demand exceeds Host, source, Anchor, Interface, user, environment, or maintenance capacity.

### Layer Failure

Several systems interfere, compete, cascade, or conceal one another's damage.

### Maintenance Failure

Required work is absent, incorrect, fraudulent, inaccessible, or based on outdated assumptions.

### Termination Failure

The Function cannot stop safely, disconnect from Supply, release stored state, or separate from the Host.

Failure may produce ordinary breakage, uncontrolled magic, source debt, injury, tracking, residue, ecological change, legal conflict, or loss of Function. It grants no automatic Development, stronger Layer, Weapon Evolution, Mutation, or affinity.

## Safety and Counterplay

Safe enchanting may require:

- material and biological tests;
- bounded prototypes;
- isolated Supply;
- explicit user and maintainer Interfaces;
- capacity margins;
- false-trigger testing;
- layer maps;
- provenance and maintenance records;
- emergency suppression and termination;
- source and person consent;
- repairable Anchors;
- environmental containment;
- monitoring for Drift and Residue;
- independent inspection.

Counterplay may target Host, Anchor, Pattern, Supply, Interface, Trigger, user, authority, environment, maintenance, Layer interaction, or aftermath.

Destroying the Host is not the only answer. A nonmagical actor may block a sensor, remove fuel, interrupt maintenance, exploit a false Trigger, isolate a network, move the object outside its Field, expose fraud, or use ordinary material limits.

## Information and Identification

Keep separate:

- actual Enchantment Pattern and Function;
- visible markings and ordinary material behavior;
- source, spirit, construct, Weapon Soul, user Habit, and residue;
- maker claims and maintenance records;
- observer readings and uncertainty;
- public item name, rarity, price, and reputation;
- hidden Layers and false Interfaces;
- player knowledge and character knowledge.

Detection may establish that magic is present without identifying its Function, source, Trigger, safety, personhood, or complete Layer map.

An Enchantment can speak, move, remember fixed data, respond, or imitate personality without being a person. Conversely, a person-like being may communicate through an Enchantment. Source-bounded evidence is required.

## Enchantment Claim Procedure

When an Enchantment matters:

1. identify the Host, continuity, occupants, custody, and personhood;
2. state the bounded Enchantment Function;
3. establish current world law, Pattern, Anchor, source, and Mana Relation;
4. assess Host Compatibility and preparation;
5. identify Interfaces, Trigger, target, boundary, authority, and consent;
6. map Supply, Load, Layers, maintenance, Integrity, and Drift;
7. separate ordinary craft, Skills, Professions, spells, Rituals, species, Soul effects, constructs, spirits, and Weapon Souls by owner;
8. resolve operation, target interaction, resistance, failure, and counterplay;
9. resolve installation, alteration, suppression, repair, or removal through the affected Layer;
10. propagate residue, custody, ecology, institution, and world consequences;
11. award Development only for capability actually practised and integrated;
12. apply Reincarnation and persistence according to Host, source, and Imprint ownership;
13. store current items, Layers, Supply, and profiles outside canon.

## Safeguards

### No Item Statistics

An Enchantment has Functions, dependencies, limits, maintenance, failure, and context rather than generic attack, defense, rarity, quality, or power bonuses.

### No Universal Enchantability

Every Host and Function requires a purpose-specific compatibility route. Material value, age, fame, maker, sentiment, and magical exposure do not guarantee it.

### No Free Permanence

Persistence requires an Anchor, Supply, Host continuity, and current world law. **Permanent** means only that no ordinary end is currently known within stated conditions.

### No Unlimited Layering

Each Layer occupies real structures, sources, Interfaces, maintenance, and Load. Matching names do not stack, and complexity adds interference and failure.

### No Skill Installation

An Enchantment may automate or support a Function but does not grant the user's Skill, judgment, understanding, or Development.

### No Source Laundering

Mana, spirits, gods, contracts, sites, institutions, users, and maintainers retain ownership. Anchoring access to a Host does not make the source internal or transferable.

### No Personhood Assumption

Speech, movement, memory, responsiveness, age, uniqueness, and enchantment do not prove a Soul or person. Existing persons are not reduced to components.

### No Consent Bypass

Ownership, custody, crafting, medical authority, employment, lineage, ritual, law, crisis, silence, or Weapon Soul Bond does not provide blanket consent to installation, activation, tracking, alteration, or removal.

### No Evolution Bypass

Body-bound Enchantments remain external magical configurations unless an explicit Species or Weapon Evolution route integrates a change through its own safeguards.

### No Reincarnation Inventory

Enchanted equipment and bodily Layers do not follow the soul by default. Knowledge and Skill may persist only through their proper Imprints.

### No Infinite Supply

Stored, ambient, linked, maintained, living, and self-regulating Enchantments retain source rate, renewal, competition, loss, and consequence.

### No Mandatory Equipment Path

Enchanted tools can be decisive while remaining optional, counterable, maintainable, and dependent. Ordinary craft, Skills, species capability, Soul Weapons, and nonmagical solutions remain meaningful.

### No Campaign Data in Canon

Current enchanted inventories, named active Hosts, hidden Layers, live Supply, charges, ownership, discovered triggers, and populated Enchantment Profiles belong in external Campaign Records.

## Worked Examples

### Temperature-Regulating Smithing Hammer

An enchanted hammer senses the temperature of the metal it strikes and releases heat from a linked forge Reservoir to keep a narrow alloy within working range.

The smith still chooses alloy, timing, force, and process through Profession and Skill. The Enchantment owns temperature regulation. Moving the hammer away from its forge disconnects Supply, and a damaged sensor can overheat the work despite the head remaining physically sound.

### Talking Spear Without a Weapon Soul

A spear recites tactical warnings when its etched sensor detects three predefined formations. Its message library is fixed, and every response traces to the Enchantment Pattern.

The speech does not prove awareness. A Dormant Weapon Soul might separately exist in the vessel, but the Enchantment owns the warnings until distinct evidence establishes otherwise.

### Layered Harbor Ward

A harbor wall carries separate Layers for storm-current redirection, ship identification, warning bells, and emergency closure. Each uses different Anchors and sources.

The identification Layer drifts after a registry change and begins rejecting allied monster vessels. The storm ward remains functional. Repairing the registry Interface does not require rebuilding every Layer, but operators must check that the emergency Trigger did not use the same obsolete data.

### Living Carapace Treatment

A beetle monster consents to a living Enchantment grown through its outer carapace. Symbiotic filaments filter one toxic Mana State while feeding on minerals from the monster's diet.

The treatment is not a Species Trait or Evolution. Molting sheds most Anchors, requiring careful renewal. It creates a real survival advantage but also dietary dependence and vulnerability to filament disease.

### Enchanted Soul Weapon Vessel

A willing Weapon Soul permits an Enchantment Layer that cools its metal vessel after repeated heat exposure. The Layer uses an external crystal and does not alter personality, memory, Bond, or Soul Weapon Form.

During a later Weapon Evolution Route, the crystal may remain external, be rejected, or support a valid Source Integration. Repeated use alone cannot make its cooling soul-bound.

### Reincarnated Enchanter

A master enchanter Reincarnates in an Age where familiar rune metals no longer hold Mana. The soul remembers diagnostic principles and former failures but owns no workshop, tools, sources, or enchanted inventory.

The enchanter studies living inks used by local monsters and gradually builds a new craft. Retained expertise accelerates good questions; current materials, relationships, Skills, and validation rebuild the capability.

### Conflicting Armor Layers

Armor has one Layer that stiffens material under impact and another that increases flexibility during movement. Both depend on the same crystal lattice and activate from overlapping pressure signals.

At a running fall, both trigger and overload the lattice. The wearer can remove one Layer, install a coordinating controller, or accept a narrow operating limit. Adding more Mana does not solve contradictory Pattern demands.

### Self-Regulating Road Beacon

A road beacon draws weak ambient Mana by day, stores a bounded amount, and emits direction light at night. Feedback reduces brightness when Supply is low.

It is self-regulating, not infinite. Weeks of clouded Mana weather deplete it, migrating creatures compete for the same Field, and cracked glass distorts its sensor. Ordinary maintenance remains decisive.
