# Adaptive Skills

## Purpose

This document defines how stable Skills may emerge when a being repeatedly and meaningfully adapts to a recurring problem, context, embodiment, or constraint.

Adaptive Skills allow novel play to become canonical capability without turning every repeated action, clever tactic, injury response, or named combination into another permanent Skill.

## Core Rule

An **Adaptive Skill** emerges when recurring need, active engagement, variation, feedback, adjustment, integration, meaningful consequence, and coherent function produce a distinct reliable capability that the current tree does not already represent adequately.

Repetition creates opportunity for evidence. It does not create the Skill by itself.

## Shared Skill Model

Use all ten dimensions in the [Skill Engine index](README.md#shared-skill-model):

| Dimension | Adaptive-skill question |
| --- | --- |
| **Skill Identity** | What recurring problem and coherent learned function distinguish this capability? |
| **Origin** | Which need, constraint, embodiment, environment, experiment, community, or cross-system interaction produced it? |
| **Requirements** | Which body, senses, tools, relationships, environment, magic, knowledge, and other owners remain necessary? |
| **Persistent Familiarity** | What legitimate Skill XP and Skill Imprint form after emergence rather than being assumed from repetition? |
| **Current Access** | Can the practitioner identify, reconstruct, and deliberately engage the developed method? |
| **Embodied Expression** | What can the present body and supporting systems actually perform? |
| **Practised Reliability** | Does the method survive relevant variation, pressure, fatigue, and imperfect conditions? |
| **Contextual Effectiveness** | Where does the adaptation solve its problem, and where is it overfit or harmful? |
| **Evolution State** | Is it an emerging candidate, recognized Skill, branch, or later transformed expression? |
| **Visibility** | Who notices, names, teaches, misclassifies, or remains unaware of the capability? |

No number of repetitions, crises, or crossed systems can be summed into an emergence score.

## Terms

### Adaptive Skill Candidate

A recurring developing method that may become an Adaptive Skill but has not yet shown enough coherence, distinction, integration, or reliability to be represented as one.

### Emergence Evidence

Observable change showing that a candidate has become a stable learned capability: reliable problem recognition, integrated method, correction from feedback, useful transfer across relevant variation, and bounded failure behavior.

## Emergence Conditions

An Adaptive Skill requires all of the following in forms appropriate to the capability.

### Recurring Need

The practitioner faces a recognizable class of problems more than once. The need may come from survival, disability, changed embodiment, scarce resources, mixed teams, unusual tools, hostile environments, or deliberate ambition.

Recurrence need not be frequent or numerically uniform. A problem can recur across a few consequential situations or many ordinary ones. The question is whether the learner had enough distinct evidence to build a method rather than repeat one accident.

### Active Engagement

The practitioner notices, attempts, compares, chooses, or meaningfully participates in the adaptation. Being carried by an ally, protected by equipment, or subjected to an effect does not make that effect a personal Skill.

Active engagement can be nonverbal, instinct-led, distributed, assisted, or partially unconscious where the being's actual cognition supports learning. It still requires the learner's own changed capability.

### Variation

The method encounters differences relevant to its claimed scope: new terrain, timing, bodies, partners, materials, signals, pressure, opposition, or resource levels.

One fixed rehearsal may create a habit. Variation reveals whether the learner recognizes the problem and adjusts rather than merely reproducing a protected sequence.

### Feedback

The learner receives usable information about what worked, failed, cost too much, harmed something, or behaved unexpectedly. Feedback may be sensory, social, magical, environmental, measured, taught, or inferred from consequences.

Feedback must be available to the learner through a real route. The GM cannot claim invisible correction that no participant or system could detect.

### Adjustment

Later attempts change in response to feedback. Adjustment may alter timing, posture, allocation, signal choice, tool use, risk, sequence, cooperation, or interpretation.

Blind persistence despite changing results is repetition, not evidence of adaptation.

### Integration

Perception, judgment, execution, correction, and recovery become a connected method. The capability can be reconstructed and applied without depending entirely on the original accident or one external guide.

Integration does not require perfect theory. A practitioner may embody a method tacitly while still showing stable recognition and correction.

### Meaningful Consequence

The adaptation matters to a real objective, responsibility, constraint, or risk. Consequence may include survival, protecting others, completing work, preserving resources, avoiding harm, maintaining trust, or producing a reliable result.

Pain, danger, killing, and deprivation are not required. Safe but demanding experiments, instruction, rehabilitation, and cooperative practice may provide meaningful consequences.

### Coherent Function

The result addresses a bounded recurring problem with enough internal unity to be represented and trained. It must do more than collect unrelated actions under an appealing name.

### Distinct Representation

The capability is not already represented adequately as an existing Skill, technique, branch, Profession practice, species trait, magical effect, equipment feature, or relationship. Novel origin does not require a new label when an existing Skill already owns the change.

### Practised Reliability

The method works consistently enough within its stated scope that success is not explained only by luck, one opponent, one environment, or continuing supervision.

Emergence can be recognized before mastery. The Skill may remain narrow, costly, fragile under unfamiliar pressure, or dependent on explicit requirements.

## Emergence Routes

### Spontaneous Emergence

A practitioner improvises repeatedly under real conditions and gradually notices that the improvisations share a stable method. Recognition may occur after the capability already has some reliability.

Spontaneous does not mean causeless or instant. Earlier attempts, failures, adjustments, and integration must be traceable.

### Deliberate Cultivation

A practitioner identifies a recurring problem and designs varied exercises, tools, feedback, and applications to solve it. A desired name or planned outcome remains a hypothesis until the changed capability is demonstrated.

### Survival Adaptation

Scarcity, predation, disaster, disability, or hostile conditions may create strong recurring need. Only the adaptive work becomes Development. Exposure, suffering, or survival alone grants nothing.

### Hybrid Behavioral Technique

Methods from several existing Skills or instincts may be coordinated into a new behavior. If the result remains ordinary simultaneous use, record coordination or a technique. If integrated practice produces a distinct recurring function, it may become an Adaptive Skill or later fusion candidate according to ownership.

### Body-Specific Adaptation

A being may learn around unusual proportions, missing structures, extra limbs, transformation, injury, artificial components, or a newly matured form. The Skill owns learned control; Physical or Species Development owns bodily change.

### Environmental Adaptation

A practitioner may develop a method for low mana, extreme pressure, unstable footing, unusual gravity, darkness, corrosive air, or another recurring condition. Bodily acclimation remains Physical Development, inherited range remains Species Development, and environmental protection supplied by equipment remains external.

### Social Adaptation

A recurring communication or coordination problem may develop a stable method suited to specific sensory models, cultures, roles, or mixed groups. Relationships, trust, authority, and consent remain separately owned.

### Magical Adaptation

A caster may develop around damaged Channels, changed laws, scarce resources, hostile interference, or nonstandard tools. The Adaptive Skill governs the learned method; Magical Development and the magic source govern access, capacity, cost, and effects.

### Cross-System Adaptation

A stable method may coordinate body, Skill, magic, tools, allies, environment, or Soul history. Every supporting effect retains its Owning System. The Adaptive Skill owns only the learned coordination or problem-solving function that was actually developed.

## Representation Test

When adaptive behavior appears, classify it at the smallest truthful scale.

### Ad Hoc Tactic

A one-time or weakly repeated solution tailored to immediate facts. Record the outcome and learning clues, not a persistent capability.

### Habit

A response that has become easy to initiate through repetition. It may support or obstruct a Skill but has not shown enough adaptive judgment or variation to be one.

### Technique

A specific execution method inside an existing Skill. Use this when the parent Skill already owns the function and the innovation does not require independent Development.

### Skill Branch

A coherent narrower route within an existing Skill distinguished by sustained requirements, context, method, or tradeoffs. Use a branch where the new capability remains meaningfully dependent on the parent identity.

### Adaptive Skill

A stable, distinct, trainable capability with its own bounded function, requirements, evidence, and failure states that no existing Skill represents adequately.

### Species or Physical Adaptation

A change to the body, regulation, acclimation, tissue, inherited structure, or species potential. Skill may develop in using the result, but cannot claim the bodily change as its own effect.

### Evolution Matter

A possible species-stage, branch, mutation, or transformed form belonging to Phase 4. Behavior may later become evidence for an evolution route, but adaptive practice cannot declare the biological outcome.

## Recognition and Naming

An Adaptive Skill can exist before anyone names it. Naming helps teaching, reflection, records, and Visibility; it does not cause emergence.

The initial name should describe the coherent function rather than dramatize one success. Local cultures or system interfaces may assign different names to the same Skill Identity, while similar names may hide materially different Skills.

Recognition should identify:

- the recurring problem;
- the integrated method;
- current requirements;
- demonstrated scope and variation;
- known costs and failure states;
- relation to existing Skills and systems;
- evidence that the learner, not merely external support, changed.

## Failure and Maladaptation

### Overfitting to One Opponent

A fighter learns one rival's rhythm so deeply that the method fails against different reach, tempo, intent, or anatomy. This may remain a tactic or become a narrow specialty; it is not general combat mastery.

### Reinforcing Injury

A practitioner develops movement that avoids immediate pain by overloading another structure. The adaptation is real but harmful. Rehabilitation may preserve useful awareness while replacing the damaging method.

### Trauma Response

Hypervigilance, freezing, appeasement, aggression, or dissociation may become reliable responses to cues. Reliability does not make them Skills automatically, desirable Development, or a uniquely profitable route. Represent the actual effect and use care, agency, and recovery rules.

### Context-Dependent Habit

A low-mana casting sequence works in one chamber because its ambient rhythm is constant. Outside that chamber it collapses. The result is a local habit or tactic until the practitioner understands and adapts to relevant variation.

### False Confidence

Protected practice, hidden assistance, poor feedback, or a few lucky successes make the learner overestimate the method. Fair tests reveal the limits without retroactively declaring all learning unreal.

### Harm to Other Capabilities

A winged being learns to brace its wings as shields, but constant stiffness degrades fine flight control. The defensive method may be real while creating incompatible habits, opportunity costs, or need for retraining.

### Failed Emergence

A candidate may remain incomplete because the problem disappears, feedback is unavailable, requirements cannot be met, the method never becomes coherent, or an existing Skill already represents it. Failed emergence does not create a consolation Skill or erase genuine clues learned.

## Worked Examples

### Fighting in an Unfamiliar Body

A reincarnated swordsman awakens in a four-armed body with different reach and balance. Early motions are distorted carryover. Through varied sparring, recovery analysis, and deliberate role separation among the arms, the fighter develops **Multi-Line Guarding**. It is a current monster-tree Adaptive Skill, not four copies of Swordsmanship and not a free trait.

### Navigating Through Vibration

A blind burrowing monster repeatedly crosses unstable tunnels. It compares echoes through its feet, tests surfaces, maps signal loss, and learns to distinguish hollows from moving bodies. The trait supplies vibration sense; **Substrate Mapping** is the learned Adaptive Skill. On stone streets its effectiveness changes and requires recalibration.

### Casting Around Damaged Magical Channels

A caster with a damaged Channel cannot sustain a former flow pattern. Under supervised practice, the caster develops staggered pulses, rest intervals, and external grounding. **Interrupted-Flow Casting** preserves a bounded function with lower output and new timing risks. It does not heal the Channel or make damage profitable.

### Coordinating a Mixed-Species Team

A leader repeatedly works with beings that communicate through speech, scent, light, and shared vibration. The team develops cross-confirmation signals, delay-aware orders, and explicit failure calls. **Multimodal Field Coordination** belongs to the practitioners who actually learn their roles; it does not create trust or perfect mutual understanding.

### Surviving Low-Mana Environments

A magical scout learns to budget sensing pulses, distinguish silence from suppression, route tasks through mundane tools, and recover after sparse use. **Low-Mana Operations** is a Skill in decision and control. Bodily tolerance, mana capacity, and the environment's laws remain separate.

### Using Wings as Shields

A large flier repeatedly protects grounded companions from debris. Early blocks tear feathers and destabilize landings. Adjusted angles, partial folding, ally spacing, and immediate recovery create **Wing Guarding**, a body-specific technique or Adaptive Skill depending on distinct practice. Wings remain a trait, and defensive specialization may reduce aerial flexibility.

### Communicating Without a Shared Language

Travelers repeatedly negotiate using objects, gesture, drawings, timing, and confirmation. They develop a stable method for establishing referents, checking consent, and detecting false agreement. **Common-Ground Signaling** is not fluency, cultural knowledge, diplomacy mastery, or mind reading; it is a bounded social Adaptive Skill.

## Adaptive Skills and Reincarnation

Once a genuine Adaptive Skill forms, its Skill XP and Skill Imprint persist under ordinary Skill rules. The solved constraint, supporting body, environment, tools, allies, and external effects do not persist with it.

A later incarnation assesses the Skill through [Reincarnation Skill Crossover](REINCARNATION_SKILL_CROSSOVER.md). Repeatedly seeking incompatible bodies or recreating the same constraint does not award a fresh copy. Only genuinely new integrated learning can deepen or transform the retained history.

## Safeguards

- **No action spam:** repeated inputs, named combos, macros, drills, or staged loops do not create Skills without changed capability.
- **No crisis requirement:** safe training and thoughtful experimentation can satisfy meaningful consequence; suffering is not optimal progression.
- **No result-only proof:** survival, victory, damage, speed, spectacle, or one successful improvisation does not establish emergence.
- **No external-support theft:** tools, allies, magic, institutions, and transformations retain their effects and requirements.
- **No trait conversion:** learned use cannot turn anatomy, acclimation, mutation, or Evolution into a personal Skill effect.
- **No duplicate labels:** if an existing Skill or branch owns the adaptation, update that record rather than creating another Skill.
- **Routine consolidation:** when later evidence shows that several legitimately emerged records represent one capability, apply [Skill Consolidation](SKILL_CONSOLIDATION_AND_SCOPE.md) without denying their original emergence or counting shared Development twice.
- **No free fusion:** coordinating multiple Skills does not create fusion or duplicate their benefits.
- **No unlimited scope:** an adaptation remains bounded by the problem and variation through which it developed.
- **No retroactive invention:** the GM must be able to identify prior attempts, feedback, and change before recognizing a hidden emergence.
- **No compulsory behavior:** adaptation does not override current agency merely because a response has become familiar.

## GM Procedure

When an Adaptive Skill may emerge:

1. state the recurring problem and proposed bounded function;
2. identify the learner's active engagement and actual Origin;
3. trace variation, feedback, adjustment, integration, and meaningful consequence;
4. identify all body, tool, social, magical, environmental, and metaphysical requirements;
5. test whether an existing Skill, technique, habit, branch, Profession, trait, effect, or relationship already owns it;
6. distinguish the learner's change from external support;
7. examine reliability, contextual limits, maladaptation, and failure behavior;
8. classify the result at the smallest truthful representation;
9. if a Skill emerged, record all ten shared Skill dimensions and its relation to the current tree;
10. reject duplicate XP, action-spam, crisis farming, body bypass, and premature Evolution claims.
11. during ordinary review, flag substantially overlapping established Skills for evidence-based consolidation rather than spawning further narrow copies.

## Scope Boundaries

This document does not define:

- Skill evolution or fusion;
- biological adaptation formulas, mutation, species stages, or Evolution;
- the complete magic system;
- automatic system-interface notifications or universal emergence thresholds;
- one catalog of possible Adaptive Skills;
- campaign-specific adaptations or playtest results.
