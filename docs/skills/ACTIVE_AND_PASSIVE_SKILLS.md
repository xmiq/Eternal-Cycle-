# Active and Passive Skills

## Purpose

This document defines active and passive expression as modes through which a Skill operates. These modes describe initiation, direction, maintenance, monitoring, correction, and interruption. They are not absolute power categories, rarity tiers, or separate sources of capability.

A Skill may be mostly active, mostly passive, or use different modes for different functions. Its mode does not change the Skill's owner, waive its requirements, or determine its effectiveness without context.

## Core Rule

An **Active Expression** requires deliberate invocation, direction, timing, selection, or maintained attention. A **Passive Expression** operates through stable Habit, perception, adaptation, automatic regulation, conditioned response, or continuously available competence without requiring deliberate control of every step.

Passive does not mean free, permanent, invisible, or infallible. Active does not mean externally visible, slow, magical, or stronger. Both modes remain bounded by Current Access, embodiment, practice, resources, capacity, context, and disruption.

## Expression Modes and the Shared Model

Apply mode claims to the complete [Shared Skill Model](README.md#shared-skill-model):

| Dimension | Expression-mode question |
| --- | --- |
| **Skill Identity** | Which bounded learned capability is expressing, rather than which mode is being used? |
| **Origin** | How was this active, passive, or mixed expression legitimately developed? |
| **Requirements** | Which body structures, senses, resources, tools, attention, triggers, permissions, relationships, or environments does the mode require? |
| **Persistent Familiarity** | What history of deliberate or automatic expression persists without guaranteeing current access? |
| **Current Access** | Can this incarnation initiate, permit, notice, interpret, redirect, or stop the mode? |
| **Embodied Expression** | Can the current body and available systems perform and regulate it? |
| **Practised Reliability** | Does the mode remain dependable through pressure, interruption, misleading cues, and conscious correction? |
| **Contextual Effectiveness** | Is active control, passive response, or mixed operation useful for this objective and risk? |
| **Evolution State** | Has part of the Skill become internalised, deliberately reclaimed, translated, fused, or otherwise transformed? |
| **Visibility** | Who can perceive the action, its signs, its consequences, or only the practitioner's claim? |

Modes are recorded per function and context. A Skill is not assigned one permanent active/passive label when its initiation, maintenance, and correction operate differently.

## Terms

### Skill Expression Mode

The way a specific function of a Skill is initiated, directed, maintained, monitored, corrected, or interrupted. Active, passive, and mixed expressions remain modes of one capability rather than separate power categories.

### Active Expression

A Skill expression requiring deliberate invocation, direction, timing, selection, or maintained attention from its present owner.

### Passive Expression

A Skill expression operating through integrated Habit, perception, adaptation, conditioned response, automatic regulation, or continuously available competence without deliberate management of every step.

### Internalisation

Development through which parts of a previously deliberate process become stable perception, regulation, judgment, or response. Internalisation reduces some conscious load but does not erase costs, limits, or the ability to retrain and override.

### Background Load

The attention, energy, sensory bandwidth, bodily capacity, magical throughput, memory, or opportunity consumed by a maintained or automatic expression even when it is not the focus of deliberate thought.

### Conscious Override

The current incarnation's deliberate attempt to pause, redirect, suppress, or replace an automatic response. Override may carry delay, effort, reduced performance, or risk without surrendering executive agency.

## Modes Across One Skill

Initiation, continuation, selection, and correction can use different modes.

| Pattern | Example |
| --- | --- |
| **Active initiation, active maintenance** | A caster deliberately shapes and continuously directs a moving barrier. |
| **Active initiation, passive maintenance** | A practitioner enables a trained sensory filter that then regulates a known range of input. |
| **Passive initiation, active follow-through** | A learned cue produces an alert, after which the practitioner deliberately chooses a response. |
| **Passive initiation, passive response** | A conditioned recovery step triggers after a familiar loss of balance. |
| **Passive monitoring, active correction** | Background analysis flags an anomaly that the practitioner consciously investigates. |
| **Mixed parallel expression** | A duellist's stable guard and distance sense support deliberate feints and attacks. |

Mixed operation must identify which choices remain deliberate. Calling a whole Skill passive cannot conceal decisions that still require timing, targeting, consent, resource allocation, or moral judgment.

## Common Forms

### Toggled Expressions

A toggled expression begins or ends through a deliberate command, intention, posture, phrase, signal, or other established input. After activation it may require active maintenance or shift to passive regulation.

The toggle does not make the effect self-sustaining. Startup time, shutdown delay, accidental activation, resource drain, suppression, and missed feedback remain possible.

### Maintained States

A maintained state persists while its requirements are supplied. Deliberate attention may continuously steer it, or internalised regulation may handle familiar adjustments within a bounded range.

Passive maintenance still incurs Background Load and may fail gradually, end abruptly, or continue harmfully when the practitioner overlooks changed conditions.

### Reflexes

A reflexive expression links a recognised cue to a rapidly available response. Skill-developed reflexes are shaped through practice and correction; bodily reflexes and [Retained Instincts](../soul/RETAINED_INSTINCTS.md) retain their own ownership.

Fast initiation does not guarantee correct cue recognition, target selection, complete execution, or suitability. A reflex may create an orientation, guard, withdrawal, or interruption while deliberate action remains with the current incarnation.

### Conditioned Responses

A conditioned response becomes easy to initiate when a learned trigger occurs. It may support safety and speed, but narrow conditioning can fire on deceptive cues, persist after circumstances change, or conflict with another response.

Conditioning proves neither broad judgment nor a whole Skill. Retraining must test false cues, changed contexts, and recovery after interruption.

### Sensory Filters

A sensory filter prioritises, suppresses, combines, or interprets recurring input. It may reduce noise, highlight a known pattern, or protect against overload while consuming sensory and attentional capacity.

Filters can hide anomalies, amplify bias, miss unfamiliar channels, or become overwhelmed. They do not create senses the body or another valid system does not possess.

### Resistance

Learned resistance may use breathing, posture, attention, exposure management, magical regulation, pain interpretation, or interruption of a known influence. The Skill owns the trained response; anatomy, immunity, protective equipment, blessings, and magical defences remain separately owned.

Passive resistance is not immunity. Dose, novelty, duration, fatigue, incompatible embodiment, and competing demands can exceed it.

### Bodily Regulation

A practitioner may internalise regulation of breath, tension, balance, temperature response, pain management, exertion, or recovery pacing within real bodily limits. Physical Development owns bodily capacity; the Skill owns learned monitoring and control.

Automatic regulation can conserve attention but may become maladaptive under injury, illness, different anatomy, or an environment whose signals resemble familiar conditions.

### Automatic Defence

An automatic defence prepares or begins a bounded response when a trained threat pattern is recognised. It may adjust a guard, interrupt a movement, brace, disperse force, raise an alarm, or trigger an available defensive method.

It cannot guarantee detection, choose perfectly among allies and enemies, create unavailable resources, or perform unrestricted action without current agency. Deception, surprise, saturation, and conflicting threats remain effective counters.

### Background Analysis

Background analysis compares incoming information with trained patterns and raises candidate interpretations. It may support diagnosis, navigation, tactical awareness, magical monitoring, language, or craft.

It has bounded throughput. Too many streams, poor data, unfamiliar patterns, fatigue, and confirmation bias can produce silence, false alarms, or shallow interpretation. A flagged pattern is evidence for attention, not infallible truth.

### Social Presence

Posture, pacing, listening habits, attention distribution, emotional regulation, turn-taking, and culturally learned signals may become partly passive. This can create a recognisable presence without becoming an aura or control effect.

Social expression remains observer- and culture-dependent. It creates no trust, authority, attraction, fear, obedience, or accurate interpretation by itself.

### Group Coordination

A practised group may internalise spacing, handoffs, signals, role coverage, fallback behaviour, and mutual correction. Some responses can become rapid and low-attention because participants share expectations.

The group owns its coordinated capability as established by the Skill or fusion rules. Passive coordination does not erase consent, transmit every member's Skill, prevent disagreement, or remain reliable after membership and goals change.

## Costs and Capacity

Passive expression moves work; it does not abolish it. Possible costs include:

- Background Load on attention, perception, memory, or decision capacity;
- ongoing stamina, mana, bodily regulation, material, or equipment demand;
- reduced sensitivity to information filtered as irrelevant;
- fewer resources available for conflicting active expressions;
- slower deliberate override or switching;
- maintenance practice needed to preserve calibration;
- opportunity costs from posture, focus, social presentation, or environmental setup;
- accumulated fatigue that is noticed only after performance degrades;
- vulnerability to cues designed to exploit the automatic pattern.

Active expressions also have costs: initiation time, decision load, visible or inferable preparation, interruption risk, delayed reaction, and the need to choose among competing actions.

There is no universal number of passive Skills a being can sustain. Capacity is assessed from the actual body, mind, magic, Skill demands, environment, and current condition for the stated situation.

## Disruption

### Surprise

An unexpected event may occur before active initiation or outside a passive cue set. A rapid response can still choose the wrong pattern when recognition is incomplete.

### Overload

Too many threats, signals, maintained states, decisions, or resource demands can exceed processing and regulation. Failure may appear as delay, tunnel vision, dropped maintenance, false alarms, or cascading mistakes.

### Fatigue

Fatigue reduces attention, timing, sensory discrimination, inhibition, correction, and resource control. Passive expressions may continue with declining quality because they do not demand conscious notice.

### Injury or Illness

Damage can alter movement, sensation, Channels, cognition, pain, or regulation. An automatic response calibrated to the healthy body may become impossible or harmful.

### Conflicting Inputs

Signals may suggest incompatible responses, several internalised modes may compete, or prior-life patterns may disagree with present evidence. Hesitation and inappropriate selection are valid failure outcomes.

### Suppression

Magic, technology, law, restraint, hostile influence, environment, or another established system may block initiation, maintenance, perception, or correction. Suppression follows its own rules and does not erase the Skill Imprint.

### Unfamiliar Embodiment

A new or altered body changes proportions, senses, latency, force, movement, resources, and threat cues. Persistent familiarity may make old responses feel immediate while their present execution remains uncalibrated.

### Conscious Override

The current incarnation may pause, redirect, or replace an automatic pattern. Override can cost attention, create a brief delay, reduce performance, or require retraining, especially under fear or overload. Those costs create tension without transferring executive control away from the current self.

## Current-Incarnation Agency

The active incarnation remains the executive owner of deliberate action. A passive Skill, Retained Instinct, Soul Echo, former Habit, Soul Title, prophecy, or institutional interpretation cannot silently choose speech, allegiance, targets, morality, relationships, or intentional action for the player.

Automatic influence may legitimately create:

- noticed urgency, aversion, attraction, tension, or familiarity;
- an orienting movement, startle, guard, sensory emphasis, or interrupted action;
- a candidate interpretation or warning;
- a cost, delay, or reduced confidence when consciously overridden;
- a need for retraining, accommodation, mediation, or recovery.

It may not silently create:

- possession or a second active incarnation;
- compulsory deliberate action without an explicit contested rule;
- perfect past-life behaviour in an incompatible body;
- hidden loss of player choice presented as ordinary passive mastery;
- moral truth or infallible judgment.

Where an explicit influence, compulsion, Soul Intrusion, or Echo Delegation rule applies, that system must state resistance, consent, scope, duration, knowledge, costs, and recovery. It cannot be relabelled as a passive Skill to avoid those safeguards.

## Reincarnation and Internalisation

Internalised expression may contribute to a Skill Imprint, Skill XP, Retained Instinct, or other proper persistent owner according to how it formed. These records remain distinct.

After Reincarnation:

- retained familiarity may make relearning active steps faster;
- a Retained Instinct may supply a cue or orientation without the whole Skill;
- a Soul Echo may explain or demonstrate its former response without installing current reliability;
- old passive patterns may be inaccessible, fragmented, distorted, harmful, or incompatible;
- the new body must calibrate timing, sensation, capacity, and safe limits;
- internalisation must be re-established through present practice before it can carry current automatic responsibility;
- Conscious Override remains available to the current incarnation even when an old response feels natural.

Reincarnation does not create a growing stack of always-on passives. Every present expression must have current access, a valid body and route, real capacity, and ongoing costs.

## Worked Examples and Failure Cases

### Internalised Guard and Deliberate Attack

A swordsman maintains distance and a familiar guard with partly passive posture and visual attention while selecting attacks deliberately. Internalisation frees some attention but does not parry automatically from every angle.

Against an unfamiliar many-limbed opponent, the filter tracks the wrong threat rhythm. The swordsman must consciously widen attention, sacrificing attack timing while recalibrating.

### Toggled Mana Filter

A mage deliberately enables a filter that suppresses familiar ambient mana noise. Once active, the filter runs passively and flags known spell signatures.

In a new Age, harmless background magic resembles an attack signature while dangerous magic uses an unfamiliar channel. False alarms consume attention, and the filter must be disabled or retrained.

### Learned Venom Resistance

A hunter learns to notice early symptoms, control exertion, slow absorption where physically possible, apply a prepared treatment, and avoid panic. Some regulation becomes passive.

This is not biological immunity. A different venom, larger dose, missing antidote, damaged circulation, or an incompatible reincarnated body can overwhelm the trained response.

### Breath and Pain Regulation

A climber internalises breathing rhythm, tension release, and pain interpretation for cold ascent. The mode reduces conscious load in familiar conditions.

After chest injury, the old rhythm worsens damage. Passive continuation is a failure; Conscious Override and medical adaptation are required.

### Automatic Defence

A guard develops a rapid brace and shield turn when peripheral motion crosses a trained line. The response reduces delay against familiar attacks.

A child running for safety triggers the same cue while the real attacker approaches slowly. Recognition and target judgment remain fallible, and the current guard chooses whether to complete the action.

### Background Diagnosis

A healer's observation habits passively compare colour, breathing, movement, scent, and mana signs. An anomaly prompts deliberate examination.

Fatigue and cultural bias make the healer overvalue a familiar symptom. The passive flag is useful evidence, not a diagnosis, and active testing must correct it.

### Social Presence

A mediator internalises open posture, measured pacing, attentive silence, and nonthreatening turn-taking. These habits support conversation without demanding constant rehearsal.

In a culture where sustained eye contact signals challenge, the same presence creates hostility. World Recognition and social meaning remain contextual.

### Pack Coordination

A practised pack automatically widens spacing when one member detects an ambush cue, while leaders deliberately choose whether to retreat, surround, or negotiate.

When a new member misreads the cue, the passive formation opens the wrong corridor. Collective practice helps recovery but does not erase individual error or command agency.

### Past-Life Reflex in a New Body

A reincarnated aerial predator feels an immediate urge to bank away from a descending shadow. In a terrestrial human body, the old wing response is impossible and a sudden turn risks a fall.

The Retained Instinct supplies warning and directional bias. The current incarnation chooses a grounded response and may later translate the cue through present training; the former reflex does not take control.

## GM Procedure

When adjudicating an active or passive expression:

1. identify the Skill and the specific function being expressed;
2. separate initiation, direction, maintenance, monitoring, correction, and stopping;
3. assign active, passive, or mixed mode to each relevant part;
4. identify Current Access, embodiment, triggers, resources, attention, tools, permissions, and relationships;
5. record Background Load and active decision costs;
6. test Practised Reliability against surprise, overload, fatigue, injury, conflicting inputs, suppression, and changed embodiment;
7. identify what information and choices remain with the current incarnation;
8. distinguish Skill expression from traits, bodily capacity, magic, equipment, Soul effects, and group ownership;
9. adjudicate Conscious Override and its causal cost without removing agency;
10. communicate observable signs, uncertainty, and failure rather than treating the mode label as a guarantee.

## Safeguards

- **No free passive:** every passive expression retains requirements, capacity use, opportunity cost, maintenance, and disruption.
- **No infallible automaticity:** a rapid or continuous process can miss, misclassify, overload, conflict, or continue inappropriately.
- **No visible-active assumption:** deliberate thought, timing, selection, and internal regulation may be externally subtle or undetectable.
- **No mode multiplication:** active and passive functions of one Skill do not become duplicate Skills or duplicate effects merely because their modes differ.
- **No universal internalisation:** mastery may internalise bounded steps, not every judgment, target choice, moral decision, or unfamiliar variation.
- **No body bypass:** passive expression cannot create senses, immunity, anatomy, Channels, energy, or movement the current body lacks.
- **No external-effect capture:** equipment, magic, allies, institutions, relationships, and environments remain owners or dependencies.
- **No passive stack:** past lives do not accumulate an unlimited set of simultaneous current effects without access, compatibility, capacity, and practice.
- **No concealed compulsion:** a passive label cannot bypass consent, resistance, agency, Soul Intrusion, or influence safeguards.
- **No perfect group mind:** passive coordination preserves participant differences, communication limits, consent, and changing membership.

## Scope Boundaries

This document does not define:

- complete Hidden Skill or discovery rules;
- Conceptual Skill effects;
- universal attention, mana, stamina, or passive-slot formulas;
- biological immunity, species traits, mutations, or Evolution;
- complete magic, social influence, group-mind, or equipment systems;
- contested compulsion or Soul Intrusion procedures;
- campaign-specific Skills or modes.
