# Monster Skill Trees

## Purpose

This document defines how monster learned capabilities form through species embodiment, Current Instincts, ecology, behavior, cognition, social learning, magical traits, and individual experience.

It establishes monster-native Skill structures without treating monsters as incomplete humans or prematurely defining the biological evolution branches reserved for Phase 4.

## Core Rule

A **Monster Skill Tree** is a contextual map of learned capabilities available through a monster's actual ways of sensing, moving, thinking, surviving, cooperating, creating, and transmitting knowledge.

Species traits and instincts create possibilities and biases. Meaningful experience, feedback, adjustment, and integration create Skills. No species label, natural weapon, instinct, kill count, or evolution event grants identical mastery to every member.

## Shared Skill Model

Use the ten dimensions in the [Skill Engine index](README.md#shared-skill-model) for every monster Skill or branch:

| Dimension | Monster-tree application |
| --- | --- |
| **Skill Identity** | Name the bounded learned function rather than the species trait, body part, prey type, or broad behavior alone. |
| **Origin** | Record the species, ecology, individual problem, community, tradition, Profession, magical route, teacher, or other provenance. |
| **Requirements** | State necessary anatomy, senses, cognition, materials, habitat, group structure, magic, tools, and relationships. |
| **Persistent Familiarity** | Preserve legitimate Skill XP and Skill Imprints without carrying the former species, body, instinct, or entire tree. |
| **Current Access** | Identify what the current individual can notice, recall, imitate, reconstruct, or be taught. |
| **Embodied Expression** | Check the current form's scale, structures, senses, movement, regulation, magical organs, and environment. |
| **Practised Reliability** | Assess use across pressure, hunger, injury, unfamiliar prey, changing terrain, social disruption, and imperfect information. |
| **Contextual Effectiveness** | Compare the Skill with the present objective, habitat, opposition, group, resources, and ecological consequences. |
| **Evolution State** | Record only Skill refinement or other Phase 3 transformation; biological Evolution remains separately owned by Phase 4. |
| **Visibility** | State whether the individual, group, prey, rivals, scholars, institutions, or user recognize or misclassify the Skill. |

These dimensions are qualitative and cannot be summed into a monster level, threat score, or universal Skill rating.

## Terms

### Monster Skill Tree

A contextual analytical structure connecting monster-native Skills, foundations, branches, requirements, techniques, traditions, and mastery paths through a stated embodiment, ecology, cognition, society, or individual history.

### Ecological Skill Route

A learning route shaped by recurring interaction with a habitat, resource, prey, predator, competitor, symbiont, or environmental hazard. Ecology creates problems and feedback; it does not grant the resulting Skill automatically.

### Monster Tradition

A transmissible body of monster practice, standards, stories, demonstrations, signals, tools, tests, roles, and judgments. Its form may be oral, chemical, gestural, architectural, magical, imitative, distributed, inherited through constructed records, or otherwise compatible with its practitioners.

## Owning-System Boundaries

Monster capability often involves several systems at once. Each effect keeps one owner.

| Capability | Owner | What it does not establish |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Species Trait** | [Species Development](../progression/SPECIES_DEVELOPMENT.md) | Reliable control, tactical judgment, or a learned Skill |
| **Current Instinct** | Current species, body, and upbringing | Conscious technique, adaptability, perfect information, or compulsion |
| **Learned Skill** | Skill Development and this Skill tree | Missing anatomy, a new species trait, or automatic biological change |
| **Adaptive Skill** | Later Phase 3 adaptive-skill rules | Evolution, mutation, or a reward for repetition alone |
| **Evolutionary Trait** | Species Development and later Phase 4 rules | Prior mastery or every Skill commonly associated with the evolved form |
| **Magical Ability** | Its species, magical, divine, item, or other source | Control, efficiency, interpretation, spells, or magical mastery by itself |
| **Environmental Adaptation** | Physical or Species Development according to its route | A learned technique unless meaningful practice develops one separately |

A dragon's breath organ may produce flame; breath control, range judgment, ally-safe use, aerial timing, and deliberate shaping are learned capabilities. A spider may inherit spinnerets and respond to web tension; designing load-bearing structures under unfamiliar conditions requires learned construction.

## Natural Capacity Is Not Mastery

### Natural Weapons

Claws, teeth, horns, tails, constriction, venom, breath organs, corrosive secretions, sonic structures, and similar features create Current Access to possible actions. Their safe and effective use depends on calibration, perception, timing, restraint, maintenance, positioning, and experience.

A newly matured venomous monster may inject venom reflexively. It does not therefore know dose control, selective incapacitation, venom conservation, treatment of accidental exposure, or coordinated use around allies.

### Instinct

Current Instinct can orient attention and initiate useful responses before conscious analysis. It may guide stalking distance, flight correction, nesting material, migration, threat display, or care behavior.

Instinct is not automatically:

- consciously accessible;
- reliable outside familiar conditions;
- adaptable to deception or novelty;
- morally authoritative;
- identical among all members of a species;
- immune to training, culture, conflict, or deliberate refusal.

A response becomes evidence of Skill where an individual can integrate feedback, distinguish relevant variation, and express the capability with growing reliability. Less reflective learning may still be genuine learning when the being can perceive consequences and alter later behavior.

## Tree Formation

### Embodiment

Monster trees begin from the body's actual possibilities. Limbs, scale, material, senses, speed, lifecycle, magical organs, distributed control, and means of communication shape available roots and prerequisites.

Embodiment constrains a route without dictating one destiny. Two winged monsters may learn fundamentally different flight Skills because one soars through thermal vision while another maneuvers by pressure sensing in tunnels.

### Ecology and Recurring Problems

Predators, prey, scavengers, symbionts, parasites, builders, migrators, guardians, and decomposers encounter different recurring problems. Ecological Skill Routes develop when an individual meaningfully adjusts to those problems rather than merely occupying a niche.

Scarcity may teach selective hunting, storage, migration, cooperation, cultivation, trade, or theft. It does not force one route or reward suffering by itself.

### Cognition and Learning Method

Cognition affects how a monster stores, tests, communicates, and combines learning. A being may reason with language, images, scent maps, rhythmic cycles, distributed consensus, programmed comparison, emotional association, mimicry, or other established processes.

Humanlike introspection is not the test for agency. A being has meaningful learning agency when it can register alternatives, respond to consequences, form or revise preferences within its nature, and alter future behavior. The GM should communicate those choices through cues the being can actually perceive and express.

### Social Learning

Parents, packs, swarms, colonies, solitary mentors, rivals, constructed archives, magical bonds, and institutions may transmit Skills. Social learning can provide safer practice, accumulated solutions, standards, and correction without granting mastery on membership.

A distributed group does not automatically possess perfect information or a single Skill. Communication range, delay, dissent, signal quality, memory ownership, casualties, and role-specific practice still matter.

### Individual Experience

Individuals develop different Skills because their bodies, environments, roles, teachers, injuries, choices, and problems differ. Common upbringing may create shared foundations while preserving variation in reliability, specialisation, and judgment.

A monster can learn beyond its expected ecology through sustained meaningful experience. It must obtain a valid route, understand or embody relevant feedback, and meet current requirements. Unusual learning can create a rare branch without rewriting the whole species.

## Ecological and Behavioral Routes

### Predation and Evasion

Predation may develop observation, approach, pursuit, ambush, target selection, energy budgeting, restraint, group timing, or safe feeding. Evasion may develop concealment, route choice, decoy behavior, threat reading, refuge selection, or coordinated escape.

Killing prey proves an outcome, not mastery. A successful ambush against one predictable target may fail against scent masking, unfamiliar terrain, defended young, disease, or organized resistance.

### Territory and Nesting

Territorial Skills may include boundary signaling, patrol design, intrusion assessment, escalation control, resource mapping, negotiation, and defense. They do not create ownership or compel outsiders to recognize a claim.

Nesting may include material selection, structural placement, concealment, sanitation, climate regulation, nursery care, and repair. A Current Instinct may begin the behavior; robust construction under changing conditions requires learning.

### Pack and Swarm Coordination

Pack coordination may develop spacing, role exchange, pursuit relays, silent signaling, protection, retreat, and shared target judgment. Loyalty, command authority, and trust remain Social Development or relationships.

Swarm behavior may depend on local signals, role differentiation, thresholds, chemical traces, resonance, or distributed feedback. An emergent pattern is not automatically an individually owned Skill. Record who or what learns: an individual, a stable collective, a role network, or a coordinating institution.

### Camouflage and Mimicry

Color change, shape, scent production, voice structures, or magical masking may be traits. Choosing backgrounds, controlling movement, reproducing a relevant signal, reading observers, and adapting after detection are learned Skills.

Mimicry reproduces appearance or behavior within its actual sensory and cognitive limits. It does not grant the copied being's knowledge, authority, relationships, memories, or Skill mastery.

### Venom, Webs, and Parasitism

Venom Skills may develop metering, delivery choice, conservation, mixture, timing, and hazard management. Venom chemistry remains a trait or produced resource unless another system changes it.

Web Skills may develop anchoring, tension control, repair, prey discrimination, signaling, shelter, transport, or construction. Silk production remains separate from knowing how to use it.

Parasitic Skills may include host identification, entry timing, concealment, resource regulation, exit, or nonlethal coexistence. Parasitic anatomy and lifecycle remain species-owned. Agency, consent, resistance, and harm rules apply wherever another person is affected.

### Flight, Burrowing, and Aquatic Hunting

Flight Skills may involve takeoff, stall recovery, current reading, formation, landing, aerial pursuit, hovering, or confined-space maneuvering. Wings or levitation access do not grant them.

Burrowing Skills may involve substrate reading, ventilation, reinforcement, vibration management, navigation, collapse response, and surface concealment. Digging anatomy alone does not supply structural judgment.

Aquatic hunting may combine pressure, current, salinity, sound, light, buoyancy, concealment, and three-dimensional pursuit. Terrestrial combat labels cannot be pasted onto these requirements without translation.

### Magical Sensing

A magical sense supplies access to an established signal. Learned discrimination separates background from anomaly, tracks change over time, identifies uncertainty, and avoids overload or false interpretation.

The Skill cannot perceive information the sense does not carry. Full magical rules remain owned by the later Magic phase.

## Tools, Craft, and Knowledge

### Tool Use

Monster tools may be held, worn, grown, secreted, sung into form, assembled collectively, embedded in territory, or operated through magic. Tool use requires compatible manipulation, perception, maintenance, and feedback.

Using a human-made tool does not make the Skill human. Its Skill Identity follows the developed function and method. Conversely, a human cannot claim a monster tool Skill without its real requirements or a translated route.

### Monster Craftsmanship

Monster craft may include woven lairs, resin architecture, cultivated fungi, bone shaping, current-guiding reefs, memory crystals, programmed bodies, enchanted territories, or conventional metalwork. Craft roots arise from the makers' bodies, materials, needs, and traditions.

Craftsmanship remains distinct from species secretion, Profession standing, equipment quality, and magical effect. A material produced naturally still requires judgment to turn it into a reliable work.

### Monster Scholarship

Scholarship may use writing, oral memory, scent archives, dream transmission, inherited machine records, distributed recollection, ritual reenactment, landscape marks, or other stable evidence practices.

Knowledge and research methods can be sophisticated without resembling a human academy. Scholarship develops source judgment, comparison, preservation, interpretation, and correction; it does not turn stored facts into execution mastery.

## Social Organisation and Civilisation

Highly social monsters may develop languages, laws, rites, trade, education, warfare, care practices, governance, archives, Professions, and institutions. These structures can preserve Monster Traditions adapted to nonhuman senses, lifecycles, bodies, and values.

Examples include:

- a migratory flight-school taught through changing formation positions;
- a hive archive encoded in chamber scent, vibration, and appointed interpreters;
- a dragon lineage that teaches oath memory through witnessed hoard objects;
- an artificial polity that audits copied records against independent sensory logs;
- an aquatic city whose law and navigation are recorded in maintained current patterns.

Institutional access and social recognition remain separate from Skill. Monster civilisation does not erase species variation, convert its members to human progression, or guarantee moral uniformity.

## Worked Examples

### Wolf-Like Pack Hunters

The species supplies scent, teeth, gait, hearing, and social tendencies. Young hunters begin with pursuit and grouping biases but must learn wind use, relay timing, prey selection, feints, disengagement, and protection of injured members. One pack develops endurance pursuit; another specializes in silent forest encirclement. Neither route is a universal wolf level.

### Spider Monsters

Spinnerets, silk composition, venom, and vibration structures are traits. A juvenile can produce a crude web through Current Instinct. Repeated repair under rain, unfamiliar anchor geometry, prey escape, and structural failure develops web construction. A city-dwelling spider lineage later teaches bridge-webs and alarm networks as a Monster Tradition; this remains Skill, not a new evolution stage.

### Dragons

A dragon's wings, breath organ, magical sense, and scale are species-owned. Flight control, breath shaping, aerial grappling, hoard curation, oath interpretation, scholarship, and territorial diplomacy develop separately. An ancient dragon may master legal memory yet remain an undisciplined flier after a long ground-bound life.

### Slime and Amorphous Beings

An amorphous body may distribute sensation and reshape within its trait limits. Learned Skills include isolating contaminants, moving fragile contents, using temporary pseudopods as tools, and coordinating shape changes under pressure. Dividing attention does not grant extra turns, perfect parallel thought, or automatic mastery of every assumed shape.

### Aquatic Predators

A reef hunter inherits gills, pressure sensing, and a propulsion form. It learns to use current shadows, distinguish injured motion from bait, coordinate vertical pursuit, and avoid reef collapse. On land, retained familiarity may inform spatial prediction, but the aquatic Skill does not directly provide terrestrial locomotion.

### Undead

An undead form may lack breath, pain, hunger, or ordinary fatigue according to its source. It must still learn balance, force control, sensory interpretation, maintenance, and tactics for that body. Memories from a former living person may provide knowledge or Persistent Familiarity but do not guarantee Current Access, calibration, identity continuity, or a free Skill bundle.

### Artificial Beings

A constructed guardian begins with designed sensors, movement limits, and programmed routines. Fixed instructions are not Skills by themselves. When the being compares outcomes, revises methods, integrates exceptions, and develops reliable judgment, learned capability can emerge. Copied data does not equal copied mastery, and maintenance access remains externally attributable.

### A Social Monster Civilisation

A termite-like civilisation builds climate-regulated towers through specialized castes, chemical notation, vibration councils, and collective apprenticeship. Individual builders learn local tasks; coordinators learn signal interpretation; archives preserve structural failures. No individual automatically owns the whole city's architecture Skill, while the institution's coordination remains real and causally important.

## Safeguards

- **No trait duplication:** anatomy, senses, natural weapons, magical organs, secretions, and lifecycle structures remain species-owned.
- **No identical species mastery:** shared traits or upbringing may create common access without equal learning, reliability, or specialisation.
- **No instinct-equals-Skill shortcut:** instinctive orientation requires meaningful learning before it supports adaptable reliable technique.
- **No kill-count progression:** killing, eating, surviving, or winning does not grant a Skill without relevant integrated change.
- **No evolution bundle:** maturation, transformation, mutation, or Evolution creates changed embodiment, not automatic mastery of its uses.
- **No renamed-human default:** use monster embodiment, senses, ecology, cognition, materials, and culture to determine the route.
- **No immutable behavior assumption:** instinct can conflict with experience, culture, deliberate choice, or changing conditions without ceasing to matter.
- **No cognition hierarchy:** unfamiliar or less language-driven thought does not erase agency, while unfamiliar cognition does not grant perfect coordination or analysis.
- **No ecological destiny:** a niche creates recurring pressures, not a mandatory personality, Profession, allegiance, or Skill path.
- **No Phase 4 shortcut:** this document cannot define evolution stages, branches, unlock requirements, mutations, or resulting forms.

## GM Procedure

When placing or adjudicating a monster Skill:

1. state the bounded Skill Identity and Origin;
2. separate species traits, Current Instincts, learned methods, magic, adaptations, and equipment;
3. identify the individual's embodiment, cognition, ecology, community, and actual learning route;
4. name current Skill Prerequisites and their Owning Systems;
5. distinguish Persistent Familiarity, Current Access, and Embodied Expression;
6. require evidence of feedback, adjustment, integration, and Practised Reliability;
7. assess Contextual Effectiveness without converting it to a universal threat score;
8. record Evolution State only as a Skill claim and leave biological Evolution to Phase 4;
9. establish Visibility and observer-specific recognition;
10. test whether the proposed label duplicates a trait, instinct, magical effect, Profession, relationship, collective outcome, or existing Skill.

## Scope Boundaries

This document does not define:

- monster species catalogs, ecological populations, or food webs;
- species stages, biological evolution branches, hidden evolution conditions, mutations, or hybrid forms;
- complete adaptive Skill, Skill evolution, Skill fusion, or crossover rules;
- the complete magic system;
- one universal monster tree or taxonomy;
- fixed cultures or institutions for every monster species;
- campaign-specific monsters, settlements, factions, or world state.

Those systems may use these Skill boundaries without treating a reference as implementation.
