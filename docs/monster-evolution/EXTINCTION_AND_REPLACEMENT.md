# Extinction and Replacement

## Purpose

This document defines how monster populations disappear, ecological functions fail, other populations expand into changed conditions, magical catastrophes reshape habitats, and damaged ecologies recover or settle into new states. It preserves extinction as a lasting world consequence without treating every decline as final or every vacant niche as a request for a replacement species.

## Core Rule

**Extinction** is the loss of a defined lineage or population from a defined scope after its viable routes of continued renewal within that scope have ended.

Extinction is an outcome of interacting causes. It is not an Evolution stage, reward, encounter-balancing tool, or automatic result of low population. Replacement is likewise causal: another population may perform some former functions because conditions, access, traits, learning, institutions, and time permit it. The world does not generate an equivalent species merely because a niche became vacant.

Every extinction, replacement, collapse, invasion, or recovery claim must identify:

1. the lineage, population, function, and geographic or world scope;
2. the relevant timescale and lifecycle;
3. the pressures, dependencies, and causal sequence;
4. remaining individuals, dormant stages, refugia, renewal routes, and uncertainty;
5. ecological, magical, social, and institutional consequences;
6. counterforces, intervention, adaptation, migration, and possible Evolution;
7. what persists after loss and what cannot be restored;
8. the evidence available to different observers.

## Terms

### Local Extirpation

The loss of a population from a defined habitat or region while viable members or renewal sources remain elsewhere.

### Global Extinction

The end of all viable members and ordinary renewal routes of a defined lineage across the world's current reachable scope.

### Functional Extinction

A condition in which a population still exists but can no longer perform a formerly consequential ecological, reproductive, magical, social, or institutional function at a meaningful scale.

### Extinction Debt

Future loss made likely by damage already sustained, such as insufficient renewal, fragmented habitat, missing partners, narrowed variation, or the delayed failure of a vulnerable lifecycle stage.

### Ecological Collapse

A sustained breakdown of several connected ecological functions and relationships such that the prior ecology cannot maintain itself without major reorganisation or intervention.

### Replacement Species

A species or stable lineage that expands, arrives, emerges, or is introduced into conditions changed by another population's decline and assumes some of its former ecological relationships.

### Functional Replacement

The restoration or substitution of a lost ecological function by one or more species, societies, institutions, magical processes, or environmental changes without recreating the former lineage.

### Invasive Population

A population whose arrival or expansion beyond its prior range causes sustained disruptive pressure because local relationships and counterforces do not presently contain its effects.

### Refugium

A causally established place, host, dormant stage, constructed reserve, or protected condition in which a threatened population can remain viable while disappearing elsewhere.

### Ecological Recovery

The restoration or reorganisation of viable ecological functions, relationships, renewal, and resilience after decline, disturbance, extirpation, or collapse.

### Reintroduction

The deliberate return of a still-viable lineage to a region from which it was locally extirpated.

## Scales of Loss

### Population Decline

A population may shrink, fragment, age, lose reproductive capacity, or abandon part of its range without becoming extinct. Decline should track the limiting relationship: bodies may survive while nurseries fail, adults may remain while a required host disappears, or a society may preserve eggs without the habitat needed for hatching.

Low numbers matter differently across species. One vast collective, a grove of linked bodies, a self-copying construct lineage, and a short-lived swarm require different evidence. Do not use one universal minimum population.

### Local Extirpation

Local Extirpation ends a population's current presence in a named area. It may leave:

- an empty or altered niche;
- predators, prey, parasites, symbionts, and societies without a former relationship;
- abandoned structures, nests, magic, waste, remains, or cultural records;
- a possible route for recolonisation or Reintroduction from elsewhere.

Local absence does not prove Global Extinction. Equally, a rumor that survivors might exist does not erase a well-supported local loss.

### Functional Extinction

Functional Extinction can precede bodily disappearance. Examples include:

- too few pollinators reaching dispersed host populations;
- an apex population too fragmented to regulate prey movement;
- a magical filter species surviving only in captivity and no longer cleansing its habitat;
- a sapient lineage retaining members but losing every institution capable of maintaining an essential ecological practice;
- only one reproductive form remaining in a lifecycle that requires several complementary forms.

The function and scale must be named. A population can be functionally extinct in one relationship while remaining active in another.

### Global Extinction

Global Extinction is a strong world-truth claim. Establish it only after considering:

- mobile, hidden, remote, subterranean, planar, dormant, larval, seed, spore, egg, host-bound, or constructed forms;
- known migration and dispersal routes;
- alternate renewal processes;
- viable captive, cultivated, summoned, archived-body, or artificial populations where canon supports them;
- whether separated forms belong to the same lineage;
- the reliability and limits of available evidence.

A dormant stage counts as continued viability only if it remains capable of resuming the lineage under plausible conditions. Preserved remains, memories, souls, descriptions, fossils, inert templates, and names are evidence of a lost lineage, not living continuation by themselves.

## Establishing Extinction Fairly

World truth and observer belief remain separate. Scholars may wrongly declare extinction; communities may preserve accurate survivor knowledge; the player may know less than the GM. The Campaign Record should distinguish:

- actual status and scope;
- evidence supporting that status;
- public or institutional claims;
- hidden but previously established survivors or Refugia;
- uncertainty that remains honestly unresolved.

Do not create a hidden survivor retroactively merely to reverse an inconvenient consequence. A Refugium must follow prior ecology, migration, preservation, world geography, magic, or another established cause and should leave proportionate signs where discovery is possible.

Conversely, certainty should not require omniscient inspection of every location. The GM can make a reasoned world-truth determination from the lineage's known scope, renewal, evidence, elapsed time, and established uncertainty.

## Extinction Processes

Extinction commonly develops through interacting pressures rather than one final death.

### Renewal Failure

A population cannot replace losses because births, spawnings, constructions, awakenings, metamorphoses, hosts, care systems, or vulnerable life stages fail.

### Habitat Loss and Fragmentation

Necessary conditions shrink or become separated. Survivors may be individually healthy while movement, mating, collective assembly, migration, or access to lifecycle sites becomes impossible.

### Dependency Loss

A host, symbiont, prey, magical source, institution, maker, maintenance process, or seasonal event disappears. The dependent population may carry an Extinction Debt before visible decline begins.

### Extraction, Conflict, and Persecution

Hunting, harvesting, domestication, war, containment, pollution, deliberate extermination, and displacement can exceed renewal or destroy key relationships. Individual combat strength does not protect a slow or narrow lifecycle from organised pressure.

### Disease and Regulatory Failure

Disease, parasites, curses, magical contamination, or incompatible environmental change may reduce survival or renewal. A disease need not kill adults to end a lineage if it disrupts a required stage.

### Evolutionary Trap

A formerly effective cue, specialization, or route can become harmful after rapid change. Evolution is not foresight and does not guarantee escape from a trap.

### Compound Failure

Several survivable pressures may combine into terminal loss. Scarcity can weaken renewal, fragmentation can prevent migration, and disease can then eliminate isolated Refugia. Record the chain rather than naming one dramatic event as the entire cause.

## Extinction Debt

Extinction Debt separates immediate appearance from long-term viability. A population may seem stable while:

- all remaining members are beyond a renewal stage;
- necessary genetic, template, caste, symbiont, or magical variation has been lost;
- a long-lived adult population has no surviving young;
- habitat fragments are each below lifecycle requirements;
- stored resources, artificial maintenance, or a fading magical source merely delay failure;
- institutions preserving renewal are collapsing;
- an irreversible catastrophe has not yet reached a dormant stage.

Debt is not destiny. Intervention may repair the causal deficit if a valid source, enough time, and workable capability remain. Denial, delay, or failed intervention can make later recovery harder or impossible.

## Ecological Collapse

Ecological Collapse is broader than one extinction. It occurs when multiple connected functions fail and reinforcing consequences prevent the previous network from maintaining itself.

A collapse may involve:

- disrupted resource collection, decomposition, purification, or habitat construction;
- cascading population losses or uncontrolled expansion;
- breakdown of magical regulation or containment;
- soil, water, atmosphere, mana, dream, spiritual, or constructed-medium degradation;
- failure of migration, reproduction, pollination, host transfer, or lifecycle timing;
- loss of societies or institutions that maintained ecological relationships;
- simplification into a less diverse but still persistent ecology;
- transition into a new stable or unstable regime.

Collapse has a scope. One valley can collapse while a continent remains resilient. A world can retain abundant life while losing the functions that made a former civilization possible.

Do not treat collapse as a scripted apocalypse or universal meter. Trace initiating causes, reinforcing loops, surviving functions, Refugia, counterforces, and the scale at which recovery is still possible.

## Replacement and Succession

Loss changes opportunities and pressures. Possible successors include:

- a nearby population expanding into released resources or habitat;
- an introduced population surviving where former counterforces are absent;
- several species dividing the predecessor's former functions;
- a related population diverging over generations;
- an unrelated population reaching a similar function through Convergent Evolution;
- a stable Hybrid Form entering a new niche;
- a society, institution, construct, spell, or technology providing Functional Replacement;
- no replacement, leaving a persistent loss or changed environment.

A Replacement Species is not the extinct species under a new name. Similar function does not imply shared anatomy, Soul lineage, Skill access, cultural role, vulnerabilities, or complete ecological equivalence.

Replacement often creates new consequences. A new pollinator may favor different plants. A construct purifier may require makers and fuel. A smaller predator guild may regulate prey numbers without reproducing the former migration patterns. Record gains, omissions, dependencies, and novel effects.

### Empty Niches Are Not Containers

A niche is a relationship network, not a slot waiting to be filled. When one participant disappears, the habitat, resources, prey, predators, disease, magic, and societies also change. The former niche may cease to exist before another population can occupy anything similar.

### Convergence Does Not Restore Identity

Convergent Evolution can produce a similar solution under similar pressures, but provenance remains distinct. A later gliding ambush predator does not resurrect an extinct winged lineage merely because observers give it the old name.

### Divergence and Replacement

A surviving population may spread into several changed habitats and diverge. Each branch remains constrained by available variation, origin, transition routes, time, ecology, and embodiment. Vacancy does not guarantee rapid or beneficial branching.

## Invasive Populations

Invasiveness describes a population in a receiving ecology, not a species' moral nature or universal essence.

Assess:

1. the arrival or range-expansion pathway;
2. which local resources and conditions permit establishment;
3. which predators, diseases, competitors, institutions, or magical limits are absent or ineffective;
4. the disruptive effects and who experiences them;
5. whether the population also provides new functions;
6. how effects vary across habitats and time;
7. containment, coexistence, adaptation, removal, or collapse possibilities.

Arrival can follow migration, trade, war, escaped cultivation, summoning, dungeon rupture, habitat corridors, climate shifts, magical transport, deliberate release, or Evolution into a previously inaccessible tolerance.

A native population can become invasively expansive after counterforces disappear. A newcomer can integrate without becoming invasive. Sapient members retain personhood and agency; ecological harm does not justify treating every individual as mindless, guilty, or disposable.

Control efforts have consequences. Removing a long-established invasive population may trigger another cascade, and institutions may exaggerate or deny ecological claims for political reasons. Separate world truth from rhetoric.

## Magical Catastrophes

Magical catastrophes can alter material conditions, resources, affinities, Channels, boundaries, lifecycle rules, time, dreams, spirits, weather, or other established magical relationships.

Every catastrophe must retain:

- a source or governing law;
- spatial and temporal footprint;
- onset, persistence, decay, recurrence, or propagation behavior;
- affected materials, beings, life stages, magic, and institutions;
- observable signs and uncertainty;
- counterforces, shelters, and unaffected Refugia;
- consequences when the source is suppressed, exhausted, redirected, or transformed.

Magic does not make extinction arbitrary. A catastrophe may be sudden, but its scope, selectivity, survival, and aftermath still follow established causality. It grants no automatic Mutation, Evolution, Skill, Soul growth, or useful adaptation to survivors.

If a catastrophe changes a world's magical law, forms dependent on the old law may decline, become latent, migrate, require maintenance, or disappear. New forms still need valid origins and cannot be declared replacements solely because the genre changed.

## Recovery

Ecological Recovery does not always restore a prior state. It may produce:

- **recolonisation:** survivors return through ordinary movement or dispersal;
- **Reintroduction:** caretakers deliberately return a still-viable lineage;
- **habitat repair:** limiting physical, magical, social, or constructed conditions are restored;
- **relationship repair:** hosts, symbionts, migration routes, lifecycle sites, or institutions are restored;
- **assisted transition:** populations receive bounded help to move, adapt, or use valid Evolution Routes;
- **Functional Replacement:** other participants restore selected functions;
- **new equilibrium:** a different ecology becomes self-maintaining without recreating all former relationships.

Evaluate recovery through viable renewal, restored functions, reduced dependency failure, resilience to recurring pressure, and the costs transferred elsewhere. Population increase alone may conceal continuing Extinction Debt.

### Reintroduction

Reintroduction requires a viable source population or renewal form belonging to the lineage. It also requires a receiving habitat, lifecycle support, sufficient variation or complementary forms where relevant, disease controls, and social or institutional capability.

Releasing bodies without repairing the cause of Local Extirpation repeats the loss.

### Reconstruction and De-Extinction

Remains, records, magical imprints, artificial templates, preserved tissue, Archive information, or related species may support a future reconstruction method if another owning system establishes it. Such work must identify:

- the material and informational source;
- what body and lifecycle can actually be produced;
- missing variation, symbionts, instincts, culture, and environment;
- whether the result is the former lineage, a descendant, a Hybrid Form, a construct, or a new analogue;
- Soul access and personhood under Soul rules;
- ecological consequences and continued maintenance.

Information alone does not create a living population. A reconstructed analogue does not silently restore lost individuals, relationships, culture, Skills, or ecological history.

## Evolutionary Rescue

A threatened population may avoid extinction through migration, current adaptation, social change, technology, magic, or valid Monster Evolution. Evolutionary rescue is possible only when:

- viable members and renewal remain long enough;
- relevant variation or a valid Evolution Route exists;
- pressure and Response Evidence are meaningful;
- transition conditions, compatibility, resources, and opportunity can be met;
- enough resulting forms survive and renew within the changed ecology;
- the response does not create a worse trap or unmanageable dependency.

Pressure does not owe a population a solution. Killing, suffering, repetition, rarity, desperation, and approaching extinction do not create a route by themselves. Rescue may preserve a descendant form while the predecessor becomes locally or globally extinct.

## Souls, Reincarnation, and Lost Species

Soul persistence and species persistence are different truths.

A soul that once inhabited an extinct species may retain only records protected by the Soul Engine, such as compatible Stat XP, Skill Imprints, Retained Instincts, Echoes, Titles, or Resonance. It does not carry the species' anatomy, population, renewal process, ecosystem, or complete progression tree.

An extinct form is not an ordinary Reincarnation candidate when no viable origin or embodiment route exists in the current world. A future valid reconstruction, Evolution, Hybrid Origin, divine process, or changed Age may create a new candidate route, but that route must define its body and consequences independently. Memory of a body is not a womb, egg, forge, habitat, or species.

The detailed interaction of Soul systems with Evolution belongs to [Soul Interaction with Evolution](SOUL_INTERACTIONS.md). Established Soul rules remain authoritative.

## Societies and Extinction

Sapient populations may anticipate, misunderstand, accelerate, resist, or survive ecological loss through:

- law, preservation, migration, cultivation, habitat engineering, and protected Refugia;
- conflict, persecution, extraction, denial, or unequal sacrifice;
- archives, oral traditions, education, institutions, and cultural memory;
- diplomacy across species and regions;
- deliberate Functional Replacement or restoration;
- contested definitions of purity, nativeness, extinction, and acceptable recovery.

Biological continuity does not guarantee cultural continuity. A lineage can survive while languages, institutions, relationships, and practices disappear. Cultural loss is not Global Extinction, but it is a real world consequence owned by social and historical records.

Likewise, a culture may outlive a body lineage through records, adoption, institutions, successors, or other peoples. That continuity does not make the extinct species biologically present.

## Long-Term World Consequences

Extinction and replacement can alter:

- Food Webs, Carrying Capacity, disease, resource flows, and habitat structure;
- migration corridors, predator-prey relationships, symbioses, and magical regulation;
- available Evolution Routes, Hybrid Origins, materials, medicines, and Skills;
- economies, settlement patterns, law, warfare, religion, identity, and political legitimacy;
- ruins, artifacts, Legacy Weapons, archives, myths, Soul Titles, and institutional memory;
- which bodies can be born, built, sustained, or offered through Reincarnation;
- later replacement, invasion, collapse, and recovery trajectories.

Time skips preserve these consequences and advance their causal chains at appropriate resolution. A World Reset may transform laws, geography, or available origins according to its own future rules; it does not silently reverse extinction. If a later Age contains a similar species, establish whether it is a surviving lineage, recreated form, convergent analogue, new origin, or genuinely restored lineage.

## GM Procedure

When adjudicating extinction, replacement, collapse, invasion, or recovery:

1. define the lineage, function, habitat, world scope, and timescale;
2. identify lifecycle and ordinary renewal routes;
3. trace pressures, dependencies, vulnerable stages, and counterforces;
4. distinguish decline, Local Extirpation, Functional Extinction, and Global Extinction;
5. examine established Refugia, dormant stages, migration, artificial support, and uncertainty;
6. record Extinction Debt and delayed consequences where causally supported;
7. trace Ecological Cascades and possible collapse without scripting every outcome;
8. evaluate successor access, compatibility, provenance, and omitted functions before naming replacement;
9. assess invasive effects contextually and preserve sapient agency;
10. give magical catastrophes source, footprint, duration, selectivity, and aftermath;
11. require viable sources and repaired causes for recovery or Reintroduction;
12. separate species continuity from Souls, memories, culture, Skills, and reconstructed analogues;
13. advance off-screen and time-skipped consequences through the World Engine;
14. record all current populations, losses, interventions, and beliefs only in the external Campaign Record.

## Worked Examples

### Delayed Loss of a Decomposer

A cave decomposer is eliminated from several chambers, but accumulated nutrients initially keep primary collectors healthy. Years later, waste locks away minerals and poisons spawning pools. The decomposer's Local Extirpation created an Extinction Debt for several dependent populations before visible collapse began.

### Functionally Extinct Magical Filter

A mana-filtering species survives in one protected enclosure. It is not globally extinct, but it no longer regulates the regional mana flow. Magical contamination spreads while caretakers decide whether habitat repair and Reintroduction are possible.

### Replacement Without Restoration

After a large burrower disappears, smaller diggers, plant roots, and a canal-building society collectively restore soil aeration. Functional Replacement occurs, but deep mineral turnover and old nesting chambers remain lost. No single successor becomes a duplicate of the burrower.

### Invasive Mana Grazer

A grazer arrives through a trade corridor and consumes a mana growth that had no effective local browser. It expands rapidly, suppresses native collectors, and also reduces dangerous seasonal surges. Its invasive effects are real but mixed; its sapient handlers and wild populations require separate moral and political judgments.

### Magical Catastrophe and Refugium

A long-lived magical storm makes exposed air lethal to a winged lineage. A previously established sealed canyon preserves a viable population, so the species suffers broad Local Extirpation rather than Global Extinction. When the storm decays, recolonisation remains limited by lost migration knowledge and changed prey.

### Evolutionary Rescue With Loss

A shoreline population has a valid route into a salt-tolerant, slower-growing form. Some transition and establish a new lineage while the original freshwater form vanishes locally. Evolution preserved descendants, not the unchanged predecessor, and the reduced renewal rate creates new vulnerabilities.

### An Extinct Former Body

A reincarnating soul once lived as a globally extinct monster. Its Retained Instincts still orient it toward the old species' vibration patterns, but its current body lacks the necessary organs. Unless the world establishes a valid new embodiment route, the extinct form is unavailable and the instinct remains translated, latent, or inaccessible.

## Safeguards

- **No extinction by bookkeeping:** no universal population threshold, meter, or single failed roll establishes extinction.
- **No retroactive survivor:** Refugia and dormant continuation require prior causal support and fair evidence.
- **No convenient resurrection:** Souls, memories, remains, records, and Archive information do not restore a lineage by themselves.
- **No vacancy spawning:** an empty niche does not automatically create a Replacement Species or Evolution Route.
- **No duplicate replacement:** similar function does not copy identity, anatomy, Skills, culture, or progression.
- **No guaranteed evolutionary rescue:** pressure and desperation may end in migration, intervention, change, or extinction.
- **No catastrophe handwave:** magical loss retains source, scope, duration, selectivity, counterforces, and aftermath.
- **No invasive essence:** invasiveness is contextual and does not determine morality, personhood, or universal behavior.
- **No consequence erasure:** time skips, off-screen play, Reincarnation, and changing protagonists do not reset ecological history.
- **No perfect restoration:** recovery may create a new equilibrium and must account for missing functions and transferred costs.
- **No campaign canon:** current extinct species, populations, disasters, and restoration projects remain external state.

## Scope Boundaries

This document does not define named extinctions, current population figures, a species catalog, a disaster table, universal recovery times, reconstruction magic, World Reset mechanics, or any campaign's ecological state.
