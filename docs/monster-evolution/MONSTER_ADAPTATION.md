# Monster Adaptation

## Purpose

This document defines Monster Adaptation as a cross-system description of how a current monster responds to changing conditions. It distinguishes adaptation from Adaptive Skills, Species Traits, Current Instincts, Mutations, and Evolution while preserving embodiment, current-life effort, maintenance, failure, and system ownership.

## Core Rule

**Monster Adaptation** is a functional adjustment by a current monster, group, or population in response to a condition, problem, opportunity, or changed relationship.

Monster Adaptation is not a separate Development Track, score, resource, or universal mechanic. Classify each Adaptation Response by what actually changed:

- bodily capacity belongs to Physical Development;
- a learned method belongs to the Skill Engine;
- existing anatomical expression belongs to Species Development;
- an inherited structure belongs to a valid origin route;
- cue-driven orientation may be a Current Instinct;
- magical access or regulation belongs to Magical Development and its source;
- collective practice belongs to Social, Profession, Skill, institution, or technology owners;
- habitat and population change belongs to ecology and the World Engine;
- structural variation outside maturation may be Mutation;
- lasting reorganisation into a distinct form belongs to Evolution.

One response can coordinate several owners. Each contribution is recorded once and retains its limits.

## Terms

### Monster Adaptation

A functional adjustment by a current monster, group, or population in response to a condition, problem, opportunity, or changed relationship. The term describes a result across existing owners rather than a separate progression system.

### Adaptation Response

A specific bodily, behavioral, learned, magical, social, ecological, structural, or evolutionary change made in relation to a defined condition.

### Adaptation Profile

A contextual map of the Adaptation Responses currently supporting a being, group, or population, including their owners, dependencies, maintenance, limits, and failure behavior. It is not a score or transferable package.

### Adaptation Maintenance

The continuing use, exposure, recovery, resources, practice, relationships, infrastructure, or conditions required to preserve an Adaptation Response at its current reliability.

### Adaptation Lag

The gap between a changed condition and the time required for a valid response to develop, spread, or become reliable.

## The Ownership Test

Ask one question first: **what changed?**

| Observed change | Primary owner |
| --- | --- |
| Immediate stress, dormancy, reflexive withdrawal, or current condition | Current body and existing Species Traits |
| Improved endurance, tolerance, recovery, coordination, or sensory discrimination within the current body | [Physical Development](../progression/PHYSICAL_DEVELOPMENT.md) |
| A stable learned method, tactic family, control pattern, or problem-solving capability | [Skill Engine](../skills/README.md) |
| Expression of anatomy already supplied by the current form | [Species Development](../progression/SPECIES_DEVELOPMENT.md) |
| An inherited anatomical, sensory, magical, material, or lifecycle structure | Species origin and Species Trait rules |
| A cue-linked pattern of attention, urgency, valuation, or response supplied by the current form | Current Instinct rules |
| A changed organ, tissue, module, or structure outside ordinary maturation | [Mutations](MUTATIONS.md) or another source-owned alteration |
| Magical Access, capacity, affinity, Channel regulation, or learned magical use | [Magical Development](../progression/MAGICAL_DEVELOPMENT.md), Skill, and source rules |
| Shared behavior, institution, Profession, technology, education, or policy | [Intelligent Monster Societies](MONSTER_SOCIETIES.md) and relevant Development owners |
| Changed niche, migration, food web, resource flow, or population distribution | [Monster Ecology](MONSTER_ECOLOGY.md) and World Engine |
| A lasting transition into a distinct form or Species Stage | [Monster Evolution](MONSTER_EVOLUTION.md) |

Do not choose the most exciting label. Use the smallest truthful owner or combination of owners.

## Adaptation Versus Adaptive Skills

An **Adaptive Skill** is a learned capability that emerges when repeated meaningful behavior develops a stable, distinct method not adequately represented by an existing Skill.

Monster Adaptation is broader. A monster may adapt without creating any new Skill:

- an existing Skill may improve or be applied in a new context;
- the body may condition itself through Physical Development;
- a Current Instinct may alter attention;
- a society may construct shelter;
- a species may migrate;
- a Mutation may change structure;
- a valid Evolution may change form.

An Adaptive Skill owns only the learned method. It cannot claim:

- increased tissue tolerance;
- a new organ or Species Trait;
- magical power supplied by a source;
- a tool's function;
- an ally's action;
- a collective institution;
- an Evolution Route or Evolved Form.

If an existing Skill already represents the method, update that Skill rather than creating an Adaptive Skill with an adaptation-themed name.

## Adaptation Versus Species Traits

A **Species Trait** supplies anatomy, physiology, sense, cognition route, magical structure, material, reproduction, or lifecycle characteristic of the current form.

Using a Species Trait under new conditions can produce adaptation in several ways:

- Trait Expression changes under health, maturity, or environment;
- Physical Development improves capacity or regulation;
- Skill improves deliberate use;
- current instinct changes attention or response;
- equipment or magic extends function;
- Evolution later reorganises the trait.

The trait itself is not earned by adapting. A claw does not become a learned Skill, and practiced claw use does not make claws portable through Reincarnation.

An individual structural change outside ordinary form is a Mutation or another alteration until a valid origin or Evolution rule integrates it.

## Adaptation Versus Instinct

A **Current Instinct** is a cue-linked orientation supplied by the current embodiment or valid origin. It can provide rapid attention, urgency, aversion, attraction, posture, or simple response tendency.

Instinct is not complete adaptation:

- it may be poorly suited to changed conditions;
- it can conflict with knowledge, values, or another instinct;
- reliable refinement may require Skill;
- safe expression may require Physical Development;
- culture may redirect or suppress its social meaning;
- current agency remains intact.

A repeated learned response is not reclassified as instinct merely because it becomes habitual or Passive. A Retained Instinct is a Soul Imprint from prior embodiment, not current anatomy and not compulsory behavior.

## Adaptation Versus Evolution

Adaptation changes function relative to a problem. Evolution changes organised Species Potential through a lasting transition into a distinct form or Stage.

A response remains non-evolutionary when it is adequately explained by:

- immediate condition;
- Trait Expression;
- Physical Development;
- existing or Adaptive Skill;
- magical regulation;
- equipment or external support;
- behavior, migration, symbiosis, or social practice;
- institution or technology;
- Mutation that has not been integrated by an Evolution Route.

Adaptation can create Response Evidence and Route Readiness. It does not become Evolution by accumulating enough uses. The actual route, compatibility, Transition Window, reorganisation, cost, and resulting form remain necessary.

After Evolution, prior Adaptation Responses are reassessed. Some remain compatible, some require recalibration, some become latent, and some cease because their structures or conditions no longer exist.

## Adaptation Versus Mutation

Mutation changes current or inherited structure outside ordinary maturation. Adaptation describes functional response.

A Mutation may be:

- an adaptive response when it improves fit under a current condition;
- maladaptive under another condition;
- neutral to the original pressure;
- useful only after Skill and Physical Development;
- harmful but later integrated into an Evolution Route.

Usefulness does not turn Mutation into Evolution, and harm does not make it Corruption without a corruptive source.

## Forms of Adaptation Response

### Immediate Regulation

Stress responses, dormancy, emergency metabolism, protective posture, sensory gating, or retreat use current structures. They may preserve survival without creating Development.

### Behavioral Adjustment

Changing route, schedule, target, diet, social distance, shelter use, or risk can solve a problem through choice, Habit, Tactic, Current Instinct, or learned Skill.

### Bodily Conditioning

Meaningful use, survivable exposure, feedback, recovery, and resources may develop endurance, tolerance, coordination, perception, regulation, or recovery within current Physical Potential.

### Learned Adaptation

A Skill is adapted, refined, translated, or created to solve a recurring problem. The method needs meaningful variation, correction, integration, and bounded failure.

### Magical Adaptation

The being changes magical control, Channel regulation, source use, or spell method under existing magical rules. Magic cannot invent anatomy or immunity merely because adaptation would be convenient.

### Social and Technological Adaptation

People coordinate care, education, policy, construction, tools, migration, law, exchange, or other collective responses. Collective Capability remains attached to roles, resources, and infrastructure.

### Ecological Adaptation

A being or population changes niche, relationship, territory, migration, diet, reproduction, or resource use. These changes may rely on behavior, Skills, society, or form but are recorded ecologically at population scale.

### Structural Adaptation

A Mutation, inherited variation, artificial modification, symbiosis, or other source changes structures in a way that affects fit. Provenance and inheritance remain explicit.

### Evolutionary Adaptation

A valid Evolution Route reorganises form in response to integrated history. Evolution is one possible adaptation scale, not the default or inevitable answer.

## The Adaptation Process

Assess adaptation through a causal loop:

1. **condition:** define the actual problem, opportunity, or relationship;
2. **exposure:** identify who encounters it, how often, and under what stakes;
3. **response:** identify behavior or change attempted through current access;
4. **feedback:** observe success, failure, cost, and unintended consequence;
5. **adjustment:** preserve, refine, replace, combine, or abandon the response;
6. **integration:** determine what has actually changed and which owner records it;
7. **variation:** test whether it remains useful outside the exact original case;
8. **maintenance:** identify resources, practice, exposure, care, and conditions it requires;
9. **consequence:** update ecology, relationships, pressure, and later options.

Repetition without feedback and change is maintenance or grinding, not new adaptation.

## Embodiment

Every personal Adaptation Response is limited by the current body.

Embodiment determines:

- available senses, movement, organs, materials, scale, and action economy;
- survivable exposure and recovery;
- which feedback can be perceived;
- compatible tools, magic, and environments;
- the ceiling and direction of Physical Development;
- which Skills can be expressed or translated;
- current lifecycle and transition options.

Two species facing the same pressure may adapt differently because their structures, needs, cognition, resources, and societies differ. One may adapt behaviorally while another conditions its body, changes habitat, uses magic, or evolves.

Former embodiment supplies no current structure. Functional correspondence can support translation, but resemblance in purpose does not make anatomy interchangeable.

## Timescale, Reversibility, and Maintenance

Adaptation Responses occur on different timescales:

- immediate reactions may last moments;
- behavior and simple learning may change over encounters;
- Physical and Skill Development require meaningful practice and recovery;
- institutions and technologies may require coordination across lives;
- population change and Evolution may unfold across renewal cycles.

An Adaptation Response may be reversible, persistent, dormant, seasonal, maintained, or lost through rust and changed conditions.

Ending the original pressure does not automatically erase adaptation. Pressure Release may:

- make a response less useful;
- expose its costs;
- allow unused capacity or Skill to rust;
- remove the maintenance stimulus;
- create a new opportunity for generalisation or regression;
- leave a stable, harmless response in place.

State what maintains each response rather than assuming permanent accumulation.

## Adaptation Lag

Conditions can change faster than valid responses develop. Adaptation Lag matters when:

- exposure becomes lethal before recovery or learning;
- lifecycle windows are too slow;
- knowledge cannot spread;
- institutions resist change;
- resources for a response are inaccessible;
- a specialist form cannot generalise quickly;
- uncertainty hides the real source.

Failure to adapt in time may cause injury, displacement, dependency, population decline, or extinction. The system does not guarantee a useful response because the stakes are high.

## Maladaptation and Overfitting

An adaptation can be real and still harmful, brittle, or poorly suited.

Common forms include:

- solving one opponent's pattern while becoming vulnerable to variation;
- suppressing pain while worsening injury;
- specialising around temporary abundance;
- developing a habit that conflicts with changed anatomy;
- relying on an institution or symbiont that later fails;
- conditioning under unsafe technique;
- interpreting a Retained Instinct as current certainty;
- changing ecology in a way that destroys the response's own resource base.

Maladaptation is not proof that Development was fake. Correcting it may require unlearning, rehabilitation, changed Skill, social reform, migration, or another Evolution Route. Do not reward deliberate harm as an efficient adaptation method.

## Combined Responses

Most meaningful adaptations combine owners.

A heat-survival profile might include:

- current heat-shedding Species Traits;
- Physical Development in regulation and recovery;
- a learned shelter and hydration Skill;
- clothing or architecture;
- social scheduling and care;
- magical cooling from a maintained source;
- migration during extreme seasons.

Record each contribution once. Their coordination may itself be a Skill or institution only when it has a distinct developed method. Do not sum the components into one adaptation score or copy shared evidence into every record.

## Reincarnation and Retained Adaptation

Final Death ends the current body's conditioning, Trait Expression, Mutations, current instincts, current form, and ecological position.

What may persist through its own rules includes:

- Stat XP from legitimate Physical Development;
- Skill XP and Skill Imprints;
- Retained Instincts;
- knowledge and memory access;
- Soul Titles, Echoes, Resonance, Depth, and Avatar integration;
- Social and Profession Imprints where canon permits.

The next body must rebuild current capacity. Retained history may improve orientation, learning, and Conversion Efficiency where compatible. It cannot reassemble the former Adaptation Profile as a package or bypass the new body's limits.

## Adaptation and Evolutionary Pressure

Evolutionary Pressure and adaptation form feedback:

1. pressure makes a problem consequential;
2. beings attempt responses;
3. successful and failed responses change exposure and ecology;
4. buffers may reduce pressure;
5. specialisation may create new dependencies;
6. Response Evidence may make a compatible Evolution Route relevant;
7. Evolution or social change alters later pressure.

The GM follows this loop rather than escalating pressure until Evolution occurs. A successful non-evolutionary response is a legitimate outcome.

## GM Procedure

When an adaptation claim arises:

1. define the condition, exposure, stakes, and current embodiment;
2. state the specific Adaptation Response rather than using adaptation as a complete explanation;
3. apply the Ownership Test to body, Skill, trait, instinct, magic, society, ecology, Mutation, and Evolution;
4. identify meaningful attempts, feedback, correction, integration, variation, and recovery;
5. distinguish current access from reliable expression;
6. state maintenance, dependencies, reversibility, Adaptation Lag, and failure behavior;
7. examine maladaptation, overfitting, and consequences;
8. record each contribution once and reject duplicate Development;
9. preserve current-life effort and Reincarnation boundaries;
10. store current Adaptation Profiles and population responses only in the external Campaign Record.

## Worked Examples

### Climbing Under New Gravity

A many-limbed monster enters a higher-gravity habitat. Its limbs are Species Traits. Increased joint stability and endurance are Physical Development. A new three-point movement pattern may become an Adaptive Skill if existing climbing Skills do not represent it. Shelter design belongs to craft or technology. None is Evolution unless a valid route reorganises the form.

### Predator Avoidance Without Evolution

A prey species changes activity time, develops warning calls, and shares protected burrows. Current instincts orient attention; learned alarm interpretation is Skill; communal shelter is Collective Capability; population timing is ecological change. The response succeeds without a new form.

### New Organ Without Mastery

A Mutation creates a pressure-sensing sac. The structure supplies sensory access but initially causes overload. Physical Development improves filtering tolerance, and a Skill develops for interpreting gradients. The Mutation does not grant either result automatically.

### Adaptive Skill Versus Trait Expression

A web-producing monster alters silk thickness in dry weather through existing gland regulation. The physiological range is Trait Expression. Choosing anchor patterns and tension based on humidity is a learned method. Only the latter can be an Adaptive Skill.

### Evolution Invalidates an Old Response

A monster evolves from a flexible crawler into a plated form. Its former squeeze-through-space Skill Imprint remains, but current armor prevents expression. Route-planning knowledge may transfer, while the movement method becomes latent and new balance requires practice.

### Retained Instinct Misfit

A reincarnated aerial hunter feels urgency around open sky while living in a heavy burrowing body. The Retained Instinct may aid weather attention but cannot produce flight. Treating the urge as a command would be maladaptation; the current life may reinterpret it through surface navigation or ignore it.

## Safeguards

- **No adaptation track:** Monster Adaptation creates no score, XP pool, rank, or universal progression record.
- **No duplicate ownership:** one change is recorded under its actual owner or explicit combination, never in every related system.
- **No Adaptive Skill inflation:** a new Skill requires a distinct learned method not represented by an existing Skill.
- **No trait learning:** practice cannot turn anatomy into a portable Skill or Soul trait.
- **No instinct compulsion:** current and Retained Instincts orient response without replacing agency or complete Skill.
- **No exposure reward:** pain, danger, disease, repetition, and survival do not guarantee beneficial adaptation.
- **No evolution inflation:** usefulness, permanence, or dramatic appearance does not make a response Evolution.
- **No body bypass:** all current personal responses respect current anatomy, health, lifecycle, resources, and recovery.
- **No permanent stack:** responses can rust, conflict, lose maintenance, become maladaptive, or end with the body.
- **No guaranteed rescue:** Adaptation Lag, failure, displacement, and extinction remain possible.

## Scope Boundaries

This document does not redefine Physical Development, Adaptive Skill creation, Species Traits, instincts, Mutation, Evolution, magic, social systems, or World Engine procedures. It classifies their interaction around adaptation and contains no current Adaptation Profiles or population state.
