# Species Stages

## Purpose

This document defines Species Stages as local forms within a particular monster lifecycle or Evolution family. It prevents stage labels from becoming a universal level ladder while preserving meaningful changes in anatomy, needs, vulnerability, social role, and route access.

## Core Rule

A **Species Stage** is a distinct, relatively stable form recognised within one species' lifecycle or Evolution structure.

A Stage is meaningful only when it changes organised Species Potential, lifecycle function, embodiment, needs, relationships, or available routes. Stage names do not measure universal power. A first Stage in one species cannot be ranked against a fourth Stage in another, and a later Stage need not be larger, stronger, rarer, or more intelligent.

## Terms

### Species Stage

A distinct, relatively stable form within a particular species' lifecycle or Evolution structure, defined by its own embodiment, Species Potential, needs, and transition relationships.

### Stage Transition

A bounded change from one Species Stage to another. It may be ordinary maturation, metamorphosis, Evolution, Mutation, or another established process; the route determines ownership.

### Transition Window

A period or condition during which a particular Stage Transition can begin. A window is route-specific and may depend on lifecycle, health, environment, resources, relationships, or another established source.

### Terminal Stage

A Stage with no currently supported further Stage Transition in a defined route family. Terminal means presently mapped, not metaphysically final or universally apex.

## Stage Versus Other Change

| Change | Distinction |
| --- | --- |
| Age | Elapsed time; age alone does not prove a new form |
| Maturation | Expected development within the current form; it may occur without a distinct Stage |
| Metamorphosis | A structured lifecycle reorganisation; it becomes a Stage Transition when it produces another defined Stage |
| Transformation | A source-owned temporary or lasting form change; it is not a Stage unless the species rules integrate it as one |
| Mutation | A source-owned structural alteration; it may affect a Stage without creating a new one |
| Evolution | A lasting transition into a distinct form or Stage through a valid Evolution Route |
| Social rank | Recognition or authority; it cannot establish biological or metaphysical Stage by decree |

Use a Stage only when it clarifies play. Minor size changes, seasonal coloration, health states, equipment configurations, and ordinary trait expression do not need separate Stages.

## Defining a Species Stage

Every Stage definition records:

- its form identity and relationship to the species or form family;
- origin and valid ways of entering it;
- anatomy, material, senses, movement, organs, Channels, and scale;
- Species Traits and current instincts;
- lifecycle role, duration, and expected maturation;
- resource, habitat, care, host, social, or magical needs;
- typical vulnerabilities and failure conditions;
- what Skills and Development can presently access or must recalibrate;
- available Stage Transitions and their windows;
- routes closed by entering or leaving the Stage;
- ecological and social consequences.

A label without these consequences is taxonomy, not a complete playable Stage.

## Stage Structures

Species may organise Stages in different ways.

### Sequential

One Stage normally follows another because the lifecycle depends on the order. Sequential does not mean universally stronger. An egg, dispersal form, reproductive adult, and dormant elder may each be essential for different purposes.

### Branching

A Stage may lead to several supported forms according to pressure, resources, behavior, relationships, choice, and route conditions. Branching is governed by [Branching Evolution](BRANCHING_EVOLUTION.md).

### Cyclic

A species may revisit forms in a recurring lifecycle. Returning to a known Stage can require real transition costs and does not erase Development or consequences.

### Seasonal or Conditional

A stable form may recur under defined environmental or magical conditions. If the change is temporary expression within one form, it may be transformation or Trait Expression rather than a separate Stage.

### Role-Differentiated

A collective or social species may develop body forms associated with care, defense, reproduction, construction, travel, or another function. Role does not imply lack of personhood or agency. Social assignment alone cannot create anatomy unless a valid origin or transition route does so.

### Open-Ended

A form family may have no fixed terminal Stage. New routes can become possible through world change, Mutation, Hybridization, or other established causes. Open-ended does not authorize arbitrary invention; each route still needs provenance and fair evidence.

## Transition Windows

A Transition Window may depend on:

- maturity, moulting, spawning, dormancy, deathlike suspension, or renewal;
- health and accumulated transition resources;
- season, habitat, mana condition, host, symbiont, or social protection;
- developed control of current Species Traits;
- a ritual, constructed chamber, catalyst, or institution;
- meaningful pressure and Response Evidence;
- a permitted choice, bond, Soul condition, or sacrifice.

Missing a window may delay, redirect, or close one route. It does not automatically kill the subject or lock all future Evolution. A permanently missed route must have a causal reason, not a punishment for failing to guess hidden timing.

## Ordinary and Evolutionary Stage Transitions

Not every Stage Transition is Evolution.

### Ordinary Lifecycle Stage

If every normally developing member enters a form through expected maturation, Species Development owns the transition. It may still be dramatic and dangerous.

### Evolutionary Stage

If entry depends on route-specific integration, pressure, branching conditions, or a lasting reorganisation beyond ordinary maturation, Monster Evolution owns the transition.

### Source-Owned Stage

Artificial, divine, corruptive, symbiotic, undead, or magical sources may establish Stages under their own rules. The Stage retains its provenance, maintenance, reversibility, and consequences.

The same outward form can arise through different sources. Similar appearance does not erase ownership or make transition conditions interchangeable.

## Stage Order and Skipping

There is no universal rule that every Stage must be visited.

A route may permit:

- direct transition between nonadjacent mapped forms;
- replacement of one Stage by an alternative;
- return to an earlier functional form through a new route;
- entry into a form from several origins;
- permanent inability to enter a Stage after incompatible reorganisation.

Skipping is valid only if the route explains how required structures, integration, and lifecycle functions are supplied. Soul history, high Development, external power, or a large resource payment cannot bypass a necessary Stage by implication.

## Gains, Losses, and Dependencies

Every Stage has a whole-body budget. A new structure changes energy, material, regulation, space, action, care, and ecology.

A later Stage may:

- gain a movement mode while losing access to confined shelter;
- gain reproduction while losing dispersal mobility;
- gain a Magical Organ while acquiring dangerous resource needs;
- gain sensory range while becoming vulnerable to overload;
- gain durability while increasing maturation time;
- lose a prior natural weapon because its material supports another structure;
- require collective care despite greater combat capability.

Do not preserve earlier Stages as stacked passive traits. The current Stage supplies current anatomy. Prior Stage Skills and Development remain accessible only where the new embodiment supports them.

## Stage Identity and Agency

Stage change can alter sensation, instinct, appetite, scale, communication, social recognition, and self-understanding. It does not automatically replace personality, loyalty, morality, memory, or player control.

A culture may celebrate, fear, regulate, rename, or misunderstand Stages. Those beliefs affect World Recognition and social opportunity; they do not silently rewrite biology.

Where a sapient being can choose among Stage routes, the GM presents known stakes and preserves meaningful agency. Where transition is involuntary, the cause and opportunities to resist or prepare must remain consistent with canon.

## Death and Reincarnation

Final Death ends the current Stage. Reincarnation supplies a new body's lifecycle and Stage. A soul does not begin the next life at its former Stage, skip maturation, or retain prior anatomy.

Prior Stage experience may persist as legitimate Retained Instincts, Skills, Stat XP, memory, Soul Titles, Echo history, or other Soul Imprints. Those records can guide redevelopment or qualify as permitted evidence without becoming a portable Stage.

## GM Procedure

When defining or adjudicating a Species Stage:

1. identify why a separate Stage is useful rather than ordinary maturation or Trait Expression;
2. define the Stage's embodiment, lifecycle function, needs, vulnerabilities, and Species Potential;
3. identify every valid origin and transition owner;
4. define Transition Windows, resources, agency, duration, and failure states;
5. record gains, losses, inaccessible prior structures, and new dependencies;
6. separate anatomy from Skills, Development, culture, rank, magic, and Soul history;
7. identify outgoing routes without treating them as a mandatory ladder;
8. test whether the Stage can persist ecologically and socially;
9. reject universal rank comparisons and automatic Stage skipping;
10. keep current individuals and populations in the external Campaign Record.

## Worked Examples

### Dispersal Form to Rooted Form

A light, mobile Stage carries spores across large distances. Under a valid transition, it becomes a rooted adult that gains powerful environmental sensing and reproduction but loses ordinary movement. The adult is later in the lifecycle, yet it is not universally stronger because relocation becomes nearly impossible.

### Alternative Defensive Stages

An immature shell-bearing monster can enter either a mobile plated Stage or a stationary fortress Stage. One preserves travel and independent feeding; the other gains local protection while requiring collective provisioning. The two are branches, not Stage Two and Stage Three.

### Cyclic Magical Stage

A species enters a luminous maintenance form during a recurring mana season, then returns to its ordinary material form. If both are stable, distinct embodiments with defined transitions, they may be cyclic Stages. If only coloration and mana output change, the effect is Trait Expression instead.

### Missed Window Without Arbitrary Punishment

A metamorphic Stage normally requires a protected water chamber. A displaced individual misses the ordinary season but retains an alternative delayed route because its current body has not dismantled the necessary structures. Whether the route remains open follows anatomy and lifecycle evidence, not GM preference.

## Safeguards

- **No universal ladder:** Stage count and order have meaning only inside one route family.
- **No later-equals-stronger rule:** every Stage is assessed by context, function, gains, and costs.
- **No age threshold alone:** time can open a lifecycle window but cannot purchase transition.
- **No stacked Stages:** current embodiment replaces prior Stage anatomy.
- **No free skipping:** nonadjacent transition requires an explicit route and supplied structures.
- **No role determinism:** body form does not dictate personhood, culture, profession, or moral worth.
- **No hidden timing trap:** important windows have fair clues and causal provenance.
- **No Soul carryover:** former Stages end at Final Death.
- **No false finality:** Terminal Stage means no currently supported route, not universal completion.
- **No taxonomy inflation:** trivial variation does not require a new Stage.
