# Branching Evolution

## Purpose

This document defines how one monster form can support several Evolution routes without becoming a deterministic menu or a universally fixed tree. It owns branch relationships, route maps, convergence, divergence, specialisation, generalisation, route closure, and discovery at the structural level.

## Core Rule

An **Evolution Branch** is one causally distinct route from a current form toward an Evolved Form.

Branches describe supported possibilities. They do not prescribe destiny, guarantee availability, rank outcomes, or prove that the map is exhaustive. A branch exists because a transition mechanism, compatible form, evidence, and consequences support it, not because a designer needs another option.

## Terms

### Evolution Branch

A causally distinct Evolution Route from a current form toward a particular Evolved Form under its own conditions, tradeoffs, and consequences.

### Route Map

A representation of known Evolution Routes and relationships among forms. It records current knowledge and canon; it is not the mechanism causing Evolution.

### Branch Point

A form or transition context from which more than one Evolution Branch is genuinely supportable.

### Route Family

A set of related forms and routes sharing meaningful origin, transition structures, or inheritance without implying a universal hierarchy.

### Branch Commitment

The point at which entering or completing one route changes the body or conditions enough to close, alter, or make other routes materially harder.

### Route Convergence

The relationship in which different Evolution Routes lead to forms with substantially similar function or, where provenance supports it, the same recognised form.

### Route Divergence

The relationship in which related origins develop distinct forms through different pressures, resources, behaviour, values, or transition histories.

## Route Maps Are Descriptive

A Route Map can answer:

- which routes are currently known;
- which current forms can enter them;
- what evidence, sources, and Transition Windows support them;
- where routes share ancestry or mechanisms;
- which branches close or alter others;
- which outcomes converge functionally;
- what remains uncertain.

A Route Map cannot establish:

- that every member sees or consciously selects every route;
- that all possible future Evolutions already exist on the page;
- that moving farther from the base means greater power;
- that one route must follow another because of layout;
- that hidden conditions can be invented retroactively;
- that a form is available in the current world merely because it is theoretically valid.

The [Evolution Tree Template](../../templates/EVOLUTION_TREE_TEMPLATE.md) is a recording tool. Canonical rules and world causality remain authoritative.

## Creating a Valid Branch

Every Evolution Branch defines:

1. **origin:** the current form or forms that can enter it;
2. **provenance:** why the transition exists;
3. **problem and function:** what recurring conditions or integrated way of living make it relevant;
4. **conditions:** compatibility, evidence, resources, relationships, lifecycle, and opportunity;
5. **transition:** onset, process, agency, vulnerability, failure, and completion;
6. **result:** anatomy, Species Potential, traits, needs, losses, and later routes;
7. **tradeoffs:** what the form gives up, depends on, or makes harder;
8. **consequences:** ecological, personal, social, magical, and World Engine effects;
9. **knowledge:** clues, uncertainty, and how the route can be discovered;
10. **ownership:** where every non-species capability remains defined.

If two proposed branches have the same form, function, requirements, costs, and consequences, they are probably duplicate labels rather than distinct routes.

## Availability Is Contextual

A branch can be:

- **structurally possible:** provenance and species rules support it somewhere;
- **locally possible:** current world conditions still provide the route's source and opportunity;
- **personally compatible:** the current body can enter it;
- **evidenced:** the being has relevant integrated history;
- **ready:** conditions align sufficiently for transition;
- **known:** observers possess reliable information about it.

These are separate facts. A route may be real but unknown, known but locally extinct, available to another population but incompatible with this body, or ready but deliberately refused.

Do not represent these facts as one unlock bar.

## Branch Choice and Agency

Sapient monsters may choose among understood routes, seek conditions, refuse transition, or accept uncertainty. Their choice matters but does not waive compatibility, resources, timing, or consequences.

Some routes are instinctive, involuntary, collective, imposed, or too poorly understood for informed selection. Their causes must still be fair and consistent. Coercion, corruption, divine alteration, and artificial engineering retain their own agency rules.

The GM presents evidence and known stakes rather than a game menu unless the setting contains an in-world interface that validly does so. Even then, the interface may be incomplete or mistaken according to established rules; it cannot create unsupported routes.

## Branch Commitment and Lock-In

Branches need not close at the moment they become relevant. Commitment may occur when:

- transition consumes or replaces structures needed by another route;
- a lifecycle window passes;
- a symbiosis or social role becomes incompatible;
- a resource dependency is permanently integrated;
- scale, material, cognition, or magical regulation changes;
- a choice or sacrifice is part of the route's actual meaning;
- entering one Stage changes the set of valid successors.

Lock-in is not automatically permanent. Another valid route may later recreate compatibility, but it must pay its own costs. Regret or retained knowledge does not restore lost anatomy by itself.

Where two routes remain physically compatible, a later form may integrate aspects of both only through an explicit branch or later Hybridization rule. It cannot collect both outcomes merely because both were once available.

## Divergent Evolution

Route Divergence occurs when related forms develop different solutions.

Drivers may include:

- different climates, terrain, predators, resources, diseases, or magical conditions;
- uneven exposure within one habitat;
- different symbionts, institutions, professions, technologies, or social values;
- distinct current-life Development and behavior;
- Mutation or hybrid origin;
- prior Branch Commitment;
- permitted Soul evidence.

Divergence may produce separate species, stable forms within one species, castes, or temporary route families. Define reproductive, metaphysical, social, and taxonomic relationships explicitly rather than assuming every branch creates total separation.

## Convergent Evolution

Route Convergence occurs when different origins solve similar problems through similar function.

### Functional Convergence

Forms perform a similar function through different structures. This is the default interpretation.

### Structural Convergence

Forms become anatomically similar because their valid mechanisms permit similar structures. Provenance and inherited history remain distinct.

### True Route Convergence

Different origins enter the same recognised Evolved Form only when the receiving form and transition explicitly support both origins. Shared appearance or function is not enough.

Convergence never transfers Skills, Development, Soul Imprints, or cultural knowledge between origins.

## Specialisation Branches

A specialisation branch narrows the conditions under which a form excels.

It must state:

- the bounded function or niche being refined;
- resources and conditions needed to sustain it;
- abilities or tolerances reduced, lost, or made costly;
- vulnerabilities under Pressure Release;
- why the result is more than a developed Skill;
- what later routes remain possible.

Specialisation does not mean a straight power increase. Extreme performance in one domain can coexist with severe dependence elsewhere.

## Generalisation Branches

A generalisation branch broadens viable conditions or functions.

It must state:

- which tolerances, resources, or environments become available;
- what peak efficiency, scale, or refinement is traded away;
- how the body supports several functions without copying every specialist trait;
- which Skills still require separate practice;
- where broad access remains shallow or unreliable.

Generalisation is not an all-purpose branch. It solves uncertainty and variation at the cost of focused advantage.

## Regression and Simplification Branches

A branch may remove expensive structures, reduce scale, simplify a lifecycle, suppress hazardous magic, or restore a more sustainable dependency pattern.

Do not label these branches lesser by default. They may open habitats and relationships unavailable to elaborate forms. They also create real loss: body-dependent capabilities become inaccessible and cannot be summoned from Soul history.

## Branches Across Individuals and Populations

One population can contain several forms where ecology and origin support coexistence. Different routes may depend on role, life stage, location, choice, or inherited variation.

Avoid assuming:

- every member has the same branch availability;
- one exceptional individual redefines the species;
- a majority form makes minority forms invalid;
- social caste and Evolution Branch are identical;
- population frequency measures route superiority.

The World Engine records current distribution. This document defines reusable route relationships only.

## Discovering New Branches

A previously undocumented branch may become canonical when the world supplies:

- a valid transition source or newly available condition;
- coherent relationship to existing Species Potential;
- fair evidence and discoverable clues;
- meaningful gains, losses, dependencies, and consequences;
- ownership compatibility with Skill, Development, Soul, Magic, and World Engine rules;
- distinction from existing forms and Mutations.

New discovery does not mean spontaneous invention at the moment of need. The cause may be newly created, newly encountered, or previously hidden, but it must exist before determining the outcome.

## GM Procedure

When defining or adjudicating branches:

1. identify the current form and all causally supported routes relevant to the situation;
2. distinguish structural possibility, local availability, personal compatibility, evidence, readiness, and knowledge;
3. define each branch's provenance, transition, resulting form, tradeoffs, and consequences;
4. identify when Branch Commitment occurs and why;
5. preserve player choice where the route permits informed agency;
6. test for duplicate labels, stacked traits, universal superiority, and hidden counters;
7. evaluate convergence by function and provenance rather than appearance alone;
8. evaluate divergence without assuming total species separation;
9. keep non-evolutionary responses and stasis valid;
10. update route maps only after the route is supported by canon, not to foreshadow an arbitrary outcome.

## Worked Examples

### Three Answers to the Same Floodplain

A lowland monster family faces recurring floods. One branch becomes a buoyant generalist able to travel between wet and dry ground. A second specialises into a rooted filter form sustained by stable currents. A third retains its form and builds elevated communal shelters through Skills and institutions.

The pressure supports two branches and one non-evolutionary answer. None is the mandatory flood upgrade.

### Functional Convergence Without Shared Species

A cliff species develops membrane gliding, while an unrelated cavern species develops magical buoyancy sacs. Both cross gaps efficiently. Their structures, magical requirements, failure states, and later routes remain different, so they are functionally convergent rather than one species.

### Divergence Inside One Habitat

Members living near a settlement gain stable access to heat, metal waste, and negotiated protection. Distant members face predators and seasonal scarcity. Their route histories diverge despite geographic proximity because exposure and social relationships differ.

### Branch Commitment

A shell-bearing form can pursue either distributed flexibility or a fused fortress shell. Once fusion replaces the joints required for distributed movement, the flexible branch closes. A later route could recreate articulation, but prior availability is not enough.

### A New Route With Prior Cause

A magical fault slowly changes mineral composition across a habitat. Years of observable shell instability, new deposits, and failed moults precede discovery of a stabilised crystalline branch. The route is newly known, not retroactively invented.

## Safeguards

- **No menu ontology:** a Route Map records knowledge; it does not cause or exhaust Evolution.
- **No branch ranking:** distance, rarity, number, and visual complexity do not measure universal power.
- **No deterministic response:** pressure can support multiple branches, stasis, adaptation, migration, or failure.
- **No free multi-branching:** combining routes requires explicit compatibility and transition rules.
- **No duplicate branches:** cosmetic names do not justify mechanically identical forms.
- **No retroactive discovery:** new routes need prior cause and fair evidence.
- **No Soul unlock list:** Soul history cannot make every prior species branch available.
- **No social caste essentialism:** institutions and roles do not create biological destiny by label.
- **No forced lock-in:** closure needs structural or causal explanation.
- **No false exhaustiveness:** an unknown future route is possible only through later established causality, not GM convenience.
