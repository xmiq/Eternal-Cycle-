# Mutations

## Purpose

This document defines Mutations as source-owned structural variation within or beyond a current monster form. It explains origin, stability, inheritance, integration, consequences, and the boundary between Mutation, adaptation, Evolution, corruption, artificial change, and Soul persistence.

## Core Rule

A **Mutation** is a change in a body's inherited or current structure outside ordinary maturation, caused through an established biological, environmental, magical, corruptive, artificial, divine, symbiotic, or other route.

A Mutation is not automatically beneficial, heritable, stable, controllable, or evolutionary. It changes what this body has. It grants no learned method, Soul progression, universal rarity, or right to a later form unless another rule explicitly receives it.

## Terms

### Mutagenic Source

The established cause and mechanism producing a Mutation.

### Stable Mutation

A Mutation the current body can maintain without continuing uncontrolled change under its stated resources and conditions.

### Unstable Mutation

A Mutation whose structure, regulation, expression, or spread remains unreliable, progressive, contradictory, or dependent on unresolved conditions.

### Heritable Mutation

A Mutation that a valid origin or renewal process can transmit to later bodies according to the species' actual inheritance rules.

### Integrated Mutation

A Mutation incorporated coherently into current Species Potential or a later Evolved Form, with defined regulation, costs, and interactions. Integration does not erase provenance.

### Mutation Load

The combined regulatory, material, energetic, developmental, magical, and structural burden created by Mutations in one current form. Mutation Load is qualitative, not a slot total.

## Provenance

Every Mutation identifies:

- source and mechanism;
- time and mode of exposure or origin;
- structures affected;
- whether change is local, systemic, inherited, infectious, progressive, or self-limiting;
- resources and regulation required;
- interaction with current Species Potential;
- stability and failure behavior;
- whether removal, suppression, inheritance, or integration is possible;
- ecological and social consequences.

"Random mutation" may describe an observer's uncertainty. It is not permission to ignore causality. The source can be probabilistic while still bounded by material, magic, biology, and form.

## Mutation Versus Adjacent Systems

### Adaptation

Adaptation is a functional response within current possibility. A Mutation changes structure. A being may adapt to use a Mutation, but developed control belongs to Physical Development, Skill, magic, or another appropriate owner.

### Species Trait

A Mutation may create or alter a Species Trait in this body. It becomes a normal Inherited Trait only if a valid origin mechanism transmits and stabilises it across renewal. One mutated individual does not redefine the species.

### Evolution

Evolution reorganises the form through a valid route. A Mutation may:

- create new compatibility;
- close routes;
- provide Response Evidence;
- destabilise transition;
- become integrated into an Evolved Form;
- remain independent after Evolution;
- be discarded during transition.

Mutation alone is not an Evolution Transition.

### Skill

A new organ can create Skill access. It does not grant reliable use. A mutated venom gland supplies material capability; dose control, targeting, restraint, and tactical use require present practice.

### Transformation

A temporary source-owned change is transformation rather than Mutation unless it leaves a structural alteration after the temporary form ends.

### Corruption and Purification

Corruption is defined by a harmful, alienating, coercive, unstable, or value-laden source, not unusual appearance. Corruption may cause Mutations. Purification removes or stabilises the corruptive source according to its rules but does not automatically restore an earlier body.

### Artificial and Divine Alteration

Designed or divine changes retain their source's authority, maintenance, purpose, and constraints. They may also count as Mutations when they alter current or inherited structure outside ordinary maturation, but the label does not erase external ownership.

## Sources of Mutation

### Inherited Variation

A body's valid origin may include structural variation not typical of its wider population. The origin process determines transmission and expression.

### Environmental Exposure

Radiation, toxins, minerals, pressure, temperature, disease, parasites, magical contamination, or other conditions may alter structures where an established mechanism permits it. Exposure does not guarantee a useful or themed result.

### Magical and Spiritual Influence

Mana fields, curses, blessings, rituals, divine action, Soul phenomena, or world instability may mutate a body only through defined access and ownership. Soul change and body change remain separate records.

### Symbiosis and Parasitism

A host and another being may exchange material, regulation, organs, or lifecycle functions. Each participant retains identity and agency unless their species rules establish otherwise.

### Artificial Intervention

Surgery, [Alchemy](../magic/ALCHEMY.md), grafting, construction, selective renewal, magical engineering, and other techniques can create Mutations. The relevant Profession, Skills, materials, maintenance, consent, and failure behavior remain real.

### Transition Failure

Interrupted maturation, metamorphosis, transformation, or Evolution may leave structural changes. Failure does not award a curated subset of intended benefits. Resulting Mutations follow the disrupted mechanism and consequences.

## Expression and Stability

A Mutation can be:

- dormant until a lifecycle or environmental trigger;
- continuously expressed;
- limited to one tissue, limb, organ, module, or magical structure;
- systemic across the form;
- mosaic, with different regions expressing different structures;
- progressive or degenerative;
- conditional on a host, resource, spell, device, or habitat;
- self-limiting after a defined change;
- conflicting with another Mutation or Species Trait.

Stability depends on integration, health, material, energy, magical regulation, immune or equivalent response, lifecycle, and environment. A visually stable Mutation may still impose hidden metabolic, reproductive, cognitive, or social costs.

## Benefits, Harms, and Tradeoffs

A Mutation may be useful, harmful, mixed, neutral, or context-dependent.

Possible benefits include new structure, tolerance, sensing, resource use, defense, movement, or compatibility. Possible costs include energy demand, pain, instability, reduced fertility or renewal, sensory overload, social consequence, lost routes, disease susceptibility, resource dependency, or conflict with existing anatomy.

Do not balance every Mutation as a neat exchange of equal powers. Require causal consequences rather than a point budget. A beneficial Mutation can still be rare or costly to maintain; a harmful Mutation can later become useful under changed conditions without having been secretly designed as a reward.

## Mutation Load and Compatibility

Multiple Mutations share one body. Assess whether their structures and regulation can coexist.

Conflict may arise through:

- competing material or energy demands;
- incompatible temperatures, fluids, mana states, or signals;
- duplicated organs without control architecture;
- altered scale or geometry;
- immune, spiritual, or constructed rejection;
- contradictory lifecycle triggers;
- action-economy and sensory overload;
- unstable feedback between magical effects.

There is no universal Mutation slot limit. The body's actual integration burden determines viability. More Mutations do not mean greater Development or power.

## Inheritance

A Mutation becomes heritable only when the species' origin or renewal process can encode, reproduce, construct, transmit, or otherwise supply it.

Inheritance must define:

- which origin process carries the change;
- what variation or failure is possible;
- whether the source remains necessary;
- whether expression is dormant, conditional, partial, or stable;
- interactions with hybrid origin and lifecycle;
- costs to renewal, care, or population viability.

Bloodline, eggs, spores, fission, templates, construction plans, magical seeding, host transfer, and collective renewal may all work differently. Do not assume human genetics as the universal model.

A Heritable Mutation may eventually contribute to a stable population form or Evolution Route. That is a later classification supported by persistence and integration, not an automatic promotion.

## Mutation and Reincarnation

Final Death ends bodily Mutations. Reincarnation does not transfer altered anatomy, inherited code, grafts, magical organs, resistances, or Mutation Load into the new body.

Experience may persist through Retained Instincts, Skills, Stat XP, Echoes, memory, Titles, or other valid Soul Imprints. Those records can help recognise, manage, or relearn around a similar Mutation. They cannot recreate it.

A Soul phenomenon that directly changes a new body requires an explicit receiving rule and is not justified merely because the former body was mutated.

## Mutation and Species Classification

Do not create a new species entry for every unusual body.

Consider a distinct form classification only when variation is:

- stable enough to describe coherently;
- materially different in Species Potential or lifecycle;
- supported by a repeatable origin or transition route;
- relevant to ecology and later development;
- not better represented as one individual's condition.

Institutional or cultural labels may still exist for rare Mutations, but social recognition does not establish biological inheritance or Evolution.

## GM Procedure

When adjudicating a Mutation:

1. identify the current form and Mutagenic Source;
2. explain the mechanism and structures affected;
3. distinguish Mutation from ordinary Trait Expression, injury, adaptation, Skill, transformation, and Evolution;
4. define expression, stability, maintenance, Mutation Load, and failure behavior;
5. state benefits, harms, dependencies, incompatibilities, and route effects;
6. require practice for deliberate control;
7. establish inheritance only through the actual origin process;
8. preserve source ownership for magical, corruptive, artificial, divine, symbiotic, and Soul effects;
9. test for consumed-trait copying, stacked bonuses, duplicate species, and Reincarnation transfer;
10. record current bodies and population frequency only in the external Campaign Record.

## Worked Examples

### Useful but Unstable Sense

Magical exposure creates a second vibration organ. It detects distant movement but overloads the existing sensory network during storms. Physical acclimation and a learned filtering Skill may improve control. The organ itself remains a Mutation, and its instability can close a delicate Evolution Route.

### Transition Failure

An interrupted moult leaves one limb with the intended Evolved Form's plating while the rest of the body remains unchanged. The plate is heavy, poorly supplied, and not proof of partial branch completion. A later route may integrate or discard it.

### Heritable Construct Variation

A self-assembling species copies a revised joint pattern into successor templates. The change is heritable through construction rather than blood. Faulty copying and maintenance demands remain part of its origin rules.

### Mutation That Becomes Route Evidence

A stable mineral-processing organ lets a monster survive an environment inaccessible to its original form. Over time, compatible pressure and integrated use may support a mineralised Evolution Branch. The Mutation creates compatibility and evidence; it does not skip transition.

### Purification Without Reversion

A curse causes spreading luminous scales and dependency on tainted water. Purification stops the spread and removes the dependency, but existing scales remain because structural change already occurred. Recovery and later integration follow current embodiment.

## Safeguards

- **No random reward table:** uncertain cause does not mean causeless or conveniently beneficial change.
- **No automatic Evolution:** structural variation does not complete a form transition by itself.
- **No Skill grant:** anatomy creates access, not learned control.
- **No consumption copying:** eating a being does not copy its Mutations without an explicit source mechanism.
- **No unlimited stacking:** Mutations share one body's material, regulation, and action constraints.
- **No automatic inheritance:** transmission requires a valid origin process.
- **No Reincarnation transfer:** bodily change ends with the body.
- **No cosmetic corruption rule:** provenance and effect, not appearance, determine corruption.
- **No guaranteed purification reset:** removing a source does not erase structural consequences.
- **No species inflation:** one unusual body is not automatically a distinct species or branch.

## Scope Boundaries

This document does not provide specific mutagens, mutation tables, inheritance formulas, body statistics, medical procedures, or live mutated beings.
