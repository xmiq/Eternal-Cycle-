# Monster Ecology

## Purpose

This document defines how monsters participate in habitats, resource flows, food webs, reproduction, competition, symbiosis, migration, territory, and extinction pressure. It establishes the ecological causes that later Monster Evolution rules may use without turning survival, killing, feeding, or occupying a niche into automatic progression.

Monster ecology includes biological, magical, spiritual, artificial, undead, and other valid forms. It does not assume that every monster eats flesh, reproduces sexually, ages like a human, or exists primarily to be fought.

## Core Rule

**Monster Ecology** is the causal network of conditions and relationships through which monster individuals, populations, other beings, resources, and environments enable, constrain, and change one another over time.

An Ecological Role or niche describes a relationship, not a power rank or destiny. Ecology creates recurring problems, available resources, feedback, constraints, and possible future evolution routes. It does not grant a Species Trait, Skill, mutation, evolution, Soul growth, or mastery merely because a monster survives, kills, feeds, migrates, reproduces, or becomes important to an ecosystem.

Every ecological claim must identify:

1. the habitat and timescale;
2. the resources or conditions involved;
3. the participating individuals or populations;
4. the direction and strength of their dependencies;
5. the limiting factors and counterforces;
6. the uncertainty and evidence supporting the claim;
7. the immediate and downstream consequences of change.

## Terms

### Monster Ecology

The causal network connecting monsters with habitats, resources, other beings, magic, Soul phenomena, reproduction, disease, waste, disturbance, and environmental change.

### Habitat

The physical, magical, spiritual, constructed, social, or mixed conditions in which a being or population presently lives and can access necessary resources.

### Ecological Niche

The bounded pattern of conditions, resources, activities, tolerances, relationships, and consequences through which a being or population persists. A niche is not a class, personality, combat role, or mandatory evolution path.

### Food Web

The network through which matter, energy, magic, or another established consumable resource moves among sources, consumers, scavengers, parasites, decomposers, and the environment. A Food Web is not limited to flesh-eating relationships.

### Carrying Capacity

The changing ability of a habitat to sustain a particular population under current resources, waste, disease, predation, competition, reproduction, migration, magic, infrastructure, and disturbance. It is contextual rather than one permanent number.

### Symbiosis

A sustained close relationship between unlike beings or populations. Its effects may be mutually beneficial, one-sided, harmful, conditional, or differently understood by the participants.

### Territory

A bounded area or resource network that a being or group occupies, uses, patrols, marks, negotiates over, or defends. Territory creates practical and social claims through causality; occupancy alone grants no universal ownership or authority.

### Migration

Repeated, directional, or large-scale movement in response to lifecycle, resources, climate, magic, danger, reproduction, social organization, or disturbance.

### Ecological Cascade

A causal sequence in which change to one ecological relationship alters other populations, resources, habitats, or pressures. A cascade is a possibility shaped by counterforces, not a predetermined script.

### Extinction Pressure

Conditions that reduce a population's ability to survive, replace losses, reproduce or renew, maintain necessary relationships, and occupy a viable habitat over time. Extinction Pressure is not a universal meter or guarantee of extinction.

## Ownership Boundaries

Monster Ecology owns the reusable relationships and qualitative procedure for describing how monsters participate in ecosystems.

It does not absorb adjacent systems:

| Claim | Owning system |
| --- | --- |
| A current body's anatomy, lifecycle, Species Traits, maturation, or valid body-changing route | [Species Development](../progression/SPECIES_DEVELOPMENT.md) |
| Learned hunting, migration, nesting, cooperation, sensing, craft, or resource-management methods | [Skill Engine](../skills/README.md) and relevant Development Tracks |
| Current bodily acclimation, conditioning, recovery, and regulation | [Physical Development](../progression/PHYSICAL_DEVELOPMENT.md) |
| Magical sources, Channels, Reserves, effects, and magical environmental laws | [Magical Development](../progression/MAGICAL_DEVELOPMENT.md) and the [Magic rules](../magic/README.md) |
| Soul identity, persistence, Resonance, Echoes, Titles, instincts, and Avatar Expression | [Soul Engine](../soul/README.md) |
| Current populations, quantities, resource stocks, wars, economies, disasters, and live world change | [World Engine](../world-engine/WORLD_ENGINE_OVERVIEW.md) and the external Campaign Record |
| Lasting transition into a distinct monster form or stage | Later Monster Evolution rules |

The same event may affect several owners. A drought can reduce carrying capacity, prompt migration, create opportunities for new Skills, change bodily condition, and make an evolution route relevant. Each result still needs its own causal evidence and owner.

## Ecological Model

Build an ecology through six connected layers.

### 1. Environment

Identify the medium and conditions in which life or equivalent persistence occurs:

- terrain, water, atmosphere, pressure, gravity, temperature, light, and season;
- mana density, magical weather, spiritual boundaries, curses, blessings, and other established supernatural conditions;
- structures, ruins, settlements, machines, dungeons, roads, farms, and altered habitats;
- hazards, refuges, nesting sites, migration corridors, and barriers;
- the timescale on which those conditions change.

An environment creates possibilities and limits. It does not prescribe one species, culture, or response.

### 2. Resource Inputs and Flows

Identify what participants require and where it comes from. Resources may include:

- food, water, air, minerals, heat, light, shelter, and physical space;
- mana, elemental exposure, ritual residue, divine attention, dream activity, emotion, memory traces, or another source established by its Owning System;
- hosts, prey, symbionts, caretakers, constructed maintenance, replacement parts, or suitable spawning media;
- information, safe routes, social care, inherited infrastructure, and collective labor where these are genuine dependencies.

Every resource needs a source, renewal or depletion behavior, access route, and consequence of use. Declaring a monster magical does not make its food, mana, waste, repair, or habitat infinite.

### 3. Ecological Roles and Relationships

Identify how participants affect resource flow, survival, reproduction, habitat structure, and one another. One species may hold several roles at different ages, places, seasons, or social positions.

Roles are descriptive. A monster can be a predator in one relationship, prey in another, a symbiont to a third species, and an ecosystem engineer through its nesting behavior.

### 4. Limiting Factors and Carrying Capacity

Identify what currently limits persistence. Relevant factors may include:

- total resource supply and renewal;
- access to nesting, spawning, metamorphosis, repair, or refuge sites;
- waste accumulation and habitat damage;
- disease, parasites, predators, and competitors;
- dependency on one host, magical condition, social institution, or seasonal event;
- reproductive rate, maturation time, care requirements, and vulnerable life stages;
- migration routes and barriers;
- conflict, extraction, domestication, persecution, conservation, or habitat engineering by civilizations.

Carrying Capacity changes when any of these factors change. A habitat may temporarily support an overshoot, hide a delayed collapse, or gain capacity through cooperation and engineering. It should not be treated as an exact universal formula before the World Engine defines local methods.

### 5. Disturbance and Response

Disturbance includes climate shifts, fire, flood, disease, magical surges, new predators, resource abundance, invasive species, war, settlement growth, dungeon activity, divine action, and other causes.

Possible responses include:

- altered behavior or schedule;
- Skill development or social learning;
- bodily acclimation within current potential;
- migration, dormancy, dispersal, or concentration;
- changes in reproduction, care, territory, diet, or cooperation;
- population decline, local disappearance, or replacement;
- later mutation or evolution where those systems provide a valid route.

No response is guaranteed. Several populations facing the same disturbance may solve it differently or fail for different reasons.

### 6. Consequence and Feedback

Every response changes later conditions. Migration may relieve scarcity while spreading disease. A new predator may reduce herbivores while allowing vegetation to recover. Magical feeding may stabilize a dangerous region while making the ecosystem dependent on continued consumption.

Trace consequences far enough to preserve the next meaningful decision. Do not force every causal possibility to occur, and do not stop a chain merely because it leaves the player's immediate view.

## Niches

An Ecological Niche is more than a location or diet. It may include:

- environmental tolerances and required conditions;
- source, timing, and method of sustenance;
- shelter, territory, nesting, spawning, or maintenance needs;
- predators, prey, competitors, hosts, and symbionts;
- movement and migration patterns;
- lifecycle and reproductive dependencies;
- social organization and learned resource practices;
- magical or spiritual relationships;
- effects on the habitat and other participants.

A species' potential niche may be wider than the niche it currently occupies. Competition, fear, disease, missing knowledge, institutions, or inaccessible migration routes may narrow actual use. Conversely, tools, cooperation, magic, or habitat engineering may open a niche without changing the species biologically.

Niche change does not automatically mean evolution. A population may alter behavior, learn a Skill, use infrastructure, migrate, or depend on another being while retaining the same form.

## Food Webs and Resource Webs

Food Webs should record the relevant flow rather than assume a simple linear chain.

### Sources and Primary Collectors

Some beings transform sunlight, minerals, ambient mana, heat, dreams, ritual waste, decay, or another established input into usable material or energy. The source's system defines whether that conversion is possible and what it costs.

Primary collection does not make a population self-sufficient. It may still require water, trace materials, hosts, safe sites, seasonal conditions, maintenance, or social support.

### Predators and Prey

Predation transfers resources by capturing, consuming, draining, infecting, or otherwise exploiting another organism or valid source.

- Predator and prey are relationship-specific labels, not universal power rankings.
- A predator's success depends on detection, approach, capture, handling, competition, injury risk, energy return, and prey response.
- Prey can alter habitat, coordinate, migrate, deceive, resist, bargain, or become dangerous.
- Sapience does not remove ecological needs, and ecological need does not erase agency, law, or moral consequence.
- Killing proves one outcome. It grants no automatic Skill, Species Development, evolution progress, Soul growth, or ownership of the victim's traits.

### Scavengers

Scavengers consume or repurpose remains, abandoned resources, shed structures, magical residue, or failed constructs. They may reduce disease and waste, compete with predators, transport material, or spread contamination.

Scavenging does not transfer the identity, Soul Imprints, Skills, Titles, memories, mutations, or evolution routes of what was consumed. Any exceptional transfer requires an explicit owning rule with compatibility, limits, and consequences.

### Decomposers and Recyclers

Decomposers return locked resources to the environment. They may be microbes, fungi, slimes, spirits, constructs, magical processes, or larger monsters.

Removing a decomposer can cause delayed scarcity or waste accumulation even when no dramatic predator-prey event occurs. Decomposition may also release dangerous mana, disease, curses, or Archive-relevant traces according to their proper systems.

### Parasites and Hosts

Parasites depend on hosts for resources, shelter, reproduction, transport, transformation, or access. The relationship may be lethal, chronic, episodic, inherited, negotiated, or capable of becoming mutualistic.

Parasitic anatomy and lifecycle remain species-owned. Host identification, restraint, concealment, or nonlethal regulation may be Skills. Where a sapient being is affected, ecology does not waive consent, resistance, harm, or social consequence.

## Symbiosis

Symbiosis may involve exchange of nutrition, protection, transport, habitat construction, waste processing, reproduction, sensory information, magic, or social services.

Distinguish:

- **mutualism:** each participant receives a meaningful benefit under current conditions;
- **commensalism:** one benefits while the other's material effect is limited or unresolved;
- **parasitism:** one benefits through a cost or harm to the other;
- **conditional symbiosis:** the relationship changes category when resources, life stages, environments, or behavior change.

These descriptions do not settle consent, affection, morality, ownership, or political legitimacy. A biologically mutual relationship can be socially coercive; a costly relationship can be knowingly accepted; different participants can misunderstand the exchange.

Symbiosis may alter available Skills, habitats, migration, reproduction, and future evolution routes. It does not merge species, souls, action economies, Development, or progression trees.

## Territorial Behaviour

Territory may secure food, mates or spawning opportunities, metamorphosis sites, mana sources, shelter, trade routes, sacred ground, archives, or social identity.

Territorial behavior can include marking, patrol, display, negotiation, tolls, shared boundaries, seasonal rotation, layered use, exclusion, and violence. Current Instincts may orient the behavior; learned signaling, mapping, negotiation, and defense remain Skills or Social Development.

Territory does not automatically create:

- legal or metaphysical ownership;
- perfect awareness of every intrusion;
- hostility toward every outsider;
- a permanent border;
- one dominant individual or hierarchy;
- an evolution branch.

Resource shifts, migration, succession, defeat, alliances, law, infrastructure, and ecological damage can all change territorial relationships.

## Migration

Migration may follow:

- seasonal food, water, climate, or mana cycles;
- breeding, spawning, maturation, molting, metamorphosis, or repair;
- prey movement or predator avoidance;
- disease, disaster, war, habitat loss, or magical instability;
- pilgrimage, trade, education, diplomacy, labor, or social tradition;
- dispersal into a newly available niche.

Migration depends on routes, timing, navigation, energy, rest sites, social coordination, and tolerable conditions. A blocked corridor can affect distant ecosystems. Arrival may create competition, symbiosis, cultural contact, disease exchange, or invasive pressure.

Migratory orientation may be a Current Instinct, Retained Instinct, Species Trait, learned Skill, cultural tradition, or combination. Identify the owner of each contribution.

## Reproduction and Renewal

Ecological continuity requires a valid route by which a population replaces losses or renews itself. Depending on the species, this may involve:

- sexual or asexual reproduction;
- eggs, spores, seeds, fission, budding, hosts, gestation, or spawning grounds;
- construction, copying, repair, assembly, summoning, awakening, or reanimation;
- transformation of environmental material under an established magical or spiritual law;
- recruitment into a stable collective where membership, identity, and personhood remain explicit.

Do not assume that every individual reproduces, that reproduction is voluntary, that all offspring share one culture, or that more offspring always increase long-term survival. Care, maturation, resources, disease, predation, social support, and vulnerable stages may be more limiting than births.

Reproduction supplies valid origins and population continuity. It does not grant the parent's Skills, Soul Imprints, Soul Titles, mutations, memories, or complete Development unless a separate inheritance rule explicitly owns the transfer.

## Competition

Competition occurs when participants constrain one another's access to a resource, condition, route, host, territory, role, or opportunity.

Competition may be:

- **indirect:** participants deplete or occupy the same resource without direct confrontation;
- **direct:** participants interfere, exclude, threaten, hunt, sabotage, regulate, or negotiate with one another;
- **within a species:** individuals, castes, generations, institutions, or populations pursue incompatible access;
- **between species:** unlike bodies or societies overlap in part of a niche;
- **asymmetric:** one participant is strongly affected while the other experiences little pressure.

Competition may lead to partition, migration, cooperation, innovation, specialization, generalization, conflict, decline, or later Evolution. No one outcome follows automatically.

## Magical Ecosystems

Magic may function as resource, habitat condition, hazard, signal, predator, symbiont, waste product, reproductive requirement, or ecological engineer.

Until complete Magic and World Engine rules exist, every magical ecological claim must state at least:

- the local source or law;
- who or what can access it;
- renewal, depletion, or transformation behavior;
- relevant environmental dependencies;
- effects of scarcity, excess, contamination, or interruption;
- observable signs and uncertainty;
- which parts remain Provisional during alpha play.

Ecology cannot define an unrestricted spell, mana economy, divine authority, Soul effect, or universal magical law. A creature that feeds on mana does not own every spell stored in that mana, and a creature that consumes spiritual residue does not consume the associated soul by implication.

Magical ecosystems can form feedback loops. A monster may absorb unstable mana and make a region habitable, while its waste feeds another species and its decline allows dangerous accumulation. Such a role creates dependence and consequence, not automatic moral virtue or universal power.

## Environmental Carrying Capacity

Assess Carrying Capacity qualitatively for one population, habitat, and period.

Ask:

1. Which resources are essential, substitutable, or merely helpful?
2. How quickly do they renew, move, decay, or become inaccessible?
3. Which life stage has the narrowest requirements?
4. What waste, disease, predators, competition, or conflict increases with density?
5. Which symbionts, infrastructure, institutions, or caretakers raise capacity?
6. Which seasonal, magical, political, or ecological changes make support unstable?
7. Can migration or dormancy bridge the limiting period?
8. What signs appear before collapse or recovery?

Use descriptions such as expanding, stable, stressed, overshooting, declining, fragmented, recovering, or unknown when useful. These are scoped observations, not universal population ranks.

## Extinction Pressure

Extinction Pressure rises when a population repeatedly cannot meet the requirements of survival, renewal, recruitment, reproduction, migration, or necessary relationships.

Possible causes include:

- habitat destruction or fragmentation;
- loss of prey, hosts, symbionts, caretakers, or magical conditions;
- new predators, competitors, parasites, or disease;
- extraction, persecution, domestication, war, or institutional policy;
- reproductive failure, vulnerable life stages, low variation, or long maturation;
- environmental or magical change faster than behavior, movement, or form can respond;
- an evolutionary specialization that becomes a trap when conditions reverse.

Distinguish local disappearance from global extinction. Absence from one place does not prove that no population remains elsewhere, while hidden survival needs prior causal support and discoverable evidence rather than retroactive convenience.

Extinction is not caused solely by losing battles. A personally formidable species may collapse through low reproduction, narrow habitat, disease, loss of mutualists, or inability to migrate. A physically weak species may persist through dispersal, care, infrastructure, or broad tolerance.

[Extinction and Replacement](EXTINCTION_AND_REPLACEMENT.md) defines extinction, replacement, collapse, invasive populations, and recovery in full. This section establishes only the ecological pressure leading toward those outcomes.

## How Ecology Drives Future Evolution

Ecology affects future Evolution by shaping:

- which problems recur long enough to matter;
- which resources and structures are available for change;
- which traits create useful tradeoffs in the current niche;
- which life stages survive to reach a transition;
- which symbioses, cultures, or technologies open new routes;
- which predators, competitors, diseases, and hazards test current forms;
- which migrations or disturbances expose a population to new conditions;
- which specializations become liabilities;
- which consequences make a branch sustainable after transition.

Ecology creates **routes, evidence, and constraints**, not rewards. Later Evolution rules must still establish a valid current body-changing route, compatible form, meaningful integrated response, transition conditions, gains, losses, changed needs, and consequences.

The same pressure can support several responses. Scarcity might favor storage, migration, cooperation, dormancy, broad diet, smaller bodies, magic use, habitat engineering, or decline. Different species may reach similar functions through unrelated anatomy, and related populations may diverge because they occupy different niches.

## GM Procedure

When defining or updating a monster ecology:

1. set the habitat, spatial scope, and timescale;
2. identify environmental and magical conditions without inventing unsupported laws;
3. trace essential resource inputs, renewal, movement, use, and waste;
4. identify life stages, reproduction or renewal routes, and vulnerable transitions;
5. map predators, prey, scavengers, decomposers, parasites, symbionts, competitors, and ecosystem engineers that materially matter;
6. identify territories, migration routes, barriers, and seasonal changes;
7. assess Carrying Capacity and its current limiting factors qualitatively;
8. identify disturbances, Extinction Pressure, and plausible counterforces;
9. separate Species Traits, Current Instincts, Skills, Development, magic, Soul effects, and social institutions by owner;
10. trace likely Ecological Cascades without predetermining them;
11. record world-specific state only in the external Campaign Record;
12. expose uncertainty and fair evidence rather than inventing hidden populations or dependencies retroactively.

## Worked Examples

### Web Hunters and Cave Recyclers

A web-building predator captures small flying creatures near warm cavern vents. Its abandoned silk traps moisture and shelters decomposer slimes, which recycle remains into mineral-rich seepage used by fungus-like primary collectors.

If the web predator disappears, flying prey may increase initially, but the recyclers lose shelter and the collectors later lose nutrients. The result is a possible Ecological Cascade, not proof that every cave follows the same pattern. Web production is a Species Trait, deliberate web placement is Skill, and no participant gains evolution merely by performing its role.

### Mana-Bloom Overshoot

A periodic mana bloom increases an established magical resource. Several consumers reproduce rapidly and temporarily exceed the habitat's ordinary Carrying Capacity. When the bloom ends, competition, migration, dormancy, predation, and die-off become plausible responses.

The bloom does not grant a universal magic bonus or evolution threshold. A later route might use repeated bloom conditions as one pressure or resource, but must define compatibility, transition, and post-bloom costs independently.

### The Contextual Apex Predator

A large hunter has no ordinary predator while healthy and adult. It remains dependent on one migratory prey species, a slow maturation period, and mineral pools needed for egg development. A blocked migration route and contaminated pools threaten the hunter more than direct combat.

The hunter is apex only within a stated relationship and habitat. Its size is not a universal tier, and its decline may release smaller predators while also increasing the prey population.

### Conditional Symbiosis

A shell-bearing monster shelters heat-producing microbes that let it survive a cold season. The microbes gain nutrients and transport. During an unusually warm period, their heat becomes harmful and the monster must shed, suppress, relocate, or renegotiate the relationship through whatever routes its body and cognition permit.

The same Symbiosis shifts from mutual benefit toward parasitic cost as conditions change. Neither participant merges with the other or inherits the other's Skills by association.

### Civilized Resource Management

An intelligent burrowing species learns to rotate nursery chambers, cultivate decomposers, and restrict harvesting near molting sites. These practices raise local Carrying Capacity through Skills, Professions, Social Development, and institutions rather than biological evolution.

If the institution fails, the ecological effect can fail even though the species remains unchanged. Sophisticated management does not make the monsters human or turn learned policy into an Inherited Trait.

## Safeguards

- **No experience ecology:** habitats do not emit XP, evolution points, or automatic rewards.
- **No kill or consumption progression:** predation, scavenging, parasitism, and feeding do not transfer traits, Skills, Soul Imprints, or evolution progress by themselves.
- **No combat hierarchy:** predator, prey, apex, parasite, and scavenger are contextual relationships rather than universal power tiers.
- **No ecological destiny:** a niche does not determine personality, morality, intelligence, culture, allegiance, Profession, or future branch.
- **No infinite magical resource:** magical sustenance needs a source, access route, renewal behavior, limits, and consequences.
- **No species stereotype:** shared ecology may create common pressures without making every member identical in Skill, behavior, culture, or Development.
- **No automatic evolution:** pressure and survival create evidence and routes only where the later Evolution system recognizes them.
- **No Soul transfer by feeding:** consuming bodies, magic, memories, residue, or remains does not copy a soul or its progression without an explicit Soul rule.
- **No retroactive ecology:** consequential hidden populations, resources, predators, or dependencies require prior causal support and fair discoverability.
- **No player-centered stasis:** ecology continues through ordinary world causality when the player is absent, dead, or in Interlife.
- **No World Engine replacement:** this document defines ecological relationships and qualitative assessment, not universal population formulas or campaign state.

## Scope Boundaries

This document does not define:

- a catalog of monster species or diets;
- live populations, maps, territories, migrations, or resource totals;
- complete population, disease, economy, climate, war, or disaster procedures;
- exact magical resources or spell effects;
- evolutionary-pressure qualification;
- species stages, branches, mutations, hybridization, apex forms, or transition procedures;
- extinction, replacement, invasive-species, or recovery procedures;
- any campaign's current ecology.

The other Monster Evolution rules may use these ecological foundations without treating a reference as completed implementation.
