# Economies

## Purpose

This document defines how Eternal Cycle represents production, exchange, allocation, trade, prices, wealth, obligations, credit, taxation, distribution, and economic adaptation.

Economies connect people, institutions, resources, capabilities, routes, information, authority, custom, trust, and coercion. They do not reduce all value to money or make markets the automatic answer to scarcity.

All named currencies, prices, contracts, debts, businesses, trade routes, and holdings are external campaign state.

## Core Rule

An **Economic Network** is a scoped pattern through which actors produce, control, exchange, allocate, transfer, consume, maintain, or owe goods, services, access, and obligations.

An economy is not one actor, market, treasury, or score. Every material economic claim identifies:

- actors and institutions involved;
- thing, service, access, or obligation at issue;
- production or source route;
- exchange or allocation method;
- place, time, and reachable participants;
- information and bargaining conditions;
- authority, custom, consent, coercion, and enforcement;
- distribution of benefit, cost, control, and risk;
- dependencies, alternatives, and uncertainty.

## Forms of Allocation and Exchange

Economies may combine:

- direct household or community provision;
- gifts and reciprocal exchange;
- barter;
- customary shares;
- communal stores and rationing;
- markets and negotiated prices;
- wages and contracted service;
- tribute, tax, tithe, levy, rent, toll, or fee;
- patronage and office-based allocation;
- debt and credit;
- command, requisition, seizure, theft, or coercion;
- sacred obligation, covenant, or source agreement;
- ecological exchange or symbiosis where personhood and ownership remain clear.

No form is inherently efficient, fair, voluntary, stable, or dominant. Several may govern the same item for different users or circumstances.

## Economic Claims

### Exchange Claim

An **Exchange Claim** states that specified parties can transfer defined goods, services, access, rights, or obligations under stated terms, timing, information, authority, and enforcement.

Possibility does not prove agreement. Agreement does not prove performance. Performance does not prove fairness, legality, legitimacy, or future repetition.

### Production Claim

A production claim identifies inputs, capable actors, tools, source permissions, process time, maintenance, output, quality, failure, waste, and distribution. Demand cannot create absent capability or inputs.

### Allocation Claim

An allocation claim identifies who controls a Stock or Flow, the rule used to distribute it, eligible recipients, priority, exclusion, delay, leakage, and appeal or resistance.

### Ownership and Control Claim

Ownership, custody, possession, effective control, legal title, customary right, source consent, and practical access are separate. A title may be unenforceable; a custodian may not own; an owner may lack access; a sentient source may refuse use.

## Value and Price

**Economic Value** is the contextual importance an actor or institution places on a good, service, access route, relationship, symbol, or obligation for a particular decision. It may reflect survival, use, scarcity, identity, law, faith, status, affection, strategy, or expected future conditions.

A **Price** is one stated exchange term under particular conditions. Price may be expressed in coin, goods, labor, access, time, risk, favor, oath, information, Mana, or another accepted medium.

Price does not equal:

- intrinsic worth;
- moral value;
- production cost;
- total social cost;
- purchasing access;
- scarcity by itself;
- the value held by every party;
- the capabilities of an item or person.

Prices may be bargained, customary, posted, administered, subsidized, rationed, manipulated, coerced, fraudulent, symbolic, or unavailable.

## Currency and Exchange Media

A currency or exchange medium works only within networks that recognize, verify, transfer, store, and enforce it.

Assess:

- issuer or source;
- trust and acceptance;
- unit and divisibility;
- verification and forgery risk;
- storage, transport, and decay;
- conversion routes and spread;
- legal or customary status;
- supply and issuance constraints;
- distribution and concentration;
- redemption promises or backing;
- exclusion of actors unable or unwilling to use it.

No currency is universal across worlds, species, Ages, or Gates. A large nominal holding may become unusable after institutional collapse, source refusal, counterfeiting, territorial change, or Reset.

## Purchasing Access

**Purchasing Access** is an actor's practical ability to complete one exchange under current terms.

It depends on more than wealth:

- accepted medium or collateral;
- market or provider access;
- legal and social permission;
- information and communication;
- transport and timing;
- bargaining power and discrimination;
- trust, reputation, identity, and documentation;
- source compatibility and consent;
- ability to bear risk and future obligation.

A region may contain abundant wealth while most residents lack Purchasing Access to food, medicine, land, or safe transport.

## Wealth and Economic Position

**Economic Position** describes an actor's contextual access to Stocks, income Flows, productive assets, claims, relationships, credit, authority, protection, and obligations.

Wealth is distributed and multidimensional. Distinguish:

- ownership from effective control;
- liquid exchange media from illiquid property;
- income from accumulated Stock;
- productive assets from consumable reserves;
- gross holdings from debt and maintenance;
- private access from public or communal provision;
- recognized claims from hidden, disputed, or unenforceable claims;
- present wealth from resilience to disruption.

Economic Position does not grant Development, Skill, Class, Soul growth, magic, loyalty, wisdom, legitimacy, or safety.

## Productive Capacity

**Economic Capacity** is the bounded ability of an Economic Network to produce, distribute, maintain, or replace a specified output under current conditions.

It may depend on:

- Effective Supply and source renewal;
- capable and willing people;
- tools, sites, infrastructure, knowledge, and standards;
- Professions, institutions, and coordination;
- time, care, safety, and maintenance;
- financing, trust, authority, and contracts;
- transport, storage, and market access;
- magical sources, compatibility, and operating conditions;
- waste absorption and ecological limits.

Capacity is not actual output. Idle capacity, overworked capacity, surge capacity, brittle specialization, and repair capacity may differ.

## Supply, Demand, and Price Formation

Supply and demand are scoped pressures, not commands.

**Demand** requires both desire or obligation and a route to seek, request, claim, or purchase the thing. Need without Purchasing Access may remain unmet rather than appearing as effective market demand.

**Supply** requires a valid source, production, access, willingness, distribution route, and offered terms. Existing Stock withheld, hidden, sacred, unsafe, or inaccessible is not automatically offered supply.

Price and allocation may respond through:

- negotiation and competition;
- custom, law, or administration;
- rationing and priority;
- substitution and innovation;
- hoarding or reserve release;
- new entry or withdrawal;
- monopoly, cartel, patronage, or coercion;
- gifts, relief, theft, seizure, or revolt;
- changes in information and expectations.

Never infer one price movement from supply or demand in isolation. Timing, inventories, expectations, power, alternatives, information, and institutions matter.

## Transaction Friction

**Transaction Friction** is a route-specific cost or constraint on completing an exchange.

It may include:

- search and information cost;
- travel, transport, handling, and delay;
- verification and quality uncertainty;
- negotiation, translation, and documentation;
- law, toll, tax, tariff, taboo, or permit;
- trust, collateral, escrow, and enforcement;
- theft, violence, corruption, and sabotage risk;
- currency conversion and price uncertainty;
- source consent and magical compatibility;
- social exclusion or reputational risk.

Friction can protect as well as obstruct. Inspection may slow trade while preventing contamination. Deliberate redundancy may cost more while improving resilience.

## Contracts, Credit, and Obligations

An **Economic Obligation** is an established duty to transfer value, perform service, provide access, refrain from action, or bear risk under stated conditions.

Track:

- parties and their understanding;
- thing promised;
- timing and conditions;
- authority and consent;
- collateral, guarantor, witness, or enforcement;
- transferability and inheritance;
- default, renegotiation, forgiveness, refusal, and dispute;
- information asymmetry and coercion;
- effects on relationships and future access.

Credit moves access across time; it does not create resources or eliminate risk. Debt can finance recovery, bind relationships, transfer future output, concentrate power, or become unenforceable.

Death and Reincarnation do not automatically transfer personal obligations to a later body. Law, Soul recognition, covenant, inheritance, Soul Title, witness, and actual agreement must supply any route.

## Taxation and Public Provision

Taxes, levies, tithes, and contributions require a claimed authority, assessment rule, collection capacity, payer scope, payment form, timing, exemptions, enforcement, and legitimacy.

Collection is not revenue until it reaches usable control. Revenue is not public capacity until capable actors, resources, procedures, maintenance, and distribution convert it into outcomes.

Public provision may be funded through money, labor, goods, office duty, sacred obligation, patronage, rents, or direct control of sources. It creates dependencies and distribution choices rather than automatic legitimacy.

## Concentration, Inequality, and Power

**Economic Concentration** exists when control of a material source, route, capability, claim, medium, institution, or reserve rests with few actors.

Concentration may create coordination, standards, large projects, or resilience in one respect while producing exclusion, bargaining imbalance, succession risk, corruption opportunity, or a single point of failure.

Inequality must be scoped. Differences in coin, land, food access, legal claims, information, debt, productive assets, magical access, and political influence are not interchangeable. Distribution matters more than one aggregate wealth total.

Economic power influences actors through real options, dependencies, contracts, control, and institutions. It does not compel loyalty or replace faction rules.

## Informal, Hidden, and Illicit Economies

Not all exchange is recorded or authorized. Family provision, mutual aid, smuggling, black markets, theft, forbidden services, monster-human trade, sacred gifts, and unrecognized currencies may sustain real networks.

Official records can miss them. Illegality does not prove secrecy, immorality, inefficiency, or absence of rules. Legal status changes risk, enforcement, information, and legitimacy; it does not erase the exchange.

## Shocks, Adaptation, and Persistence

An **Economic Shock** is a comparatively rapid change to source, production, access, information, trust, authority, demand, route, currency, or obligation.

Resolve:

- direct exposed networks and actors;
- reserves and immediate substitutes;
- contract and debt effects;
- information delay and expectations;
- distribution of loss and opportunity;
- entry, exit, hoarding, rationing, and policy responses;
- bottlenecks and Counterforces;
- recovery, lock-in, and new dependency;
- persistent debt, inequality, ruined capacity, changed trust, or institutional precedent.

Economies adapt through actors. They do not automatically return to equilibrium, and recovery of output does not mean restoration of prior distribution or relationships.

## Economic Resolution Procedure

When an economic question matters:

1. **Define the network and claim.** Name actors, item or obligation, scope, interval, and decision.
2. **Trace source and production.** Use Resources and owning capability systems.
3. **Identify allocation method.** Market, custom, ration, gift, command, obligation, or mixed form.
4. **Establish access and information.** Determine who can participate and what each party knows or believes.
5. **Establish terms and power.** Include price, bargaining, law, consent, coercion, enforcement, and alternatives.
6. **Apply Transaction Friction.** Preserve route, delay, risk, verification, and conversion.
7. **Check distribution.** Identify benefit, exclusion, cost, control, debt, and displaced burden.
8. **Check capacity and bottlenecks.** Do not convert demand into output.
9. **Resolve actor responses.** Apply goals, institutions, expectations, coordination, and Counterforces.
10. **Update claims once.** Record transfers, obligations, prices, access, capacity, and uncertainty under their owners.
11. **Propagate material consequences.** Pass changed resource use, population access, ecological pressure, faction power, unrest, advancement, or Magic dependence through Causal Event Chains.
12. **Record externally.** Current prices, holdings, contracts, currencies, and events belong in the Campaign Record.

## Cross-System Interfaces

### Populations and Resources

[Populations](POPULATIONS.md) supply participants, needs, households, cohorts, and distributed capability. [Resources and Food](RESOURCES_AND_FOOD.md) establish actual inputs and Effective Supply. Economies allocate and exchange them but cannot create absent people, matter, renewal, or consent.

### Development, Skills, and Human Systems

Capability affects production, verification, negotiation, transport, and administration. [Development](../progression/README.md), [Skills](../skills/README.md), and [Human Classes and Professions](../human/README.md) own personal mastery and recognition. Wages, demand, wealth, or office do not grant capability.

### Monster Evolution and Societies

Monsters may produce, exchange, owe, gift, raid, bargain, maintain territory, or reject human economic categories. [Monster Evolution](../monster-evolution/README.md) owns forms and capabilities. Economic pressure may alter exposure and opportunity but does not determine Evolution.

### Souls and Reincarnation

Worldly holdings, contracts, titles, and currencies do not persist with a soul by default. Soul Titles and recognizability can affect reactions only through their rules. A later incarnation needs an actual legal, relational, evidentiary, or covenant route to claim old assets or obligations.

### Soul Weapons

Weapon Souls are economic actors when they choose, not commodities because they have weapon forms. Custody, service, bond, consent, and ownership claims remain distinct under [Soul Weapon](../soul-weapons/README.md) rules.

### Magic

Magic may alter production, transport, verification, communication, preservation, risk, and scarcity through valid [Magic-World Claims](../magic/WORLD_ENGINE_INTERACTIONS.md). It does not create infinite supply, universal payment, perfect contracts, or costless automation. Divine and sentient sources retain agency.

## Worked Examples

### Grain Price After a Road Closure

A road closes while local granaries still hold reserves. Some merchants expect a long disruption and withhold sales; officials impose rationing; a monastery distributes food by obligation; wealthy households bid for private stores.

There is no single automatic price. Different allocation networks coexist. Need without Purchasing Access remains real, and later supply depends on route repair, substitute transport, authority, trust, and time.

### Guild Monopoly with Real Benefits

One guild controls safe enchantment inspection. Its standards reduce catastrophic failures, but high fees exclude small workshops and its archives become a single point of failure.

Concentration provides coordination and protection while creating bargaining power, exclusion, and succession risk. The framework preserves both rather than labeling the guild wholly efficient or corrupt.

### Reincarnated Debtor

A merchant dies owing a personal debt and reincarnates decades later as an unknown monster. The debt does not follow automatically.

An immortal creditor may recognize a Soul Title, but recognition is not enforcement. Any renewed obligation depends on covenant terms, law, evidence, identity, consent, or a valid Soul-binding mechanism under the owning systems.

### Currency Beyond a Gate

Travelers carry valuable imperial coin into a contacted world that neither recognizes the issuer nor values the metal. Their nominal wealth has little Purchasing Access.

They may barter useful goods, earn trust, find a converter, or discover the metal is sacred and cannot be traded. Gate contact does not create a universal exchange rate.

### Monster-Human Service Exchange

A territorial monster guides caravans through dangerous wetlands in exchange for medicine and protection of its nesting sites.

The agreement involves service, ecological obligation, trust, and enforcement. The monster is a contracting person, not a transport resource. Breach can affect trade, ecology, factions, and future relations.

### Magical Automation

A Ritual reduces milling labor but needs rare Reagents, specialized operators, periodic source permission, and costly repair. Output rises near one city while villages without access lose buyers and local repair knowledge.

The technology changes Economic Capacity and distribution. It does not make labor, scarcity, or maintenance disappear.

## Safeguards

- Never reduce an economy to one market, treasury, currency, price index, or wealth score.
- Never equate Price with intrinsic worth, moral value, cost, scarcity, or universal preference.
- Never treat need as effective demand or existing Stock as offered supply.
- Never let demand manufacture resources, capability, Magic, labor consent, or output.
- Never assume markets, commands, gifts, taxes, or customs are automatically fair or efficient.
- Never erase informal networks because official records omit them.
- Never treat ownership, possession, custody, control, access, and source consent as identical.
- Never make debt, taxes, contracts, or property follow a reincarnating soul without a valid route.
- Never make wealth grant Development, Skill, Class, Soul growth, magic, loyalty, or legitimacy.
- Never treat Weapon Souls, monsters, spirits, gods, or workers as commodities by classification.
- Never assume an economic shock returns to equilibrium or restores prior distribution.
- Keep every named price, currency, holding, trade route, contract, debt, and transaction outside this repository.

## Scope Boundaries

This document does not define:

- campaign prices, currencies, businesses, contracts, taxes, holdings, or trade histories;
- universal price formulas, exchange rates, interest rates, wages, wealth bands, or economic stages;
- resource production, ecological effects, faction policy, war, unrest, disease, advancement, or World Stability outcomes;
- personal capability, Class, Profession, Magic, Soul, Evolution, or Weapon Function;
- a moral ranking of market, communal, customary, command, sacred, or reciprocal systems.
