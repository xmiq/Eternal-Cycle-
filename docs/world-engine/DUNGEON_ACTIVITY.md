# Dungeon Activity

## Purpose

This document defines how dungeons form, persist, change, affect their surroundings, receive visitors, lose functions, and leave legacies within the [World Engine](README.md).

It is a qualitative simulation framework rather than a dungeon catalogue, map generator, encounter table, loot table, or campaign record. Ecology, Populations, Resources, Magic, Technology, Monster Evolution, Skills, Development, Souls, Soul Weapons, factions, conflict, and disease retain their established owners.

## Core Rule

> A dungeon exists and acts only through an established boundary, sustaining basis, internal conditions, participants, inputs, processes, exchanges, and consequences.

Therefore:

- **dungeon** is a contextual world classification, not one universal metaphysical species;
- a cave, ruin, monster settlement, magical site, sealed realm, living body, or dangerous building is not automatically a dungeon;
- a Dungeon Core is optional rather than universal;
- floors, rooms, traps, monsters, bosses, treasure, quests, safe zones, and reset points are not defining requirements;
- inhabitants are not automatically hostile, mindless, summoned, owned, or replaceable;
- resources, bodies, artifacts, Mana, food, and crafted objects require real sources;
- death does not trigger automatic replenishment;
- exploration does not grant ownership;
- clearing a site does not reset it, freeze it, or guarantee safety;
- deeper does not mean stronger;
- Dungeon Activity is not level-scaled to visitors;
- time alone does not cause expansion, reconfiguration, spawning, or reward refresh;
- danger, killing, suffering, discovery, and survival do not automatically grant progression.

## What Is a Dungeon

A **Dungeon** is a bounded place or spatial system whose sustained internal conditions, structure, processes, or relationships create materially distinct patterns of access, habitation, hazard, resource flow, transformation, or outward consequence.

A valid Dungeon claim identifies:

1. a practical boundary;
2. a sustaining basis;
3. at least one materially distinct internal condition or process;
4. actual routes connecting it to the surrounding world;
5. enough continuity for its identity to matter beyond one isolated scene;
6. evidence and uncertainty appropriate to the observer.

The same site may be classified differently by different audiences. Explorers may call an underground monster city a dungeon while its residents call it home. A state may classify a sacred forest as a dungeon to justify military control. A scholar may reject both labels because the site lacks the claimed magical boundary.

Classification affects behavior, law, fear, investment, and expectation. It does not by itself change world truth.

### What a Dungeon Is Not

A Dungeon is not automatically:

- a building;
- an underground place;
- a magical object;
- a monster nest;
- an ecosystem isolated from the outside;
- a prison, trial, game, or divine test;
- an enemy faction;
- a person;
- a source of treasure;
- an infinite space;
- a pocket world;
- a world-contact Gate;
- a Soul Space or Soul Gate;
- a universal progression route.

Any of those may be true for one Dungeon only through its owning rules and current evidence.

## Dungeon Origins

**Dungeon Formation** is the causal process through which a place or spatial system acquires the boundary, sustaining basis, distinct conditions, exchanges, and continuity needed for a valid Dungeon claim.

Possible origins include:

- natural caves, reefs, forests, ruins, fault systems, glaciers, deep waters, or other environments whose current relationships create distinct internal conditions;
- constructed fortresses, mines, laboratories, archives, prisons, temples, cities, machines, Ritual Structures, or abandoned infrastructure;
- living organisms, colonies, symbioses, distributed bodies, or cultivated habitats;
- long-term Mana concentration, distortion, source activity, Divine action, Enchantment networks, Alchemical contamination, or another established magical cause;
- ecological succession after settlement collapse, war, disaster, extraction, or extinction;
- a sealed, folded, expanded, recursive, dream-linked, spiritual, or otherwise unusual space when a completed owning rule establishes that geometry;
- overlap with another region, Age, realm, or world after the relevant Gate or Reset rules establish it;
- deliberate creation by people, monsters, spirits, gods, constructs, Weapon Souls, factions, or institutions;
- several causes acting together across time.

Origin does not decide purpose, morality, ownership, present activity, or permanence. A place built as a prison may become a settlement. A sacred trial may become an ecosystem after its god withdraws. A natural cavern may never become a Dungeon despite being dangerous and full of monsters.

## Dungeon Boundary

A **Dungeon Boundary** is the practical transition across which one or more material Dungeon conditions change enough to matter for access, simulation, or consequence.

The boundary may be:

- architectural, geological, ecological, hydrological, atmospheric, magical, legal, perceptual, spiritual, biological, or relational;
- sharp, gradual, porous, conditional, seasonal, directional, nested, mobile, or disputed;
- visible, concealed, mapped, misunderstood, or detectable only through compatible senses or instruments.

One Dungeon can contain several internal boundaries. Two Dungeons can overlap without becoming one system if their sustaining bases and consequences remain distinct.

A boundary does not block passage by definition. It records where relevant conditions change.

### Dungeon Access Route

A **Dungeon Access Route** is a currently usable path by which a specified being, object, signal, resource, agent, or effect can cross a Dungeon Boundary under stated conditions.

An entrance is not usable merely because it exists. Consider:

- body size, movement, senses, environment, life stage, and equipment;
- timing, tides, seasons, pressure, atmosphere, temperature, and Mana;
- permissions, keys, relationships, language, rituals, source consent, and law;
- navigation, communication, support, rest, food, water, repair, and return;
- barriers, defenders, hazards, disease, observation, and uncertainty.

Routes can be opened, closed, forgotten, redirected, guarded, negotiated, destroyed, or created. Each change needs a cause.

## Sustaining Basis

A **Dungeon Sustaining Basis** is the current set of causes and relationships that preserves the Dungeon's distinct boundary, structure, internal conditions, or recurring processes.

It may involve:

- geology, climate, water, pressure, or ordinary ecology;
- active construction, maintenance, repair, governance, or inhabitation;
- a Mana Source, Ritual, Enchantment, Divine relationship, curse, or other magical system;
- organisms, symbionts, colonies, digestion, growth, reproduction, or behavior;
- machinery, records, standards, operators, supply, and Technical Infrastructure;
- a spirit, construct, god, Weapon Soul, distributed intelligence, or other person;
- spatial laws, Gate conditions, Age remnants, or Reset legacies established by later owners;
- several mutually dependent causes.

The basis can be redundant, layered, damaged, contested, or partially unknown. Destroying one component may leave the Dungeon active through others.

### Dungeon Core

A **Dungeon Core** is an optional concentrated component that materially coordinates, anchors, supplies, records, or regulates part of a Dungeon's Sustaining Basis.

A Dungeon Core may be:

- an ordinary mechanism or control site;
- a Mana reservoir or Enchantment Host;
- a living organ, colony node, egg, root system, or symbiont;
- a Ritual focus or Divine relic;
- a construct, spirit, god, Weapon Soul, or other person;
- a misleading local interpretation of several distributed processes.

The label does not grant personhood, omniscience, ownership of inhabitants, control of every process, unlimited production, or a guaranteed weakness. If a Core is a person, consent and agency remain. If it is destroyed, resolve its actual functions rather than declaring the Dungeon cleared by convention.

## Dungeon Structure and Topology

**Dungeon Topology** is the current spatial and relational arrangement of Dungeon regions, routes, barriers, interfaces, distances, and connections.

Topology can be:

- stable, changing, cyclical, damaged, repaired, growing, excavated, flooded, overgrown, folded, or partly inaccessible;
- Euclidean or unusual only where an established source supports the difference;
- represented by rooms, floors, layers, territories, organs, tunnels, currents, memories, networks, habitats, or another useful local model.

Floors and depth labels are map conventions unless a real process makes them meaningful. They do not imply difficulty tiers, sequential progression, or one final boss.

### Reconfiguration

**Dungeon Reconfiguration** is a material change in Dungeon Topology, boundary, access, or functional arrangement caused by an established process.

Possible causes include:

- construction, excavation, repair, sabotage, settlement, or collapse;
- erosion, flood, fire, freezing, growth, decay, migration, or burrowing;
- machinery, Enchantment Drift, Ritual schedules, source decisions, or defensive action;
- living-body movement, metamorphosis, healing, injury, or disease;
- Gate, Age, or World Reset effects after their owning rules establish them.

Reconfiguration is not random merely because observers do not understand it. Hidden causes must exist before they materially change an outcome.

## Dungeon Conditions

A **Dungeon Condition** is one current scoped feature of the Dungeon that can affect participants, routes, processes, or consequences.

Relevant conditions may include:

- light, temperature, pressure, air, water, terrain, gravity, sound, visibility, and timekeeping;
- Mana state, affinities, source access, interference, residue, Drift, or magical restrictions;
- food, shelter, materials, hazards, waste, and renewal;
- populations, territories, disease, symbioses, predators, caretakers, and ecological functions;
- architecture, machinery, traps, wards, signals, records, and maintenance;
- law, custom, claims, patrols, markets, worship, diplomacy, and conflict;
- spatial distortion, dream effects, memory effects, or spiritual relationships when established by an owning system.

No condition is universal to all Dungeons. Record only conditions relevant to current decisions or persistent consequences.

## Dungeon Activity

**Dungeon Activity** is the current operation, change, and exchange produced by a Dungeon's Sustaining Basis, internal participants, conditions, inputs, cycles, disturbances, and responses.

Activity can include:

- ordinary habitation, movement, feeding, growth, decay, work, trade, worship, governance, and conflict;
- construction, maintenance, repair, excavation, cultivation, containment, and research;
- ecological succession, reproduction, predation, migration, disease, and waste processing;
- Mana flow, Ritual operation, Enchantment function, Alchemical reaction, or source response;
- opening, closing, moving, or degrading Access Routes;
- defending, warning, negotiating, hiding, guiding, trapping, expelling, or welcoming entrants;
- producing or consuming resources through valid Flows;
- emitting heat, water, creatures, pollution, signals, knowledge, magic, disease, or other consequences beyond the boundary;
- dormancy, reactivation, reconfiguration, expansion, contraction, collapse, or recovery.

Activity is not synonymous with hostility or visible motion. A sealed archive can be active through preservation and monitoring. A sleeping living Dungeon can sustain digestion and symbiosis. A ruin may be inactive as machinery while highly active as an ecosystem.

### Activity Drivers

A **Dungeon Activity Driver** is a current cause that materially changes or sustains a Dungeon process.

Drivers may include:

- resource input or depletion;
- population change or migration;
- maintenance, neglect, damage, or repair;
- visitors, extraction, settlement, conflict, or institutional decisions;
- season, climate, disaster, disease, or ecological succession;
- Mana, source, Ritual, Enchantment, Divine, or Alchemical change;
- a Core or controller's decision;
- Gate, Age, or Reset conditions once established.

A driver creates only the changes its route supports. Visitor arrival does not wake every Dungeon. Monster deaths do not command replacements. A calendar interval does not refresh contents.

## Activity Regimes

A **Dungeon Activity Regime** is a recurring or persistent pattern of Dungeon Activity under a known range of conditions.

Possible findings include:

- continuously maintained;
- seasonally active;
- responsive to entry, extraction, threat, worship, or source contact;
- gradually expanding or contracting;
- intermittently unstable;
- dormant but sustained;
- reactivating after a named change;
- collapsing, transforming, or recovering.

These are descriptive findings, not universal stages. A Dungeon can combine several regimes in different regions or processes.

### Dormancy

**Dungeon Dormancy** is a condition in which one or more visible or resource-intensive Dungeon processes are reduced, suspended, inaccessible, or awaiting enabling conditions while enough continuity remains for possible reactivation.

Dormant does not mean empty, dead, safe, inactive in every respect, or guaranteed to awaken. Determine what continues, what stops, what degrades, what remains aware, and what could restore activity.

### Reactivation

**Dungeon Reactivation** is the resumed or transformed operation of a dormant, suppressed, disconnected, or degraded Dungeon process through a valid enabling route.

Possible routes include renewed supply, repaired infrastructure, returning inhabitants, restored source consent, seasonal change, broken containment, excavation, migration, or an Age transition. Entry alone is not a universal trigger.

## Expansion, Contraction, and Influence

**Dungeon Expansion** is a supported outward extension of a Dungeon Boundary, Topology, Sustaining Basis, or materially distinct conditions.

**Dungeon Contraction** is a supported loss, withdrawal, containment, or reorganization of that scope.

Expansion may occur through construction, growth, colonization, erosion, contamination, Mana spread, route creation, spatial effects, political classification, or ecological transformation. Distinguish physical expansion from a wider sphere of influence, reputation, legal claim, resource network, or migration consequence.

Neither expansion nor contraction is inherently hostile, healthy, unstable, or permanent. Resolve affected land, beings, routes, habitats, claims, and Counterforces.

### Dungeon Exchange

A **Dungeon Exchange** is a Flow of beings, resources, energy, Mana, information, disease, waste, authority, or consequence across a Dungeon Boundary.

Every exchange identifies:

- source and destination;
- route and timing;
- quantity or qualitative scale where relevant;
- compatibility and transformation;
- control, consent, and interception;
- loss, waste, and side effects;
- evidence and uncertainty.

A Dungeon is rarely closed in every respect. Even sealed sites exchange heat, pressure, magic, stories, claims, or maintenance unless a specific rule establishes true isolation.

## Populations, Ecology, and Inhabitants

Dungeon inhabitants remain ordinary world participants under their owning systems.

- [Populations](POPULATIONS.md) owns composition, cohorts, births, deaths, maturation, arrival, departure, continuity, and capability distribution.
- [Ecology and Migration](ECOLOGY_AND_MIGRATION.md) owns habitat, relationships, niches, functions, disturbance, succession, routes, displacement, and settlement.
- [Monster Ecology](../monster-evolution/MONSTER_ECOLOGY.md) owns monster-specific ecological relationships.
- [Monster Evolution](../monster-evolution/MONSTER_EVOLUTION.md) owns Evolution Routes and transitions.
- [Disease Evolution](DISEASE_EVOLUTION.md) owns Disease Processes, transmission, intervention, and agent change.

A Dungeon does not spawn beings by default. A new inhabitant requires reproduction, migration, construction, summoning, transformation, revival, release, or another valid route. Bodies and remains do not vanish unless a causal process removes or transforms them.

### Inhabitant Agency

Sapient inhabitants have goals, knowledge, relationships, institutions, territories, rights, and choices. They are not encounters waiting for visitors.

Non-sapient beings still follow actual needs, senses, behaviors, ecology, and pressures. They do not attack because they carry a Dungeon label.

Calling a community a Dungeon Population does not establish collective hostility, criminality, ownership by a Core, inability to negotiate, or permission to kill or displace it.

## Resources, Treasure, and Extraction

[Resources and Food](RESOURCES_AND_FOOD.md) owns all Dungeon resource claims. A resource requires a source, quality, Stock or Flow, Access Route, extraction process, renewal conditions, losses, waste, claims, and affected parties.

**Dungeon Yield** is the actual material or informational output obtained from one bounded Dungeon interaction after access, extraction, transformation, loss, and consequence. It is not a guaranteed reward category.

Possible yields include:

- mined, harvested, cultivated, salvaged, traded, gifted, or recovered materials;
- records, maps, testimony, observations, techniques, and historical evidence;
- negotiated access, service, sanctuary, relationships, or source cooperation;
- crafted goods and ordinary possessions belonging to inhabitants;
- bodies or remains only under the same ethical, ecological, legal, and personhood rules that apply elsewhere.

Treasure does not replenish unless a valid process replaces it. A chest is not unowned because it is hidden. A slain being's possessions remain subject to custody, inheritance, law, relationships, and consequence.

### Extraction Pressure

Repeated delving can alter:

- Stocks, renewal, waste, and source behavior;
- habitat and population viability;
- local prices, labor, institutions, and faction interests;
- route safety, secrecy, mapping, and defensive response;
- disease exposure and ecological transfer;
- Dungeon maintenance, stability, reconfiguration, or collapse.

Extraction success can make later extraction harder, easier, less valuable, politically contested, or ecologically damaging. It does not guarantee a self-balancing replacement mechanism.

## Structures, Traps, and Hazards

A trap, ward, hazard, puzzle, barrier, patrol, or environmental danger has an origin, function, trigger, target assumptions, maintenance state, evidence, failure modes, and counterplay.

It may arise from:

- ordinary terrain or ecology;
- deliberate construction or defense;
- abandoned machinery or Technical Infrastructure;
- Magic under its specialist owner;
- living behavior or bodily processes;
- damage, Drift, disease, waste, or unintended interaction.

A hazard does not know visitor level. A puzzle is not obligated to be fair unless a designer intended a fair test and retained the capability to provide one. Hidden dangers must have prior causal existence and proportionate signs or discoverable evidence where fair agency requires them.

## Control, Claims, and Personhood

Separate:

- physical possession;
- custody;
- access control;
- maintenance responsibility;
- legal claim;
- territorial control;
- cultural or sacred relationship;
- authorship or construction;
- inhabitant self-government;
- source consent;
- Dungeon or controller personhood.

Building, discovering, mapping, entering, clearing, defending, funding, naming, worshipping, or surviving a Dungeon does not automatically grant ownership.

### Agentive Dungeons

A Dungeon may have agency only through an established person or coordinated system capable of perception, interpretation, choice, and action.

Possible agentive forms include:

- a living being whose body contains the Dungeon;
- a spirit, god, construct, colony, distributed mind, or awakened site;
- a site-bound Weapon Soul under [Unconventional Soul Weapons](../soul-weapons/UNCONVENTIONAL_SOUL_WEAPONS.md);
- a council, institution, or community operating the site.

Do not infer a single Dungeon mind from responsive mechanisms, coordinated ecology, a controlling faction, or visitor belief. If several minds contribute, preserve their separate information and agency.

An agentive Dungeon does not own every inhabitant, body, resource, Soul, thought, or action within its boundary.

## Exploration and Delving

**Dungeon Delving** is purposeful entry into or movement through a Dungeon to pursue a stated objective under current routes, knowledge, support, and risk.

Objectives may include:

- travel, rescue, diplomacy, trade, migration, settlement, pilgrimage, repair, care, research, mapping, containment, extraction, sabotage, defense, or escape;
- communication with inhabitants, sources, controllers, or a Dungeon person;
- investigation of history, disease, ecology, Magic, or technology.

Delving is not synonymous with combat or extraction.

### Delving Capability

Assess the objective contextually through:

- current bodies, Skills, Development, senses, health, and compatibility;
- information, maps, guides, communication, and uncertainty;
- tools, provisions, transport, shelter, repair, medicine, Magic, and fallback routes;
- time, distance, environment, fatigue, recovery, and return requirements;
- relationships, law, consent, territory, language, and faction response;
- hazards, inhabitants, Counterforces, and changing conditions.

No one Dungeon rank or party level decides success. A physically weak scholar may navigate a deadly archive through language and preparation; a powerful combatant may fail because it cannot breathe, communicate, repair a bridge, or return before a flood.

## Knowledge, Maps, and Uncertainty

A **Dungeon Map Claim** records one observer's evidence about Dungeon Topology, conditions, routes, landmarks, timing, and uncertainty at a stated date.

Maps can be:

- accurate within one body scale but unusable by another;
- correct before reconfiguration;
- deliberately false or politically restricted;
- based on incomplete exploration;
- unable to represent nonvisual, relational, cyclical, or unusual geometry;
- reliable for travel while wrong about origin or purpose.

Player Knowledge, Character Knowledge, world truth, mistaken belief, and hidden information remain separate under the [Game Master Framework](../gm/GAME_MASTER_FRAMEWORK.md). A hidden passage or change must have a prior cause; the GM cannot create it retroactively to force an outcome.

## Dungeon Collapse, Transformation, and Legacy

**Dungeon Collapse** is the failure, loss, or major reorganization of enough Sustaining Basis, structure, access, or internal function that the former Dungeon can no longer continue in the same material form.

Collapse can be:

- physical, ecological, magical, technical, institutional, spatial, biological, or mixed;
- sudden or gradual;
- local or system-wide;
- destructive, liberating, regenerative, or differently harmful to different participants.

Collapse does not automatically kill every inhabitant, expel every visitor, release treasure, close every route, remove every hazard, or end the site's importance.

A Dungeon may instead undergo **Dungeon Transformation**, where its identity, Sustaining Basis, conditions, inhabitants, or functions change enough to require a new current model while preserving causal continuity.

A **Dungeon Legacy** is a persistent consequence remaining after activity, control, identity, or operation changes. Legacies may include ruins, altered ecology, refugees, infrastructure, disease, resource depletion, pollution, records, claims, institutions, myths, Soul Titles, Weapon Soul relationships, unexploded hazards, or changed routes.

## Reincarnation and Soul Systems

Reincarnation does not provide:

- a Dungeon checkpoint or respawn location;
- restored ownership, access, keys, maps, contracts, offices, or faction standing;
- automatic knowledge of current Topology or conditions;
- immunity to former hazards;
- rights over inhabitants or a former body;
- a guarantee that the Dungeon still exists.

Eligible Soul memory, [Soul Echoes](../soul/SOUL_ECHOES.md), [Retained Instincts](../soul/RETAINED_INSTINCTS.md), [Soul Resonance](../soul/SOUL_RESONANCE.md), [Soul Titles](../soul/SOUL_TITLES.md), and [Soul Avatars](../soul/SOUL_AVATARS.md) may support recognition, perspective, relationships, or relearning through their established rules. They do not update themselves to current world truth.

A Dungeon may remember a Soul through inhabitants, records, a Weapon Soul, a source relationship, or its own established agency. Recognition influences only current reactions and opportunities; it does not compel trust, hostility, access, or destiny.

## Soul Weapons

A Weapon Soul entering a Dungeon remains a person and partner rather than a key, detector, loot category, or universal countermeasure.

A site, building, landscape, living body, distributed structure, or composite network can serve as a Soul Weapon Vessel only through the continuity, topology, personhood, Awakening, Bond, and Manifestation rules in the [Soul Weapons framework](../soul-weapons/README.md). Dungeon classification does not awaken a Weapon Soul or make the Dungeon a Vessel.

A site-bound Weapon Soul may participate in Dungeon Activity, negotiate access, maintain functions, refuse extraction, or outlive former inhabitants. It does not automatically control the whole Dungeon.

## Development, Skills, and Progression

Dungeon conditions can provide opportunity, instruction, feedback, pressure, variation, consequence, and meaningful practice. Actual progression remains with its owner.

- [Development](../progression/README.md) requires current embodied effort, integration, evidence, recovery, and track-specific rules.
- [Skills](../skills/README.md) require valid representation, practice, reliability, and tree ownership.
- [Monster Evolution](../monster-evolution/README.md) requires valid pressure, Species Potential, Route Conditions, and transition rules.
- [Soul growth](../soul/README.md) requires meaningful Soul-owned change rather than danger exposure.
- [Soul Weapon Awakening](../soul-weapons/AWAKENING_CONDITIONS.md) requires its complete shared conditions rather than merely fighting in a Dungeon.

Repeated entry, distance traveled, rooms cleared, monsters killed, treasure recovered, traps survived, deaths suffered, or time spent does not create Dungeon XP or guarantee any owned progression.

## World-Domain Interactions

### Populations and Ecology

Dungeon boundaries can create habitats, refuges, barriers, migration routes, ecological traps, Novel Ecologies, and concentrated dependencies. Apply Population and Ecology rules rather than generic spawning or biome tags.

### Resources and Economies

Dungeon access can alter supply, Price, labor, settlement, transport, claims, waste, and Economic Concentration. A lucrative site can be depleted, monopolized, abandoned, protected, or made uneconomic by maintenance and risk.

### Factions, War, and Unrest

Factions may claim, study, regulate, settle, exploit, defend, worship, conceal, negotiate with, or destroy a Dungeon through actual information, decision, and mobilization routes. Strategic value does not erase inhabitants or source agency.

### Disease

Dungeon environments can support Disease Agents, Reservoirs, Vectors, exposure routes, medicine, isolation, or care. Unfamiliar air, monsters, ruins, Magic, and foreign origin are not diseases by themselves.

### Technology and Magic

Dungeon structures, traps, transport, preservation, cultivation, and maintenance may be Technical Systems. [Technology and Magical Advancement](TECHNOLOGY_AND_MAGICAL_ADVANCEMENT.md) owns adoption, repair, diffusion, and technical legacy.

Every magical Dungeon effect requires an established Mana Relation and specialist owner under [Magic](../magic/README.md). The Dungeon label does not supply Mana, waive costs, create a spell, or authorize a source.

### World Stability, Ages, Resets, and Gates

[World Stability](WORLD_STABILITY.md) owns stability claims. A Dungeon affects a Stability Referent only through its actual Sustaining Basis, activity, inhabitants, exchanges, dependencies, and consequences; clearing or losing one never adds or removes abstract stability.

[Ages and World Resets](AGES_AND_WORLD_RESETS.md) owns historical transitions and World Revalidation. A Dungeon may preserve, distort, or be transformed by those systems only through its actual Sustaining Basis, footprint, and dependencies. [World Gates and World-Contact Events](GATES_AND_WORLD_CONTACT.md) owns extraordinary contact interfaces and their channels.

A doorway, teleportation circle, spatial fold, sealed threshold, and Dungeon Access Route are not automatically world-contact Gates. A Dungeon surviving an Age transition does not prove immunity to a World Reset.

## Dungeon Activity Procedure

When Dungeon Activity matters:

1. **Test the classification.** Identify the practical boundary, Sustaining Basis, distinct conditions, exchanges, continuity, audience, and uncertainty.
2. **State the objective and resolution.** Name the current decision, place, participants, timescale, and required detail.
3. **Load the current Dungeon model.** Include Topology, Access Routes, conditions, participants, resources, activity regimes, and known legacies.
4. **Identify owners.** Separate ecological, population, resource, magical, technical, factional, bodily, Soul, Weapon Soul, and other claims.
5. **Apply Activity Drivers.** Establish the actual input, decision, disturbance, maintenance, entry, extraction, or source change.
6. **Resolve internal responses.** Let inhabitants, controllers, systems, ecologies, structures, and sources act from their own information and capability.
7. **Resolve boundary exchanges.** Track what enters, leaves, transforms, becomes blocked, or creates external consequence.
8. **Update structure and activity.** Change only supported conditions, routes, Topology, regimes, expansion, contraction, dormancy, or collapse.
9. **Propagate consequences.** Use [Causal Event Chains](CAUSAL_EVENT_CHAINS.md) for relevant world domains, delays, feedback, and Counterforces.
10. **Preserve uncertainty and legacy.** Record what is established, inferred, disputed, hidden, pending, and persistent in the external Campaign Record.

## Worked Examples

### Irrigation Ruin Without a Core

An abandoned underground irrigation network contains failed Enchantments, seasonal floods, fungal farms, nesting monsters, and repair machines following damaged instructions. No single Core controls it.

Its Dungeon identity comes from a maintained boundary of pressure and Mana conditions, connected Topology, interacting processes, and persistent outward water effects. Destroying one machine changes local flow but does not clear or reset the system.

### A Living Dungeon

Travelers enter air chambers inside a continent-sized leviathan to reach a symbiotic settlement. Digestion cycles change routes, immune organisms respond to injury, and the leviathan can communicate slowly through pressure and dreams.

The Dungeon is also part of a person. Entry, settlement, surgery, and extraction implicate bodily integrity and consent. The leviathan does not own every symbiont, and the inhabitants are not parasites by default.

### Monster City Misclassified as a Dungeon

A human guild maps a subterranean city, labels districts as floors, calls officials bosses, and offers rewards for recovered civic seals. The city's residents maintain law, trade, schools, and defenses.

The physical place may qualify as a Dungeon, but the guild's vocabulary does not turn citizens into enemies or property. Its classification becomes a factional and legal cause with diplomatic and conflict consequences.

### A Person-Like Core

A spirit regulates climate and routes through a sacred cavern. It can close passages, negotiate water release, and ask visitors for repair of damaged shrines.

The spirit is an agentive Core for specific functions, not an omniscient Dungeon mind. It cannot command independent inhabitants, create unlimited water, or waive the Mana and ecological routes supporting its work.

### Extraction Changes the Dungeon

Delvers harvest luminous moss used in healing. Repeated extraction reduces food for a pollinating insect, which lowers seed dispersal for structural root bridges. Routes collapse, local medicine becomes scarce, and the harvesting guild faces opposition.

The moss does not respawn on schedule. Renewal depends on habitat, reproduction, season, and changed behavior.

### Seasonal Dormancy

A volcanic archive seals itself during high Mana storms because its cooling Enchantments cannot safely operate. Machinery idles, caretakers migrate to outer chambers, and heat-resistant organisms remain active.

The archive is dormant only in specified functions. Early reactivation requires restored cooling, operators, and safe source conditions rather than a visitor crossing the threshold.

### Reincarnated Delver with an Obsolete Map

A Soul returns centuries after mapping a famous labyrinth. Retained memory reveals the old organizing logic, but floods, settlement, excavation, and a World Reset have changed routes and Mana behavior.

The old map provides hypotheses and warnings. It does not update automatically, restore access rights, or make the current body compatible with the environment.

### Cleared Fortress Becomes a Refuge

An armed faction is defeated in a fortified Dungeon. Survivors flee, traps remain, prisoners need care, and neighboring refugees occupy safe sections while the water system degrades.

Combat ends one controller's operations. It does not reset the fortress, remove hazards, transfer legitimate ownership, or decide the site's next identity.

## Safeguards

- Do not define Dungeon as one universal metaphysical species or assume every culture recognizes the category.
- Do not require a Dungeon Core, floors, bosses, traps, treasure, monsters, quests, or a final chamber.
- Do not create beings, resources, artifacts, Mana, rooms, or hazards without causal sources.
- Do not respawn inhabitants, refill treasure, rebuild structures, or refresh conditions on a timer without an established route.
- Do not make Dungeon Activity scale to visitor level, party strength, narrative importance, or desired reward.
- Do not make depth, floor number, age, rarity, or size a universal threat rank.
- Do not treat inhabitants as hostile, mindless, guilty, owned, disposable, or incapable of society because of location.
- Do not infer one Dungeon mind, Core, controller, purpose, or owner from responsive conditions.
- Do not let exploration, mapping, clearing, conquest, construction, or survival automatically grant ownership, authority, Skill, Development, Soul growth, Evolution, or loot.
- Do not turn ecological pressure, repeated combat, death, extraction, or suffering into Dungeon XP.
- Do not let Magic bypass Mana, source, cost, consent, failure, maintenance, or specialist ownership.
- Do not let Reincarnation create checkpoints, current maps, restored keys, former standing, or hazard immunity.
- Do not let a Soul Weapon become a universal key, detector, countermeasure, loot item, or Dungeon Core by label.
- Do not erase bodies, remains, possessions, waste, disease, damage, displacement, or Dungeon Legacies after a scene.
- Do not declare a Dungeon cleared merely because one threat, controller, Core, or visible population is removed.
- Do not confuse Dungeon Access Routes with Soul Gates or world-contact Gates.
- Do not replace GM judgment with automatic room, encounter, hazard, loot, or reset generation.
- Do not store named live Dungeons, maps, current inhabitants, resources, hazards, routes, claims, expeditions, or Dungeon Profiles in this repository.

## Scope Boundaries

This document does not define:

- a universal Dungeon anatomy, origin, purpose, lifecycle, floor structure, Core, or reward model;
- Dungeon generators, encounter tables, room tables, loot tables, monster tables, maps, or templates;
- personal Skills, Development, Classes, Professions, Evolution, Soul growth, or Soul Weapon Functions;
- Mana, Spell, Ritual, Enchantment, Alchemical, Divine, or forbidden-magic effects;
- species reproduction, ecological adaptation, disease mechanisms, resource creation, prices, faction decisions, or conflict outcomes;
- World Stability, Age transitions, World Resets, world-contact Gates, or long-horizon abstraction;
- live Dungeon state or campaign history.
