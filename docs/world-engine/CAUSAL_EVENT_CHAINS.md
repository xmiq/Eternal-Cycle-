# Causal Event Chains

## Purpose

This document defines how Eternal Cycle traces change from established causes through autonomous responses, delays, consequences, and feedback. It turns [Rule Zero](../core/DESIGN_PHILOSOPHY.md#rule-zero) into a playable procedure without scripting future history or replacing GM judgment.

It uses [World-State Variables](WORLD_STATE_VARIABLES.md) as its state grammar. Later World Engine domains define which population, resource, economic, ecological, factional, military, disease, advancement, dungeon, Stability, Age, Reset, and Gate claims are valid. This document defines how valid claims connect.

## Core Rule

A **Causal Event Chain** is a revisable network of established causes, possible responses, material constraints, and consequences across time.

Every resolved transition must answer:

1. what changed directly;
2. what process or actor can receive that change;
3. by what route the next effect can occur;
4. what conditions enable, limit, delay, redirect, or prevent it;
5. who knows enough and has enough agency to respond;
6. what remains uncertain;
7. which owning system resolves the next claim;
8. what state, pending consequence, or legacy is recorded afterward.

A plausible sequence is not automatically true. A desired ending cannot be used to manufacture its earlier causes.

## Chain and Script

A script fixes an outcome because the planned story requires it. A Causal Event Chain preserves several outcomes until established conditions settle them.

The difference is practical:

| Scripted assertion | Causal treatment |
| --- | --- |
| The shortage causes rebellion. | The shortage changes access and creates pressures; informed actors may ration, trade, hoard, migrate, protest, reform, repress, or fail to coordinate. |
| The dragon's death starts a war. | The death changes authority, deterrence, territory, belief, and opportunity; factions act through their own knowledge and interests. |
| Healing magic ends disease. | A valid magical effect changes particular outcomes; access, capacity, adaptation, trust, distribution, and time determine wider consequences. |
| The player left, so nothing changed. | Relevant actors and processes continue through elapsed time, capability, information, constraints, and opposition. |

Deliberate plots remain possible. A prophecy, conspiracy, invasion plan, succession scheme, or Reset project can be an in-world cause. It receives no immunity from discovery, refusal, scarcity, interruption, adaptation, or failure.

## Chain Components

### Causal Node

A **Causal Node** is an event, action, decision, state change, threshold crossing, discovery, failure, or established external input that may have consequences.

A node records only what has occurred or become true. It does not include every imagined downstream outcome.

### Causal Link

A **Causal Link** is a supported route by which one node or condition can contribute to another. It identifies:

- transmitting process or actor;
- required access, information, capability, authority, resource, or compatibility;
- relevant scope and timing;
- counterforces and alternatives;
- receiving rule owner;
- uncertainty about whether or how the transition occurs.

Correlation, genre expectation, symbolic resemblance, or dramatic usefulness alone is not a Causal Link.

### Active Cause

An **Active Cause** is currently exerting a material influence through a valid route. Causes may be physical, ecological, social, informational, institutional, magical, spiritual, or mixed.

Several causes may combine. One cause may be necessary without being sufficient, sufficient only under local conditions, or merely contributory.

### Counterforce

A **Counterforce** is an established actor, process, resource, relationship, constraint, adaptation, or alternative that opposes, absorbs, redirects, or transforms a cause.

A Counterforce need not restore the previous state. Evacuation can prevent deaths while leaving displacement. A substitute route can preserve trade while enriching a new intermediary. Suppression can end visible protest while deepening hidden opposition.

### Causal Branch

A **Causal Branch** is one supported continuation among several possibilities. Branches remain possibilities until their requirements occur and their uncertainty is resolved.

Branches can coexist. A drought may cause conservation in one district, flight in another, price manipulation by one guild, and ritual petition by a temple.

### Causal Convergence

**Causal Convergence** occurs when distinct causes contribute to one later condition. The GM preserves the material contributors instead of assigning the entire result to the most visible event.

For example, unrest may reflect food access, disputed legitimacy, demobilized soldiers, propaganda, debt, and a triggering arrest. Removing one cause may reduce pressure without resolving the others.

### Feedback Loop

A **Feedback Loop** exists when a consequence returns as an input to an earlier process.

- Reinforcing feedback amplifies a trend, such as flight reducing the labor available for repair, causing further service failure and more flight.
- Balancing feedback limits or reverses a trend, such as rising prices attracting supply, reducing scarcity if routes, safety, trust, and purchasing access permit it.

A loop is not infinite by declaration. Every pass consumes time and encounters changing stocks, capacities, information, adaptation, thresholds, and limits.

### Pending Consequence

A **Pending Consequence** is an established downstream change whose causal requirements are met but whose delay has not elapsed, whose receiving process is still operating, or whose resolution awaits a stated condition.

Pending Consequences belong in an external Campaign Record. They identify cause, expected receiving scope, earliest relevant time or trigger, uncertainty, counterforces, and review condition. They are not guaranteed scenes.

### Causal Horizon

A **Causal Horizon** is the furthest point to which a chain must currently be traced to preserve a meaningful decision, foreseeable consequence, or future dependency.

The GM does not simulate every possible consequence forever. Extend the horizon when:

- a consequence becomes decision-relevant;
- elapsed time reaches a Pending Consequence;
- a dependency crosses scope or resolution;
- an actor gains information or capacity to respond;
- a feedback loop materially changes direction;
- play returns to an affected place, group, or process.

## Causal Sufficiency

Before resolving a link, test its support.

### Necessary Conditions

A necessary condition must be present for this route to function. Removing it closes that route but may leave alternatives.

### Contributory Conditions

A contributory condition changes likelihood, speed, scale, distribution, or consequence without settling the outcome alone.

### Sufficient Local Conditions

Conditions are sufficient only for a stated effect, scope, and receiving rule. A direct bridge collapse can be sufficiently established by the resolved physical event. Regional famine is not thereby established.

### Triggering Conditions

A trigger initiates or exposes a process whose deeper causes already exist. It does not erase those causes from the record.

### Inhibiting Conditions

An inhibiting condition weakens, delays, narrows, or blocks one route. It is not proof that the underlying pressure or all alternative routes have ended.

No condition is globally necessary, sufficient, or inhibiting unless its owning rules establish that scope.

## Actors, Information, and Agency

People, monsters, spirits, gods, institutions, Weapon Souls, and other persons do not act as automatic links.

For an actor-mediated transition, establish:

- what the actor perceives or believes;
- how information reached them;
- whether they trust or understand it;
- their goals, needs, fears, relationships, obligations, and identity;
- current capability, access, authority, time, and willingness;
- internal disagreement or coordination where the actor is collective;
- alternatives and anticipated consequences;
- opposition, refusal, deception, and error.

An optimal response known to the GM is not automatically available to an actor. A mistaken belief can drive a valid action. A true fact cannot drive someone who has no route to learn it.

Player action enters the same model. It may create unusually important causes, but it does not pause other agency, force adoption, or guarantee the intended downstream result.

## Direct and Downstream Effects

Resolve an established action under its owning system before tracing world consequences.

1. **Direct effect:** what the owning rule says changed.
2. **Immediate exposure:** which subjects, places, relationships, and processes actually receive it.
3. **First response:** what affected actors or processes can do now.
4. **Downstream consequence:** what follows after dependencies, counterforces, and delay.
5. **Feedback:** how that consequence alters earlier conditions or creates a new chain.
6. **Legacy:** what remains after the active process ends.

Do not bundle downstream claims into the direct effect. A fire may destroy stored food. Price change, migration, political blame, disease exposure, and institutional reform require their own links.

## Time and Sequence

Each material link has a relevant timescale:

- immediate transmission;
- response and coordination delay;
- travel or communication delay;
- biological, ecological, developmental, or magical process time;
- construction, maintenance, depletion, or recovery time;
- institutional or cultural adoption time;
- generational or Age-scale change.

Concurrent processes may interact before either finishes. Sequence matters: relief arriving before a reserve is exhausted produces a different state from relief arriving afterward.

When exact ordering is uncertain, preserve the competing sequences and resolve only what current evidence, procedures, or meaningful uncertainty support.

## Branch Resolution

Resolve a branch only when it becomes material.

1. Remove branches whose necessary conditions are no longer possible.
2. Preserve branches still supported by current state and actor choices.
3. Apply deterministic consequences only where the owning rules and established facts make them deterministic.
4. Use ordinary GM judgment for uncertain but non-mechanical detail.
5. Use a documented campaign method for meaningful uncertainty.
6. Use a narrow [Provisional Rule](../gm/ALPHA_PLAYTEST_RULES.md#provisional) when a missing mechanic truly decides the result.
7. Record the resolved node and any still-Pending Consequences externally.

Randomness can resolve uncertainty; it cannot supply missing causes, erase known constraints, or select a preferred plot.

## Chain States

A tracked chain or branch may be:

- **active:** causes currently transmit through a valid route;
- **pending:** requirements are met but a delay or review condition remains;
- **stalled:** a required route, actor, resource, or condition is currently absent;
- **contained:** Counterforces prevent material propagation while maintenance continues;
- **redirected:** the original pressure now produces a materially different consequence;
- **resolved:** no tracked downstream consequence remains within the current horizon;
- **transformed:** the chain has become a new process with different sustaining causes;
- **obscured:** world truth remains insufficient to classify it confidently.

These are descriptive states, not universal ranks. A stalled chain may restart if its missing route returns. A resolved chain may leave World Legacies.

## Cross-Domain Handoffs

A Causal Event Chain may cross several owners. Each owner resolves only its claim.

| Handoff | World Engine responsibility | Specialist responsibility |
| --- | --- | --- |
| World pressure to person | Establish access, exposure, incentive, risk, and consequence | [Development](../progression/README.md) and [Skills](../skills/README.md) resolve capability change |
| Habitat change to monster | Establish changed niche, resources, threats, routes, and duration | [Monster Evolution](../monster-evolution/README.md) resolves Adaptation, Mutation, and Evolution |
| Institution to human | Establish office, curriculum, recognition, resources, authority, and exclusion | [Human systems](../human/README.md) resolve Class, Profession, and social framework claims |
| Event to continuing soul | Establish death context, witnesses, relationships, time, and worldly aftermath | [Soul Engine](../soul/README.md) resolves identity, persistence, Echoes, Titles, Depth, Resonance, and Reincarnation |
| Event to Weapon Soul | Establish custody, environment, relationships, danger, and consequences | [Soul Weapons](../soul-weapons/README.md) resolve personhood, bond, Awakening, Evolution, and Manifestation |
| Magic to world | Receive a bounded direct effect and footprint | [Magic](../magic/README.md) resolves source, access, formation, cost, failure, and magical effect |

A chain may return later with changed inputs. Repeated exposure still does not let the World Engine manufacture the receiving system's result.

## Resolution Procedure

When a material cause enters play:

1. **Name the resolved input.** Cite the owning rule or established world event.
2. **Load relevant state.** Use the external Campaign Record and the narrowest useful resolution.
3. **Record the direct node.** Separate what happened from what might follow.
4. **Map exposed subjects.** Identify who or what actually receives the change.
5. **Propose material links.** Include only consequences that can affect a decision, dependency, or future state.
6. **Test every link.** Identify transmitter, requirements, scope, timing, owner, evidence, uncertainty, and alternatives.
7. **Consult actors.** Resolve information, motive, capacity, consent, coordination, and opposition.
8. **Apply Counterforces.** Let prevention, adaptation, substitution, refusal, and recovery change the route.
9. **Resolve due branches.** Preserve unresolved futures rather than preselecting them.
10. **Update state once.** Record changed variables under their owners, with distribution and uncertainty.
11. **Record delays and feedback.** Create Pending Consequences and review conditions externally.
12. **Set the Causal Horizon.** Stop when further detail cannot yet change a meaningful decision or preserved dependency.
13. **Communicate proportionately.** Reveal only what the current information model allows, including observable signs of material pressure where fair choice requires them.

## Compression and Expansion

At low detail, retain:

- decisive causes;
- autonomous actors whose choices can reverse the result;
- bottlenecks and Counterforces;
- irreversible or identity-defining consequences;
- unresolved branches that may matter later;
- distribution, information, and uncertainty that could change the conclusion;
- Pending Consequences and World Legacies.

Compression may combine repetitive links into one supported interval. It cannot turn possibility into fact, erase a material minority, assume perfect coordination, or conceal that a result depends on an unresolved specialist claim.

Expansion restores detail from established causes. It does not retroactively invent convenient hidden actors, capabilities, or exceptions.

The later Simulation Abstraction task defines the complete procedure for moving between resolutions.

## Worked Examples

### Shortage Diverted by Local Action

A failed harvest reduces accessible grain in three settlements. This establishes a resource input, not rebellion.

Merchants know one alternative route, but bandit attacks increase its risk. A monastery has reserves and a duty of relief, while the local governor intends to preserve military stores. Villagers organize repairs to an old river landing.

The chain branches into rationing, guarded trade, negotiated release, hoarding, and transport restoration. If the landing opens before household reserves fail, scarcity may ease while debt and political resentment remain. The original pressure was real; the expected rebellion was not destiny.

### Apex Loss and Uneven Ecology

Hunters eliminate an apex predator from one valley. Prey survival rises, but only where forage, disease, competing predators, and movement routes permit. Crop damage increases near farms; a scavenger population declines; a neighboring predator moves into the vacated range.

The World Engine records ecological links and resulting pressures. It does not grant a monster Evolution. Any Adaptation, Mutation, or Evolution is returned to Monster Evolution rules with the established exposure and duration.

### Magical Communication Network

A Ritual network makes long-distance messages faster for subscribing cities. Magic establishes valid range, operators, source, costs, failures, and direct transmission.

The World Engine traces adoption. Some cities cannot afford maintenance. One priesthood refuses the source relationship. Faster information improves coordination for one alliance, encourages centralized authority, changes market expectations, and makes sabotage more valuable. None of those consequences is contained in the Spell itself.

### Death, Succession, and Reincarnation

A ruler dies permanently in public while their continuing soul enters Interlife.

The death directly ends the current body's worldly action. Succession depends on law, recognition, heirs, force, information, and faction choices. The Soul Engine separately resolves Echoes, Titles, Resonance, and Reincarnation. A later incarnation does not automatically reclaim office, property, or allegiance.

The political chain can continue for decades while the soul is absent. Its consequences may later become part of the new incarnation's environment without becoming retained world ownership.

### False Cure Rumor

A traveler reports that one herb cures a spreading illness. The belief moves faster than verification. Demand rises, prices change, foragers damage habitat, and some patients delay effective care.

The rumor is a real informational cause even if the cure is false. Disease rules own efficacy and transmission. Economy and ecology rules later own their downstream claims. Correcting the rumor requires trusted information routes; merely knowing the truth as GM does not inform the population.

### Contained Reinforcing Loop

A damaged aqueduct reduces service, illness removes repair labor, and lower repair capacity further reduces service. This is reinforcing feedback.

A neighboring guild provides temporary workers, households conserve water, and healers prioritize the repair crews. These Counterforces may contain the loop without restoring the aqueduct. Debt, deferred maintenance, and political obligations remain as legacies.

## Safeguards

- Never begin with a desired future event and backfill convenient causes.
- Never treat a pressure, correlation, threshold, trope, prophecy, or likely outcome as a command.
- Never let an actor respond to information they cannot access or understand.
- Never assume collective actors possess one mind, perfect coordination, or unanimous goals.
- Never erase Counterforces, substitutes, refusal, adaptation, or recovery because they reduce drama.
- Never use randomness to create missing capability, authority, source, or access.
- Never resolve a specialist effect under the World Engine merely to keep a chain moving.
- Never trace every imaginable consequence; use a Causal Horizon tied to meaningful decisions and dependencies.
- Never stop a material chain solely because the player is absent.
- Never make the player the cause of unrelated world change by narrative gravity.
- Never rewind harm when an initiating cause ends.
- Never turn reinforcing feedback into unbounded growth; re-evaluate constraints and state after every material pass.
- Keep every named node, branch, pending consequence, and current chain state outside this repository.

## Scope Boundaries

This document does not define:

- current campaign events, histories, actors, or chain records;
- complete variables or formulas for any later World Engine domain;
- universal probabilities, thresholds, event tables, or random generators;
- encounters, quests, plots, or mandatory story beats;
- specialist capability, Soul, Evolution, Class, Soul Weapon, or Magic outcomes;
- the final simulation-resolution procedure;
- World Stability, Ages, Resets, or Gates.
