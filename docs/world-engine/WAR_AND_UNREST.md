# War and Unrest

## Purpose

This document defines how political contestation, collective disruption, coercive escalation, organized violence, territorial control, cessation, and conflict legacies operate within the [World Engine](README.md).

It does not create universal combat math, military levels, damage rules, encounter budgets, moral alignments, or scripted wars. Individual action remains governed by actual embodiment, [Capability Assessment](../progression/CAPABILITY_ASSESSMENT.md), Skills, Development, equipment, Soul Weapons, Magic, information, environment, and choice. Faction-scale action uses [Faction Behaviour](FACTION_BEHAVIOUR.md).

## Core Rule

> War and unrest emerge when particular actors organize around contested conditions and use disruption, coercion, or force through real faction, institutional, logistical, and social routes. They persist only while those routes and reasons persist.

Therefore:

- disagreement does not inevitably become violence;
- hardship does not automatically create revolt;
- a declaration does not mobilize an army;
- military victory does not automatically achieve a political objective;
- occupation does not create ownership, legitimacy, loyalty, or peace;
- unrest is not synonymous with riot, criminality, revolution, or war;
- the side with greater destructive force is not universally superior;
- a conflict continues off-screen according to its causes and capacities rather than waiting for the player;
- cessation of fighting does not erase injuries, displacement, obligations, fear, ecological damage, institutional change, or memory.

## Conflict Forms

### Contestation

**Contestation** is active disagreement over authority, access, obligations, identity, territory, resources, policy, safety, recognition, or another consequential condition. It may be expressed through negotiation, litigation, ritual challenge, elections, refusal, strikes, boycotts, migration, protest, sabotage, coercion, or violence.

Contestation is not automatically dysfunction. It can expose hidden burdens, correct capture, clarify incompatible claims, or produce new institutions.

### Civil Unrest

**Civil Unrest** is sustained or recurring collective disruption within a social order through which actors challenge, refuse, obstruct, evade, or demand change to current authority, burdens, distribution, recognition, safety, or institutions.

Civil Unrest may involve:

- assemblies, demonstrations, petitions, vigils, and occupations of public space;
- strikes, boycotts, slowdowns, refusal, tax resistance, or withdrawal of cooperation;
- mutual aid, parallel administration, sanctuary, or alternative institutions;
- evasion, sabotage, blockades, riots, insurrection, mutiny, or revolt;
- repression, surveillance, concession, reform, counter-mobilization, or negotiated settlement.

It may be peaceful, mixed, or violent. Its existence does not prove that all residents participate, that all participants share one objective, that authorities are legitimate or illegitimate, or that escalation is inevitable.

### Armed Conflict

**Armed Conflict** is organized, recurring use of materially harmful force between actors pursuing incompatible or contested objectives. It can remain local, intermittent, asymmetric, undeclared, or embedded within broader unrest.

### War

**War** is sustained organized conflict in which factions or institutions mobilize substantial coercive and destructive capacity across multiple actions to compel, deny, defend, control, exhaust, displace, or transform opposing actors and conditions.

War need not involve states, formal declarations, continuous battle, or symmetrical forces. Raids, insurgency, siege, magical interdiction, proxy conflict, ecological manipulation, and control of movement may all be part of war when their coordination and continuity support a broader conflict objective.

The labels used by participants may differ from world truth. A ruler may call a war a policing action; rebels may call scattered violence a liberation war; distant observers may not know whether a ceasefire holds. Resolve the actual organization, actions, and consequences rather than accepting one label as authoritative.

## No Universal Escalation Ladder

Conflict forms are not mandatory stages. A petition may produce reform, a violent incident may remain isolated, an apparent peace may conceal coercion, and a war may end through exhaustion without resolving the original dispute.

**Escalation Pressure** is a scoped condition that makes broader, more destructive, less reversible, or less controlled conflict responses more likely or attractive to particular actors. Examples include:

- perceived threat, humiliation, impunity, revenge, or existential fear;
- failed communication, uncertainty, propaganda, or misread mobilization;
- narrowing peaceful routes or punishing compromise;
- unequal burdens, exclusion, institutional capture, or broken obligations;
- access to armed supporters, magical force, fortified sites, or outside patrons;
- leaders whose position benefits from continued confrontation;
- retaliation cycles, atrocities, or fragmented command;
- short decision windows and irreversible preparations.

Counterforces can reduce or redirect escalation:

- trusted mediation, verification, communication, and time;
- credible concessions, reform, restitution, or accountability;
- protected exit, sanctuary, evacuation, or face-saving alternatives;
- defensive success that changes risk without demanding retaliation;
- resource relief, restored services, or reduced dependency;
- dissent, refusal, desertion, or loss of faction cohesion;
- exhaustion, changed leadership, outside guarantees, or common threats.

Pressure never decides an actor's choice. De-escalation does not require forgiveness, agreement, trust, or return to the prior order.

## Causes, Triggers, and Objectives

### Causes Are Layered

A conflict may have several interacting causes:

- incompatible claims to authority, territory, access, resources, or obligations;
- unequal distribution, exploitation, exclusion, dependency, or insecurity;
- ecological change, migration, shortages, disease, or infrastructure failure;
- religious, cultural, historical, or symbolic disputes;
- institutional capture, succession, disputed legitimacy, or broken procedure;
- fear of future loss, preventive strategy, or security dilemmas;
- outside intervention, patronage, trade incentives, or proxy relationships;
- personal ambitions translated into faction action through real routes.

Species, culture, religion, ancestry, form, and Soul identity do not cause violence by themselves. Actors interpret conditions through histories and institutions; those interpretations can support solidarity, prejudice, restraint, negotiation, or conflict.

### Triggers Are Not Complete Causes

A triggering arrest, assassination, failed Ritual, border encounter, shortage, prophecy, or insult may alter timing and coordination without explaining why large groups respond. Trace the conditions, interpretations, relationships, and action routes that make the trigger consequential.

Removing one trigger does not automatically remove the underlying pressures. Conversely, severe pressure may remain politically inactive when communication, organization, opportunity, or willingness is absent.

### Conflict Objectives

A **Conflict Objective** is a result a faction attempts to achieve through contestation, coercion, or organized force. State it in actionable terms:

- what condition is to change or remain;
- who or what is affected;
- the relevant territory, route, institution, source, or relationship;
- the time horizon and acceptable costs;
- the action and support routes;
- protected commitments and forbidden methods, if any;
- stopping, revision, and negotiation conditions;
- how the faction would recognize success or failure.

Public justifications, leaders' private objectives, members' interests, field commands, and allies' goals may differ. Objectives can change during conflict as information, losses, opportunities, institutions, and leadership change.

## Mobilization and Participation

War uses the [Faction Mobilization](FACTION_BEHAVIOUR.md#from-decision-to-action) rules. A declaration or crisis does not place every member under effective command.

Mobilization may require:

- lawful or customary authority, persuasion, bargaining, or coercion;
- recruitment, training, role assignment, and command relationships;
- food, water, Mana access, medicine, shelter, transport, maintenance, and pay;
- weapons, tools, Soul Weapon consent, magical sources, Ritual preparation, and specialists;
- maps, intelligence, communication, codes, interpreters, and reconnaissance;
- replacement, care, rest, repair, burial, evacuation, and reintegration routes;
- continuing support from households, workers, institutions, patrons, allies, and affected populations.

Participation remains differentiated. Volunteers, conscripts, officers, healers, logisticians, scouts, spies, ritualists, laborers, contractors, coerced people, local guides, dependents, and bystanders do not have identical authority, knowledge, consent, risk, or responsibility.

Refusal, evasion, desertion, mutiny, conscientious objection, divided loyalty, and surrender remain possible when actors have causal routes to them. Coercion may compel action without creating trust, legitimacy, or reliable long-term commitment.

## Operational Capacity

**Operational Capacity** is a faction's current ability to perform and sustain one specified conflict function under stated conditions. There is no universal army strength score.

Assess separate functions such as:

- detect and interpret threats;
- mobilize or disperse forces;
- communicate decisions;
- move through a particular environment;
- hold or deny a route;
- protect, evacuate, hide, or supply a Population;
- conduct a raid, siege, blockade, pursuit, defense, or withdrawal;
- perform and sustain a magical operation;
- treat casualties and replace losses;
- maintain legitimacy, discipline, secrecy, or coalition support;
- recover after disruption.

Each function depends on current participants, Skills, Development, Professions, command, Faction Cohesion, intelligence, logistics, resources, equipment, Magic, Soul Weapon cooperation, geography, ecology, time, opposition, and uncertainty.

### Force Projection

**Force Projection** is the ability to deliver and sustain a specified coercive or destructive effect beyond an actor's secure base through actual movement, communication, supply, access, and recovery routes.

Potential destruction at home does not prove distant reach. A dragon may be devastating near its lair but unable or unwilling to sustain a campaign across hostile climate. A portal network may increase reach while creating fixed bottlenecks and maintenance dependencies.

### Readiness and Reserves

Readiness is function- and time-specific. A force prepared to defend a wall tonight may be unable to pursue for a month. Reserves matter only when they are trained or usable, reachable, supplied, authorized, and released in time.

Strategic Reserves, dormant fortifications, sealed spells, old alliances, Legacy Weapons, and veteran populations are not automatically available. Check condition, access, consent, knowledge, compatibility, and mobilization.

### Command and Communication

Military command combines Formal Authority, doctrine, communication, logistics, intelligence, morale, strategy, and responsibility. Orders travel through real routes and may arrive late, be misunderstood, conflict, be falsified, or meet changed conditions.

Centralization may improve coherence while creating bottlenecks. Decentralization may improve local response while increasing divergence. Neither is universally superior.

### Intelligence and Deception

Intelligence remains an evidence process, not omniscience. Scouts, spies, divination, captured records, Soul Echoes, Weapon Souls, informants, and magical observation retain their own sources, limits, bias, consent, and counterplay.

Deception changes what observers reasonably believe through actual signals and concealment. It cannot retroactively erase contrary evidence or guarantee that every audience draws the intended conclusion.

## Resolving Conflict Actions

When an operation reaches direct action:

1. state the real objective and success condition;
2. identify participants, roles, current condition, and willingness;
3. establish information and uncertainty from the relevant perspective;
4. identify preparation, terrain, timing, tools, supply, communication, and support;
5. use [Capability Assessment](../progression/CAPABILITY_ASSESSMENT.md) for contextual advantage rather than totals;
6. resolve particular physical, Skill, magical, Soul Weapon, social, and species claims through their Owning Systems;
7. apply resistance, counterplay, interruption, retreat, surrender, and changed objectives where causally available;
8. record direct effects, costs, information, relationship changes, systemic responses, delayed effects, and persistent state;
9. update faction capacity and objectives from actual results rather than declared victory.

An operation can succeed tactically and fail strategically. Taking a bridge may expose supply, harden resistance, alienate an ally, or achieve a necessary evacuation. Losing a battle may still delay pursuit or preserve a faction's decisive capability.

## Geography, Routes, and Control

### Territorial Control

**Territorial Control** is the function-specific ability to perform, permit, prevent, observe, tax, protect, supply, or enforce particular actions in a place during a stated interval. It is not one binary color on a map.

Different actors may simultaneously control:

- roads but not forests;
- daylight movement but not night travel;
- surface ground but not tunnels, airspace, waterways, Soul routes, or magical Channels;
- taxation but not local legitimacy;
- fortified sites but not surrounding food production;
- communication but not physical occupation;
- formal law but not practical enforcement.

Control requires continuing people, information, access, logistics, and response capacity. A battlefield victory or raised flag does not establish every function.

### Occupation

**Occupation** is sustained external or contested control over specified governance, movement, resources, institutions, or security functions in a place through present coercive capacity.

Occupation does not establish ownership, consent, citizenship, legitimacy, cultural assimilation, loyalty, factual authority, or permanent continuity. Occupiers still depend on agents, interpreters, supplies, collaborators, institutions, information, and local response. Occupied people retain agency and may comply, negotiate, adapt, evade, resist, collaborate for varied reasons, or pursue unrelated survival.

### Siege and Blockade

A **Siege** uses sustained containment, pressure, or attack against a bounded site. A **Blockade** attempts to deny specified movement, information, resource, or support routes across a boundary.

Neither is a simple timer. Resolve:

- which routes are actually covered;
- hidden, magical, aerial, aquatic, subterranean, spiritual, or social alternatives;
- Stocks, Effective Supply, Sustenance Profiles, care, repair, sanitation, and morale;
- the bodies and populations affected unequally;
- sorties, smuggling, relief, negotiation, internal conflict, and outside response;
- ecological damage, disease exposure, waste, and infrastructure strain;
- whether pressure remains enforceable and politically acceptable.

Siege and blockade consequences do not become automatic surrender at a universal threshold.

## Civilian and Noncombatant Agency

People not currently participating in hostilities are not scenery, reward objects, homogeneous populations, or automatic supporters of a nearby faction. Combatant, civilian, healer, captive, dependent, messenger, official, and mixed roles may be disputed or change over time; labels do not erase actual conduct, rights, agency, vulnerability, or responsibility.

Conflict simulation preserves:

- warning, shelter, evacuation, sanctuary, and return routes;
- food, medicine, care, accessibility, lifecycle, and species-specific needs;
- family, community, institution, livelihood, spiritual, and ecological ties;
- choices to remain, flee, negotiate, hide, resist, aid, refuse, or organize;
- unequal exposure by location, body, status, role, wealth, access, or prejudice;
- loss, grief, disability, trauma, displacement, exploitation, and recovery;
- testimony, evidence, accountability, memory, and contested narrative.

Collective identity does not establish collective guilt. Responsibility follows actual decisions, participation, authority, knowledge, coercion, and consequence routes. One actor's crime cannot automatically authorize harm against every person categorized with them.

## Unrest Dynamics

When Civil Unrest matters, identify:

1. **contested conditions:** burdens, authority, access, recognition, safety, distribution, or broken commitments;
2. **affected actors:** who experiences which effects and how they interpret them;
3. **coordination routes:** organizers, networks, institutions, symbols, spaces, communication, and trust;
4. **demands and disagreements:** public claims, minimum conditions, internal divisions, and time horizons;
5. **participation costs:** lost income, retaliation, danger, stigma, care obligations, or inaccessible methods;
6. **authority response:** observation, communication, concession, reform, delay, division, repression, or counter-mobilization;
7. **audiences:** residents, institutions, outsiders, gods, spirits, factions, and potential allies;
8. **feedback:** what each action proves or appears to prove to different participants;
9. **off-ramps:** negotiation, verification, protected assembly, restitution, procedural change, safe exit, or transformed institutions;
10. **legacies:** altered trust, law, organization, memory, migration, economy, and future expectations.

Scarcity or inequality can create pressure without producing one political response. A prosperous society can experience unrest over exclusion, identity, authority, safety, or expectation. Repression can suppress visible action while intensifying hidden organization, or it can fragment coordination; the outcome depends on actual routes and interpretation.

## Conflict Economies and Ecology

War redirects labor, Stocks, infrastructure, transport, credit, taxation, production, and exchange. It can create rationing, requisition, profiteering, debt, black markets, patronage, substitution, concentration, and dependency. Apply [Economies](ECONOMIES.md) rather than treating all resources as one war meter.

Conflict also changes habitats, migration routes, predation, waste, fire regimes, Mana conditions, and ecological functions. Monsters and other populations may flee, adapt, exploit disturbance, lose transition sites, or be deliberately weaponized. Apply [Ecology and Migration](ECOLOGY_AND_MIGRATION.md) and preserve source agency and nonhuman consequences.

Using famine, disease, forced migration, ecological collapse, Soul harm, or magical contamination as a method requires actual capability and creates persistent consequences. It is never a consequence-free strategic button.

## Magic, Monsters, Souls, and Soul Weapons

### Magic

Magic may change reconnaissance, communication, concealment, transport, fortification, healing, destruction, supply, and control through established sources and procedures. It does not erase Mana limits, Access, Skill, embodiment, counterplay, maintenance, or source agency. There is no universal magical superiority over nonmagical strategy.

### Monsters

Monster warfare follows political, ecological, economic, religious, defensive, and historical causes rather than innate hostility. Species Traits and forms affect movement, senses, sustenance, infrastructure, and tactics without supplying doctrine, discipline, consent, or strategy. Evolution remains owned by Monster Evolution and cannot be awarded for military service or victory.

### Souls and Reincarnation

Final Death, Soul harm, Echo formation, Titles, Resonance, and Reincarnation use the [Soul Engine](../soul/README.md). Ordinary warfare does not bypass soul-harm safeguards or make annihilation default.

Reincarnation preserves no current military rank, command, citizenship, oath, property, enemy status, sentence, or alliance automatically. A returning war leader may retain memory or a Soul Title while lacking a suitable body, current authority, accurate information, trusted relationships, or the right to continue an old conflict.

### Soul Weapons

Weapon Souls retain personhood, consent, current condition, memory boundaries, Bonds, and independent objectives. They may serve, refuse, desert, surrender, negotiate, or remember conflict differently from wielders and institutions. They are not strategic inventory, and command authority cannot compel Bond functions beyond established rules.

Conflict can contribute meaningful shared history, awakening pressure, or evolution evidence only through the [Soul Weapon](../soul-weapons/README.md) rules. Intensity, killing, victory, repeated danger, or near death alone grants nothing.

## Cessation, Settlement, and Aftermath

### Ceasefires and Truces

A **Ceasefire** is an agreement or convergent practice to suspend specified hostile actions under stated participants, scope, timing, verification, exceptions, and response conditions. It does not resolve every objective or create trust.

A truce may be narrower, local, temporary, seasonal, ritual, or purpose-specific. Both depend on communication, authority, compliance, monitoring, and the ability to handle accidents, spoilers, and disputed evidence.

### Surrender and Settlement

Surrender changes only the forces, rights, duties, custody, territory, equipment, or actions actually covered by its terms and enforcement. One commander may lack authority to surrender every faction, community, monster polity, Weapon Soul, or future incarnation.

A settlement may exchange recognition, withdrawal, access, restitution, guarantees, reform, amnesty, trial, demobilization, migration, resource rights, or institutional change. Terms remain subject to actual authority, consent, enforcement, interpretation, and future conditions.

### Demobilization

**Demobilization** is the reduction or transformation of organized conflict capacity through release, disarmament, reassignment, reintegration, institutional change, monitoring, resource conversion, and care. It is not accomplished by declaring peace.

Former fighters retain bodies, Skills, relationships, grievances, obligations, trauma, status, weapons, knowledge, and economic needs. Institutions built for war may seek new purposes or resist losing power. Soul Weapons and magical sources require their own consent, custody, safety, and relationship resolutions.

### Conflict Legacies

A **Conflict Legacy** is a persistent condition created or transformed by war or unrest, such as:

- death, injury, disability, trauma, grief, missing persons, or altered family structures;
- displaced populations, changed settlements, contested return, or diaspora networks;
- ruins, fortifications, mines, contamination, altered Mana, and ecological succession;
- debt, ownership changes, destroyed capacity, new industries, and unequal recovery;
- laws, offices, emergency powers, veteran institutions, security practices, and captured structures;
- grudges, alliances, reputations, Soul Titles, Weapon Echoes, Archive Traces, myths, and disputed histories;
- unexploded hazards, sealed Rituals, abandoned constructs, and dormant weapons;
- changed borders, routes, languages, customs, technologies, and faction identities.

Recovery is not restoration. Rebuilt output does not revive the dead, restore prior distribution, resolve legitimacy, erase records, or return an ecology to its former state automatically.

## Simulation Procedure

When resolving war or unrest:

1. **Scope the conflict.** Identify place, participants, contested conditions, and time horizon.
2. **Separate forms.** Distinguish ordinary contestation, Civil Unrest, Armed Conflict, and War without forcing a ladder.
3. **Trace causes and triggers.** Preserve layered conditions, interpretations, and recent events.
4. **Record faction objectives.** Separate public, internal, allied, and field-level aims.
5. **Establish information positions.** Identify what each relevant actor knows, believes, hides, and can verify.
6. **Test mobilization.** Apply faction routes, participation, refusal, logistics, and time.
7. **Assess operational functions.** Compare only the capability needed for each objective and environment.
8. **Resolve actions through owners.** Use physical, Skill, profession, monster, Soul Weapon, Magic, social, and Soul rules without pooling them.
9. **Apply direct and distributed consequences.** Include civilians, resources, economies, ecology, institutions, and information.
10. **Update objectives and cohesion.** Let results, losses, evidence, dissent, and outside actors alter strategy.
11. **Test escalation and off-ramps.** Preserve several plausible responses rather than scripting continuation.
12. **Record cessation and legacies.** A stopped battle, ceasefire, surrender, settlement, and recovery are separate changes.

Use [Causal Event Chains](CAUSAL_EVENT_CHAINS.md) for branches, delays, feedback, Counterforces, and Pending Consequences. Use only enough detail to preserve choices, bottlenecks, and persistent effects.

## Worked Examples

### The Stronger Army Cannot Reach

A mountain empire has more trained soldiers, siege magic, and reserves than a valley coalition. Its roads cannot support the soldiers' Sustenance Profiles during winter, its flying scouts cannot see through local Mana weather, and its command codes are compromised.

The empire is superior at open battle near its depots but has poor Force Projection into the valley. The coalition can delay, misdirect, and survive without becoming universally stronger. The conflict turns on routes and objectives, not totals.

### A Peaceful Strike Becomes a Political Crisis

Dockworkers stop handling alchemical cargo after repeated uninvestigated injuries. Their objective is independent inspection and paid recovery time. The port authority calls the strike sabotage; merchants split over whether delay or unsafe work is the greater threat.

The unrest is real without violence. If guards seize union records, information and legitimacy may shift; if inspectors gain access, the dispute may de-escalate. Neither response is predetermined, and one thrown object does not make every striker an armed faction.

### The Occupied City Is Not Controlled in One Way

An invading force holds gates, granaries, and the governor's palace. Neighborhood councils still distribute water, tunnel communities control underground movement, shrine networks carry messages, and tax collectors falsify records.

The invader controls entry and formal decrees but lacks reliable information, night movement, local legitimacy, and full resource distribution. Mapping the city as simply conquered would erase the functions that decide occupation.

### Monster Forms Change Logistics

A multispecies polity fields gliders, burrowers, aquatic couriers, and immobile fortress forms. These bodies create unusual routes, but each has distinct sustenance, rest, communication, and care requirements.

The polity can bypass one blockade while becoming dependent on seasonal currents and transition-site medicine. Natural capability changes operations without supplying perfect coordination or free Evolution.

### The Returning General

A renowned general reincarnates after three centuries. A Soul Title and a Legacy Weapon persuade some veterans' descendants that the child is authentic. The present army uses different magic, terrain, institutions, and command law.

The child may contribute retained judgment after rebuilding access and current knowledge. They do not inherit rank or an army, and the Weapon Soul may oppose restarting a war it remembers as catastrophic.

### Victory Creates Another Conflict

A rebellion captures the capital and dissolves the secret police. It succeeds at removing the ruler but has no agreed food-distribution authority, several armed coalitions, displaced civil servants, and provinces that never accepted the rebellion's mandate.

The victory is real and incomplete. Demobilization, administration, legitimacy, resource access, accountability, and faction continuity now determine whether the new order stabilizes, fragments, negotiates, or returns to violence.

## Safeguards

- Do not reduce war to army size, troop count, one battle, one leader, one spell, one monster form, or one power score.
- Do not treat unrest as automatically violent, irrational, illegitimate, revolutionary, criminal, or unified.
- Do not infer inevitable conflict from species, culture, faith, ancestry, form, scarcity, migration, prophecy, Soul Title, or historical grievance.
- Do not let declarations, laws, borders, flags, maps, or victories establish practical control without real routes.
- Do not convert occupation into ownership, legitimacy, consent, loyalty, or cultural erasure.
- Do not make civilians passive scenery, acceptable collateral by category, quest rewards, or one homogeneous allegiance.
- Do not assign collective guilt from identity or proximity; trace actual action, authority, knowledge, coercion, and responsibility.
- Do not grant Skills, Development, Stat XP, Soul Depth, Resonance, Titles, Evolution, Soul Weapon awakening, or magical resistance merely for fighting, killing, suffering, surviving, winning, or dying.
- Do not allow command to erase the agency of soldiers, conscripts, monsters, civilians, gods, spirits, or Weapon Souls.
- Do not make atrocities consequence-free, strategically perfect, or required for optimal progression.
- Do not use hidden forces, reinforcements, weapons, or betrayals without prior causal existence and fair evidence appropriate to their effect.
- Do not make peace erase aftermath or make every unresolved grievance restart war automatically.
- Do not use this document to implement disease evolution, advancement, dungeon activity, World Stability, Ages, resets, Gates, or long-horizon simulation before their roadmap tasks are complete.
- Do not store named wars, current fronts, live armies, active unrest, casualty lists, occupied places, leaders, treaties, or conflict timelines in this repository.

## External Campaign Records

A campaign may maintain a qualitative **Conflict Profile** containing only what current play requires:

- scope, forms, participants, factions, affected populations, and perspectives;
- contested conditions, layered causes, triggers, and uncertainty;
- public, internal, allied, and operational objectives;
- mobilization routes, participation, refusal, command, and communication;
- function-specific Operational Capacities, logistics, dependencies, and bottlenecks;
- Force Projection, relevant geography, routes, and Territorial Control;
- civilian conditions, displacement, care, resources, economy, ecology, and Magic effects;
- escalation pressures, Counterforces, negotiation routes, and Pending Consequences;
- current operations and actual results;
- ceasefires, surrender terms, settlement claims, compliance, and disputes;
- demobilization, recovery, accountability, and Conflict Legacies;
- evidence state and information boundaries for every material claim.

This Profile is external campaign state. The repository contains no completed Conflict Profile or live war.
