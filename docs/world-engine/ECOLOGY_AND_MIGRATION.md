# Ecology and Migration

## Purpose

This document defines how Eternal Cycle simulates current ecological relationships, habitat change, disturbance, recovery, range shifts, dispersal, migration, displacement, arrival, and cross-region consequences.

The completed [Monster Ecology](../monster-evolution/MONSTER_ECOLOGY.md) rules define how monsters participate in habitats, niches, Food Webs, Symbiosis, Territory, Carrying Capacity, and ecological pressure. This document does not redesign them. It supplies the broader World Engine procedure for current ecosystem state and movement involving all populations, civilizations, environments, and established supernatural processes.

## Core Rule

An **Ecosystem** is a scoped network of living, embodied, constructed, magical, spiritual, social, and environmental participants whose conditions and relationships materially affect one another's persistence.

An ecological outcome follows actual relationships across time. No environment seeks balance, rewards purity, punishes civilization, guarantees adaptation, or restores a prior state by narrative instinct.

Migration is the movement of embodied beings or populations through viable routes for established reasons. It changes origin, transit, destination, and connected ecosystems. Migrants remain persons or living beings with distinct capabilities, information, relationships, and agency; they are not a faceless event token.

## Ownership Boundary

The World Engine owns:

- current habitat conditions and connectivity;
- current distribution and interaction of populations;
- changes in resource webs, disturbance, recovery, and ecological feedback;
- movement pressures, routes, departure, transit, arrival, settlement, return, and displacement;
- world-side consequences passed among regions and systems;
- current ecological uncertainty and external campaign records.

Existing systems retain:

- anatomy, Species Traits, life cycles, Adaptation, Mutation, Hybridization, Evolution, extinction, and replacement under [Monster Evolution](../monster-evolution/README.md);
- individual Development and learned Skills under their owners;
- Soul identity, Reincarnation, Retained Instincts, and Soul Avatars under the [Soul Engine](../soul/README.md);
- magical sources, laws, effects, and compatibility under [Magic](../magic/README.md);
- resource access and renewal under [Resources and Food](RESOURCES_AND_FOOD.md);
- demographic membership and continuity under [Populations](POPULATIONS.md).

Ecology can supply exposure, pressure, access, consequence, and opportunity. It cannot manufacture an owned capability or transition.

## Ecological State

An **Ecological State** is the current, scoped configuration of:

- physical, magical, spiritual, constructed, and social habitat conditions;
- participating populations and life stages;
- resource and waste Flows;
- predation, competition, Symbiosis, parasitism, care, cultivation, and other relationships;
- occupied and available Ecological Niches;
- migration routes, barriers, refuges, breeding sites, and seasonal cycles;
- disturbance, recovery, succession, and legacies;
- observation, uncertainty, and hidden dependencies.

Record only relationships that can change a meaningful process. An ecosystem model is not an exhaustive species catalogue.

## Habitat and Connectivity

A Habitat includes the accessible conditions a being or Population needs, not merely the terrain label on a map.

**Habitat Connectivity** describes whether usable paths link the places required by one life cycle, population, or ecological function. It depends on:

- route existence and condition;
- timing and season;
- body and movement compatibility;
- rest, feeding, spawning, repair, shelter, and refuge sites;
- barriers, roads, settlements, borders, predators, war, and law;
- magical boundaries, Currents, Gates, curses, and source permission;
- knowledge, navigation, social access, and safety.

A habitat may remain large in area while becoming unusably fragmented. A narrow corridor can sustain distant regions; losing it may produce delayed effects long after local damage appears small.

## Ecological Relationships and Functions

An **Ecological Function** is a material effect one participant or process performs within a stated ecosystem, such as pollination, predation, decomposition, soil formation, water regulation, Mana absorption, refuge construction, seed transport, disease regulation, or waste conversion.

Function is relational, not destiny or moral worth. One participant can perform several functions, and several participants can provide overlapping or partial substitutes.

When a participant declines, test:

- how concentrated the function was;
- whether alternatives exist and are accessible;
- whether timing and scale match the need;
- what learning, migration, adaptation, infrastructure, or Magic would be required;
- what new costs and dependencies substitution creates;
- whether the function itself is beneficial to every affected party.

Do not label a species universally essential or harmful from one local relationship.

## Carrying Capacity and Population Pressure

Use the canonical contextual definition of [Carrying Capacity](../monster-evolution/MONSTER_ECOLOGY.md#environmental-carrying-capacity) for one Population, habitat, and period.

Capacity changes with resources, access, waste, disease, predation, competition, life cycle, migration, infrastructure, cooperation, Magic, disturbance, and season. It may rise through habitat engineering or fall through hidden depletion.

Temporary abundance can produce **Ecological Overshoot** when current use or Population exceeds what the habitat can continue sustaining through its actual renewal routes. Overshoot may be buffered by reserves, imports, movement, dormancy, cooperation, or reduced use. It does not guarantee crash, Evolution, or conflict.

## Disturbance

An **Ecological Disturbance** is a bounded event or changed regime that materially alters habitat, relationships, resource Flows, population distribution, or ecological functions.

Disturbance may include:

- storms, fire, flood, drought, volcanic or geological change;
- disease, predation, arrival, local disappearance, or population surge;
- harvesting, farming, settlement, roads, extraction, pollution, or restoration;
- war, abandonment, fortification, displacement, or trade;
- magical surges, source changes, Ritual infrastructure, Drift, or Gate contact;
- dungeon expansion, collapse, or changed boundaries.

Disturbance is not synonymous with harm. Some processes depend on periodic fire, flood, migration, decay, grazing, or magical fluctuation. Suppressing disturbance can create different risks.

## Resistance, Resilience, and Recovery

Distinguish:

- **Ecological Resistance:** how strongly the current system limits change during an exposure;
- **Ecological Resilience:** its ability to sustain or reorganize material functions after change without assuming a return to the prior configuration;
- **Ecological Recovery:** the actual process through which populations, relationships, habitats, and functions rebuild, substitute, migrate, or reorganize;
- **Ecological Legacy:** persistent effects such as altered soil, missing symbionts, new corridors, contamination, learned avoidance, changed fire regime, or institutional dependence.

Recovery requires sources, propagules or participants, routes, time, tolerable conditions, and freedom from renewed disruption. A recovered function can be performed by a different ecology. That may preserve some lives while transforming identity, culture, risk, and future options.

## Ecological Succession

**Ecological Succession** is directional change in ecosystem composition and relationships as participants arrive, decline, modify conditions, and enable or constrain later participants.

Succession is contingent. It depends on source populations, routes, residual conditions, climate, soil, structures, Magic, disturbance recurrence, and intervention. It has no universal final stage.

A ruined city may become wetland, forest, undead territory, cultivated settlement, dungeon margin, magical desert, or a mixed Novel Ecology depending on causality.

## Novel Ecologies

A **Novel Ecology** is a persistent ecosystem configuration with combinations of participants, conditions, or relationships not previously established in that scope.

Novel does not mean unnatural, inferior, superior, stable, or safe. It may emerge through migration, Evolution, infrastructure, Magic, Gate contact, extinction, restoration, or long disturbance.

Assess its actual functions, dependencies, conflicts, and resilience rather than comparing it to an imagined pristine baseline.

## Ecological Cascades

Use [Causal Event Chains](CAUSAL_EVENT_CHAINS.md) to resolve an Ecological Cascade.

For each link:

1. identify the changed relationship or condition;
2. identify exposed populations, life stages, places, and times;
3. test substitute functions and Counterforces;
4. apply delays and seasonal order;
5. distinguish direct mortality from later continuity effects;
6. preserve distribution and migration options;
7. return Adaptation, Mutation, Evolution, Skill, Development, and Magic claims to their owners;
8. stop at the current Causal Horizon.

A cascade can branch, stall, reverse, or reorganize. Removing a predator does not universally increase all prey; removing prey does not instantly kill all predators.

## Movement Types

### Routine Mobility

Daily or local movement within a Population's ordinary range may affect resource use and exposure without becoming Migration.

### Dispersal

**Dispersal** is movement away from an origin that changes local distribution or establishes access to another area. It may be individual, life-stage-specific, episodic, or one-way.

### Migration

Migration is repeated, directional, or large-scale movement connected to life cycle, resources, climate, Magic, danger, reproduction, social organization, work, culture, or disturbance.

### Relocation

Relocation is a deliberate move intended to establish residence, habitat use, work, sanctuary, or institutional presence elsewhere.

### Displacement

**Displacement** is movement driven by conditions that materially remove safe or viable choice to remain. Flight from war, habitat loss, persecution, disaster, source failure, or ecological collapse may be displaced even when movers choose among available routes.

### Return and Circulation

Return movement reconnects an origin after absence. Circulation involves repeated movement among several places. Neither guarantees restoration of prior status, property, habitat, relationships, or ecological conditions.

## Migration Pressure and Choice

**Migration Pressure** is a condition that makes movement more relevant, necessary, attractive, or likely for a scoped Population without deciding that movement occurs.

Push and pull are not sufficient categories by themselves. Resolve:

- what movers know or believe about origin, route, and destination;
- who can decide and who is carried, compelled, left behind, or unable to move;
- bodies, numbers, life stages, care needs, possessions, and dependencies;
- cultural obligations, territory, memory, burial or spawning sites, institutions, and relationships;
- route capacity, timing, safety, cost, law, weather, and navigation;
- alternatives such as adaptation, aid, dormancy, resistance, negotiation, or local reorganization;
- expected and actual consequences.

Migration can be strategic, traditional, desperate, coerced, exploratory, commercial, devotional, reproductive, or mixed.

## Migration Routes

A **Migration Corridor** is a route and supporting sequence of conditions that enables a specified Population to move between required places during a relevant interval.

It may need:

- traversable terrain or medium;
- food, water, Mana, shelter, hosts, repair, or rest sites;
- navigation cues and knowledge;
- legal passage, negotiated access, or secrecy;
- protection from hazards, predators, factions, and disease;
- bridges, vessels, roads, portals, guides, or Ritual infrastructure;
- timing compatible with climate, life cycle, and destination capacity.

A corridor is more than a line on a map. Partial blockage can strand a particular cohort while others pass.

## Origin, Transit, and Destination

Migration changes several scopes.

### Origin

Possible consequences include reduced pressure, lost labor or care, abandoned niches, changed authority, remittances, grief, altered reproduction, and future return claims.

### Transit

Movement consumes resources and exposes movers and route communities to weather, predation, exploitation, disease, conflict, trade, information exchange, and ecological disturbance.

### Destination

Arrival may change Population composition, housing, labor, culture, institutions, resource demand, ecology, disease exposure, faction politics, and knowledge. Destination responses depend on actual capacity, law, perception, interests, relationships, and coordination.

### Connected Regions

Migration can redirect trade, predators, prey, seeds, parasites, Magic, rumor, technology, obligations, and conflict beyond origin and destination.

No stage determines a universal reception outcome.

## Settlement and Integration

Arrival is not the same as viable settlement or integration.

Assess:

- habitat and Sustenance Profile compatibility;
- access to shelter, territory, work, care, and institutions;
- existing inhabitants and ecological relationships;
- legal status, recognition, language, custom, and discrimination;
- internal organization, disagreement, and leadership;
- return, onward movement, seasonal circulation, and divided households;
- effects on source regions and existing destination populations;
- time and intergenerational change.

Integration does not require cultural erasure. Separation does not prove hostility. Conflict and cooperation both require actual causes.

## Range Expansion and Invasive Pressure

Range expansion is movement or reproduction beyond a prior occupied area. It is not automatically invasive.

Use the completed definition of [Invasive Population](../monster-evolution/EXTINCTION_AND_REPLACEMENT.md#invasive-populations): sustained disruptive pressure in a receiving ecology where current relationships and Counterforces do not contain the effects.

Invasiveness describes a relationship in one receiving ecology. It is not species essence, moral corruption, collective guilt, or permission for extermination. Humans, monsters, plants, microbes, constructs, and magical processes can all create invasive pressure under appropriate conditions.

## Civilization as Ecology

Settlements, farms, roads, mines, sewers, temples, dungeons, markets, and magical infrastructure are habitat features and ecological actors.

Civilizations may:

- create refuge, food, heat, waste, barriers, corridors, and Novel Ecologies;
- cultivate, domesticate, protect, persecute, displace, or depend on other populations;
- suppress one disturbance while amplifying another;
- move species intentionally or accidentally;
- preserve habitats through law, custom, sacred relationship, or management;
- become dependent on hidden ecological functions.

Civilization is not outside nature, and ecological consequence does not imply that all construction or change is wrong.

## Magical and Spiritual Ecology

Magic enters ecology only through established sources, laws, effects, footprints, and agency.

Track:

- Mana Fields and Currents as world conditions under Magic rules;
- beings that consume, transform, channel, regulate, or avoid established magical inputs;
- source consent and refusal;
- magical contamination, Drift, residue, and cleanup;
- Ritual, Enchantment, Alchemical, Divine, Gate, and dungeon effects as bounded inputs;
- dependence on maintained magical infrastructure;
- changed migration cues, barriers, habitats, and life cycles.

Ecological demand cannot create Mana, a spell, affinity, Divine response, or valid magical conversion.

## Ecology and Migration Procedure

When ecological change or movement matters:

1. **Define scope and question.** Identify ecosystem, Population, interval, and decision.
2. **Load established relationships.** Include habitats, resources, populations, functions, routes, disturbances, and uncertainty.
3. **Apply the direct change.** Use the owning resource, population, magic, war, disease, dungeon, or other rule.
4. **Identify exposed relationships.** Preserve life stages, places, seasons, and distribution.
5. **Test Counterforces and substitutes.** Include redundancy, behavior, infrastructure, refuge, and agency.
6. **Resolve timing and feedback.** Apply delayed reproduction, depletion, recovery, succession, and migration.
7. **For movement, trace all stages.** Origin, departure, transit, arrival, settlement, return, and connected regions.
8. **Return owned transitions.** Evolution, Adaptation, Skills, Development, Soul effects, and Magic remain specialist claims.
9. **Update ecological state.** Record changed relationships, connectivity, functions, pressures, and legacies once.
10. **Preserve uncertainty.** Separate observed absence from extinction and forecast from settled outcome.
11. **Set the next review condition.** Season, threshold, arrival, disturbance, recovery milestone, or renewed exposure.
12. **Record externally.** Named ecosystems, routes, populations, and events belong in the Campaign Record.

## Worked Examples

### Road Through a Breeding Corridor

A new road crosses the only wet-season breeding route for an amphibious monster. The road improves human trade but fragments Habitat Connectivity, adds noise and mortality, and creates pools favored by a parasite.

Possible responses include crossing structures, changed travel timing, relocation, population decline, learned avoidance, or an Evolutionary Pressure. The World Engine resolves current exposure and ecology; Monster Evolution owns any bodily transition.

### Predator Return

A predator returns to a valley after decades. It reduces one herbivore near open ground, changes grazing behavior, supplies remains to scavengers, threatens livestock, and becomes sacred to one community.

The outcome is not a simple restoration of balance. Fences, hunting, prey behavior, alternate predators, disease, tourism, worship, and settlement patterns create branches.

### Displaced Forest People

Fire and magical contamination force a forest community toward a city. Some members remain to protect sacred sites; some travel by river; elders need slower routes; one symbiotic species cannot survive urban conditions.

Population, resource, economy, ecology, and faction claims differ across cohorts. Migration pressure does not make the community one actor or make assimilation inevitable.

### Apparent Invasion

A large insect population appears after unusual rains. Officials call it invasive, but evidence shows dormant local eggs responding to a historical cycle.

The surge may still disrupt crops. The origin claim changes responsibility, expected recurrence, and possible Counterforces. Official belief can drive policy without becoming ecological truth.

### Ruined City Succession

After abandonment, broken aqueducts create marshes, buildings shelter monsters, gardens spread, and residual Enchantments warm several districts. A Novel Ecology develops over generations.

Reoccupation would disturb an existing system rather than restore empty land. The old city's history matters, but it does not prescribe the new ecology's final form.

### Gate-Borne Organism

A small organism crosses through a temporary Gate. It survives only near magical residue and initially causes little change. Years later, Ritual infrastructure expands the same conditions, allowing its range to grow.

Gate arrival alone did not make it invasive. Sustained effects, local Counterforces, habitat compatibility, and delayed infrastructure created the later ecological chain.

## Safeguards

- Never portray an ecosystem as seeking one ideal balance or final state.
- Never treat Ecological Niche, function, rarity, or importance as a power rank, destiny, or moral value.
- Never infer extinction from absence, range loss, or failed observation alone.
- Never make disturbance universally harmful or recovery synonymous with return to the past.
- Never make migration automatic, uniform, or consequence-free.
- Never treat migrants as one mind, a resource Flow without personhood, or an automatic threat.
- Never label all range expansion or migration invasive.
- Never use invasive status as species essence or collective moral guilt.
- Never let ecological pressure directly grant Evolution, Mutation, Skills, Development, Soul growth, or Magic.
- Never let civilizations stand outside ecological causality or make all ecological change proof of wrongdoing.
- Never assume magical ecology has infinite sources or non-agentive spirits.
- Keep every named ecosystem, migration route, population movement, and current ecological state outside this repository.

## Scope Boundaries

This document does not define:

- canonical ecosystems, species, migrations, climates, regions, or historical events;
- anatomy, life cycles, Evolution Routes, Adaptations, Mutations, Hybridization, extinction, or replacement;
- disease transmission, faction policy, war, border law, prices, advancement, dungeons, Gates, or World Stability;
- universal Carrying Capacity, resilience, migration, succession, or invasion formulas;
- moral permission for settlement, hunting, displacement, conservation, eradication, or compulsory integration;
- live maps, route records, or campaign world state.
