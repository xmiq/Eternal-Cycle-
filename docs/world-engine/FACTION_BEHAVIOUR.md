# Faction Behaviour

## Purpose

This document defines how factions form intentions, perceive conditions, coordinate action, adapt, divide, and persist within the [World Engine](README.md).

A faction is not one mind, one character sheet, one morality, or one guaranteed action. It is a changing coordination among distinct actors whose interests overlap enough to support some shared action through actual relationships, information, resources, procedures, identities, and routes.

This document owns faction-level world behaviour. It does not redefine [Institutions and Academies](../human/INSTITUTIONS_AND_ACADEMIES.md), personal [Social and Leadership Development](../progression/SOCIAL_AND_LEADERSHIP_DEVELOPMENT.md), [Monster Societies](../monster-evolution/MONSTER_SOCIETIES.md), or the capabilities of Souls, Skills, Classes, Professions, Soul Weapons, and Magic.

## Core Rule

> A faction acts only when particular participants can perceive or interpret a situation, place it on a decision route, authorize or support a response, mobilize the necessary people and means, and carry the response into effect.

Therefore:

- a threat known to one scout is not automatically known to every member;
- a leader's preference is not automatically a faction decision;
- an authorized policy is not automatically implemented;
- formal membership is not automatic agreement, loyalty, consent, or obedience;
- a shared identity is not a pooled Skill, Development track, Soul property, or universal motive;
- a faction may act inconsistently because different participants act from different information, incentives, authorities, and relationships;
- inaction can result from ignorance, disagreement, delay, incapacity, resistance, competing priorities, or a deliberate choice.

## What Counts as a Faction

A **Faction** is a scoped coordination among distinct actors pursuing one or more overlapping interests through recognizable relationships and action routes while retaining their separate agency.

A faction can be:

- a formal body, such as a council bloc, guild chapter, military command, temple order, trading consortium, or royal court;
- an organized part of a larger Institution, such as reformers within an academy or rival offices within a church;
- an informal network, such as smugglers, dissidents, families, patrons, informants, or migrating clans;
- a movement whose supporters coordinate without one central Institution;
- a monster pack, colony caucus, territorial alliance, or multispecies polity when its members actually coordinate interests and action;
- a temporary Coalition among otherwise separate parties;
- a supernatural coordination involving spirits, gods, distributed beings, Soul Weapons, or other persons, subject to their established agency and communication routes.

A useful faction boundary identifies:

1. **Scope:** which issue, place, time, or relationship makes the grouping relevant.
2. **Participants:** who can contribute to decisions or action, and in what role.
3. **Interests:** what overlapping concerns support coordination.
4. **Action routes:** how information, authority, support, resources, and instructions move.
5. **Boundary conditions:** how participation, refusal, entry, exit, and representation work.
6. **Continuity:** what lets the faction remain recognizably connected across changing members or leadership.

Being affected by the same event does not by itself create a faction. Neither a species, culture, Profession, social class, Population, settlement, faith, state, or Institution automatically has one shared agenda. Any of these may contain several factions, overlap several factions, or temporarily coordinate as one.

## Distinguishing Related Structures

| Structure | Distinguishing question |
| --- | --- |
| **Population** | Who is included in a demographic scope? |
| **Culture** | What meanings and practices are shared unevenly? |
| **Institution** | What durable roles, procedures, authority, knowledge, and resources coordinate activity? |
| **Faction** | Which actors currently coordinate around overlapping interests, and through what routes? |
| **Coalition** | Which distinct parties cooperate on stated objectives while retaining separate identities and exit conditions? |
| **State or polity** | Which institutions and relationships claim or exercise governance in a territory or over a people? |

One body may satisfy several descriptions. A merchant guild may be an Institution, contain rival factions, join a Coalition, serve a Population, and exercise state authority in a port. Each claim keeps its own scope and evidence.

## Membership and Participation

### Different Relationships to a Faction

The following are separate facts:

- formal membership;
- practical participation;
- leadership or office;
- support for one objective;
- material dependence;
- patronage or clientage;
- sympathy or identification;
- coerced compliance;
- representation by another actor;
- being governed, protected, taxed, employed, or claimed by the faction;
- being part of the audience whose recognition matters.

A subject is not necessarily a member. A beneficiary is not necessarily a supporter. A supporter is not necessarily authorized to speak. A formal member may obstruct policy, and an outsider may provide decisive support.

### Overlap, Entry, and Exit

Actors may belong to several factions whose demands align in one context and conflict in another. Membership changes through recruitment, invitation, birth, appointment, oath, dependency, adoption, coercion, conversion, alliance, defection, expulsion, withdrawal, capture, or changed identification.

Entry does not transfer every secret, relationship, resource, Skill, or authority. Exit does not erase knowledge, obligations, reputation, relationships, injuries, or consequences already created. Whether an actor can leave safely depends on real law, geography, resources, surveillance, relationships, and power.

### Representation

Representation requires a valid source and scope. Election, appointment, delegated authority, acclaim, custom, expertise, threat, or control may let one actor speak or decide for particular purposes. None establishes universal consent or perfect correspondence with every represented person's interests.

## Interests, Objectives, and Priorities

A faction's **interests** are conditions its participants have reason to protect, pursue, change, or avoid. Interests may be material, social, ecological, political, religious, magical, professional, existential, symbolic, or relational.

A **Faction Objective** is a current proposed or accepted result around which some portion of the faction can coordinate. A useful objective states:

- the intended result;
- the relevant place, subjects, and time horizon;
- who sponsors, authorizes, opposes, or must implement it;
- why it matters to different participants;
- acceptable costs and protected commitments;
- dependencies, risks, and stopping conditions;
- how success, failure, or revision could be recognized.

The faction's public declaration, formal doctrine, leaders' preferences, operating priorities, and members' lived interests may differ. The GM preserves those differences instead of selecting one statement as the faction's true personality.

Priorities change when conditions, information, leadership, membership, capability, costs, relationships, or perceived legitimacy change. Old commitments and procedures create path dependence, but they do not make adaptation impossible.

## Distributed Information

A faction has no automatic shared awareness. Its **Faction Information Position** is the issue-specific distribution of observations, records, interpretations, uncertainty, secrecy, and access among relevant participants and communication routes.

Information may be:

- observed directly by a few actors;
- delayed by distance, language, embodiment, infrastructure, or procedure;
- filtered through rank, office, trust, fear, prejudice, incentives, or secrecy;
- fragmented among specialists who cannot yet combine it;
- intentionally concealed, falsified, restricted, or selectively disclosed;
- misunderstood through doctrine, rumor, propaganda, or unfamiliar phenomena;
- accurate but unavailable to whoever can authorize a response;
- received but not believed, prioritized, or actionable.

Institutional Memory, records, Soul Echo testimony, prophetic claims, divine messages, Soul Weapon recollection, and magical observation remain fallible inputs under their owning systems. A faction does not gain the player's knowledge, the GM's world truth, or another member's private knowledge without a causal route.

## Decision Routes

Faction decisions can arise through formal procedure, delegated office, consensus, vote, command, bargaining, custom, ritual, distributed convention, patronage, threat, emergency practice, or spontaneous convergence. The chosen route determines who can participate, what evidence matters, what delay occurs, and what makes the result contestable.

Resolve a decision by asking:

1. Who recognizes that a decision is needed?
2. Who can place it before relevant participants?
3. What information reaches them, and how do they interpret it?
4. Who possesses formal authority, practical influence, vetoes, or agenda control?
5. Which interests, obligations, fears, and alternatives matter to each decisive actor?
6. What procedure, bargain, coercion, or convention produces the stated result?
7. Who accepts, contests, ignores, delays, or secretly undermines it?

Formal authority, cultural legitimacy, source consent, technical competence, factual truth, practical enforcement, and member support remain separate. A valid decision can be unpopular or unenforceable; a widely followed practice can lack legal authority; a powerful leader can still be wrong.

## From Decision to Action

**Faction Mobilization** is the process of turning an accepted or attempted objective into coordinated action through actual participants, communication, resources, access, logistics, authority, consent, and timing.

Use this action sequence when the distinction matters:

1. **Perceive:** some participants encounter a condition or claim.
2. **Interpret:** they assign meaning under incomplete information.
3. **Raise:** the issue reaches an agenda or action route.
4. **Decide:** authorized or influential participants select, reject, defer, or revise a response.
5. **Mobilize:** people, resources, relationships, permissions, and instructions are assembled.
6. **Execute:** particular actors attempt particular actions in the world.
7. **Observe:** evidence of results reaches some participants.
8. **Adapt:** objectives, procedures, relationships, or future expectations may change.

Every stage can succeed partially, fail, split, leak, be delayed, or create a different result from the stated objective. Record the actual effects, not merely the announcement.

### Faction Action Claims

A **Faction Action Claim** asserts that a faction knew, decided, promised, prohibited, supported, opposed, provided, or did something. Validate it by identifying:

- the faction version and relevant scope;
- the participants and representatives involved;
- the information they possessed;
- the authority, support, dissent, and procedure;
- the resources, communication, and execution route;
- the actual result and uncertainty.

For an Institution acting as a faction, also apply its [Institutional Action Claim](../human/INSTITUTIONS_AND_ACADEMIES.md#institutional-action-claim). A seal, title, founder, majority, prophecy, Soul Title, or public slogan cannot by itself establish action by every participant.

## Capacity Is Function-Specific

A faction's ability to negotiate, investigate, feed members, transmit orders, patrol territory, perform a Ritual, enforce a decision, hide activity, or recover after disruption can differ sharply. Do not assign one faction power score.

Each capability depends on the actual combination of:

- participants' current Skills, Development, embodiment, health, access, and willingness;
- institutional roles and procedures;
- resources, supply routes, infrastructure, tools, Soul Weapons, and magical sources;
- information quality, communication, trust, and security;
- relationships, permissions, legitimacy, and opposition;
- geography, ecology, timing, and competing obligations;
- bottlenecks, dependencies, maintenance, and replacement routes.

Collective Capability belongs to that coordination. It is not copied into each member, leader, reincarnated founder, or successor.

## Cohesion, Dissent, and Compliance

**Faction Cohesion** is the issue-specific ability and willingness of relevant participants to continue coordinating despite disagreement, cost, uncertainty, and pressure. It is not affection, loyalty, moral agreement, uniformity, obedience, or a universal statistic.

Cohesion can be supported by:

- shared interests, identity, trust, or danger;
- credible procedures and fair burden-sharing;
- effective communication and dispute resolution;
- material interdependence or protected relationships;
- legitimacy, habit, success, or meaningful commitments;
- coercion, surveillance, or lack of alternatives, though these create different fragilities.

Cohesion can be weakened or redirected by:

- unequal costs and benefits;
- secrecy, misinformation, humiliation, or betrayal;
- conflicting obligations, identities, or time horizons;
- leadership failure, succession disputes, or lost legitimacy;
- resource shortages, communication breakdown, or inaccessible members;
- capture of decision routes by a narrow interest;
- credible alternatives, safe exit, or rival coordination.

Compliance under coercion does not prove trust or durable cohesion. Dissent can improve decisions, preserve excluded knowledge, or expose capture; it is not automatically dysfunction. A cohesive faction can pursue a disastrous objective, while a divided faction may still coordinate one narrow response.

## Strategy, Learning, and Adaptation

Factions form plans from bounded information, prior habits, doctrine, capabilities, relationships, risks, and anticipated responses. They are neither perfectly rational nor required to repeat mistakes forever.

Adaptation requires a route by which consequences become evidence and evidence changes interpretation, authority, procedure, incentives, membership, or action. Learning may be uneven:

- field actors learn before distant leaders;
- records survive while tacit practice is lost;
- success reinforces a mistaken explanation;
- punishment suppresses accurate reporting;
- one office reforms while another resists;
- a new generation inherits procedures without understanding their origin;
- a Soul, Weapon Soul, spirit, or long-lived monster remembers events that the current Institution has mythologized differently.

Factions can innovate by combining members' real Skills, Development, Professions, Species Traits, magical access, tools, and relationships. Coordination creates opportunities for learning; it does not directly grant personal or Soul-level progression.

## Relationships Between Factions

Faction relationships are issue-specific and can include alliance, rivalry, trade, patronage, dependence, mutual deterrence, infiltration, shared membership, ideological affinity, negotiated coexistence, competition, or open opposition.

An alliance does not merge knowledge, interests, authority, or capability. A rivalry does not require constant hostility. Separate factions may cooperate on migration and compete over trade; one faction may depend economically on a rival it condemns publicly.

Relations change through actual contact, promises, enforcement, betrayal, changing interests, third parties, succession, material conditions, and remembered consequences. Reputation influences expectations but does not predetermine response.

## Authority, Legitimacy, and Audiences

Factions act before multiple audiences: members, subjects, rivals, dependents, institutions, cultures, gods, spirits, monsters, Soul Weapons, and affected populations. Each audience may judge the same action differently.

Public Reputation, Formal Authority, Cultural Legitimacy, Symbolic Authority, trust, fear, coercion, factual expertise, and enforcement power remain distinct. A Soul Title such as Monster King may influence recognition, fear, opportunities, or expectations without creating office, consent, permanent loyalty, or command over monsters.

Gods, spirits, and Divine Sources may recognize, reject, bargain with, or divide a faction according to their own agency and Jurisdiction. Their recognition does not make a faction morally correct or internally unanimous.

## Continuity and Transformation

**Faction Continuity** is the persistence of recognizable coordination through actual interests, relationships, participants, identity, records, procedures, assets, commitments, and successor recognition. A name alone is insufficient.

A faction may undergo:

- **succession:** new participants inherit some roles, resources, relationships, and unresolved obligations;
- **reform:** objectives or procedures change while enough continuity remains;
- **capture:** a narrow interest redirects decision routes and resources while preserving the outer structure;
- **schism:** incompatible coordination separates into successor factions with contested identity or inheritance;
- **merger:** previously distinct factions combine some structures and claims without erasing prior relationships or dissent;
- **fragmentation:** action routes fail and local groups act independently;
- **dissolution:** coordination no longer persists, though records, debts, reputations, members, assets, and consequences may remain;
- **revival:** later actors reconstruct a faction through surviving routes rather than restoring it automatically from a name or legend.

Whether continuity survives is contextual. A faction can keep a name while becoming materially different, or lose its name while preserving substantial relationships and purposes.

### Reincarnation

Death and Reincarnation do not preserve membership, office, authority, followers, contracts, secrets, or access as Soul property. A reincarnated founder may retain Soul-level memory or Imprints under the [Reincarnation](../soul/REINCARNATION.md) rules, but must encounter, recognize, persuade, negotiate with, infiltrate, or rebuild any world-bound faction through current-life routes.

A faction may remember a prior incarnation through records, witnesses, Soul Titles, prophecies, Soul Weapons, spirits, or surviving relationships. Recognition still does not guarantee identity proof, trust, loyalty, legitimacy, or restored command.

## Cross-System Interfaces

### Soul Engine

Souls retain their own identity, memory, Titles, Resonance, Depth, Echoes, Constellations, and Avatar limits. These may affect recognition, interpretation, relationships, or available evidence, but they do not turn a faction into a Soul construct or make social continuity persist automatically through Reincarnation.

### Development and Skills

Individual members contribute current, embodied capability. Social Development, Leadership Development, Professional Development, Skills, and knowledge affect what particular actors can do reliably. Faction participation creates practice, instruction, and consequence opportunities; it cannot award Development or Skills by affiliation alone.

### Human Classes, Professions, and Institutions

Classes, Professions, Offices, certifications, and Institutions define their own access, authority, procedure, and recognition. A faction may use, contest, span, or capture these structures, but does not replace them. Institutional action remains subject to its dedicated rules.

### Monsters and Evolution

Monster factions use the same agency, information, coordination, and consequence rules while preserving species-specific embodiment, senses, communication, ecology, society, Development, Adaptation, Mutation, and Evolution ownership. A hive, pack, colony, brood, or telepathic network may coordinate unusually closely, but exact unity requires an established mechanism and does not erase personhood by default.

Faction success, loyalty, fear, territory, or leadership cannot directly grant an Evolution Route or Species Stage. It may change access, pressures, relationships, habitats, or opportunities that the Monster Evolution system evaluates.

### Soul Weapons

A Weapon Soul is a person, not faction equipment. It may join, advise, represent, oppose, withdraw support, keep confidence, or pursue overlapping interests according to its own agency and Bond. Custody of a Soul Weapon does not transfer the Weapon Soul's consent or make its knowledge faction property.

### Magic

Magical communication, divination, compulsion, Rituals, Enchantments, Divine Sources, and magical infrastructure use the completed [Magic](../magic/README.md) rules. Magic may alter speed, reach, secrecy, evidence, coordination, enforcement, or risk; it does not create omniscience, unlimited compliance, costless mobilization, or universal access.

### Populations, Resources, Economies, and Ecology

Factions respond to and reshape [Populations](POPULATIONS.md), [Resources and Food](RESOURCES_AND_FOOD.md), [Economies](ECONOMIES.md), and [Ecology and Migration](ECOLOGY_AND_MIGRATION.md) through causal action. They may control routes, allocate burdens, organize care, protect habitats, exploit dependencies, negotiate movement, or conceal scarcity. Those domains retain ownership of their states and consequences.

## Simulation Procedure

When faction behaviour matters:

1. **Scope the faction and issue.** Identify which coordination is relevant, where, and over what interval.
2. **Identify participants and relationships.** Separate leaders, offices, members, supporters, subjects, clients, coerced actors, outsiders, and affected audiences.
3. **State interests and objectives.** Preserve public, formal, operational, and internal differences.
4. **Establish the information position.** Determine who knows or believes what, through which routes, with what uncertainty.
5. **Trace decision routes.** Identify agenda control, authority, influence, vetoes, procedures, and dissent.
6. **Test mobilization.** Locate actual people, capability, resources, access, communication, willingness, timing, and bottlenecks.
7. **Resolve execution.** Apply the owning systems to what particular actors attempt.
8. **Record actual consequences.** Update relationships, resources, information, legitimacy, cohesion, capability, obligations, and external world state where causally supported.
9. **Allow adaptation.** Decide what result evidence reaches whom and what they can plausibly change.
10. **Preserve pending consequences.** Use [Causal Event Chains](CAUSAL_EVENT_CHAINS.md) for delayed, branching, or cross-domain effects.

Use only the detail needed to distinguish meaningful choices and consequences. A quiet trade association need not receive the same resolution as a divided council deciding whether to evacuate a city.

## Worked Examples

### The Council That Has Not Yet Learned

A border scout confirms that wyverns have abandoned their range. The report reaches a clerk, but a storm closes the courier route and the clerk distrusts the scout's monster ancestry.

The capital council does not yet know the confirmed fact. Ranchers hear rumors and move herds; one council faction treats this as panic, another begins private investigation, and local wardens mobilize without central authorization. The faction response is distributed, delayed, and partially contradictory because the information route matters.

### A Monster Corridor Coalition

Several intelligent monster communities depend on one seasonal Migration Corridor. They form a Coalition to negotiate passage with a human canal authority. The communities share an objective but differ over tolls, sacred sites, hunting rights, and whether force is acceptable.

The Coalition can field translators, scouts, and negotiators only where members contribute them. One community may leave after securing an alternate route. Their cooperation does not merge Species Traits, Evolution Routes, cultures, or authority.

### The Reincarnated Founder

A founder reincarnates centuries later and proves knowledge of a guild's lost initiation rite. That is compelling evidence, but the modern guild has new members, debts, laws, patrons, and purposes.

The founder gains attention and perhaps Symbolic Authority. They do not automatically regain office, property, secrets, contracts, loyalty, or control. Different factions within the guild may seek guidance, fear displacement, challenge the proof, or exploit the claim.

### The Weapon Soul Refuses

A military faction plans to use a bonded Soul Weapon as the seal of a contested succession. The wielder supports the plan; the Weapon Soul remembers an earlier betrayal and refuses participation.

The faction cannot treat custody as consent. It may negotiate, find another symbol, conceal the refusal, pressure the wielder, or fracture over legitimacy. The Weapon Soul's choice changes the available route without deciding the political outcome by itself.

### Captured Magical Standards

A licensing Institution was created to verify dangerous Ritual work. A wealthy faction gains control of appointments, testing sites, and records, then burdens rivals while exempting allied workshops.

The Institution still has formal procedures, but its action is captured. Competence, legal authority, public legitimacy, factual safety, and enforcement now diverge. Rival practitioners may form a reform faction, evade licensing, expose records, or create alternate verification routes. None of those reactions is predetermined.

### Scarcity Produces a Schism

A river federation loses half its Effective Supply after upstream contamination. Urban delegates prioritize reserve protection; farming districts demand immediate releases; spirit mediators insist that extraction stop until the source recovers.

The shared faction identity does not yield one response. Unequal burdens, different time horizons, and contested evidence weaken issue-specific cohesion. A provisional bargain might hold, one bloc might capture release authority, or the federation might split. [War and Unrest](WAR_AND_UNREST.md) owns escalation beyond ordinary faction contestation.

## Safeguards

- Do not give a faction one mind, alignment, emotion, memory, knowledge pool, capability score, or inevitable response.
- Do not infer membership, support, consent, loyalty, representation, or obedience from shared species, culture, territory, religion, employment, dependency, or fear.
- Do not let leaders, founders, majorities, Soul Titles, prophecies, or official statements automatically speak for every participant.
- Do not treat a decision, law, command, promise, or plan as implemented without mobilization and execution.
- Do not pool members' Skills, Development, Soul properties, Species Traits, Classes, Soul Weapons, or magical access.
- Do not grant progression, Evolution, authority, legitimacy, or moral correctness through faction success or affiliation alone.
- Do not make cohesion a loyalty meter or disagreement an automatic failure.
- Do not make factions perfectly rational, permanently hostile, passively frozen off-screen, or secretly aware of world truth.
- Do not make diplomacy, betrayal, capture, schism, merger, or reform deterministic outcomes.
- Apply [War and Unrest](WAR_AND_UNREST.md) when faction contestation escalates into Civil Unrest, Armed Conflict, War, occupation, or conflict aftermath; do not use this document to replace that owner.
- Apply [Disease Evolution](DISEASE_EVOLUTION.md) when faction decisions concern Disease Processes, transmission, public health, or disease legacies; do not use faction motives to determine medical truth.
- Do not use this document to replace [Technology and Magical Advancement](TECHNOLOGY_AND_MAGICAL_ADVANCEMENT.md), [Dungeon Activity](DUNGEON_ACTIVITY.md), [World Stability](WORLD_STABILITY.md), or [Ages and World Resets](AGES_AND_WORLD_RESETS.md), or to resolve Gates or long-horizon abstraction before their roadmap tasks are complete.
- Do not store named live factions, current leaders, memberships, objectives, resources, relationships, decisions, or actions in this repository.

## External Campaign Records

A campaign may maintain a qualitative **Faction Profile** containing only the state needed for play:

- name, version, scope, and current identity;
- participant categories, leadership, offices, representation, and boundaries;
- stated purposes, active interests, objectives, and protected commitments;
- information distribution, assumptions, secrecy, and communication routes;
- decision procedures, authority, influence, vetoes, and dissent;
- function-specific capabilities, resources, dependencies, and bottlenecks;
- relationships, audiences, reputation, legitimacy, and opposition;
- cohesion by relevant issue;
- current actions, actual results, Pending Consequences, and uncertainty;
- succession, capture, schism, merger, continuity, and dissolution risks.

This is external campaign state. The repository defines the schema and rules but contains no completed Faction Profile, named live faction, or current world value.
