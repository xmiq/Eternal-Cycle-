# Ages and World Resets

## Purpose

This document defines how Eternal Cycle recognizes major historical eras, resolves transitions between them, and adjudicates exceptional world-scale transformations called World Resets.

Ages and World Resets preserve causal continuity. They can transform geography, ecology, civilization, technology, Magic, species viability, and the practical genre of play without deleting protected Soul history or turning the world into a fresh save file.

Current Ages, transition events, Reset conditions, survivors, timelines, and aftermath belong in an external Campaign Record.

## Core Rule

An **Age** is a contextual historical era whose materially influential arrangements remain coherent enough that observers can describe a recognizable world order across a stated scope and period.

An **Age Transition** occurs when lasting changes make a new Age description more useful and accurate than the former one.

A **World Reset** is an exceptional causal transformation that reconfigures enough coupled foundational world conditions that all affected claims must be revalidated rather than assumed to continue unchanged.

Every World Reset participates in an Age Transition. Not every Age Transition is a World Reset.

Neither event erases prior history. The new world inherits survivors, absences, adaptations, ruins, records, relationships, obligations, damage, and other [World Legacies](WORLD_STATE_VARIABLES.md#legacy-condition) through the routes that actually persist.

## Distinct Forms of Change

| Change | What it means | What it does not mean |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Elapsed time** | Causes and actors continue for a stated interval | Automatic growth, recovery, decay, or historical importance |
| **Time Skip** | The GM compresses narration between two established moments | A world event, causal shortcut, or permission to invent an outcome |
| **Ordinary historical change** | Material conditions alter while the current Age remains a useful description | Stasis or insignificance |
| **Age Transition** | The prior Age Signature no longer describes enough influential conditions, and a successor order becomes materially distinct | Automatic catastrophe, universal agreement, or total replacement |
| **Catastrophe** | A destructive or disruptive event causes scoped losses | Necessarily an Age Transition or World Reset |
| **World Reset** | A causal mechanism reconfigures coupled foundational conditions and forces broad revalidation | A clean slate, world-health correction, or total destruction |
| **Reincarnation transition** | One soul follows the seven-step procedure from Final Death through embodiment | A World Reset or Age Transition merely because a life ended |

Classification follows actual scope and consequences, not the event's dramatic name. A culture may call a royal succession "the Reset" without it qualifying as a World Reset, while a gradual change in world law may qualify even without one visible explosion.

## Ages

### Age Claim

An **Age Claim** is a dated, scoped interpretation that a period has a sufficiently recognizable arrangement of influential conditions to be treated as one historical era.

A valid claim identifies:

- geographic, social, ecological, metaphysical, and temporal scope;
- the audience or tradition using the Age name;
- the conditions considered characteristic;
- major exceptions and regions that do not fit;
- evidence, uncertainty, and contested interpretations;
- the boundaries used for the claim and why they matter.

An Age is not a metaphysical box that makes every place alike. Several Age Claims may overlap, and different cultures may divide the same history differently.

### Age Signature

An **Age Signature** is the selective set of influential arrangements by which an Age Claim is recognized.

It may include:

- dominant or widely consequential political forms;
- important population distributions and species relationships;
- prevailing ecological order and major migration routes;
- common technical infrastructure and constraints;
- active magical laws, sources, institutions, and practices;
- major religions, cosmologies, or divine relationships;
- economic and resource networks;
- known Dungeons, boundaries, and supernatural regions;
- communication, travel, war, health, and knowledge conditions;
- remembered assumptions about what kinds of life and action are possible.

The signature is descriptive. It does not grant capability, enforce conformity, or become a civilization level. A dominant condition may coexist with local alternatives, enclaves, resistance, ruins, and unknown systems.

### Age Boundary

An **Age Boundary** is the evidence-based interval or event by which one Age Claim is separated from another.

Boundaries may be:

- tied to a visible event;
- distributed across decades or centuries;
- different by region or institution;
- recognized only in hindsight;
- politically disputed;
- marked by one calendar while ignored by another.

There is no universal threshold of changed domains, years, casualties, territory, or witnesses. The boundary is useful only if it explains material differences without hiding important continuity.

### Age Names and Perspective

Age names are social and historical claims. "Age of Dragons," "Silent Century," and "Second Dawn" may reflect power, propaganda, scholarship, fear, survival, or later myth.

One name cannot establish that:

- dragons ruled every place;
- all records fell silent;
- dawn literally occurred twice;
- one culture's experience represents the whole world;
- the named group caused every characteristic condition.

The GM should preserve the distinction among world truth, contemporary names, later scholarship, institutional doctrine, and player-facing convenience.

## Age Transitions

An Age Transition is a causal process, not a chapter marker imposed from outside the world.

It normally involves:

- several material conditions changing or one foundational condition propagating widely;
- effects lasting long enough to alter institutions, ecology, practice, or expectation;
- responses and adaptations by autonomous actors;
- meaningful continuity from the preceding world;
- enough difference that a successor Age Claim becomes useful.

Age Transitions may be:

- gradual or abrupt;
- peaceful, contested, or catastrophic;
- intentional or unintended;
- recognized early or only in hindsight;
- world-spanning or limited to the scope of the Age Claim;
- produced with or without a World Reset.

The fall of an empire may begin an Age Transition if its institutions, routes, ecology, technology, and successor orders change broadly enough. The same political fall may remain ordinary change where other arrangements continue.

### Genre Change

An Age may feel like classic fantasy, cultivation, magitech, industrial fantasy, post-collapse survival, floating-island exploration, or another mode because its actual world conditions support those experiences.

Genre is a campaign-facing description of the resulting Age Signature. It is not an in-world force, Reset Trigger, reward, or permission to discard inconvenient canon.

A transition into a different genre must explain:

- which conditions changed;
- what caused those changes;
- what survived and remains recognizable;
- what became inaccessible, obsolete, transformed, or dangerous;
- how people, monsters, institutions, sources, and ecologies responded;
- which old consequences still shape the new Age.

## World Resets

The term *Reset* describes the need to establish a new baseline for many affected claims. It does not mean that history is deleted.

A valid World Reset requires:

1. **Foundational reach.** The transformation affects conditions on which multiple systems or major scopes depend.
2. **Causal basis.** A source, process, actor, or interaction capable of producing the claimed change exists.
3. **Preconditions.** The conditions that make the transformation possible or vulnerable are established.
4. **Trigger.** The event or threshold crossing that begins the active transformation is identified when one exists.
5. **Mechanism.** Each major effect has a route from cause to changed world condition.
6. **Footprint.** Scope, intensity, timing, boundaries, and uneven exposure are recorded.
7. **Survivorship.** Continuity, transformation, dormancy, loss, and uncertainty are resolved by actual owners.
8. **Aftermath.** Autonomous responses, adaptation, conflict, recovery, and legacies continue after the active event.

A Reset can be caused by a natural cycle, interacting magical laws, a divine or cosmic actor, accumulated technical failure, a world-scale organism, a damaged boundary, deliberate intervention, or another established process. None is mandatory. Calling something a World Core or balance mechanism does not grant it unexplained capability or benevolent intent.

### Reset Preconditions

A **Reset Precondition** is an established condition required for a particular World Reset mechanism to become possible, vulnerable, or active.

Examples include:

- several world-law anchors drifting out of compatibility;
- a protective boundary losing all but one maintaining source;
- a planetary organism reaching a species-specific life stage;
- rival divine Domains entering a documented convergence;
- a technical network accumulating correlated failures;
- an actor acquiring access, knowledge, and resources for a deliberate attempt.

Preconditions do not guarantee a Reset. Actors may intervene, dependencies may change, hidden Counterforces may matter, or the expected mechanism may prove misunderstood.

### Reset Trigger

A **Reset Trigger** is the event, action, or local State Threshold that begins a specific Reset Mechanism once its necessary Preconditions are present.

A trigger may be absent where transformation is gradual. When it exists, crossing it establishes only the mechanism it actually activates. One death, sacrifice, prophecy, spell, war, Dungeon clearing, quantity of Mana, or World Stability finding cannot trigger a Reset unless the complete causal route supports that function.

### Reset Mechanism

A **Reset Mechanism** is the established process by which a World Reset changes foundational world conditions.

A mechanism must identify its owners and interfaces. It may alter:

- physical geography, climate, or spatial relations;
- magical laws, sources, Fields, Currents, or boundaries;
- viable embodiments, habitats, and ecological functions;
- technical assumptions, materials, infrastructure, or communication;
- divine jurisdictions or cosmological relationships;
- access among formerly connected or separated regions;
- several coupled conditions through a documented cascade.

The mechanism does not acquire unlimited secondary effects. A law shift that changes Mana conversion does not automatically redraw continents, erase memories, kill every incompatible organism, or restore extinct species.

### Reset Footprint

A **Reset Footprint** is the actual distribution of a World Reset's direct and propagated effects across place, time, population, environment, institution, and metaphysical condition.

World-scale does not mean uniform. The footprint may include:

- unaffected refuges;
- partially transformed border zones;
- delayed effects;
- protected or shielded sites;
- populations exposed through different bodies or dependencies;
- regions changed indirectly through migration, trade, disease, war, or source loss;
- places whose condition remains unknown.

Uneven survival is not a loophole. It is a source of later relationships, inequality, conflict, knowledge, recovery, and continuity.

## Reset Survivorship

**Reset Survivorship** is the owner-by-owner assessment of what continues, transforms, becomes dormant, fails, ends, or remains uncertain through a World Reset.

Survivorship is not one yes-or-no property. For each material claim, determine:

- what exactly existed before the Reset;
- which part was directly affected;
- which dependencies survived;
- whether present conditions remain compatible;
- what changed in access, expression, reliability, recognition, or meaning;
- what evidence survives;
- which new risks, obligations, and opportunities follow.

### Possible Findings

- **Continues:** The claim remains valid under the new conditions.
- **Adapted:** The actor or system changed through a valid route and can continue differently.
- **Transformed:** Enough changed that a new current model is required, with provenance to the former one.
- **Dormant or inaccessible:** Persistent structure remains but cannot presently operate or be reached.
- **Failed or ended:** Its required basis no longer exists or was destroyed.
- **Uncertain:** Evidence is insufficient, contradictory, inaccessible, or still changing.

These findings are contextual, not a hierarchy. One entity may receive several findings for different functions.

## World Revalidation

**World Revalidation** is the post-transition process of testing every material continuing claim against the conditions that now exist.

Revalidation is required when an Age Transition or World Reset changes an assumption on which a claim depends. It asks:

- Does the subject still exist?
- Does the relevant world law still permit the function?
- Are embodiment, source, environment, tools, routes, permissions, and relationships compatible?
- What present-life or current-world work is needed to restore reliability?
- Which consequences or legacies remain even if the function fails?

Revalidation does not require re-proving unrelated facts. A Reset that changes spoken spell formation need not call every friendship, bridge, species, or profession into doubt unless the mechanism affects them.

## Persistence and Ownership

### Souls and Reincarnation

[Reincarnation](../soul/REINCARNATION.md) owns the seven-step personal transition from Final Death through embodiment. An Age Transition or World Reset does not replace, restart, or add stages to that procedure.

A Reset may occur:

- while a soul is embodied;
- during its Interlife;
- before a future candidate exists;
- after a candidate's birth but before the chosen playable entry point.

The Reset does not automatically cause Final Death. If its actual effects kill the current body, ordinary Final Death and Reincarnation rules apply. If the body survives, the incarnation continues in the transformed world.

Soul identity, Soul Imprints, Soul Depth, Soul Resonance, Soul Echoes, Soul Titles, Retained Instincts, Soul Constellations, Soul Space, Archive Attunements, Soul Avatar capacity, Soul Wounds, and other soul-level structures persist only as their Owning Systems state. A Reset does not directly erase, improve, heal, or duplicate them.

Persistent Soul knowledge may be obsolete, inaccessible, mistranslated, or dangerous under new conditions. Persistence is not current access or reliable expression.

### Bodies, Species, and Monster Evolution

Bodies remain subject to the actual Reset Footprint. Changed gravity, atmosphere, Mana ecology, food, disease, or habitat may alter viability without automatically granting Mutation, adaptation, or Evolution.

[Monster Evolution](../monster-evolution/README.md) owns species change, Mutation, Evolution Routes, and Population Evolution. A World Reset may change pressures, opportunities, requirements, and survivorship; it cannot award a new form by category.

Extinction persists unless a surviving, reconstructed, convergent, hybrid, returned, or new-origin population has valid provenance.

### Development, Skills, Classes, and Professions

Persistent potential and current capability remain separate. Old Development and Skill Imprints can support recognition, investigation, translation, and relearning without restoring a former body, institution, toolchain, authority, or reliable expression.

Classes, Professions, credentials, offices, schools, and institutions survive only through their people, practices, records, recognition, and material support. A familiar label in a new Age does not prove the same curriculum or authority.

A Reset, transition, long time skip, survival, suffering, or historical importance grants no automatic Development, Skill, Class, Profession, Stat XP, or mastery.

### Soul Weapons

A Weapon Soul and valid bond retain the persistence granted by [Soul Weapon](../soul-weapons/README.md) rules. Vessels, materials, Manifestation routes, compatible forms, sources, public recognition, and world-bound infrastructure require World Revalidation.

A Soul Weapon is not a Reset key, universal anchor, protected inventory item, or mechanism merely because it persists. Its agency, consent, memory, Soul Intertwining, damage, and current compatibility remain consequential.

### Magic

[Magic and the World Engine](../magic/WORLD_ENGINE_INTERACTIONS.md#ages-and-world-resets) owns the specialist handoff for Mana, sources, affinities, Spell Patterns, Rituals, Enchantments, Alchemy, Divine Magic, restrictions, and magical infrastructure.

A Reset may leave one effect stable, make another Drift, end a source relationship, expose a new route, or invalidate a former law. No magical claim survives or fails merely because it is old, powerful, sacred, forbidden, or carried in Soul memory.

### Akashic Archive and Historical Evidence

World Resets normally leave broad [Archive Traces](../soul/AKASHIC_ARCHIVE.md) and may preserve older Archive strata. They may also destroy Interfaces, change indexing, alter language, and make interpretation harder.

The Archive is neither a complete backup nor a Reset control panel. Reading an old Record does not restore the recorded world, prove every missing detail, or make an obsolete method work.

## World Engine Domain Revalidation

After a World Reset, revalidate only claims touched directly or through actual dependencies.

### Populations and Ecology

Resolve survival, death, reproduction, habitat, movement, displacement, life cycles, ecological functions, succession, and Novel Ecologies through [Populations](POPULATIONS.md) and [Ecology and Migration](ECOLOGY_AND_MIGRATION.md).

Do not treat survivors as unchanged representatives of the old world or new populations as restored versions of extinct ones without provenance.

### Resources and Economies

[Resources and Food](RESOURCES_AND_FOOD.md) and [Economies](ECONOMIES.md) own changed supply, access, quality, renewal, sustenance, production, allocation, obligations, currencies, Prices, and recovery.

Old currency, property, contract, debt, and ownership claims may persist socially even when institutions collapse. Their enforcement, interpretation, legitimacy, and material basis must be resolved rather than silently erased or guaranteed.

### Factions, War, and Unrest

[Faction Behaviour](FACTION_BEHAVIOUR.md) and [War and Unrest](WAR_AND_UNREST.md) own actor responses, mobilization, succession, coercion, resistance, territorial control, cessation, and conflict legacies.

A Reset does not unite survivors, suspend politics, transfer legitimate authority, or make violence inevitable. Actors respond through their own knowledge, goals, relationships, and capability.

### Disease

[Disease Evolution](DISEASE_EVOLUTION.md) owns hosts, exposure, transmission, disease states, agent change, interventions, and legacies. Changed bodies and environments may alter compatibility, but a Reset is not an automatic cure, plague, immunity event, or selective improvement.

### Technology and Magical Advancement

[Technology and Magical Advancement](TECHNOLOGY_AND_MAGICAL_ADVANCEMENT.md) owns technical continuity, adaptation, validation, adoption, implementation, maintenance, decline, and recovery.

A surviving design is not a functioning system without compatible materials, contributors, standards, infrastructure, sources, and maintenance. A new Age may recover an old function through a different Technical System without recreating the former world.

### Dungeons

[Dungeon Activity](DUNGEON_ACTIVITY.md) owns each Dungeon's Sustaining Basis, Topology, inhabitants, resources, activity, collapse, and legacy. A Reset may preserve, transform, disconnect, expose, damage, or end a Dungeon only through its actual footprint and dependencies.

Dungeon survival does not prove immunity, and Dungeon collapse does not imply that every inhabitant or route ended.

### World Stability

[World Stability](WORLD_STABILITY.md) may identify Brittle Stability, World-System Strain, Systemic Coupling, thresholds, and plausible cascading failures before or during a transition.

It does not trigger the Reset, decide what should survive, or become a score carried into the next Age. Afterward, new stability claims must use the transformed conditions and preserve Stability Displacement and other legacies.

## Agency and Intervention

A World Reset may be resisted, accelerated, redirected, narrowed, exploited, survived, or misunderstood when established actors have valid routes to do so.

Intervention requires:

- access to the relevant mechanism;
- adequate information or a bounded attempt under uncertainty;
- current capability and compatible tools or sources;
- authority, consent, or explicit conflict where other persons are affected;
- time and resources;
- consequences for success, partial success, and failure.

No protagonist, Soul Avatar, god, empire, monster, or Weapon Soul becomes the sole necessary world actor by narrative importance. Their action may matter greatly without erasing other participants or off-screen causality.

Preventing one predicted Reset does not freeze history. Pressures, damage, rival plans, depleted buffers, and alternate transitions continue unless separately changed.

## Time Skips and Compression

A **Time Skip** is a narrative change in resolution, not an event that acts on the world.

During a Time Skip:

- establish the start state and interval;
- identify active actors, processes, pressures, and Pending Consequences;
- resolve only the detail needed to preserve material causality;
- keep consequential branches open where uncertainty remains;
- summarize major changes and their routes;
- preserve records, absences, legacies, and discoverable history;
- establish the new present without inventing free progress.

A Time Skip may cross an Age Boundary or contain a World Reset, but the underlying transition still requires its own causes and resolution. Compression does not make an event inevitable or erase choices that would reasonably have occurred during the interval.

[Simulation Abstraction](SIMULATION_ABSTRACTION.md) owns the detailed method for choosing resolution across decades and centuries.

## Knowledge and Uncertainty

Separate:

- actual preconditions, triggers, mechanisms, and footprint;
- warning signs and observed evidence;
- actor predictions and models;
- religious, political, and cultural interpretations;
- later historical accounts;
- player knowledge;
- unresolved future branches.

A prophecy may motivate action without proving its model. A god may understand one Domain but not every consequence. An ancient survivor may remember one region accurately and still misread the new Age. A World-Bearing Archive reading may establish a Trace without exposing every life affected.

The GM must establish enough world truth to preserve consistency while keeping legitimately hidden information and unresolved futures distinct.

## Age and Reset Procedure

When an Age Transition or World Reset matters:

1. **State the transition claim.** Identify scope, period, current question, and whether an Age Transition, Reset, or both are proposed.
2. **Establish the preceding Age Claim.** Record the relevant Age Signature, exceptions, conditions, and legacies.
3. **Identify pressures and actors.** Load World-State Variables, stability findings, goals, dependencies, and Counterforces.
4. **Classify the cause.** For a Reset, establish Preconditions, any Trigger, and the Reset Mechanism; for an ordinary Age Transition, establish the changes producing a successor order.
5. **Resolve direct transformation.** Apply only effects supported by the cause and specialist owners.
6. **Map the footprint.** Record scope, timing, uneven exposure, refuges, boundaries, unknowns, and direct affected parties.
7. **Resolve survivorship.** Test material claims owner by owner; do not use one global survival roll or label.
8. **Resolve autonomous response.** Let populations, factions, sources, gods, monsters, institutions, Weapon Souls, and other actors respond from their own knowledge and capability.
9. **Propagate consequences.** Use [Causal Event Chains](CAUSAL_EVENT_CHAINS.md), including delays, feedback, Counterforces, and Pending Consequences.
10. **Perform World Revalidation.** Test only continuing claims whose assumptions were changed.
11. **Establish the successor Age Claim.** Describe a new Age Signature when enough durable change supports one; preserve contested boundaries and surviving alternatives.
12. **Record legacies and uncertainty.** Keep causes, losses, survivors, transformations, records, obligations, branches, and unresolved facts in the external Campaign Record.

## World Transition Profile

A campaign may maintain an external **World Transition Profile** containing only the detail needed for play:

| Field | What it records |
| --- | --- |
| **Transition claim** | Proposed classification, scope, interval, current question, evidence, and uncertainty. |
| **Preceding Age Claim** | Age name or names, audience, Signature, exceptions, boundaries, and inherited legacies. |
| **Pressures and actors** | Relevant world conditions, World-System Strain, goals, dependencies, and Counterforces. |
| **Reset basis** | Preconditions, Trigger where one exists, Mechanism, owners, and unknowns. |
| **Footprint** | Direct and propagated scope, timing, exposure, refuges, boundaries, and distribution. |
| **Survivorship** | Continuing, adapted, transformed, dormant, failed, ended, and uncertain claims by owner. |
| **Responses** | Intervention, refusal, exploitation, migration, protection, conflict, adaptation, and recovery. |
| **World Revalidation** | Changed assumptions and the continuing claims tested against them. |
| **Successor Age Claim** | Emerging Signature, exceptions, names, boundaries, and disputed interpretations. |
| **Legacies** | Survivors, absences, ruins, records, relationships, obligations, ecological change, and persistent consequences. |
| **Pending Consequences** | Delays, unresolved branches, maintenance burdens, and later review conditions. |

This Profile is live campaign state and remains outside this repository.

## Worked Examples

### Gradual Magitech Age Without a Reset

Over two centuries, several cultures validate repeatable Enchantments, build source agreements, train maintainers, standardize interfaces, and reorganize trade around magical transport. Older practices remain in remote regions and among groups excluded by the standards.

The eventual Age Transition is real because institutions, routes, work, ecology, and expectations changed durably. No World Reset occurred: each change followed ordinary technical, magical, political, and economic causality.

### Spoken Magic Becomes Embodied Signs

Several world-law anchors drift until a final incompatibility activates a documented reconfiguration. Spoken Spell Patterns lose their former Mana relation, embodied signs become viable, old Enchantments respond unevenly, and some protected sources preserve local exceptions.

This is a World Reset. Magic is revalidated effect by effect. Soul-carried knowledge helps recognize principles but does not make old speech work. Archive strata and ruined schools preserve the former Age without restoring it.

### A Reset While the Protagonist Lives

A subterranean world-organism enters a metamorphic stage that folds mountain ranges and redirects Mana Currents. The protagonist survives in a shielded valley while distant populations migrate, die, adapt, or remain unknown.

No automatic Final Death or Reincarnation occurs. The current body, relationships, inventory, and choices continue subject to the actual footprint. Survival grants no automatic progression.

### Interlife Across an Age Boundary

A monster ruler reaches Final Death before an extended ecological and political transition. During centuries of Interlife, successor factions divide the realm, climate changes migration routes, and a later Reset alters magical law.

The soul's seven-step Reincarnation procedure continues. Candidate generation uses the new world state. The old empire remains through descendants, ruins, debts, Titles, Echo memories, Archive Traces, and contested history rather than current authority.

### Stability Crisis Without an Age Transition

A portal-dependent food network approaches cascading failure. Regions diversify routes, negotiate source support, ration reserves, and repair local storage. Severe losses occur, but the major institutions and world assumptions remain recognizable.

The crisis changes history and leaves legacies. It neither requires a new Age Claim nor proves that a World Reset almost happened.

### Collapse of an Empire Begins an Age

An empire fragments after succession conflict. Trade routes, legal systems, military boundaries, schools, and irrigation institutions reorganize across several successor states. Neighboring monster societies expand diplomatic and ecological roles.

The transition may justify a new Age Claim without a World Reset. The former empire's roads, debts, languages, displaced communities, and institutions continue to shape the successor Age.

### Ancient Dungeon Survives Unevenly

A World Reset changes spatial Magic. An ancient Dungeon's physical caverns remain, one teleportation route fails, its Core loses a coordinating Function, inhabitants open new surface paths, and sealed archives become accessible.

The Dungeon neither survives unchanged nor resets cleanly. Each structure, route, Function, population, and hazard receives its own survivorship finding.

### A Failed Attempt to Reset the World

A faction assembles a Ritual intended to rewrite world law. Opponents break two anchors; the Ritual still contaminates a regional Mana Current and strands several divine agents but never reaches foundational world scale.

Intent and name do not determine classification. The result is a major magical catastrophe with possible historical consequences, not necessarily a World Reset. Its damage cannot be inflated to match the faction's plan.

### Soul Weapon Across Changed Laws

A Weapon Soul and Bonded Soul persist through a Reset that removes the source used by their former ranged Manifestation. The Weapon Soul remembers every prior use, but the route is dormant.

They may investigate another form through consent, compatible materials, Skills, and current-world sources. Persistence does not preserve identical expression or turn the Weapon Soul into a universal Reset survivor.

## Safeguards

- Never use an Age as one homogeneous civilization, culture, species order, technology level, or magical rule without stating scope and exceptions.
- Never let an Age name establish world truth by itself.
- Never require a fixed duration, event count, death toll, changed-domain count, or genre shift for an Age Boundary.
- Never make genre the cause of a transition.
- Never classify every catastrophe, war, dynasty, migration, discovery, or technological change as an Age Transition.
- Never classify an event as a World Reset from name, intent, scale of spectacle, prophecy, or narrative importance alone.
- Never use World Stability as a Reset meter or zero-state trigger.
- Never give a World Core, god, spell, artifact, Dungeon, Soul, Soul Avatar, sacrifice, or death unexplained Reset authority.
- Never apply a Reset effect outside its mechanism and footprint.
- Never assume world-scale means uniform, total, instantaneous, or equally known.
- Never erase survivors, absences, bodies, ruins, records, relationships, debts, claims, ecology, damage, or other World Legacies.
- Never restore extinct species, destroyed institutions, lost infrastructure, depleted resources, or broken relationships without a valid route.
- Never let a Reset automatically kill, reincarnate, heal, improve, corrupt, awaken, or duplicate a Soul or body.
- Never let persistent memory, Skill XP, Stat XP, Soul systems, or Soul Weapon history bypass World Revalidation and current embodiment.
- Never award progression for survival, suffering, historical importance, elapsed time, or crossing an Age Boundary.
- Never use a Time Skip to hide causally important choices, manufacture progress, or silently settle an unresolved branch.
- Never make the player the cause, target, sole solution, or sole witness of a transition by narrative gravity.
- Never store a current Age, transition timeline, Reset mechanism, survivors, changed laws, or World Transition Profile in this repository.

## Scope Boundaries

This document does not define:

- one canonical world history, current Age, timeline, calendar, geography, or Reset cycle;
- a universal number or formula for Age recognition or Reset qualification;
- a World Stability trigger, balance correction, or mandatory apocalypse;
- World Gate causes, lifecycles, transit, or contact consequences owned by [World Gates and World-Contact Events](GATES_AND_WORLD_CONTACT.md);
- Gate interaction with Reincarnation or Soul Avatars;
- the long-horizon change-of-resolution procedure owned by [Simulation Abstraction](SIMULATION_ABSTRACTION.md);
- personal Reincarnation stages, Soul persistence, progression, Evolution, Soul Weapon, or Magic effects beyond their owner interfaces;
- Age, catastrophe, Reset, setting, theme, or genre generators;
- campaign events, named survivors, live world state, or playthrough history.
